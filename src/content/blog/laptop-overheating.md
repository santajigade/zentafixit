A laptop packs a fast processor, a graphics chip and a battery into a thin case, and a small fan has to pull all that heat out through narrow vents. When anything blocks that airflow, or the chips work harder than usual, the laptop gets hot, the fan roars, and performance drops.

The usual causes are **blocked vents from a soft surface**, **dust in the fan**, **a background app keeping the processor busy**, and **a high-performance power setting**. Less often it's a **failing fan**, **dried-out thermal paste** or **a swollen battery**. Start with the free, quick fixes below.

> **Safety first:** If the case is bulging, the trackpad lifts or the lid won't close flat, the battery may be swollen. Stop using the laptop, unplug it, and don't press on it or try to puncture it. When you use compressed air, power off and unplug first, keep the can upright, use short bursts, and hold the fan still so it can't overspin. Don't open a laptop that is under warranty unless the maker says that's allowed.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. Feel where the heat is. Hot near the hinge or side vent is normal under load. Hot all over the bottom, or hot near the palm rest, points to blocked airflow or the battery.
2. Listen to the fan. Loud and steady means it's working hard. Grinding, clicking or silent while the laptop is hot points to a fan problem.
3. Close everything and wait five minutes on a desk. If it cools down, the cause is your apps or the surface. If it stays hot doing nothing, check background processes and dust.
4. Look at the case from the side. Any bulge, gap or lifted trackpad means stop and go straight to cause 8.

## 1. Blocked vents or a soft surface

Most laptops pull cool air in through vents on the bottom or sides. A bed, couch, pillow or your lap squashes those vents shut, so the fan moves less air and heat builds up fast.

**Fix:**

- Use the laptop on a desk, table or a lap desk with a hard top.
- Keep at least a few inches of open space behind and beside the exhaust vent. Don't push it against a wall or a stack of books.
- A simple laptop stand or cooling pad lifts the bottom so air can reach the intake vents.

## 2. Dust in the fans and vents

Over months, dust and pet hair collect on the fan blades and pack into the thin fins of the heat sink. Air can't pass through, so the fan spins faster and louder while the laptop still runs hot.

**Fix:**

1. Shut the laptop down fully and unplug the charger.
2. Find the intake and exhaust vents (check the bottom and the back edge).
3. If you can see the fan through a grille, hold one blade still with a toothpick or plastic tool.
4. Blow **short bursts** of compressed air into the exhaust vent first, then the intake vents. Keep the can upright so liquid propellant doesn't spray out.
5. Brush lint off the vent grilles with a soft, dry brush.

If you smoke, have pets or use the laptop in a dusty room, repeat this every few months.

## 3. Background apps working the processor

A stuck browser tab, a sync app, an antivirus scan or an update can keep the processor near full power even when you aren't doing anything. Full power means full heat.

**Fix (Windows):**

1. Press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc** to open **Task Manager**.
2. Click **More details** if you see a short list, then sort by the **CPU** column.
3. If one app sits high for minutes, save your work and close it, or right-click it and choose **End task**.
4. Check the **Startup apps** list and turn off anything you don't need at boot.

**Fix (Mac):**

1. Open **Activity Monitor** from Applications, then Utilities.
2. Click the **CPU** tab and sort by **% CPU**.
3. Quit anything that stays high and you don't recognize as a current task.

A few minutes of high CPU after a restart or during an update is normal. For deeper cleanup, see our guides to a [slow Windows computer](/pc-laptops/computer-running-slow-windows/) and a [slow Mac](/pc-laptops/mac-running-slow/).

## 4. A high-performance power mode

Laptops let you trade battery life and heat for speed. A "best performance" setting lets the chips run hotter and harder, and many gaming laptops add their own turbo modes on top.

**Fix:**

- **Windows 11:** go to **Settings**, then **System**, then **Power & battery**, and set **Power mode** to **Balanced** or a more efficient option.
- **Mac:** **Low Power Mode** is in **System Settings**, then **Battery**, on recent versions of macOS.
- If your laptop came with the maker's control app (common on gaming laptops), look for a quiet, balanced or silent profile. Check your manual for where it lives.

## 5. A hot room, sunlight or a hot charger

The fan can only cool the laptop down toward room temperature. In a hot car, a sunny window or a stuffy room in summer, it starts from a disadvantage. Charging also adds heat, especially from a fast charger while you game or edit video.

**Fix:**

- Move out of direct sunlight and away from heaters. Never leave a laptop in a parked car.
- Let a hot laptop cool before charging it. Many laptops pause charging when the battery is too warm, which is one reason a laptop can be [plugged in but not charging](/pc-laptops/laptop-plugged-in-not-charging/).
- Use the original charger or one rated for your laptop. A charger that is too hot to hold, smells burnt or has a damaged cable should be replaced.

## 6. A failing fan

Fans have bearings that wear out. A worn fan may grind, rattle, click or whine, spin slowly, or stop altogether, and the laptop then overheats under even light use.

**Fix:**

- Clean the fan first (cause 2). Dust on the blades can cause noise too.
- Run the built-in hardware diagnostics if your laptop has them. Many makers offer a diagnostic tool at startup or in their support app, and Macs have **Apple Diagnostics**. Check your maker's support site for how to start it.
- Note that some thin laptops, such as fanless MacBook Air models, have no fan at all, so silence is normal there.
- A failed fan needs replacing. On many laptops it's a single part, but it means opening the case.

## 7. Old, dried-out thermal paste

A thin layer of thermal paste sits between the processor and its heat sink to carry heat across. After several years it can dry out and work less well, so temperatures creep up even with a clean fan.

**Fix:**

- This is a job for someone comfortable taking a laptop apart. The heat sink must come off, the old paste must be cleaned away, and fresh paste applied.
- If your laptop is a few years old, out of warranty and still hot after cleaning, ask a repair shop to clean the fan and replace the paste at the same visit.

## 8. A swollen battery

Lithium-ion batteries can swell as they age or after heat damage. A swollen battery may make the laptop run warm near the palm rest, push the trackpad up, lift the keyboard or stop the lid closing flat.

**Fix:**

- **Stop using the laptop and unplug it.** Don't charge it again.
- Don't press on the bulge, puncture it or try to flatten it.
- Store it on a hard, non-flammable surface away from anything that can burn, and take it to the maker's service center or a repair shop for a battery replacement.
- Don't throw a swollen battery in the trash. Ask the repair shop to recycle it, or use a battery drop-off point.

## Shuts down from heat or won't stay on?

When a laptop switches itself off without warning, often while gaming, streaming or on a video call, it's usually a thermal shutdown: the chip hit its limit and the laptop cut power to protect itself.

1. Let it cool completely, for 15 to 30 minutes, on a hard surface.
2. Start it up and work through causes 1 to 4 before doing anything demanding.
3. If it shuts off again during light use, the fan or cooling system is likely failing (causes 6 and 7).

Safe operating temperatures vary by chip and laptop design, so there's no one number to aim for. If you want to check, use the maker's support app or the chip maker's specifications for your model rather than a figure from a forum.

## When to see a repair shop

Take the laptop to the maker's service center or a trusted repair shop if:

- The battery looks swollen or the case is bulging.
- The fan grinds, clicks or doesn't spin while the laptop is hot.
- It still overheats or shuts down after you've cleaned the vents and checked your apps and power settings.
- It needs a new fan or fresh thermal paste and you're not comfortable opening it.
- It's under warranty. Opening it yourself may void coverage, so let the maker handle the repair.
