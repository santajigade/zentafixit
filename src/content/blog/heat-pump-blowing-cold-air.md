A heat pump moves heat from the outdoor air into your home. When it's blowing cold air in heat mode, it's either **doing something normal that feels wrong**, or it has stopped moving heat.

The most common causes are **the defrost cycle, a thermostat setting, the backup (aux) heat, a dirty filter or an iced outdoor unit**. Several are normal or easy to fix.

> **Safety first:** Turn off power at the thermostat and the outdoor disconnect before touching the outdoor unit. Never chip ice off the coil with tools; the fins and refrigerant tubes are easy to puncture. Leave electrical parts, refrigerant and the reversing valve to a licensed HVAC technician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the thermostat.** Mode on **HEAT**, fan on **AUTO**, set point a few degrees above room temperature.
2. **Time the cold air.** If it lasts only a few minutes and then warm air returns, it's probably defrost (cause 1).
3. **Look at the outdoor unit.** Is the fan spinning and the unit humming? Is it covered in a solid block of ice?
4. **Measure the air.** After 15 minutes of heating, hold a thermometer at a supply vent and one at the return grille. Supply air should be clearly warmer than the return air. If it's about the same, the heat pump isn't heating.
5. **Check the filter.** A grey, clogged filter cuts airflow and can make the system shut down on safety limits.

## 1. The defrost cycle (normal)

In cold, damp weather, frost builds up on the outdoor coil. Every so often the heat pump **reverses for a few minutes** to melt it. During defrost the outdoor fan usually stops, you may see **steam rising** from the unit, and the indoor air can feel cool.

**Fix:**

- Nothing to fix if it lasts a few minutes and heating returns on its own.
- Many systems turn on the aux heat during defrost to warm the air. If you get noticeably cold air every defrost, the aux heat may not be working (cause 4).
- If defrost seems to run constantly, or the coil stays iced after defrost, see cause 6.

## 2. Heat pump air feels cooler than a furnace

A gas furnace blows short bursts of **very hot air**. A heat pump delivers **warm, not hot, air** for longer periods. That air is often below your skin temperature, so it can feel cool on your hand at the vent, even though it's warmer than the room.

**Fix:**

- Use the thermometer test above. If supply air is clearly warmer than return air and the house is slowly reaching the set point, it's working normally.
- Avoid big jumps in temperature. Set it and leave it. Large changes can bring on the costly aux heat.
- Keep doors to heated rooms open and supply vents uncovered.

## 3. Thermostat mode and settings

Simple settings cause many "cold air" calls.

**Fix:**

- **Fan ON vs AUTO:** with the fan on **ON**, the blower runs between heating cycles and circulates room air, which feels cool. Set it to **AUTO**.
- **Mode:** make sure it says **HEAT**, not COOL, AUTO with a low set point, or OFF.
- **New or replaced thermostat?** A heat pump uses an **O or B wire** to switch the reversing valve between heating and cooling. If the thermostat is set up for the wrong one, the system **cools when you ask for heat** and heats when you ask for cooling. Check the thermostat's installer settings against your system's manual. Our guide to [thermostat wire colors and terminals](/hvac/thermostat-wire-colors/) explains O and B.
- **System type:** the thermostat must be set as a **heat pump**, not a furnace or "conventional" system.
- **Batteries:** replace them if the display is dim or blank.

## 4. Aux heat not working

When it's very cold outside, a heat pump's output drops. Most homes have **auxiliary heat**, usually electric heat strips in the indoor air handler, that kicks in to make up the difference. If that backup has failed, you'll notice cold air mostly on the **coldest days** and during defrost.

**Fix:**

- Check your breaker panel. Heat strips often have their **own breaker or breakers**, sometimes at the air handler too. Reset any that have tripped, once.
- Watch the thermostat when it's cold out. Many show "AUX" or "AUX HEAT" when the backup is on. If it shows AUX but the air stays cool, the heat strips may have failed.
- If a heat strip breaker trips again, don't keep resetting it. Call a technician.

## 5. Dirty air filter

A clogged filter starves the indoor coil of air. The system runs hot, can trip a safety limit, and may shut off heating while the blower keeps running.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the system off at the thermostat.
2. Find the filter at the air handler or behind a return grille. Note the size printed on the frame.
3. Replace it if it's grey or you can't see light through it. The arrow points toward the air handler.
4. Check it monthly during heating season.

## 6. Iced-up outdoor unit

Light frost that clears during defrost is normal. A **thick block of ice** covering the coil, or ice that never clears, is not. A heavily iced unit can't absorb heat.

Common causes include a **defrost control or sensor fault**, **snow or leaves** blocking the unit, **water dripping from a gutter** onto it, or **low refrigerant**.

**Fix:**

- Clear snow, leaves and debris from around and on top of the unit. Keep it raised above snow level.
- Fix any gutter that drips onto the unit.
- To melt ice, switch the thermostat to **emergency heat** (or off) and gently run **water from a garden hose** over the coil. Never chip it with tools.
- If it ices up again within a day or two, call a technician. The defrost controls or refrigerant charge need checking.

## 7. Outdoor unit has no power

If the indoor blower runs but the outdoor unit is silent, the heat pump isn't heating. You'll get cool air unless the aux heat covers it.

**Fix:**

- Check the **breaker** for the outdoor unit. Reset it once if it tripped.
- Check the **outdoor disconnect**, a box on the wall near the unit. Make sure the switch or pull-out block is in the ON position.
- If the breaker trips again, or the unit **hums but the fan doesn't spin**, call a technician. It could be a capacitor or motor problem.

## 8. Reversing valve or refrigerant problem (pro only)

The **reversing valve** switches the heat pump between heating and cooling. If it sticks in cooling, the system blows cold air in heat mode even with correct settings. **Low refrigerant** from a leak causes weak heating and often icing.

**Fix:** These need a licensed HVAC technician. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification, and diagnosing the valve needs gauges and electrical tests.

## Blowing cold air all the time?

If the air is cold no matter what, run through these in order:

- Thermostat on **HEAT**, fan **AUTO**, set point above room temperature, set up as a heat pump.
- Outdoor unit **running**, with power at the breaker and disconnect.
- Filter clean, coil not blocked with ice.
- Turn on **emergency heat** to stay warm while you wait for a technician.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed technician if:

- The outdoor unit **ices up repeatedly** or never clears.
- Air stays cold with correct settings and the outdoor unit running.
- A breaker for the outdoor unit or heat strips **trips again**.
- The outdoor unit **hums, buzzes or clicks** but doesn't start.
- You suspect a refrigerant leak (hissing, oily spots on the lines, weak heating and icing).

Annual maintenance before heating season helps catch weak parts and low refrigerant before a cold snap does.
