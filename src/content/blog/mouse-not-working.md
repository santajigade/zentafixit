When a mouse stops working, the cursor freezes, jumps around, or clicks do nothing. It's frustrating, but the cause is almost always simple.

Most cases come down to **a loose USB plug or receiver, flat batteries, a dropped Bluetooth connection, or a dirty sensor or shiny surface**. Work through the fixes below in order. They go from quickest to most involved.

## How to tell what's wrong: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/mouse-not-working-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: try the mouse in a different USB port; if it works the old port was the problem; if not, try the mouse on another computer; if it fails there too, the mouse is faulty; if it works there, check drivers, power settings and Bluetooth on your PC" width="640" height="420" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A second port and a second computer split the problem in two.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Restart the computer.** A restart clears many temporary glitches. You can do it with the keyboard: press the Windows key, then Tab and the arrow keys to reach the power button.
2. **Try a different USB port** for a wired mouse or a wireless receiver.
3. **Test the mouse on another computer.** If it fails there too, the mouse (or its batteries) is the problem. If it works, the problem is on your PC.
4. **Try a different mouse on your PC.** If a second mouse works, your original mouse is faulty.

## 1. Check the USB connection or receiver

A wired plug or a tiny wireless receiver can work loose, especially on a laptop that gets moved around. Front panel ports and USB hubs are a common weak spot.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the mouse or receiver, wait 10 seconds and plug it back in firmly.
- Use a different port, ideally one on the back of a desktop or directly on the laptop, not a hub.
- For a wired mouse, look along the cable for kinks, cuts or a bent plug.
- If a receiver was lost or swapped, note that most receivers are paired to their own mouse. A replacement may need the maker's pairing software.

## 2. Replace or charge the batteries

Low batteries are the top cause of a wireless mouse that stops working or gets sluggish. Many mice keep working at low charge, but the cursor lags or drops out first.

**Fix:**

- Check the **power switch** on the bottom. It's easy to bump to OFF.
- Put in fresh batteries, or charge a rechargeable mouse for at least 15 to 30 minutes.
- Make sure the batteries face the right way and the contacts are clean and not corroded.
- Turn the mouse off and back on after changing batteries.

## 3. Re-pair a Bluetooth mouse

Bluetooth connections can drop after an update, after the mouse was paired with another device, or when batteries ran flat.

**Fix:**

1. Open **Settings > Bluetooth & devices** and make sure Bluetooth is on.
2. Find the mouse, choose **Remove device**, and confirm.
3. Put the mouse in pairing mode. This is usually done by holding a pairing or connect button until a light blinks. Check the manual for your model.
4. Choose **Add device > Bluetooth** and select the mouse.

If Bluetooth itself is missing or keeps dropping, see our guide on [Bluetooth that won't connect](/phones-tablets/bluetooth-wont-connect/).

## 4. Clean the sensor and check the surface

Optical and laser mice track the surface under them. A hair or dust on the sensor, or a glass, glossy or mirrored desk, makes the cursor jump, drift or stop.

**Fix:**

- Turn the mouse over and gently clean the sensor window with a dry cotton swab or a puff of air.
- Remove hair and lint stuck to the feet.
- Try a plain mouse pad or a sheet of paper. Most mice struggle on glass and very shiny surfaces.

If the cursor moves on its own, check that nothing is resting on the mouse and that a laptop touchpad isn't being brushed by your palm.

## 5. Move the receiver away from interference

Wireless mice use the crowded 2.4 GHz band, shared with Wi-Fi routers and other wireless devices. A receiver tucked behind a metal PC case, or plugged in right next to a busy USB 3.0 port or drive, can lag or drop out.

**Fix:**

- Move the receiver to a port closer to the mouse, with a clear line of sight.
- Use a short USB extension cable to bring the receiver up onto the desk.
- Keep the receiver away from USB 3.0 drives, the Wi-Fi router and other wireless receivers.

## 6. Update or reinstall the mouse driver

Windows uses a built-in driver for nearly every mouse, but it can get stuck after an update or a crash.

**Fix:**

1. Press **Windows key + X** and choose **Device Manager** (use the arrow keys and Enter if the mouse is dead).
2. Expand **Mice and other pointing devices**.
3. Right-click your mouse (or press Shift + F10) and choose **Uninstall device**.
4. Restart the PC. Windows reinstalls the driver automatically.

If the mouse stopped working right after installing the maker's software, uninstall that software and restart.

## 7. Turn off USB power saving

Windows can switch off USB devices to save power. On some PCs this makes a mouse stop responding until you replug it.

**Fix:**

1. In **Device Manager**, expand **Universal Serial Bus controllers**.
2. Open each **USB Root Hub** entry, go to the **Power Management** tab if it's there, and untick **Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power**.
3. Restart and test the mouse.

## 8. Worn buttons or a failing mouse

Mouse switches wear out. Common signs are single clicks that register as double clicks, drag and drop that lets go on its own, or a scroll wheel that jumps.

**Fix:**

- Test the buttons on another computer. If the fault follows the mouse, the mouse is the problem.
- Check the maker's warranty. Many mice have one, and a repair or replacement may be free.
- Otherwise, replace the mouse. A basic wired or wireless mouse is inexpensive.

## Mouse dead and nothing helped?

- **Laptop users:** use the touchpad, or turn it back on if it was disabled. Many laptops have a function key for this. Check the manual.
- **Use the keyboard** to get around: Windows key to search, Tab and arrows to move, Enter to select.
- **Turn on Mouse Keys** with Left Alt + Left Shift + Num Lock to move the cursor with the number pad until you sort it out.
- **Try a different mouse.** If a new mouse works fine, the old one has failed.

## When to call a pro

Call a computer repair shop if:

- No USB device works in any port, which can point to a motherboard or power problem.
- Several mice fail on your PC but work on other computers, even after a driver reinstall.
- A laptop touchpad and external mouse both stop working at the same time.

If your keyboard has stopped working too, our guide on a [keyboard not working](/gadgets/keyboard-not-working/) covers the shared causes.
