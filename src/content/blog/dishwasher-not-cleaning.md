When a dishwasher runs a full cycle and the dishes still come out with food stuck on, a gritty coating or a cloudy film, the machine isn't getting enough clean, hot, soapy water onto every surface. That can happen in the filter, the spray arms, the racks, the detergent or the water supply.

The most common causes are **a dirty filter, clogged spray arms, dishes blocking the water, the wrong detergent amount and water that isn't hot enough**. Most are free to fix in under an hour.

> **Safety first:** Turn off the dishwasher at its breaker, or unplug it if it plugs in under the sink, before removing the filter, spray arms or kick plate. Let the tub cool after a cycle, since the water and heating element can be very hot. Watch for broken glass in the bottom of the tub.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Look at what's left on the dishes.** Food bits point to the filter, spray arms or loading. A white film points to hard water or detergent. Undissolved detergent points to water temperature, fill or the dispenser.
2. **Check where the dirt is.** Dirty only on the top rack or only on one side usually means one spray arm is blocked or not spinning.
3. **Test your hot water.** Run the kitchen tap until it's hot, fill a cup and check it with a cooking thermometer. Around 120°F is a common guideline.
4. **Watch the fill.** A few minutes into a cycle, carefully open the door (most models pause when you do). You should see water covering the bottom of the tub around the filter. Little or none points to cause 8.

## 1. Dirty or clogged filter

Most newer dishwashers have a **manual-clean filter** in the floor of the tub under the lower spray arm. When it fills with food, the pump recirculates dirty water and redeposits grit on your dishes.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off power and pull out the bottom rack.
2. Twist the cylindrical filter (often a quarter turn counterclockwise) and lift it out, plus any flat mesh screen under it.
3. Rinse both under warm water and scrub with a soft brush and a little dish soap. Don't use a wire brush.
4. Check the opening for glass, seeds or bones, then reinstall the filter and lock it in place.

If water is left sitting in the bottom after a cycle, see our guide to a [dishwasher not draining](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/), since dirty water that stays in the tub ends up back on the dishes.

## 2. Clogged or stuck spray arms

Spray arms spin and push water through small holes. Seeds, labels and mineral scale can block those holes, and a cracked or jammed arm won't spin at all. One dirty rack is the classic sign.

**Fix:**

- With power off, spin each arm by hand. It should turn freely and not hit any dishes or utensils.
- Remove the arms (most unscrew, twist off or unclip; check your manual) and look through the holes toward a light.
- Clear blocked holes with a toothpick or wooden skewer and rinse the arm inside with warm water.
- Soak scaled arms in a dishwasher cleaner or warm water with a little citric acid, following the product label.
- Replace any arm that's cracked or warped.

## 3. Dishes loaded in the way

Water has to reach every dirty surface. Overloaded racks, nested bowls and big pans laid flat create shadows the spray can't reach, and tall items can stop an arm from spinning.

**Fix:**

- Face the dirty side of plates and bowls toward the center, where the spray comes from.
- Don't nest bowls or spoons, and leave space between items.
- Put large pots and pans on the bottom rack, angled down, not on top of other dishes.
- Keep tall items, cutting boards and sheet pans away from the detergent cup door and the spray arms. Spin the arms by hand after loading.
- Scrape off food scraps, but you don't usually need to pre-rinse.

## 4. Wrong detergent or the wrong amount

Too little detergent leaves food and grease behind. Too much, especially in soft water, can leave a gritty film and over time etch glassware. Old powder that has clumped from humidity also loses its punch.

**Fix:**

- Use only automatic dishwasher detergent. Never use hand dish soap, which makes heavy suds that can leak out of the door.
- Follow the detergent label and your manual for the amount. Use more for hard water and heavy soil, less for soft water.
- Buy smaller packs and store detergent in a cool, dry spot. Replace clumped powder.
- Put pods and tablets in the main dispenser and close the lid, unless your manual says otherwise.
- Make sure the dispenser lid isn't sticky or blocked. After a cycle it should be open and empty.

## 5. Empty rinse aid dispenser

Rinse aid helps water sheet off dishes in the final rinse, so it doesn't dry into spots and film. Many dishwashers are designed to be used with it, and drying often suffers without it too.

**Fix:**

- Find the rinse aid cap on the inside of the door, next to the detergent cup. Fill it to the line and wipe up any spills, which can cause suds.
- Many models have an indicator that shows when it's low. Check your manual for how to read it and how to raise the dose setting if spots continue.

## 6. Hard water film and buildup

Hard water carries calcium and magnesium. They leave a white, chalky film on glasses and inside the tub, and they can scale up the spray arms and filter. A quick test tells you whether the film is mineral or etching.

**Fix:**

1. Soak a cloudy glass in white vinegar for about five minutes. If the film wipes off, it's mineral buildup. If it stays, the glass is etched, and that damage is permanent (see cause 4).
2. Run an empty cycle with a dishwasher cleaner made for hard water, following its label. Check your manual before using vinegar or other acids in the tub.
3. Keep rinse aid filled and use a detergent formulated for hard water.
4. If you have a water softener, make sure it has salt and is regenerating. A failed softener lets hard water through.

## 7. Water not hot enough

Detergent dissolves and cuts grease best in hot water. If the first fill is cold water that was sitting in the pipe, pods may not dissolve and grease stays put. Many dishwashers heat the water further during the cycle, but they work best when the supply is already hot.

**Fix:**

- Run the kitchen tap hot until it stays hot before you start the dishwasher.
- Check the tap temperature with a cooking thermometer. Around 120°F is a common guideline. Turning a water heater much higher raises the risk of scalding at other taps, so don't go above what's safe for your home.
- If the tap never gets hot, the problem is the water heater. See our [no hot water](/appliances/no-hot-water/) guide.
- Turn on any high temperature or sanitize option your model offers for heavily soiled loads.
- If the dishes and tub feel cool at the end of a cycle even with hot tap water, the dishwasher's heater or temperature sensor may have failed. That's a technician job.

## 8. Not enough water coming in

A dishwasher uses a fairly small amount of water, so a weak fill means the spray arms can't build pressure. The cause is usually a partly closed supply valve, a kinked supply line, a clogged inlet screen or a stuck float.

**Fix:**

- Under the sink, find the dishwasher's water shutoff valve and make sure it's fully open. Check the supply line for kinks.
- Find the float in the tub floor (often a small dome or cylinder near the front). Lift it gently: it should move up and down freely. Clean away any debris around it.
- Low water pressure throughout the house affects fill too. Check other taps.
- The water inlet valve, behind the lower kick plate, has a small screen that can clog with sediment. Turn off both the power and the water before removing the kick plate. If the screen is clear and the fill is still low, the valve may need replacing. If you're not comfortable with water and wiring connections, call a technician.

## 9. The wrong wash cycle

Quick or express cycles use shorter wash times and are meant for lightly soiled loads. Using them for dried-on food or greasy pans often leaves dishes dirty. Eco cycles are designed to clean well but run longer and use less water, so they can struggle with very heavy soil.

**Fix:**

- Use Normal or Auto for everyday loads and Heavy or Pots for baked-on food.
- Save Quick cycles for lightly used dishes you're running soon after a meal.
- Don't stop the cycle early or open the door often. Your manual explains what each cycle and option does on your model.

## Still dirty after every fix?

- Run an empty cycle with a dishwasher cleaner to clear grease and scale from the tub, arms and hoses.
- Run the garbage disposal before starting the dishwasher, since both drain into the same pipe.
- Listen during the wash. A steady swishing sound means the pump is spraying. A hum with little spraying, or silence, points to a pump problem.
- If a code appears on the display, look up the exact code in your owner's manual or on the maker's website. Codes differ by brand, so don't guess.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician if:

- The filter, spray arms, detergent and loading are all sorted and the dishes still come out dirty.
- The tub barely fills, or fills with cold water even though the tap runs hot.
- The wash pump hums or is silent instead of spraying.
- You see water leaking under the dishwasher or smell burning.

A service call often starts in the range of $100 to $200 before parts, depending on where you live. On an older dishwasher with a major part failure, ask for a quote and weigh it against replacement.
