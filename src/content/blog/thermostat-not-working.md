When a thermostat stops working, the screen is usually still on. You change the setting and nothing happens. The thermostat is just a switch: it sends a 24-volt call down thin wires to your furnace or air handler, and the equipment does the work. A problem anywhere along that path looks like a "dead" thermostat.

The most common causes are **a wrong mode, setpoint or schedule**, **dead batteries**, **a tripped breaker or furnace service switch**, an **open furnace door switch** and a **tripped condensate float switch**. A blown fuse, loose wires and a badly placed thermostat come next.

> **Safety first:** Turn off power at the breaker or the furnace service switch before you remove the thermostat from its wall plate or open the furnace or air handler. Never touch wires inside the furnace with the power on, and don't connect thermostat wires together at the control board to "test" the system. That is a technician's test. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Look at the screen.** If it's completely blank, the thermostat has no power. Follow our [thermostat blank screen guide](/smart-home/thermostat-blank-screen/) instead.
2. **Check the settings.** Mode on HEAT or COOL, fan on AUTO, setpoint well past the room temperature, no hold or vacation mode active.
3. **Wait 5 minutes and listen.** Many thermostats have a built-in delay before the AC or heat pump starts. You should hear a click at the thermostat, then the furnace or air handler starting.
4. **Switch the fan to ON.** If the blower starts, the thermostat and wiring work, and the problem is on the heating or cooling side.
5. **If nothing at all happens**, check power and the safety switches at the furnace or air handler (causes 3 to 6).

## 1. Wrong mode, setpoint or schedule

This is the most common cause. A thermostat in COOL won't heat, and a programmable or smart thermostat may follow its schedule, or an eco or away mode, and quietly undo your change.

**Fix:**

1. Set the **mode** to HEAT or COOL (or AUTO if you use it) and the **fan** to AUTO.
2. Move the **setpoint** at least 3 to 5 degrees above the room temperature for heat, or below it for cooling.
3. Cancel any **hold, vacation, eco or away** setting, and check that the schedule isn't overriding you. Menu names vary, so check your manual.
4. **Wait at least 5 minutes.** Many thermostats delay the compressor after a recent run or a power blip to protect it. A countdown or "wait" message is normal.
5. On a heat pump, make sure the thermostat is set up for a heat pump. The wrong system type in the installer settings can stop heat or cooling from working. Check the manual before changing installer settings.

## 2. Dead or weak batteries

Many thermostats run on AA or AAA batteries, or use them as backup. Weak batteries can keep the screen lit without enough power to send the call. Look for a low battery icon.

**Fix:**

- Pull the thermostat body off its base or open the battery door. Some models have the battery slot on the side or top.
- Fit **fresh alkaline batteries** of the type shown, in the right direction. Don't reuse batteries from a drawer.
- Check the time, mode and schedule afterward.

## 3. A tripped breaker or furnace service switch

Your furnace or air handler has its own breaker in the main panel, and usually a **service switch** nearby that looks like a regular light switch, often on the side of the unit or on a wall or ceiling joist close to it. If either one is off, the equipment can't respond. Battery thermostats may stay lit even then. The outdoor AC or heat pump usually has its own breaker and an outdoor disconnect.

**Fix:**

1. Check the main panel for a breaker labeled **furnace, air handler, HVAC, AC or heat pump**. A tripped one sits in the middle position. Push it fully to OFF, then back to ON.
2. Find the **service switch** at the furnace and make sure it's ON. People often mistake it for a light switch and turn it off.
3. If the breaker **trips again**, don't keep resetting it. A repeat trip points to a fault in the equipment or its wiring, so call an HVAC technician.

## 4. The furnace door safety switch is open

Furnaces and many air handlers have a push-button switch behind the front panel that cuts power when the door isn't seated. It's a common cause right after someone **changes the filter** and refits the door crooked.

**Fix:**

- Turn the service switch off, then **refit the front door** so it sits flat and all its tabs or screws are in place. Push it firmly until the switch presses in.
- Turn the switch back on and wait a minute. Many furnaces run a short startup check before responding.

## 5. A condensate float switch shut the system off

An AC or high-efficiency furnace makes water that drains through a pan and a small pipe. Many systems have a **float switch** in the drain pan or drain line. If the drain clogs and water backs up, the float rises and cuts the low-voltage circuit so the system stops. On many systems the thermostat stays on but the AC won't start. On others the thermostat goes blank.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the service switch. Look for water in the **drain pan** under the indoor coil or furnace, and for a small switch clipped to the pan or the drain pipe.
2. Remove standing water with a **wet/dry vacuum** or towels.
3. Clear the drain line. Many lines have an access cap or a cleanout tee near the unit; you can vacuum the outdoor end of the line to pull out the clog.
4. Let the float drop back down, restore power and wait several minutes. If it trips again soon, the drain needs a professional cleaning or the pan may be cracked.

## 6. A blown low-voltage fuse on the control board

Many control boards have a small fuse protecting the 24-volt circuit. A short in the thermostat wiring, such as a nicked wire or an install mistake, can blow it, and then nothing on the low-voltage side works.

**Fix:**

- With the power **off**, remove the furnace door and look for a small plug-in fuse on the control board. You can usually see a broken metal strip inside a blown one.
- Replace it only with a fuse of the **exact same type and rating** shown on the fuse, the board or the wiring label inside the door. Never use a bigger one.
- If the new fuse **blows again**, there's a short in the wiring or a faulty part. Stop and call an HVAC technician.

## 7. Loose or broken wires, or no C wire

The thermostat connects to the system through thin wires: usually R (24 V power), C (common), W (heat), Y (cooling) and G (fan). A wire that has slipped out of its terminal, broken inside its insulation or been chewed by a pet or rodent stops that function. If heat works but cooling doesn't, the Y wire is a likely suspect. Many smart thermostats also need a **C wire** for steady power, or they may reboot, lose Wi-Fi or short-cycle the system.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the breaker or service switch, then remove the thermostat from its wall plate.
2. Check that each wire is **firmly seated** in the correct terminal, with clean copper showing and no nicks or green corrosion. Take a photo before you touch anything.
3. Do the same at the furnace control board terminals, with the power off.
4. If you have a smart thermostat and no C wire, check the maker's instructions. Options include a spare wire in the cable, an adapter kit, or a new cable run by a pro. Our [thermostat wire colors guide](/hvac/thermostat-wire-colors/) explains what each wire does.

## 8. A dirty or badly placed thermostat

Dust, direct sunlight, a nearby lamp or TV, a supply vent or a drafty wall can make a thermostat read too warm or too cold, so it doesn't call for heat or cooling when you expect.

**Fix:**

- Turn off the power, remove the cover and **gently brush or blow out dust**. Don't bend any small parts.
- Compare its reading with a separate thermometer placed next to it for 15 minutes. Many thermostats let you adjust a temperature offset in their settings.
- Remove nearby **heat sources**, and seal the wall hole behind the plate with a little insulation if a draft comes through it.
- If it's in a bad spot, a pro can move it to an inside wall away from vents and windows.

## Thermostat screen is blank?

A blank screen means the thermostat has no power. Many of the checks overlap with causes 2 to 7, and the [thermostat blank screen guide](/smart-home/thermostat-blank-screen/) covers them in order, plus smart thermostat sleep modes.

## Still no response from the HVAC system?

- **Furnace status light blinking?** Many control boards flash a code through a small window in the door. Look up the code on the label inside the door or in the manual.
- **Fan works but no heat?** The problem is likely in the furnace itself (ignition, gas or a limit switch). Call a technician.
- **Fan works but no cooling?** Check the outdoor unit's breaker and disconnect. If it still won't run, call a technician.
- **Everything else checks out?** Thermostats do fail. A technician can confirm which side is at fault before you buy a new one.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

- The breaker or the control board fuse **blows again** after a reset or replacement.
- The furnace shows a **blinking fault code**, or you smell gas or burning.
- The float switch keeps tripping, or there's a **leak** you can't trace.
- You need to test the system with the thermostat bypassed, add a **C wire**, or work on wiring at the control board.
- The thermostat is set correctly, has power and calls for heat or cooling, but the equipment never responds.

Most of the time it's a setting, a switch or a fuse. Check those safely, and leave the rest of the furnace to a pro.
