When a Bluetooth speaker won't connect, the speaker and your phone, computer or TV aren't completing the handshake that pairs them. Most of the time nothing is broken. The speaker just isn't ready to pair, or it's busy with another device.

The most common causes are **the speaker not being in pairing mode, the speaker connecting to another device it remembers, and an old pairing record that needs to be cleared**. Start with the quick test, then work through the fixes in order.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Turn the speaker on and watch its light.** A flashing light usually means it's ready to pair. A steady light often means it's already connected to something.
2. **Open your Bluetooth settings** on the phone or computer and look for the speaker's name.
    - **Not in the list?** It isn't in pairing mode or is connected elsewhere (fixes 1 and 2).
    - **In the list but won't connect?** Clear the old pairing (fix 3).
    - **Connects but cuts out?** Battery or distance (fixes 4 and 5).
3. **Try a different phone.** If another phone pairs fine, the problem is on your first phone's side (fixes 3 and 8).

## 1. Put the speaker in pairing mode

Turning a speaker on doesn't always make it visible to new devices. Many speakers only show up in the list when they are in pairing mode.

**Fix:**

- Press and hold the speaker's **Bluetooth** or pairing button (often marked with the Bluetooth symbol) until the light flashes or you hear a tone.
- Keep the speaker within a few feet of your phone while pairing.
- Pair within a minute or two. Many speakers leave pairing mode after a short time.
- Check the manual if your model uses a different button or combination.

## 2. Disconnect it from other devices

Speakers remember the devices they've paired with and often reconnect to the last one automatically. If a tablet, laptop or someone else's phone nearby grabs the speaker first, your phone can't connect.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/bluetooth-speaker-not-connecting-other-device.svg" alt="Diagram showing a portable speaker already connected to a laptop, so a phone trying to connect at the same time is blocked; turning off Bluetooth on the laptop frees the speaker for the phone" width="640" height="380" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>A speaker usually plays from one device at a time, and it picks the last one it knew.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

- Turn off Bluetooth on other phones, tablets, laptops and smart TVs near you, then try again.
- If the speaker lets you connect two devices at once, disconnect the one you aren't using.
- If it keeps jumping back to an old device, remove the speaker from that device's Bluetooth list too.

## 3. Forget the speaker and pair again

A stale or corrupted pairing record is a common reason a speaker shows in the list but fails to connect, or connects with no sound.

**Fix:**

1. On your phone or computer, open Bluetooth settings and tap the speaker name or its info icon.
2. Choose **Forget**, **Unpair** or **Remove device**.
3. Turn Bluetooth off and on again.
4. Put the speaker back in pairing mode (fix 1) and select it from the list.

The same steps fix most pairing trouble with earbuds and headphones too. Our guide to [Bluetooth that won't connect on iPhone and Android](/phones-tablets/bluetooth-wont-connect/) has phone-specific tips.

## 4. Charge it and restart both devices

A low battery can stop a speaker from pairing or make it drop the connection. Some speakers won't pair properly while the battery is nearly empty.

**Fix:**

- Plug the speaker in with its own cable and charger and leave it for at least 30 minutes, then try again.
- Turn the speaker off and on.
- Restart your phone or computer. This clears Bluetooth glitches more often than you might expect.
- If it won't charge or power on at all, see the last section.

## 5. Move closer and away from interference

Bluetooth is a short-range radio. Range drops through walls and floors, and other devices on the same 2.4 GHz band, such as Wi-Fi routers, microwave ovens and some cordless gadgets, can cause dropouts.

**Fix:**

- Keep the phone and speaker in the same room, ideally with a clear line between them.
- Move away from a running microwave or a Wi-Fi router while testing.
- Don't keep your phone in a back pocket or bag while you walk around. Your body blocks the signal too.
- Turn off Bluetooth on devices you aren't using to reduce clutter.

## 6. Turn off party or stereo linking modes

Many speakers can link to a second speaker of the same brand for stereo or party mode. When that mode is on or half set up, the speaker may not connect normally to your phone.

**Fix:**

- Turn off party, stereo or multi-speaker mode in the speaker's app or with its buttons (see the manual).
- Pair the speaker on its own first. Once it plays, add the second speaker.
- If the brand has an app, open it to check the speaker's status and any update.

## 7. Connecting to a computer or TV

Computers and TVs often need an extra step, and not every TV can send sound over Bluetooth.

**Fix:**

- **Windows 11:** open Settings, then Bluetooth & devices, turn Bluetooth on, and choose Add device, then Bluetooth. Put the speaker in pairing mode and pick it.
- **Mac:** open System Settings, then Bluetooth, put the speaker in pairing mode and click Connect next to its name.
- **TV:** look in the TV's sound output or Bluetooth settings. If there's no Bluetooth audio option, the TV can't do it on its own, and you'd need a Bluetooth transmitter connected to the TV's audio output.
- If a Windows PC can't see any Bluetooth devices, its Bluetooth driver may need an update. See our guide on how to [update drivers in Windows 11](/pc-laptops/update-drivers-windows-11/).

## 8. Update and reset the speaker

Speakers with an app can get firmware updates that fix connection bugs. A reset clears every remembered device and gives you a clean start.

**Fix:**

1. Update your phone's software and, if your speaker has an app, the speaker's firmware.
2. Reset the speaker using the steps in its manual or on the maker's support page. Button combinations differ by model.
3. Forget the speaker on your phone, then pair it again as new.

## Connected but no sound?

- Turn up the volume on both the phone and the speaker. They often have separate levels.
- Check the audio output. On a computer, choose the speaker as the output device in the sound settings. On a phone, check the audio output picker in the media player or control panel.
- Make sure the speaker isn't set to another input, such as AUX or USB, if it has one.
- Forget and re-pair (fix 3), which fixes many "connected, no audio" cases.

## When to contact the maker or replace it

Contact the manufacturer or check your warranty if:

- The speaker won't pair with any phone, even after a reset.
- It won't charge or turn on with its own cable and charger.
- It disconnects constantly at close range with every device.
- It got wet beyond its water rating, or the battery is swollen, very hot or smells odd. Stop using and charging it if you notice any of these.

A swollen or damaged battery is a fire risk, so don't try to open the speaker or charge it again.
