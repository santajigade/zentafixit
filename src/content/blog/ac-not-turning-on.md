When your central air won't turn on at all, the cause is usually something that cuts power or a safety switch that stops the system on purpose. Nothing in the house blows, the outdoor unit stays silent, and the thermostat may even look dead.

The most common causes are **thermostat settings or dead batteries**, a **tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect**, a **furnace door that isn't closed**, and a **full condensate pan that tripped the float switch**. Most of these take minutes to check yourself.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Check the thermostat is on COOL and set below room temperature, replace its batteries, and make sure the AC breakers and the outdoor disconnect are on. Then check the furnace or air handler door is closed and the condensate pan isn't full. > **Safety first:** An AC system runs on 240 V. Turn off power at both the breaker and the outdoor disconnect before opening any panel, and never open the electrical cover on the outdoor unit. Capacitors can hold a dangerous charge even with the power off.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Set the thermostat to COOL**, fan to AUTO, and set the temperature at least 3 °F below the room temperature.
2. **Wait 5 to 10 minutes.** Many thermostats delay the compressor after a setting change to protect it.
3. **Listen and look.** Is the indoor blower running? Go outside: is the outdoor fan spinning, or is the unit humming or clicking?
    - **Nothing runs anywhere:** check causes 1 to 5.
    - **Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent:** check causes 2, 6 and 7.
    - **Outdoor unit hums but the fan doesn't spin:** see cause 6.

## 1. Thermostat settings or dead batteries

A blank screen, a schedule overriding your setting, or the system switch on HEAT or OFF will stop the AC before anything else gets a chance.

**Fix:**

- Replace the batteries if your thermostat uses them, even if the screen still shows something. Low batteries can drop the call for cooling.
- Confirm the mode is **COOL** and any hold or schedule isn't setting a higher temperature.
- For smart thermostats, check the app for a lockout, an energy-saving event, or an "equipment off" alert. If the screen is blank and it has no batteries, it may have lost its 24 V power from the furnace (see cause 5).
- If you recently replaced the thermostat, recheck the wiring against the labels you noted on the old one.

## 2. Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect

Central AC usually has two power sources: a breaker for the indoor furnace or air handler and a separate double-pole breaker for the outdoor unit. Next to the outdoor unit there's also a **disconnect box** with a pull-out block, a lever or a fuse block.

**Fix:**

- At the panel, find breakers labeled AC, condenser, furnace or air handler. A tripped breaker sits in the middle or looks different from the rest. Push it fully to **OFF**, then back to **ON**.
- Open the outdoor disconnect box. If it has a pull-out block, make sure it's pushed in the right way (most are marked ON). If someone serviced the unit or mowed nearby, it may have been left off.
- Some disconnects contain cartridge fuses. Replacing those is best left to a technician or electrician.
- **If the breaker trips again**, stop. Don't keep resetting it. See [why breakers keep tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/) and the section below.

## 3. Furnace or air handler door not closed

Your indoor unit has a **door safety switch** (a small push button behind the lower panel). If the panel is loose or put back crooked after a filter change, the switch stays open and nothing runs.

**Fix:**

- Remove and reseat the access panel so it sits flat and the switch button is pressed in.
- Some panels need the top hooked in first, then the bottom pushed in. Tighten any screws or latches.

## 4. Clogged condensate drain or float switch

The indoor coil makes a lot of water when cooling. It drains through a pipe, and many systems have a **float switch** in the drain pan or on the drain line. When water backs up, the float switch shuts off the AC to prevent a flood.

**Fix:**

- Check the pan under the indoor unit for standing water.
- With the power off, use a wet/dry vacuum on the outdoor end of the condensate line (often near the outdoor unit or a floor drain) for a minute or two to pull out the clog.
- Empty the pan, then restore power. The float switch should reset on its own once the water drops.
- If the drain clogs often, ask a technician about a cleanout fitting or algae tablets made for condensate pans.

## 5. Blown fuse on the control board

Many furnaces and air handlers have a small fuse (often an automotive-style blade fuse) on the control board that protects the 24 V thermostat circuit. If thermostat wires touch each other or a wire shorts, this fuse blows and the thermostat may go blank.

**Fix:**

- Turn off power, remove the panel and look at the board for a fuse. Check its rating printed on top and the board label.
- If the metal strip inside is broken, replace it with the **same rating**, never higher.
- If the new fuse blows right away, there's a short in the thermostat wiring or a component. Call a technician.

## 6. Bad capacitor (pro only)

The capacitor gives the compressor and outdoor fan motor a kick to start. When it fails, the outdoor unit **hums or buzzes but the fan doesn't spin**, or it starts and stops. Capacitors are one of the most common AC failures, especially after a hot summer.

**Fix:** Turn the AC off at the thermostat so the motors don't overheat, and call an HVAC technician. A capacitor stores a charge that can injure you even with the power off. If you want to understand what's inside one, here's an explainer on [how to test a capacitor](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/how-to-test-a-capacitor/), but leave the AC one to a pro.

## 7. Stuck or failed contactor (pro only)

The contactor is a relay in the outdoor unit that the 24 V signal pulls in to send 240 V to the compressor and fan. Pitted contacts, insects or ants inside, or a burned-out coil can stop it closing. The result: the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit stays silent.

**Fix:** A technician can test the 24 V signal and the contactor and replace it if needed. Don't push the contactor in by hand, as it's live and can arc.

## 8. Thermostat wiring or transformer fault

If the thermostat is blank even with fresh batteries, and the board fuse is fine, the 24 V transformer may have failed or a thermostat wire may have come loose at the board, the thermostat or the outdoor unit.

**Fix:** Check that wires are firmly seated at the thermostat base. Beyond that, measuring the 24 V circuit is a job for a technician or a confident DIYer with a multimeter.

## Breaker trips or you smell burning?

- **Breaker trips as soon as the AC starts:** this points to a shorted compressor, a failed motor or a wiring fault. Leave the breaker off and call a pro.
- **Burning smell, smoke or scorch marks** at the indoor or outdoor unit: turn off the AC breakers and call a technician right away.
- **Ice on the lines or coil:** turn the system off and let it thaw for several hours. Our guide to an [AC running but not cooling](/hvac/ac-running-not-cooling/) covers what causes freezing.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

- The outdoor unit hums but won't start, or starts and stops repeatedly.
- The breaker trips again after one reset.
- The board fuse blows again after you replace it.
- You've checked the thermostat, breakers, disconnect, door switch and drain, and the system still won't start.
- The unit needs refrigerant or electrical work inside the cabinet.

Yearly maintenance catches many of these problems early. Changing the filter regularly and keeping the condensate drain clear are the two jobs most worth doing yourself.
