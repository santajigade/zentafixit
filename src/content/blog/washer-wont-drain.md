When a washer won't drain, the cycle stops with water still sitting in the tub, or the clothes come out soaking wet. The machine is usually fine. Something is just stopping the water from getting out.

The most common causes are **a lid or door lock that isn't latching, a kinked or clogged drain hose, a clogged drain pump filter on a front loader, or a sock jammed in the pump**. Here's how to find which, starting with the free checks.

A clog at any one stops it.</figcaption></figure>

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Unplug the washer, check that the drain hose behind it is not kinked or crushed, and on a front loader clean the drain pump filter behind the small panel at the bottom front. If the pump hums but no water moves, something is stuck in it. If water backs up out of the standpipe, the clog is in your house drain, not the washer.</p></div>

> **Safety first:** Unplug the washer before you touch the filter, hoses or pump. Water and 120 V do not mix. If the last cycle was hot, let the water cool before draining it. Turn off both water valves before pulling the washer away from the wall, and get help moving it: a washer full of water is very heavy.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/washer-wont-drain-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: run a drain and spin cycle and listen; if the pump is silent check the lid lock and error codes; if it hums with no water moving, clean the filter and pump; if water drains but spills from the standpipe, clear the house drain" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Listening to the pump during a drain cycle points you to the right cause.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Restart the washer.** Unplug it for a minute, plug it back in, close the lid or door firmly and select **Drain and Spin** (or Rinse and Spin).
2. **Listen near the bottom of the machine.** A steady pump sound with water moving is normal. A **hum with no water moving** points to a blockage. **Silence** points to the lid lock, a control fault or a failed pump.
3. **Watch the drain hose and standpipe.** If water comes out of the hose but overflows from the standpipe or laundry sink, the washer is working and the house drain is clogged (cause 5).
4. **Check the display.** If it shows an error code, look it up in your manual before you take anything apart (cause 7).

## 1. Lid or door lock problem

Most washers will not drain or spin unless they sense that the lid or door is closed and locked. If the lock or lid switch doesn't register, the washer may stop with water in it, and a lock light may blink.

**Fix:**

- Close the lid or door firmly. Check for a sock or towel caught in the door seal.
- On a top loader, look at the small plastic tab on the lid that presses into a slot on the cabinet. If it's broken or bent, the switch can't sense the lid.
- Unplug the washer for a minute to reset the controls, then try a drain cycle again.
- If the door won't lock at all or the lock light keeps blinking, the lid switch or door lock assembly may have failed. These are common replacement parts. Check your manual or call a technician.

## 2. Kinked or clogged drain hose

The flexible drain hose on the back of the washer is easy to crush when the machine is pushed back against the wall. A kink can slow draining or stop it completely.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the washer and turn off the water valves.
2. Pull the washer out carefully and check the full length of the drain hose for kinks, flat spots or a hose pinched behind the machine.
3. Straighten it and leave a few inches of space behind the washer.
4. If the hose looks fine, put a bucket underneath, remove it from the standpipe and check the end for lint or debris. You can flush it with a garden hose outdoors.
5. In a cold garage or utility room, water in the hose can freeze. Let it thaw before running the washer.

## 3. Clogged drain pump filter (front loaders)

Most front loaders have a **drain pump filter** (sometimes called a coin trap) behind a small panel at the bottom front. It catches coins, hair ties, lint and small socks before they reach the pump. When it clogs, water can't get out.

The filter sits close to the floor, so a bucket won't fit under it. Use **towels and a shallow pan**, like a baking dish, and expect to empty the pan several times.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the washer. Lay thick towels on the floor in front of the panel and set a shallow pan in front of the filter.
2. Open the access panel. Some panels flip open, others need a coin or flat screwdriver to release.
3. If there's a small drain tube next to the filter, pull it out, hold the end over the pan and remove its cap. Let the water run out, recapping it each time the pan fills.
4. When the flow stops, **slowly** turn the filter counterclockwise. More water will come out, so go a little at a time.
5. Pull the filter out and clean off lint, hair and debris. Shine a flashlight into the opening and remove anything you can see.
6. Screw the filter back in firmly, close the panel and run a drain cycle. Check for drips at the filter.

Some top loaders also have a filter, but many do not. Check your manual for its location before you start removing panels.

## 4. Something stuck in the drain pump

If the pump **hums but no water moves**, a sock, coin, bra wire or piece of plastic may be jammed in the pump's impeller (the small spinning paddle inside). On a front loader you can often see or feel the impeller through the filter opening.

**Fix:**

- With the washer unplugged and the filter removed, look into the opening with a flashlight. Remove any object with pliers or your fingers. Watch for sharp wires.
- On many front loaders you can gently turn the impeller with a finger. It should move with slight resistance. If it won't move at all, something is still jammed or the pump has seized.
- On top loaders, the pump is usually reached by tipping the machine or removing a back or front panel. If you're not comfortable with that, call a technician.
- If the pump is clear but still only hums, or is completely silent with the lid locked, the pump or its wiring has likely failed. A replacement pump is a common repair.

## 5. Clogged standpipe or house drain

The washer pumps water up the drain hose into a **standpipe** (a vertical pipe in the wall or floor) or a laundry sink. If that drain is clogged, water backs up and may overflow onto the floor, even though the washer is working.

**Fix:**

- Watch the standpipe during a drain cycle. If water rises up and spills out, the clog is in the house plumbing.
- Clear the trap or pipe with a drain snake (auger). Lint is the usual culprit. Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners, which can damage pipes and splash back.
- If the washer drains into a laundry sink, clean the sink strainer and see our guide to [fixing a clogged sink drain](/plumbing/clogged-sink-drain/).
- Don't push the drain hose too far down into the standpipe or seal it airtight. That can cause siphoning. Your manual gives the recommended hose height and depth.
- A lint trap or mesh sleeve on the end of the hose can stop lint reaching the pipe. Clean it regularly.

## 6. Too many suds or the wrong cycle

High-efficiency (HE) washers use very little water. Too much detergent, or regular detergent in an HE machine, can create a mass of suds that slows draining and may cause the washer to pause or show a suds-related code.

**Fix:**

- Run a **rinse cycle with no detergent** to clear the suds.
- Use only HE detergent in an HE washer, and use the amount your manual suggests, not the line on the cap.
- Delicate or hand-wash cycles spin slowly, so clothes come out wetter than usual. That's normal. Run a separate **Drain and Spin** if needed.
- A single heavy item, like a bath mat or comforter, can unbalance the load. The washer may stop the spin to protect itself. Redistribute it and try again.

## 7. Error codes and control problems

Many washers show a code when they can't drain within a set time. Codes and their meanings differ by brand and model, so don't guess.

**Fix:**

- Look up the exact code in your owner's manual or on the manufacturer's website. Some models also have a technical sheet stored inside the console or behind a panel.
- Fix the cause it points to (often the filter, hose or pump above), then unplug the washer for a minute to clear the code.
- If the code keeps returning with a clear filter, hose and drain, a pump, lock or control board fault is likely. That's a job for a technician.

## Tub full of water and won't drain at all?

You still need to get the water out before you can fix anything.

- **Front loader:** Don't open the door. Drain the water through the pump filter or small drain tube into a shallow pan (cause 3). It's slow but keeps the floor dry.
- **Top loader:** Bail the water out with a cup into a bucket, then soak up the rest with towels. Some people lower the drain hose into a bucket below the water level so gravity drains it, but control the flow so the bucket doesn't overflow.
- Once the tub is empty, take out the clothes, then work through causes 2 to 5.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a technician if:

- The pump is clear but still hums, or makes no sound at all during a drain cycle.
- The lid lock or door lock won't engage, or a lock code keeps coming back.
- You'd need to tip a top loader or remove cabinet panels to reach the pump.
- You see burnt wiring, smell burning or the washer trips a breaker.

If the problem is a clogged standpipe you can't clear with a snake, call a plumber instead. For a similar problem in the kitchen, see our guide to a [dishwasher not draining](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/).
