When a Roku, Fire TV Stick or other streaming stick stops working, the problem is usually simple: **the TV is on the wrong input**, the stick **isn't getting enough power**, the **remote has lost its connection**, or the **Wi-Fi has dropped**. Most fixes take a few minutes and none need tools.

Start with a full power cycle, then work through the steps below in order. They apply to Roku, Amazon Fire TV, Google TV and most other sticks that plug into an HDMI port.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Power cycle the stick:** unplug its power cable for about 30 seconds, then plug it back in and wait for the home screen.
2. **Check the picture.** No picture at all means input, HDMI or power (causes 2 and 3).
3. **Press Home on the remote.** If the picture is fine but nothing responds, it's the remote (cause 4).
4. **Open a different app.** If one app fails but others play, the problem is that app or its service (cause 6).
5. **If nothing streams** but the menus work, it's the network (cause 5).

## 1. Restart the stick and the TV

Streaming sticks are small computers and they can freeze. A full restart clears most glitches.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the stick's power cable** (not just the HDMI end) and the **TV's power cord**.
2. Wait about **30 seconds**.
3. Plug the TV in and turn it on, then plug the stick back in.
4. If the menus respond, you can also restart from the settings menu next time. On Roku it's under **Settings > System** (on some models inside **Power**), and on Fire TV under **Settings > My Fire TV**.

## 2. Wrong input or a bad HDMI connection

If the screen says **No Signal** or stays black, the TV may simply be on another input, or the HDMI connection isn't working. Some TVs also show a black screen when the HDCP copy protection handshake fails.

**Fix:**

- Press **Input** or **Source** on the TV remote and pick the HDMI port the stick is plugged into. Ports are usually numbered on the back of the TV.
- **Unplug and reseat** the stick firmly. If it's in an HDMI extender, try the stick directly in the TV.
- Try a **different HDMI port**.
- Avoid running the stick through a soundbar or receiver while you test. Plug it straight into the TV.
- Our guide to [TV no signal on HDMI](/tv-audio/tv-no-signal-hdmi/) covers port and cable problems in more detail.

## 3. Not enough power

Many sticks need more power than a TV's USB port can supply. An underpowered stick can show a black screen, a warning about power, or restart over and over. A stick powered from the TV's USB port also switches off with the TV, so it can miss updates.

**Fix:**

- Use the **power adapter and cable that came with the stick**, plugged into a **wall outlet** or a good power strip.
- Replace a cable that's damaged, loose or not the original. Some cheap cables can't carry enough current.
- Give the stick room to breathe. A stick wedged against the back of a wall-mounted TV can overheat. An HDMI extender moves it away from the hottest spot.

## 4. The remote isn't working

Most dead remotes have weak batteries or have lost their pairing. Simple infrared remotes need a clear line of sight to the stick or TV. Voice and Wi-Fi remotes connect wirelessly and don't need to point at anything.

**Fix:**

1. **Put in fresh batteries**, checking the + and - direction.
2. **Move closer** and remove anything blocking the stick or TV, such as a soundbar.
3. **Re-pair the remote.**
    - **Roku voice and enhanced remotes:** many have a small **pairing button** inside the battery compartment. Power cycle the stick, then hold that button until the remote's light flashes.
    - **Fire TV remotes:** hold the **Home** button for about 10 seconds near the stick.
    - Other brands: check the remote's manual or the maker's help page.
4. While you fix it, install the official **Roku app** or **Amazon Fire TV app** on your phone and use it as a remote. Your phone must be on the same Wi-Fi network.
5. If a new set of batteries and re-pairing don't help, a replacement remote is usually inexpensive. Buy one listed for your exact model.

## 5. Wi-Fi or network problems

If the menus work but nothing plays, or you see a "not connected" message, the stick has lost its network connection. Changing your Wi-Fi password or router means you must reconnect the stick.

**Fix:**

- Restart your **router and modem**: unplug them for 30 seconds, then wait for the internet light to come back.
- On the stick, open its **network settings** and run the connection check or select your network again. Re-enter the Wi-Fi password carefully.
- Move the router closer or remove obstacles. A stick behind a TV gets weaker Wi-Fi. Some sticks support a USB or Ethernet adapter for a wired connection.
- If shows start and then stall, see our guide to [streaming that keeps buffering](/tv-audio/streaming-keeps-buffering/).

## 6. One app or channel misbehaving

When only one app crashes, freezes or won't load, the problem is that app or its service, not the stick.

**Fix:**

- **Close and reopen** the app.
- Check whether the service is having an **outage** (try it on your phone).
- **Remove and reinstall** the app (Roku calls apps "channels"). You'll need to sign in again.
- Sign out and back in to the app if it says your account or subscription can't be found.

## 7. Software out of date

Apps sometimes stop working on old software, and updates fix bugs. Most sticks update automatically, but only while powered and connected.

**Fix:**

- **Roku:** check **Settings > System > Software update**.
- **Fire TV:** check **Settings > My Fire TV > About**, then the option to check for updates.
- Other sticks: look for an "about" or "system update" option in settings.
- Leave the stick powered on for a while after the update so it can finish.

## 8. Display settings the TV can't show

If you changed the resolution or a display setting and the screen went black or flickers, the stick may be sending a format the TV or HDMI port doesn't support, such as 4K or HDR on an older port.

**Fix:**

- Try a different HDMI port, ideally one labeled for 4K or HDR if your TV has one.
- Some sticks have a remote shortcut to reset display settings. Check the maker's help page for your model.
- In the display settings, choose **auto-detect** or a lower resolution like 1080p.

## Still not working? Factory reset

A factory reset erases everything and returns the stick to its out-of-the-box state. Use it as a last resort.

- **From the menus:** both Roku and Fire TV have a factory reset option in their settings. Look under **System** (Roku) or **My Fire TV** (Fire TV).
- **Roku reset button:** many Roku devices have a small reset button on the device. Press and hold it until the status light flashes. Check Roku's instructions for your model.
- After the reset, you'll set up Wi-Fi, sign in to your account and reinstall apps.
- If the stick still won't power on or show a picture on any port of any TV, it has probably failed.

## When to replace the stick

Replace it, or contact the maker's support, if:

- It shows no picture on **any HDMI port or TV** with the original power adapter.
- It keeps restarting or freezing even after a **factory reset**.
- The maker has stopped releasing **updates** for your model and key apps no longer work.
- It's still under **warranty**. Contact the maker before buying a new one.

Most streaming stick problems come down to input, power, remote or Wi-Fi. Check those four in order and you'll usually fix it without buying anything.
