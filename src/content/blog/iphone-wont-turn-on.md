When your iPhone won't turn on, the screen stays black no matter which button you press. Most of the time the phone isn't broken: the battery is fully drained, the software has frozen with the screen off, or it's too cold or too hot to start.

The most common causes are **a dead battery or a bad cable**, **a frozen iPhone that needs a force restart**, **extreme temperatures**, and **an update that went wrong**. Work through the fixes below in order. Only the later ones risk erasing anything.

> **Safety first:** If the phone is swollen, the screen is lifting, or the phone gets hot, smells of burning or shows a scorched port, stop charging it and don't press on it. A damaged lithium-ion battery can catch fire. Take it to Apple or an Apple Authorized Service Provider.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Plug it in** to a wall outlet with a cable and adapter you know work. Wait a few minutes and look for a charging screen or a red battery icon.
2. **Force restart it** while it's still plugged in (fix 2).
3. **Watch and listen.** Does the Apple logo appear? Does the phone vibrate, ring or make sounds when you flip the Ring/Silent switch or get a call?
4. **Think about what happened last.** Was it dropped, wet, very cold or in the middle of an update?

## 1. Charge it for up to an hour

A battery that's completely empty can't power the screen, and some phones take a while to show anything after you plug them in. Apple suggests letting it charge for up to an hour.

**Fix:**

- Use a **wall adapter**, not a computer USB port or a car charger, which may supply too little power.
- Try a different cable and adapter if you have them. Frayed or uncertified cables often fail without any sign.
- If you see a **red battery icon with a cable**, keep it plugged in. It needs more charge before it starts.
- If nothing shows after an hour, check the charging port for lint and see our guide to an [iPhone not charging](/phones-tablets/iphone-not-charging/).

## 2. Force restart it the right way

A frozen iPhone can look exactly like a dead one, with a black screen and no response to the side button. A force restart cuts power to the software and starts it again. It doesn't erase anything. The button sequence depends on your model, and the timing matters.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/iphone-wont-turn-on-force-restart.svg" alt="Force restart steps by iPhone model: iPhone 8 and later press volume up, press volume down, then hold the side button; iPhone 7 hold side and volume down together; iPhone 6s and older hold Home and the top or side button together, in each case until the Apple logo appears" width="640" height="400" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Keep holding the last button until you see the Apple logo.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

- **iPhone 8 and later** (including iPhone SE 2nd and 3rd generation and all Face ID models): press and quickly release **volume up**, press and quickly release **volume down**, then press and hold the **side button** until the Apple logo appears.
- **iPhone 7 and 7 Plus:** press and hold the **side button** and **volume down** together until the Apple logo appears.
- **iPhone 6s and older, and iPhone SE (1st generation):** press and hold the **Home button** and the **top or side button** together until the Apple logo appears.

Holding the last button can take 10 seconds or more. Keep holding until the logo appears, and try it both on and off the charger.

## 3. Let it warm up or cool down

iPhones are designed to work in roughly 32 °F to 95 °F, according to Apple. In freezing weather the battery may shut the phone down even with charge left, and in heat it may show a temperature warning or refuse to start.

**Fix:**

- **Too cold:** bring it indoors and let it warm up to room temperature on its own for 30 minutes or so, then charge it and try again. Don't put it on a heater.
- **Too hot:** move it out of the sun or the car, take off the case, and let it cool. Don't put it in the fridge or freezer.

## 4. The screen is on but you can't see it

If the phone vibrates, rings, or makes sounds but the screen stays black, the phone has turned on. The problem is the display, or the brightness is turned all the way down.

**Fix:**

- Ask Siri to **"turn up the brightness"** if Siri is set up.
- If you use VoiceOver, its Screen Curtain feature can make the screen look black while the phone works. A three-finger triple-tap on the screen turns Screen Curtain off, or ask Siri to turn off VoiceOver.
- If the phone was dropped or the screen is cracked, the display may have failed. That needs a repair.

## 5. Check for a failing battery

Older batteries lose capacity and can shut the phone off suddenly, especially when it's cold or when the phone is working hard. A phone that dies at 20% or 30% and then won't start until it's charged often has a worn battery.

**Fix:**

- Once it's working, go to **Settings > Battery > Battery Health** (the wording varies a little by model and iOS version) and look at the maximum capacity and any service message.
- If the battery has been stored empty for months, it may take longer to charge or may not recover.
- Battery replacement is a common repair from Apple or an authorized provider.

## 6. Update or restore with recovery mode

If the phone shows the **Apple logo and then goes black again**, or stays on the logo for a long time, iOS may be damaged, often after an interrupted update. Recovery mode lets a computer reinstall iOS.

**Fix:**

1. Connect the iPhone to a Mac (using Finder) or a Windows PC (using the Apple Devices app or iTunes).
2. Do the force restart sequence for your model from fix 2, but **keep holding** the last button even after the Apple logo appears. Release it when you see a picture of a computer and cable.
3. On the computer, choose **Update** first. This reinstalls iOS and keeps your data.
4. Only if Update fails, choose **Restore**. This **erases the iPhone**. You can then restore from an iCloud or computer backup if you have one.

If the download takes longer than about 15 minutes, the phone may exit recovery mode on its own. Just repeat step 2.

## 7. Liquid damage

Water can short the charging port, battery connector or screen. Newer iPhones are water resistant, not waterproof, and the resistance can weaken over time.

**Fix:**

- Tap the phone gently against your hand with the port facing down to get liquid out.
- Leave it in a dry place with some airflow. Don't use a hair dryer or heater, and don't put it in rice, which Apple advises against.
- Don't charge it with a cable while it's wet or while a liquid detected alert shows.
- If it still won't start once fully dry, contact Apple Support.

## 8. Hardware damage

A hard drop, a bent frame or a failed part (the battery, the charging port, the display or the logic board) can stop the phone from starting, and there's nothing you can do at home to fix it.

**Fix:** Book a repair with Apple Support or an Apple Authorized Service Provider. Mention exactly what you've tried, so they can skip straight to testing the hardware.

## Still black after all of that?

- **Try one more charger, cable and outlet,** and leave it plugged in overnight if you haven't yet.
- **Try recovery mode** even if you see no logo. If the computer sees the phone, the hardware is at least partly alive.
- **Back up what you can.** If the phone starts even briefly, back it up to iCloud or a computer right away.
- **Check your coverage.** AppleCare+ or a carrier plan may cover the repair. You can check coverage on Apple's website with your serial number.

## When to contact Apple Support

Contact Apple Support, visit an Apple Store, or go to an Apple Authorized Service Provider if:

- The phone is swollen, the screen is lifting, or it gets hot or smells of burning.
- It won't charge or start after an hour on a known-good charger and a force restart.
- It vibrates or rings but the screen stays black.
- Recovery mode can't update or restore it, or the computer doesn't see it.
- It was dropped or got wet and won't start once fully dry.
