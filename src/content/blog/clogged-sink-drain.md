A clogged sink drain means something is narrowing the pipe close to the sink. In bathrooms it is almost always **hair and soap scum** caught on the stopper or in the trap. In kitchens it is usually **grease, food scraps and coffee grounds** that have built up together.

The good news: most sink clogs are within a foot or two of the drain, so **a stopper cleaning, a plunger, the P-trap and a hand snake** clear nearly all of them without any harsh chemicals.

If that fails, put a bucket under the sink, remove the P-trap and clean it, then run a hand snake into the wall pipe.</p></div>

> **Safety first:** If you have already poured a chemical drain cleaner into the sink, don't plunge or open the trap. It can splash into your eyes or onto your skin. Flush with plenty of water if the drain allows, wear gloves and eye protection, and tell a plumber what product you used.

## How to tell where the clog is: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/clogged-sink-drain-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: if only one sink is slow, clean the stopper, then plunge, then clean the P-trap, then snake the wall pipe; if several drains are slow or back up into each other, the clog is in a shared or main drain and you should call a plumber" width="640" height="420" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Start with the easiest fix and work further down the pipe.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Check the other drains.** Run water in a nearby tub, shower or second sink. If only one sink is slow, the clog is in that sink's drain. If several are slow, skip to "Several drains slow at once?" below.
2. **Look at the stopper.** Lift it out or look at it with a flashlight. A mat of hair on it is very common in bathrooms.
3. **Notice how fast it drains.** A slow drain usually means a partial clog that a stopper cleaning or plunger can fix. Standing water that doesn't move at all often means the trap or wall pipe is packed.
4. **Kitchen sink with a disposal?** If water backs up only on the disposal side or the disposal hums, check step 6 first.

## 1. Clean the stopper or pop-up

Bathroom pop-up stoppers catch hair and soap, and over time they build a mat that blocks the drain almost completely.

**Fix:**

1. Try lifting the stopper straight out. Many twist and lift, or pull straight up.
2. If it won't come out, look behind the sink drain pipe for the **pivot rod** held by a nut. Unscrew the nut, pull the rod back, lift out the stopper, then refit the rod afterward.
3. Pull off the hair and gunk and scrub the stopper with an old toothbrush.
4. Push a **plastic barbed drain strip** down the drain and pull it back up to catch hair below the stopper. Repeat until it comes up clean.

## 2. Use a plunger the right way

A plunger works by pushing and pulling water to break a clog loose. It only works if water, not air, is doing the work and the pressure has nowhere else to escape.

**Fix:**

1. Use a **cup plunger** (flat rim), not a flanged toilet plunger.
2. Remove the stopper and leave enough water in the sink to cover the plunger cup. Add a little if needed.
3. **Seal the escape routes.** Hold a wet rag tightly over the overflow hole in a bathroom sink. In a double kitchen sink, plug or have someone hold a stopper in the other basin.
4. Set the cup over the drain, push down gently to remove air, then plunge with firm, quick strokes 15 to 20 times, keeping the seal.
5. Pull up sharply on the last stroke. If water rushes down, run hot tap water for a minute to flush the pipe.

Repeat a few times before moving on. If the water doesn't move at all, the trap is probably packed.

## 3. Hot water and dish soap (not boiling)

For a slow kitchen drain, hot water and dish soap can loosen soft grease. It's not a fix for a hair clog.

**Fix:**

- Squeeze some dish soap into the drain, then pour in **very hot tap water** or water from a kettle that has cooled slightly.
- Skip boiling water if you have **plastic PVC drain pipes** (white or black plastic under the sink). It can soften the pipe or loosen glued and slip joints. It can also crack porcelain or some composite sinks.
- Follow up with a few rounds of plunging.

## 4. Clean out the P-trap

The **P-trap** is the U-shaped bend under the sink. It holds water to block sewer gas, and it is also where heavy debris, hair clumps and grease settle.

**Fix:**

1. Put a **bucket** under the trap and have old towels ready. The trap is full of water.
2. Loosen the two **slip nuts** on each side of the U-bend. Plastic nuts usually turn by hand. Use channel-lock pliers wrapped in a rag on stiff or metal ones.
3. Remove the trap, empty it into the bucket and clean it out with a brush or a coat hanger.
4. Look into the **trap arm** going into the wall. If you can see or reach gunk, clear it.
5. Reassemble with the washers in the same position, hand-tighten the nuts plus a quarter turn, then run water and check for drips.

If the old trap is cracked or badly corroded, replace it. Trap kits are inexpensive and standard sizes fit most US sinks (1-1/4 inch for many bathroom sinks, 1-1/2 inch for many kitchen sinks, but measure yours).

## 5. Use a hand drain snake

If the trap is clean but water still won't go down, the clog is further into the wall pipe. A **hand snake** (drain auger) with a 15 to 25 foot cable reaches it.

**Fix:**

1. With the trap removed, feed the snake cable into the trap arm and into the wall pipe. This is easier than going through the sink drain.
2. When you feel resistance, tighten the thumbscrew and **crank the handle** while pushing gently. Let the tip bite into the clog or break through it.
3. Pull the cable back slowly, wiping it with a rag. Repeat until it moves freely.
4. Reinstall the trap and flush with hot water for a few minutes.

Wear gloves and go easy at bends. Forcing a snake too hard can scratch or damage old pipes.

## 6. Kitchen sinks: grease, food and the disposal

Kitchen clogs build slowly. Grease cools and sticks to the pipe walls, then food, starch and coffee grounds collect on it.

**Fix:**

- **Disposal side won't drain:** turn the disposal off at the switch and unplug it or turn off its breaker. Many units have a hex key socket in the center bottom that lets you turn the motor by hand to free a jam, and a red reset button. Check your manual. Never put your hand inside.
- **Dishwasher sharing the drain:** when you plunge, clamp or hold the dishwasher drain hose closed if you can reach it, or the water just pushes back into the dishwasher.
- **Grease plug:** hot water and soap (step 3) followed by plunging, then the trap and snake if needed.
- **Prevent it:** wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing, scrape scraps into the trash, and keep coffee grounds, rice, pasta and eggshells out of the drain.

## Why caustic drain cleaners are risky

Store-bought liquid and gel drain cleaners are often based on lye (sodium hydroxide) or strong acids. They work by attacking organic material, but there are real downsides:

- They can **burn skin and eyes**, and they splash back if you plunge or open the trap afterward.
- Some give off **strong fumes**, and mixing products can be dangerous.
- They can **damage old metal pipes, rubber seals and some plastic pipes** if they sit in a fully blocked drain.
- If they don't work, the plumber now has to deal with a sink full of hazardous liquid.

**Enzyme drain cleaners** are a gentler option for maintenance on slow drains. They work slowly (often overnight) and won't clear a solid blockage, but they're far safer to have around. Always read the label.

## Several drains slow at once?

If the sink, tub and toilet are all slow, or water appears in a tub or shower when you flush or run the washer, the blockage is **in a shared branch or the main sewer line**, not under one sink. Plunging a sink won't fix it.

- Stop running water to avoid an overflow.
- If you have a basement, check the floor drain for backup.
- Call a plumber. Main line clogs often need a larger powered machine, and some need a camera inspection.

A sewer smell, gurgling drains or a wet patch in the yard along with slow drains also point to a main line problem.

## When to call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber if:

- Several drains are slow or back up into each other.
- You've cleaned the trap and snaked the wall pipe and the sink is still clogged.
- The clog keeps coming back every few weeks.
- You've used a chemical cleaner and the sink is still full.
- A pipe, trap or slip joint is leaking and won't seal.

A dishwasher that won't drain can be linked to a kitchen sink clog. See our guide to a [dishwasher not draining](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/) if the problem started there.
