If your dishwasher finishes a cycle and leaves a puddle of dirty water in the bottom, it isn't pumping the water out. That water has to pass through a filter, a drain pump, a hose and your sink drain, so a blockage at any point can cause it.

The most common causes are **a clogged filter, a kinked drain hose, a garbage disposal knockout plug left in after a new install, a clogged air gap or a clogged sink drain**. Most are free to fix in a few minutes.

> **Safety first:** Turn off the dishwasher at its breaker, or unplug it if it plugs in under the sink, before reaching into the tub or under the sink. Watch for broken glass in the bottom of the tub. Never put your hand into a garbage disposal: turn off its power and use pliers or tongs.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/dishwasher-not-draining-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: clean the filter, then cancel or restart the cycle and watch the sink; if no water reaches the sink check the hose, knockout plug and pump; if water spurts from the air gap clean it; if water backs up into the sink clear the sink drain" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Watching the sink while the dishwasher drains tells you which half of the drain is clogged.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Clean the filter first** (cause 1). It's the most common cause and takes five minutes.
2. **Run the disposal** with cold water for a few seconds if you have one, so the drain is clear.
3. **Start a short drain.** On many models, pressing Cancel or Start/Reset starts a drain. Check your manual for how.
4. **Watch and listen at the sink.**
    - **Nothing reaches the sink?** The clog is in the hose, knockout plug or pump (causes 2, 3, 6).
    - **Water spurts from the air gap?** Cause 4.
    - **Water backs up into the sink?** Cause 5.

## 1. Clogged filter

Many newer dishwashers have a **manual-clean filter** in the floor of the tub, under the lower spray arm. Food scraps, seeds, labels and bits of glass collect there. When it clogs, water can't reach the pump.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off power and pull out the bottom rack.
2. Bail out standing water with a cup, then soak up the rest with a towel or turkey baster.
3. Twist the cylindrical filter (usually a quarter turn counterclockwise) and lift it out. Lift out the flat mesh screen under it if there is one.
4. Rinse both under warm water and scrub with a soft brush and a little dish soap. Don't use a wire brush.
5. Check the opening below for broken glass or bones, then reinstall the filter and lock it in place.

If your model has no removable filter, it may have a self-cleaning grinder instead. Check the manual before removing parts.

## 2. Kinked or clogged drain hose

The drain hose runs from the dishwasher, under the cabinet, to the sink drain or disposal. It's easy to crush when things are stored under the sink or when the dishwasher is pushed back into its space.

**Fix:**

- Look under the sink for kinks, flat spots or a hose squashed behind storage bins.
- Straighten any kinks. A hose that won't stay round should be replaced.
- If it looks fine, place towels and a shallow pan underneath, loosen the clamp where it joins the disposal or sink drain, and check the end for packed food. Flush it with water, then refit the clamp.

## 3. Garbage disposal knockout plug (new installs)

If the dishwasher stopped draining **right after a new garbage disposal was installed**, this is almost certainly the cause. New disposals ship with a plastic plug sealing the dishwasher inlet, so people who don't use a dishwasher don't get leaks. It has to be knocked out when a dishwasher is connected.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the disposal or turn off its breaker. Confirm it won't run.
2. Disconnect the dishwasher hose from the disposal's inlet nipple.
3. Put a large screwdriver or a dowel into the inlet and tap it with a hammer until the plug pops inward.
4. Remove the loose plug from inside the disposal with pliers or tongs. Never use your hand.
5. Reconnect the hose, tighten the clamp, restore power and run a drain.

## 4. Clogged air gap

Some kitchens have an **air gap**: a small chrome or plastic cap on the sink deck or countertop. It stops dirty sink water from siphoning back into the dishwasher. Food can clog it, so water spurts out of the cap when the dishwasher drains.

**Fix:**

- Pull or twist off the decorative cap, then unscrew or lift off the inner cover.
- Clear food and debris with a small brush, a bent wire or a paper towel. Rinse it.
- If water still spurts out, the hose between the air gap and the disposal is clogged. Check that hose the same way as cause 2.
- No air gap? The drain hose should usually rise in a **high loop** up to the underside of the counter before dropping to the drain. Whether an air gap or a high loop is required depends on local plumbing code.

## 5. Clogged sink drain or disposal

The dishwasher drains into the same pipe as your kitchen sink. If the sink is slow or the disposal is full of food, the dishwasher water has nowhere to go and may back up into the sink.

**Fix:**

- Run the disposal with cold water until it sounds clear, then start a drain again.
- If the sink itself drains slowly, clear it first with a plunger (cover the other basin and the air gap) or a drain snake. Our guide to a [clogged sink drain](/plumbing/clogged-sink-drain/) covers it step by step.
- Avoid chemical drain cleaners. They can damage the dishwasher and the disposal.

## 6. Drain pump or check valve problem

The **drain pump** sits under the tub and pushes water out. If it hums but nothing moves, something like glass, a cherry pit or a twist tie may be jammed in it. If it's silent, the pump may have failed. A **check valve** (a small flapper that stops water flowing back) can also stick shut.

**Fix:**

- With the power off and the filter removed, look into the sump with a flashlight. Remove any debris with pliers. Wear gloves for broken glass.
- On some models you can remove a cover in the sump to reach the pump impeller. Check your manual first.
- If the sump is clear and the pump still only hums or makes no sound, the pump, check valve or wiring needs repair. That usually means removing the dishwasher from the cabinet, so it's often a technician job.

## 7. Cycle interrupted or error code

A dishwasher drains at the end of each cycle. If the cycle was stopped partway, by a power cut, a tripped breaker or someone opening the door, water can be left in the tub. That's normal.

**Fix:**

- Close the door and run a short cycle, or use your model's cancel or drain function.
- If a code appears, look up the exact code in your owner's manual or on the maker's website. Codes differ by brand, so don't guess.
- Clear the cause it points to, then turn power off for a minute to reset the controls.

## Standing water and won't drain at all?

- Turn off power, bail out the water with a cup into a bucket and soak up the rest with towels.
- Clean the filter and check the sump for debris (causes 1 and 6).
- Check the hose, knockout plug, air gap and sink drain (causes 2 to 5).
- Run a short rinse or drain cycle with the door closed and watch the sink.

If the tub still holds water after all of that, the drain pump is the likely cause.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician if:

- The filter, hose, air gap and sink drain are all clear and the dishwasher still won't drain.
- The pump hums or is silent and you can't see any blockage.
- You'd need to pull the dishwasher out of the cabinet to reach the pump.
- You see water leaking under the dishwasher or smell burning.

Call a plumber if the kitchen sink drain stays clogged after plunging and snaking. A front-load washer with the same problem has a similar fix: see our guide to a [washer that won't drain](/appliances/washer-wont-drain/).
