A TV that won't turn on is usually not dead. Most of the time the cause is outside the TV: **a switched-off power strip or dead outlet**, **weak remote batteries**, or **a TV that's frozen in standby** and needs a proper reset.

If those check out, the problem may be **a setting that turns the TV off or keeps the screen dark**, an **HDMI-CEC conflict** with another device, or a real fault such as **a failed backlight or power board**. Start with the quick, free checks below.

> **Safety first:** Never take the back off a TV. The power supply inside uses capacitors that can hold a dangerous charge even after the TV is unplugged. If you smell burning, see smoke, or the cord or plug is scorched or damaged, unplug the TV and leave it unplugged. Don't keep pressing power on a TV that just made a pop or a spark.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Plug the TV straight into a wall outlet** that you know works, with no power strip, surge protector or extension cord in between.
2. **Unplug it for about 60 seconds**, then plug it back in.
3. **Look at the standby light** (a small light on the front or bottom edge of most TVs).
    - **No light at all?** Power may not be reaching the TV (causes 1 and 3). Note that some TVs keep the light off in standby, so check your manual.
    - **Light on or blinking?** Power is reaching the TV. Focus on the remote, the TV's own button, settings and the screen (causes 2, 4 to 8).
4. **Press the power button on the TV**, not the remote, and listen. A click, a startup sound or audio with a black screen means the TV is at least partly on.

## 1. Check the outlet, power strip and surge protector

Many "dead" TVs are really on a switched-off power strip, a worn-out surge protector or a dead outlet.

**Fix:**

- **Test the outlet** by plugging in a lamp or phone charger. If that doesn't work either, the problem is the outlet or circuit, not the TV. See [how to find why an outlet isn't working](/electrical/outlet-not-working/).
- **Check for a wall switch.** Some living room and bedroom outlets are controlled by a light switch.
- **Check the power strip's switch** and any reset button on it.
- **Look at the surge protector.** Many have a "protected" light. If it's off, or the surge protector is old or took a lightning hit, it may have stopped passing power. Replace it.
- **Check the power cord** at both ends. Many TVs have a detachable cord that can work loose at the back of the set.

## 2. Test the remote and its batteries

If the standby light is on but the remote does nothing, the remote is the prime suspect.

**Fix:**

- **Replace the batteries** with fresh ones, and check they're the right way round.
- **Test an infrared (IR) remote with a phone camera.** Point the remote's front end at the camera and press a button. On many phones you'll see the remote's bulb flicker on the screen. No flicker usually means a dead remote or dead batteries. Some phone cameras filter this light, so try a different camera if you see nothing.
- **Bluetooth or voice remotes** may need re-pairing after a power cut or battery change. Check your manual for the pairing steps for your model.
- **Clear the path.** A soundbar, decor or a new piece of furniture can block the TV's remote sensor.

## 3. Power cycle the TV by unplugging it

Smart TVs don't fully turn off when you press power. They sit in a low-power standby, and sometimes the software freezes there and ignores the button. A true power cycle clears it.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the TV from the wall**, not just from the power strip.
2. **Wait about 60 seconds.** Some manufacturers suggest holding the TV's power button for several seconds while it's unplugged to drain any leftover charge.
3. **Plug it back in**, wait a few seconds, and press the power button on the TV.

If it still ignores you, unplug the connected devices too and try again (see cause 8).

## 4. Press the power button on the TV itself

Every TV has a physical power button or control stick, often hidden on the bottom edge or the back. Pressing it rules out the remote.

**Fix:**

- Feel along the bottom edge and back corners for a small button or joystick. Your manual shows where it is.
- On some TVs one press brings up a small menu, and you choose power from it.
- If the TV turns on from its own button but never from the remote, go back to cause 2.

## 5. Read the standby light

The standby light is the TV's only way to talk to you when the screen is dark. What it means **varies by brand and model**, so treat the notes below as a general guide and check your manual for the details.

- **Steady light, TV won't start:** power is reaching the TV. Try causes 2 to 4 again, then the flashlight test (cause 6).
- **Light comes on, then the TV clicks off:** it may be shutting itself down to protect against a fault. Try a power cycle with nothing connected.
- **Blinking in a pattern:** many TVs use blink counts as error codes. Count the blinks, write down the pattern, and look it up in your manual or on the manufacturer's support site. The same pattern can mean different things on different brands.
- **No light at all:** either no power is arriving (cause 1) or your TV keeps the light off in standby. Your manual will say which.

## 6. Do the flashlight backlight test

If you hear sound, see the standby light change, or the TV clicks on but the screen stays black, the TV may be running with its backlight off. LCD and LED TVs light the picture from behind, and when that backlight fails the screen looks dead.

**Fix:**

1. **Darken the room**, turn the TV on and press the menu or home button.
2. **Shine a flashlight close to the screen at an angle**, a few inches away.
3. **Look for a faint picture or menu** in the beam.
    - **You see a faint image:** the TV works but the backlight or its power supply has failed. That's a repair job (see "When to get it repaired").
    - **You see nothing:** the TV may not be fully on, or the screen is off by a setting (causes 7 and 8).

OLED TVs make their own light and have no backlight, so this test doesn't apply to them.

## 7. Check sleep timers and auto power off

If the TV turns on and then shuts off by itself, a setting may be doing it on purpose.

**Fix:**

- **Sleep timer:** turns the TV off after a set time.
- **Auto power off or no signal power off:** many TVs switch off after a period with no input signal or no remote use. A source that goes to sleep can trigger it.
- **Energy saving modes** can dim the screen until it looks off. Some TVs also have a screen off or audio only option.

Setting names and menu locations vary, so look under the power, eco or general settings, or check your manual. Also make sure the TV's vents aren't blocked. A TV that overheats in a tight cabinet may shut itself off.

## 8. Rule out HDMI-CEC and the wrong input

**HDMI-CEC** lets devices control each other over HDMI. It's sold under names like Anynet+, SimpLink and Bravia Sync. It's handy, but it can make a TV turn off when a connected device turns off, or turn on to an input that's blank.

**Fix:**

- **Unplug every HDMI cable** and power cycle the TV (cause 3). If it now turns on and stays on, a connected device is the trigger.
- **Turn off HDMI-CEC** in the TV's settings, or on the soundbar, streaming device or console, then reconnect one device at a time.
- **Check the input.** A TV that's on but showing a source that's asleep can look dead. Press the input or source button and pick the right one. If you see "No signal", work through [fixes for a TV that says No Signal on HDMI](/tv-audio/tv-no-signal-hdmi/).

## TV still won't turn on at all?

If you've tried a known-good wall outlet, a 60 second power cycle with every cable removed, and the TV's own button, and you still get no light, no sound and no flashlight image, the fault is most likely inside the TV.

- **Try a different power cord** if your TV uses a detachable one of a standard type. Match the plug shape exactly.
- **Think back.** A storm, power outage or lightning strike just before the problem points to surge damage to the power board.
- **Write down what you see:** light color, blink pattern, clicks or sounds. Repair shops will ask.

Stop there. Don't open the back panel. Internal repairs involve high voltage and stored charge, and opening the TV can void your warranty.

## When to get it repaired

Contact the manufacturer or a TV repair shop if:

- The flashlight test shows a faint image (backlight failure).
- The standby light blinks a pattern your manual lists as a fault.
- The TV is completely dead on a working wall outlet after a full power cycle.
- You smell burning, heard a pop, or see damage to the cord or plug.

**Check your warranty first.** Many credit cards and retailer protection plans extend coverage. Outside warranty, repairs range from fairly cheap to a large share of the price of a new TV, so ask for a written quote. If it doesn't make sense, recycle the old set at a local e-waste drop-off.
