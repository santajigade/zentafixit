When your furnace won't turn on, the problem is usually **upstream of the burners**: the thermostat isn't calling for heat, the furnace has no power, or a safety switch is stopping it from starting.

The most common causes are **thermostat batteries or settings, a furnace power switch that's off, a tripped breaker, an open door safety switch and a clogged filter**. These are all quick to check, in that order.

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas (a rotten egg smell), don't touch any switches. Get everyone out of the house, then call your gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. Turn off the furnace power switch before opening any panel. Never bypass a safety switch, and make sure you have working carbon monoxide alarms.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Set the thermostat** to HEAT, a few degrees above the room temperature. Wait a few minutes.
2. **Listen at the furnace.** Silent, or do you hear the inducer fan, a click or a burner lighting?
3. **Look for a status light.** Many furnaces have an LED visible through a small window in the lower door. Note the blink pattern.
4. **Sort it out:**
    - **Screen blank or thermostat not calling:** cause 1.
    - **Furnace totally silent:** causes 2 to 4.
    - **Starts, then stops, or blinks a code:** causes 5 to 9.

## 1. Thermostat batteries and settings

The thermostat starts everything. If it isn't calling for heat, the furnace won't run.

**Fix:**

- Replace the **batteries** if the screen is blank or dim, or shows a low battery icon.
- Check the mode is **HEAT**, not COOL, OFF or a schedule that's holding a low temperature.
- Raise the set point **3 to 5°F above** room temperature.
- If you have a smart thermostat with no batteries and a blank screen, the furnace may have no power (causes 2 to 4) since it often powers the thermostat. If it's on but offline, see [smart thermostat not connecting to Wi-Fi](/smart-home/smart-thermostat-not-connecting-to-wifi/).

## 2. Furnace power switch is off

Most furnaces have a **service switch** that looks like an ordinary light switch, on the furnace or on a wall or ceiling nearby, often at the top of the basement stairs. People flip it off by mistake all the time.

**Fix:**

- Find the switch and make sure it's **ON**.
- If it's a regular-looking switch next to a light switch, try both.

## 3. Tripped breaker

The furnace is on its own circuit. A tripped breaker sits between ON and OFF, or shows a colored flag.

**Fix:**

1. Find the breaker labeled furnace, heat or blower.
2. Push it fully to **OFF**, then back **ON**.
3. If it trips again right away or within a few minutes, don't reset it again. Call a technician. There's a fault on the circuit or in the furnace.

## 4. Blower door safety switch is open

The panel over the blower presses a small **door switch** when it's in place. If the panel is loose, crooked or was put back badly after a filter change, the switch stays open and the furnace won't run.

**Fix:**

- Remove and refit the lower panel so it sits flat and fully latched.
- You should be able to see or feel it press the switch. Never tape or hold the switch closed.

## 5. Clogged air filter

A dirty filter cuts airflow. The heat exchanger overheats, and a **high-limit switch** shuts the burners off. After this happens several times, some furnaces **lock out** and won't restart until the power is reset.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the furnace power switch.
2. Slide out the filter. Note the size printed on the frame.
3. Replace it if it's grey or clogged, with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
4. Turn the power back on. Check the filter monthly in winter.

## 6. Dirty flame sensor

The **flame sensor** is a thin metal rod in front of the burners. It confirms the flame is lit. When it's coated with residue, the furnace lights, can't "see" the flame, and **shuts off within a few seconds**. It may retry a few times and then lock out.

**Fix:**

- Many homeowners clean it: power and gas off, remove the sensor (usually one screw), lightly rub the rod with a fine abrasive pad or emery cloth, wipe it clean and refit it. Don't touch it with bare fingers more than needed.
- If you're not sure which part it is, or you're not comfortable working near the burners and gas valve, **call a technician**. It's a quick, routine job for them.
- If cleaning doesn't help, the sensor, its wire or the ground connection may need attention from a pro.

## 7. Blocked condensate trap or drain (high-efficiency)

**High-efficiency furnaces** (usually with white plastic vent pipes) produce water. It drains through a **trap and hose** to a floor drain or pump. If that clogs, water backs up, a pressure switch can't close, and the furnace won't light. Sometimes you'll see water inside or under the cabinet.

**Fix:**

- Power off. Find the trap and drain hose, usually on the side of the furnace.
- Check for sludge or a kink. Many traps can be removed and rinsed; check your manual first.
- If a **condensate pump** is used, make sure it's plugged in and not full.
- Clear the drain line with a wet/dry vacuum at the outside end if accessible.

## 8. Gas supply is off

If the inducer fan runs and the igniter glows or clicks but there's no flame, the furnace may not be getting gas.

**Fix:**

- Check the **gas shutoff valve** on the pipe to the furnace. The handle is **in line with the pipe when open** and crosswise when closed.
- Check other gas appliances. If the stove or water heater won't light either, contact your gas company.
- On **propane**, check the tank gauge.
- Older furnaces with a **standing pilot**: if it's out, follow the relighting instructions on the label inside the door exactly. If it won't stay lit, call a pro.

## 9. Ignition or control board problem (pro only)

Hot surface igniters crack with age, and control boards, pressure switches and inducer motors can fail. These show up as a furnace that tries to start and gives up, often with a **blinking error code**.

**Fix:** Write down the blink pattern and match it to the chart on the inside of the furnace door. Then call a technician. Part replacement involves gas and line voltage.

## Furnace shows a blinking light or won't reset?

- **Read the code** against the label inside the door. Codes vary by brand and model, so don't rely on generic charts. Our [furnace blinking light codes](/hvac/furnace-blinking-light-codes/) guide explains the common categories.
- **Reset once:** power switch off, wait 30 seconds, back on.
- **Locks out again?** Don't keep resetting it. Repeated failed starts point to a real problem, such as a vent, flame sensor or ignition fault.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed technician if:

- You **smell gas** (after leaving the house and calling the gas company from outside).
- Your **carbon monoxide alarm** goes off. Get out and call 911.
- The breaker trips again, or you smell burning.
- It locks out repeatedly, or shows the same error code after the simple fixes.
- The flame sensor cleaning, condensate drain or pilot relighting is beyond your comfort level.
- You see soot, scorch marks or rust around the burners or vent.

An annual tune-up before winter keeps the flame sensor clean, checks the venting and catches weak igniters before they leave you without heat.
