When your iPhone has no sound, the cause is usually a setting, not a broken speaker. The phone has several separate sound paths (ringer, media, calls, alerts), and one switch or setting can silence just one of them.

The most common causes are **silent mode, the wrong volume slider, a Bluetooth device still connected, or a Focus like Do Not Disturb**. Less often, the phone thinks headphones are plugged in, or the speaker grille is clogged. Here's how to find which.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Unplug any headphones** and turn off Bluetooth for a moment in Control Center.
2. **Press volume up** a few times and watch the volume indicator on screen. If it shows a headphone or speaker icon instead of the phone's own speaker, go to cause 3 or 5.
3. **Play a song or video** in an app you trust, like Music or YouTube.
    - **No sound at all?** Check causes 3, 5, 7 and 8.
    - **Media plays, but calls or alerts are silent?** Check causes 1, 2 and 4.
    - **Silent in just one app?** Go to cause 6.
4. **Test the speaker directly.** Record a few seconds in Voice Memos and play it back. If that works, the hardware is fine.

## 1. Check the silent switch or Action button

Silent mode mutes ringtones, text tones and alerts. It does not mute music, videos or most games, which is why it confuses so many people. Alarms from the Clock app still sound in silent mode.

**Fix:**

- **Older iPhones** have a small Ring/Silent switch above the volume buttons. If you can see an orange stripe, the phone is on silent. Flip the switch toward the screen.
- **Newer iPhones** (starting with the iPhone 15 Pro) replace the switch with an **Action button**. By default, a press and hold toggles silent mode. If you've assigned the Action button to something else, use the silent mode control in Control Center instead.
- Look for a **crossed-out bell** at the top of the screen. That means silent mode is on.

## 2. Turn up the right volume

Your iPhone has a ringer and alerts volume that's separate from the media volume. If the side buttons only change media volume, your ringer can stay low no matter how many times you press them.

**Fix:**

1. Open **Settings > Sounds & Haptics**.
2. Drag the **Ringtone and Alert Volume** slider up. You should hear a sample tone.
3. Turn on **Change with Buttons** if you want the side buttons to control ringer volume too. With it off, the buttons only change volume while something is playing.
4. For media, turn the volume up while a song is playing, or use the volume slider in Control Center.

## 3. Disconnect Bluetooth headphones and speakers

This is a very common one. Your iPhone sends audio to the last Bluetooth device it connected to, so sound can be going to AirPods in your pocket, a speaker in another room or your car.

**Fix:**

- Open **Control Center** and look at the music controls. The small audio output icon (a triangle with rings) shows where sound is going. Tap it and choose **iPhone**.
- Or turn off Bluetooth briefly in **Settings > Bluetooth** and test again.
- If AirPods keep grabbing the audio when you don't want them to, put them back in their case and close the lid.
- For calls only, check **Settings > Accessibility > Touch > Call Audio Routing**. **Automatic** is the usual choice. If it's set to Bluetooth Headset, calls go there first.

If the problem is the other way round (your AirPods won't take the audio), see [AirPods not connecting](/phones-tablets/airpods-not-connecting/).

## 4. Turn off Focus or Do Not Disturb

A Focus mode (Do Not Disturb, Sleep, Driving, Work and others) silences calls and notifications from anyone not on its allowed list. Media still plays, so it can look like only some sounds are broken.

**Fix:**

1. Swipe down to open **Control Center** and tap **Focus**. If one is highlighted, tap it to turn it off.
2. A small moon or other Focus icon at the top of the screen, or on the Lock Screen, means one is on.
3. Check **Settings > Focus** for schedules. A Sleep or Work Focus can turn itself on at set times.
4. If one person's calls never ring, check that their contact doesn't have a silent ringtone or that you haven't silenced their messages thread.

## 5. Get it out of headphone mode

Sometimes an iPhone thinks headphones are still plugged in. The volume indicator shows a headphone icon and the speaker stays silent. Lint or debris in the charging port, or a wired adapter that was just removed, can cause this.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/iphone-no-sound-parts.svg" alt="Diagram of a generic smartphone showing the silent switch or Action button and volume buttons on the left side, the earpiece speaker at the top, the bottom speaker grille, and the charging port at the bottom" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The bottom grille handles media, ringer and speakerphone; the earpiece handles normal calls.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

1. Plug a pair of wired headphones or an adapter in, then unplug it. This often resets the detection.
2. Turn the phone off and shine a flashlight into the charging port. Gently loosen lint with a wooden or plastic toothpick. Never use metal, liquids or compressed air.
3. Restart the phone (cause 8).
4. If sound comes out of only one side of your headphones, check **Settings > Accessibility > Audio & Visual** and make sure the balance slider is centered.

## 6. Check the app's own volume and mute

Many apps have their own mute button or volume setting. Social media apps often start videos muted, and games usually have separate music and effects sliders.

**Fix:**

- Look for a speaker icon with a line through it on the video or in the app's player, and tap it.
- Open the app's settings and check its sound or audio options.
- Close the app completely (swipe up from the bottom and hold, then swipe the app away) and reopen it.
- If it's still silent in one app only, update or reinstall that app from the App Store.

## 7. Clean the speaker grilles

Pocket lint, makeup and dust can pack into the tiny speaker openings and make sound quiet, muffled or tinny. The earpiece at the top of the screen collects skin oil and is a common reason calls sound faint.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the phone off and remove the case. Some cases cover part of the speaker.
2. Brush the bottom grilles and the earpiece gently with a **soft, dry brush** (a clean, new soft toothbrush works).
3. Don't push pins, needles or toothpicks into the speaker holes, and don't use compressed air or liquids. You can push debris in or damage the speaker mesh.
4. If the phone got wet, let it dry fully with the speaker facing down before you judge the sound.

## 8. Restart and update iOS

A software glitch can stop audio until the phone restarts. Updates also fix known sound bugs.

**Fix:**

1. **Restart:** press and hold the side button and either volume button until the power off slider appears, then slide it. On iPhones with a Home button, hold the side or top button. Wait 30 seconds and turn it back on.
2. **Update:** go to **Settings > General > Software Update** and install anything available. Plug in and use Wi-Fi first.
3. If sound broke right after an update, check for a newer one in the next few days. Apple often releases quick follow-up fixes.

For exact button steps for your model, or a force restart if the screen is frozen, check Apple's support site.

## Still no sound from the speaker at all?

If you've done all of the above and the Voice Memos test plays nothing, or only crackling or buzzing:

- **Back up your iPhone first** (iCloud or a computer) before any repair or reset.
- **Reset All Settings** clears settings but not your data. It's under **Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset**. You'll need to re-enter Wi-Fi passwords afterward. Try this only if you suspect a setting you can't find.
- **Think about recent events.** A drop, water exposure or a recent screen or battery repair can disconnect or damage the speaker.
- If sound works through headphones but never through the speaker, the speaker itself is the likely fault. If it fails on headphones and speaker alike, a software or logic board problem is more likely.

If your phone also has trouble starting up, see [iPhone won't turn on](/phones-tablets/iphone-wont-turn-on/).

## When to call a pro

Contact Apple Support, an Apple Store or an Apple Authorized Service Provider if:

- The Voice Memos test plays nothing after cleaning, restarting and updating.
- The speaker crackles, buzzes or distorts at every volume.
- Sound stopped after a drop, a liquid exposure or a third-party repair.
- The phone shows a headphone icon even with a clean port and after a restart.

Apple can run a remote diagnostic and tell you whether the speaker or another part needs replacing. Check your warranty or AppleCare status first, since speaker repairs on a covered phone may cost little or nothing. For current pricing and service options, check Apple's support site.
