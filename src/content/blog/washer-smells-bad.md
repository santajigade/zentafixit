A washing machine that smells bad is almost always growing something. Warm water, lint, hair and leftover detergent collect in places that never fully dry, and bacteria and mold turn that residue into a musty, sour or swampy smell that ends up on your clothes.

The most common causes are **a dirty door gasket, a slimy detergent dispenser, too much detergent or fabric softener, and keeping the door shut between loads**. A clogged drain filter or a dry drain trap can add a stronger, sewer-like smell. Here's how to find the source and clean each spot.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Wipe out the door gasket, pull out and scrub the detergent dispenser, clean the drain pump filter on a front loader, then run the washer's cleaning cycle with a washer cleaner or the bleach your manual allows. > **Safety first:** Never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia or other cleaners, not even one after the other in the same cycle. The fumes are toxic. Wear gloves, keep the room ventilated, and unplug the washer before you open the drain filter panel or reach behind the machine. If the smell is like natural gas (rotten eggs that don't go away) and you have a gas dryer or water heater nearby, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.

## How to tell where the smell is coming from: quick test

1. **Smell the door gasket and drum** with the door wide open. Musty or mildew smell here points to causes 1, 3 and 5.
2. **Pull out the detergent drawer** (front loaders) or check the dispensers under the lid (top loaders). Black or brown slime means cause 2.
3. **Smell a fresh load right out of the washer.** If the clothes smell sour but the washer doesn't, look at cause 4.
4. **Sniff near the drain standpipe** behind the washer and any floor drain nearby. A sewer smell that is there even when the washer isn't running points to cause 7.
5. **Run the hot tap at a nearby sink.** If the hot water itself smells like rotten eggs, the problem is your water supply or water heater (cause 8).

## 1. A dirty door gasket (front loaders)

The thick rubber seal around a front loader's door has folds that hold water, lint, hair, coins and the odd sock. This is the number one source of front loader smells, and you often see gray or black spots in the bottom folds.

**Fix:**

1. Open the door and gently pull back the gasket folds all the way around. Look closely at the bottom.
2. Remove any debris, then wipe the folds with warm water and a little dish soap, or with the gasket cleaner or diluted bleach solution your manual recommends.
3. Scrub stubborn spots with an old toothbrush. Don't use abrasive pads or anything sharp that could tear the rubber.
4. Dry the folds with a towel.
5. After every wash, give the bottom of the gasket a quick wipe and leave the door open.

If the gasket is torn, stained black or still smells after cleaning, it can be replaced.

## 2. A slimy detergent dispenser

Liquid detergent and fabric softener don't always rinse out completely. The leftover film in the dispenser drawer and the cavity behind it grows slime that smells and drips into every load.

**Fix:**

1. Pull the drawer out. Most have a release tab, so check your manual if it doesn't slide free.
2. Soak it in warm, soapy water and scrub every compartment, including any removable siphon caps or inserts.
3. With a flashlight, look into the opening the drawer slides into. Scrub the top and sides with a toothbrush and wipe them clean.
4. Rinse, dry and refit the drawer.

On a top loader, wipe out the detergent, bleach and softener dispensers in the lid or around the rim, and the underside of the lid.

## 3. Residue inside the drum and tub

The hidden outer tub collects a layer of detergent, softener and lint, especially if you mostly wash in cold water. On top loaders, residue also builds up under the rim and around the agitator.

**Fix:**

1. Empty the washer.
2. Run its built-in cleaning cycle if it has one. If not, use the hottest, longest cycle your manual recommends for cleaning.
3. Use **one** cleaning product: a washer cleaner tablet or powder made for your type of washer, or liquid chlorine bleach if your manual allows it. Use the amount and the dispenser your manual specifies.
4. When it finishes, wipe the drum, the door glass and the gasket, then leave the door open to dry.
5. For heavy buildup, repeat the cycle, or follow it with a rinse cycle before using any other product.

Repeat about once a month, or as often as your manual says.

## 4. Too much or the wrong detergent

More detergent doesn't mean cleaner clothes. Extra soap doesn't rinse out, and it leaves a film in the washer and on the fabric that smells sour once bacteria get to it. Regular detergent in a high-efficiency (HE) washer makes too many suds, which makes this worse.

**Fix:**

- If your washer has the HE symbol, use only detergent marked HE.
- Measure. Use the line on the cap for your load size, not a full cap.
- Go easy on liquid fabric softener. It leaves a waxy film that holds odors.
- Wash towels and workout clothes in warm or hot water now and then if the care labels allow it.
- If clothes already smell, run them again with no softener and the recommended detergent amount.

## 5. Leaving the door or lid shut

A closed washer stays damp inside for hours or days, which is exactly what mold and mildew need. Wet laundry left in the drum sours quickly too.

**Fix:**

- Move clothes to the dryer or line as soon as the cycle ends.
- Leave the door or lid open between loads so air can dry the drum. Pull the detergent drawer out a little too.
- If you have small children or pets, keep the laundry room door closed instead of the washer door, and never let a child play inside a washer.

## 6. A clogged drain pump filter

Most front loaders have a filter in front of the drain pump that catches lint, coins, hair and small items. Old water and gunk sit there between washes and can smell like a swamp. Many top loaders don't have a filter you can reach, but some do, so check your manual.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the washer.
2. Open the small access panel at the bottom front. Lay down towels and put a shallow pan under it.
3. If there is a small drain tube, pull it out, unplug its cap and let the water run into the pan. Otherwise, slowly unscrew the filter a little at a time.
4. Remove the filter, clear out debris and rinse it under the tap. Wipe out the opening.
5. Screw the filter back in firmly and close the panel. Plug the washer in and check for drips on the next cycle.

If the washer drains slowly or not at all, see our guide on [what to do when a washer won't drain](/appliances/washer-wont-drain/).

## 7. Sewer gas from the standpipe or P-trap

Your washer drains into a pipe in the wall or floor called a standpipe. Below it is a P-trap, a U-shaped bend that holds water and blocks sewer gas from coming up. If the trap dries out, is missing, or the drain isn't vented properly, you get a sewer smell in the laundry room, and it may be there even when the washer is off.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/washer-smells-bad-standpipe.svg" alt="Diagram of a washer drain hose hooked loosely into a standpipe, which drops into a U-shaped P-trap holding water before the pipe runs into the wall; the water seal stops sewer gas coming back up" width="640" height="420" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The water in the P-trap is what keeps sewer gas out of the room.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

- **Refill a dry trap.** Pour a bucket of water down the standpipe, and down any floor drain in the laundry room that rarely gets used.
- **Check the drain hose.** It should hook loosely into the standpipe with an air gap around it. Don't tape or seal it in, and don't push it in deeper than your manual allows. A sealed or too-deep hose can siphon water out of the washer or the trap.
- **Clean the standpipe.** Lint and detergent slime build up inside it and smell. Run a drain snake or brush down it, then flush it with water.
- **Look for a missing trap.** If the standpipe goes straight into the floor with no U-bend you can find, or the smell keeps coming back after you refill it, call a plumber. Venting problems also need a plumber.

## 8. Rotten egg smell from the water itself

If your hot water smells like rotten eggs at the sink as well as the washer, the washer isn't the cause. This sulfur smell often comes from a reaction inside the water heater or from well water.

**Fix:** Have your water heater checked, or your well water tested, by a plumber or water treatment professional. Don't try to treat it by adding chemicals to the washer.

## Still smells after cleaning everything?

- **Repeat the cleaning cycle.** Long-term buildup can take two or three cycles to clear.
- **Check the outer tub.** On some washers, heavy mold or residue between the drum and the outer tub can't be reached without taking the machine apart. That's a job for an appliance technician.
- **Replace the gasket** if it is stained, torn or still smells.
- **Look for a leak under the washer.** Water pooling under the machine or in the floor can smell musty. Unplug it and turn off the water before you pull it out.
- **Smells like burning?** That's a different problem, such as a slipping belt or motor trouble. Stop the cycle, unplug the washer and have it checked.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance repair technician if:

- The smell comes back quickly even with monthly cleaning, the right detergent and the door left open.
- You see mold you can't reach, or the gasket needs replacing and you'd rather not do it.
- The washer leaks, drains poorly after you've cleaned the filter, or smells like burning.

Call a plumber if the sewer smell persists after you refill the trap, the standpipe has no trap, the drain backs up or gurgles, or your hot water smells like rotten eggs. And if you ever smell natural gas, leave first and call your gas utility from outside.
