Your central air conditioner always makes some noise, but a new rattle, buzz or screech is the system telling you something has changed. The sound itself is one of the best clues to what is wrong and how urgent it is.

The most common causes are **loose panels or debris, a failing capacitor or contactor, worn fan motor bearings and a clogged condensate drain**. Some of these you can fix in a few minutes. Others involve high voltage or refrigerant and are strictly a job for an HVAC technician.

> **Safety first:** Central AC runs on 240 V. Before you touch anything, turn the system **off at the thermostat**, then switch off the **outdoor disconnect** (the box on the wall near the condenser) and the AC breaker in your panel. Never open the electrical access panel on the outdoor unit: the **capacitor** inside can hold a dangerous charge even with the power off. Refrigerant work is for EPA-certified technicians only.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Note when the noise happens.** At startup, the whole time the system runs, or right after it shuts off? A sound only at startup often points to electrical parts. A sound the whole time often points to the fan, the blower or a loose part.
2. **Find where it comes from.** Stand by the outdoor unit, then by the indoor unit (the furnace or air handler) and then at a vent. Which one is loudest?
3. **Name the sound.** Use the chart above: rattle, buzz, click, squeal, bang, hiss or gurgle.
4. **Check the easy things first.** Look for sticks or leaves in the outdoor unit, a loose screw on a panel, a clogged air filter and a closed or blocked vent.
5. **Decide whether to stop.** Loud banging, grinding, screeching, screaming or any burning smell means turn the system off now and call a pro (see the section near the end).

## 1. Rattling: loose panels, screws or debris

Rattling is the most common AC noise and often the easiest to fix. Sticks, leaves or a pine cone can fall into the outdoor unit and hit the fan blades. Panels and screws loosen over years of vibration. A condenser that has settled on an uneven pad can also vibrate against its base or a nearby wall.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the system off at the thermostat and switch off the outdoor disconnect and breaker.
2. Wearing gloves, look through the top grille and the side fins for debris. Remove what you can reach without taking the unit apart.
3. Tighten any loose screws on the outer cabinet panels (not the electrical access panel).
4. Clear plants and clutter back at least 2 feet from all sides so nothing touches the cabinet.
5. Check the indoor unit too: a loose furnace or air handler door, or a loose duct, can rattle.
6. If the unit rocks or sits crooked on its pad, a technician can level it. If the rattle comes from inside the cabinet, call a pro.

## 2. Buzzing or humming: electrical parts (pro only)

A steady low hum from the compressor is normal. A loud buzz, or a hum while the outdoor fan doesn't spin, is not. Common causes are a failing **capacitor**, a worn **contactor** (the switch that turns the outdoor unit on), loose wiring, or a fan or compressor motor that is struggling to start.

**Fix:** Turn the system off at the thermostat and call an HVAC technician. A motor that can't start draws a lot of current and runs hot, so don't keep the system running. Don't poke the fan blades with a stick to get them going. The capacitor can store a dangerous charge, and testing it safely takes the right meter and training. If you're curious, here is how [a capacitor stores charge](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/capacitor-working-principle/).

## 3. Clicking: thermostat, relay or contactor

A single click when the system starts and another when it stops is normal. That is the thermostat and the contactor switching. Rapid or repeated clicking, especially when the outdoor unit never starts, can point to a thermostat problem, low voltage on the control circuit, or a faulty relay or contactor.

**Fix:**

- Replace the thermostat batteries if it uses them and the screen is dim or blank.
- Make sure the thermostat is set to COOL with the setpoint a few degrees below room temperature.
- Check that the furnace or air handler door is fully closed. Many units have a safety switch behind the door.
- If the clicking continues, turn the system off and call a technician to check the contactor and control wiring. Our guide to [an AC that won't turn on](/hvac/ac-not-turning-on/) covers the other checks.

## 4. Squealing or screeching: fan or blower motor

A high-pitched squeal usually comes from a motor. Outdoors, it is often the **condenser fan motor bearings** wearing out. Indoors, it is the **blower motor**. Some older blowers use a belt, and a worn or loose belt squeals too. A squeal at startup that fades can be an early sign. A constant screech is more urgent.

**Fix:**

- Turn the system off and check whether the outdoor fan blades spin freely by hand, only after the disconnect and breaker are off. Stiff or wobbly blades point to worn bearings.
- Many modern motors are sealed and can't be oiled. Check the motor label or your manual before adding any oil.
- If you have a belt drive blower and see a cracked or glazed belt, a technician can replace and tension it.
- A worn motor needs replacing by a technician. Running it until it seizes can damage other parts.

## 5. Banging or clanking: a loose or broken part

Banging or clanking means something is loose or broken and hitting other parts. It could be a fan blade that has come loose, a broken motor mount, or a part inside the compressor. Banging at startup can also come from the compressor itself.

**Fix:** Turn the system off at the thermostat right away and call an HVAC technician. A loose fan blade can hit the cabinet and break more parts, and a failing compressor gets worse the longer it runs. If you hear banging from the ducts instead (often as the system starts or stops), the metal ducts may be flexing with the change in pressure. Make sure the filter is clean and the vents are open, and mention it to the technician.

## 6. Hissing, bubbling or screaming: refrigerant or pressure (pro only)

A brief hiss or gurgle for a few seconds after the system shuts off can be normal as pressures even out. A hiss or bubbling that goes on while the system runs, especially at the copper refrigerant lines, can mean a **refrigerant leak**. A loud, high-pitched scream from the outdoor unit can mean very high pressure inside the compressor and is a reason to shut the system off at once.

**Fix:** Turn the system off and call a licensed HVAC technician. Refrigerant doesn't get used up, so a low level means a leak that has to be found and repaired. Under EPA rules, refrigerant work requires a certified technician. Other signs of a leak include ice on the lines, oily residue on the copper fittings and weak cooling. See [AC unit freezing up](/hvac/ac-unit-freezing-up/) if you also see ice.

## 7. Gurgling or dripping: condensate drain

Your AC pulls water out of the air at the indoor coil. That water runs into a pan and out through a drain line. If the line is partly blocked, water can back up and gurgle or bubble as it drains. A clogged drain can also trip a float switch that shuts the system off, or cause a leak.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the system off at the thermostat and the power switch at the indoor unit.
2. Look for standing water in the drain pan.
3. Find where the drain line ends outside or at a floor drain, and use a **wet/dry vacuum** on that end to pull out the clog.
4. If there is a capped access tee near the indoor unit, you can pour in a cup of distilled white vinegar to help keep the line clear.
5. If water is pooling around the indoor unit, the drain line or pan is the likely cause, not the noise source.

## 8. Whistling: restricted airflow

Whistling at the vents or the return grille usually means air is being forced through too small a gap. Common causes are a clogged air filter, a filter that is too restrictive for your system, closed or blocked vents, or a gap around the filter slot or a duct joint.

**Fix:**

- Replace a dirty filter. Check it monthly in cooling season and follow the filter maker's guidance on how often to change it.
- Open closed supply vents and move furniture or rugs off vents and returns.
- Make sure the filter fits its slot and the slot cover is in place.
- If one duct joint whistles, a technician can seal it. Foil tape made for ducts is also sold for small gaps.

## Loud grinding, screaming or a burning smell?

Turn the system **OFF** at the thermostat and leave it off if:

- You hear loud grinding, banging, screeching or a high-pitched scream.
- You smell burning or see smoke from either unit.
- The outdoor unit hums or buzzes but the fan never starts.
- The AC breaker trips again after one reset.

Running the system in this state can damage the compressor, which is usually the most expensive part to replace. If you see smoke or flames, leave the house and call 911.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

- The noise is buzzing, squealing, banging, hissing or screaming.
- The rattle comes from inside the cabinet, not a loose outer panel or debris.
- The outdoor fan doesn't spin, spins slowly or wobbles.
- You suspect a refrigerant leak or see ice or oily residue on the lines.
- The noise is still there after you have cleared debris, tightened panels, changed the filter and cleared the drain.

Tell them what the sound is, where it comes from and when it happens (startup, running or shutdown). A short phone recording of the noise can help them bring the right parts.
