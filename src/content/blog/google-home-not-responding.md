When a Google Nest speaker or display stops answering "Hey Google," it is usually not broken. Something between your voice and Google's servers has been cut off: the microphone, the Wi-Fi, the wake word, or a setting in the Google Home app.

The most common causes are **the microphone switch turned off, a dropped Wi-Fi connection, and the speaker not hearing you over noise**. Work through the quick test below, then the numbered fixes in order.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Look at the lights or screen.** Orange or red lights, or a crossed-out microphone icon on a display, usually mean the mic is off (fix 1). No lights at all point to power (see the last section).
2. **Stand close and say "Hey Google, what time is it?"** in a normal voice. If it lights up but doesn't answer, suspect the internet (fix 2). If it doesn't light up, suspect the mic or the wake word (fixes 1 and 3).
3. **Open the Google Home app.** If the speaker shows as offline or unavailable, it has lost Wi-Fi (fix 2).
4. **Test another speaker or your phone's assistant.** If everything in the house stopped at once, the problem is your internet or Google's service, not this speaker.

## 1. Turn the microphone back on

Every Nest speaker and display has a physical microphone switch, usually on the side or back. When it's off, the speaker can't hear you at all, and most models show orange or red lights or a mic icon to warn you.

**Fix:**

- Find the switch near the power cable or on the back of the device and slide it to the on position.
- Watch for the warning lights to go off, then try the wake word again.
- If small children or pets reach the speaker, check the switch first whenever it stops listening.

## 2. Check Wi-Fi and the internet

The speaker sends your voice to Google over the internet. If your Wi-Fi is down, the speaker is too far from the router, or the router changed (new password, new network name, new router), it can't answer. It may say it's having trouble connecting.

**Fix:**

1. Check that the internet works on your phone while connected to the same Wi-Fi.
2. Restart your modem and router: unplug them for about 30 seconds, then plug them back in and wait a few minutes.
3. If you changed your Wi-Fi name or password, open the Google Home app, find the speaker and update its Wi-Fi network in the device settings. You may need to set it up again.
4. If the speaker is far from the router, move it closer or add a mesh point nearby.

If your phone says it's connected to Wi-Fi but nothing loads, our guide to [Wi-Fi connected but no internet](/networking/wifi-connected-no-internet/) walks through the fixes.

## 3. Adjust Hey Google sensitivity and Voice Match

If it responds sometimes but misses the wake word other times, it may not be hearing "Hey Google" clearly. On many Nest speakers and displays you can change how sensitive it is to the wake word in the Google Home app. Voice Match helps it recognize the people who use it.

**Fix:**

- In the Google Home app, open the speaker's settings and look for a Hey Google sensitivity option. Raise it a step and test again.
- Retrain Voice Match in the app's Assistant settings if it answers other people but not you.
- Say the wake word clearly and pause briefly before the rest of the request.

## 4. Move it away from noise and walls

The microphones need to hear you over the room. A speaker tucked in a cabinet, pressed against a wall, behind a TV or next to a loud fan, dishwasher or range hood can miss the wake word.

**Fix:**

- Put the speaker on an open surface at about chest height, with a few inches of space around it.
- Keep it away from noisy appliances, air vents and the TV speakers.
- If it's playing music loudly, speak a bit louder or closer. The speaker has to hear you over its own sound.

## 5. Restart the speaker

A restart clears temporary glitches, and it fixes many cases where the speaker seems frozen or slow to answer.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the power cable from the speaker or the wall outlet.
2. Wait about 60 seconds.
3. Plug it back in and wait until it finishes starting up before you test it.

You can also restart it from the device's settings in the Google Home app if the app can still see it.

## 6. Check the volume and audio output

Sometimes the speaker hears you and answers, but you can't hear it. The volume may be at zero, or replies may be going to a Bluetooth speaker, a speaker group or a TV.

**Fix:**

- Say "Hey Google, volume 5" or use the volume control on the device (tap or touch the sides or top, depending on the model).
- In the Google Home app, check whether a default speaker or a Bluetooth speaker has been set for music or replies, and change it back.
- Disconnect paired Bluetooth devices you no longer use.

## 7. Check Downtime and the Google Home app

Digital Wellbeing settings in the Google Home app can include Downtime, which pauses the assistant on chosen devices during set hours. If the speaker stops working at the same time every night, this is the likely cause. The speaker can also stop working properly if it was removed from your home in the app or is linked to a different Google account.

**Fix:**

- In the Google Home app, check Digital Wellbeing for Downtime or filters on that device and adjust the schedule.
- Make sure the speaker appears in your home in the app and is signed in to the right Google account.
- If it controls lights or plugs but those commands fail, check that the linked device still works in its own app.

## 8. Use the right power adapter and let it update

A weak or wrong power adapter can make a speaker restart on its own, drop off Wi-Fi or stop responding. Nest speakers and displays update their software automatically, and a speaker that has been unplugged for a long time may need a while to catch up.

**Fix:**

- Use the power adapter that came with the speaker, plugged straight into a wall outlet rather than a loose power strip.
- Leave the speaker plugged in and online for a while to let it update.
- If it restarts by itself or the light flickers, try a replacement adapter from Google for your model.

## 9. Factory reset as a last resort

A factory reset clears all settings and links, so you have to set the speaker up again in the Google Home app. Use it only after the steps above.

**Fix:**

- Each model resets differently, usually by holding a button or the top of the speaker for several seconds, sometimes with the mic switch off first. Follow Google's reset steps for your exact model.
- After the reset, set it up again in the Google Home app and update the Wi-Fi details.

## Speaker won't turn on or light up at all?

- Check that the outlet works by plugging in a lamp or phone charger.
- Check both ends of the power cable and try the original adapter if you've been using another one.
- Look for damage, a burning smell or heat at the adapter. If you find any, unplug it and stop using it.
- If it still shows no lights with a known-good adapter and outlet, the speaker likely has a hardware fault.

## When to contact Google support

Contact Google support or the store where you bought it if:

- It won't power on with the original adapter and a working outlet.
- It restarts by itself again and again, even after a factory reset.
- It won't complete setup on a Wi-Fi network your other devices use without trouble.
- The microphone or speaker stopped working after a drop or spill.

Check your warranty first, since a faulty unit may be replaced for free.
