A microwave that lights up, hums and spins the turntable but leaves food cold is frustrating. Sometimes the cause is a simple setting. Other times, a part in the high-voltage circuit that makes the heat has failed.

The common causes are **demo mode or a low power level, a door that isn't closing properly, a weak outlet or extension cord, or a failed magnetron, high-voltage diode or capacitor**. You can safely check the first few yourself. The last group is a job for a technician, or a sign it's time to replace the microwave.

> **Safety first:** Never remove the outer cabinet of a microwave. The high-voltage capacitor inside can hold a dangerous, potentially fatal charge **even after the microwave is unplugged**. The checks in this guide only use the controls, the door, the outlet and the outside of the unit. If you see sparks, smell burning or hear loud buzzing, stop using it and unplug it.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/microwave-not-heating-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: check the display for demo or lock, then heat a cup of water on high for one minute; if it won't start check the door and outlet; if it runs but the water stays cold it needs a technician; if the water gets hot check power level and cookware" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The water test tells you whether the microwave makes heat at all.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Look at the display.** Does it say DEMO, show a lock symbol, or show a power level other than high? Fix that first (causes 1 and 2).
2. **Do the water test.** Put about 1 cup of water in a microwave-safe mug or glass measuring cup. Heat it on **high for 1 minute**.
3. **Feel the water carefully.** It should be clearly hot. If it's cold or barely warm, the microwave isn't producing heat.
4. **Note what else happens.**
    - **Won't start at all?** Causes 3 and 4.
    - **Runs normally but the water stays cold?** Rule out cause 1, then see cause 6.
    - **Water gets hot, but food doesn't?** Causes 2 and 5.

## 1. Demo mode or child lock is on

Many microwaves have a **demo mode** for showroom floors. In demo mode, the light, fan and turntable work and the timer counts down, but the microwave produces no heat. It can be switched on by accident with a button combination, or after a power outage on some models.

A **child lock** (control lock) stops the microwave from starting at all, and may show a lock symbol or a short message on the display.

**Fix:**

- Look for "DEMO", "D" or a lock symbol on the display.
- Look up how to exit demo mode or unlock the controls in your owner's manual. It's often a held button or a short key sequence, but it varies by brand, so follow your manual.
- Unplugging for a minute can reset some displays, but it doesn't clear demo mode on every model.

## 2. Power level set too low

If someone used a low power setting for defrosting or softening butter, some models remember it. At 10 to 30 percent power, the microwave cycles on and off and food heats slowly.

**Fix:**

- Clear the settings and start a fresh cook on **high** (full) power.
- Check whether a sensor or auto-cook program is selected. These choose their own power and time.
- Large, dense or frozen foods take longer. Stir or rotate halfway through, and use the defrost setting for frozen food.

## 3. Door not closing or a door switch problem

A microwave has several **door switches** (interlocks) that stop it running while the door is open. If the door doesn't close fully, or the latch hooks don't press the switches, the microwave may not start or may stop partway.

**Fix (outside only):**

- Close the door firmly. Check that nothing is caught in it and that food spills aren't keeping it from sealing.
- Look at the latch hooks on the door. If one is broken, bent or loose, the switches can't engage.
- Clean the door edge and the inner frame with a damp cloth and mild soap.
- If the door closes well and the microwave still won't start, or a fuse keeps failing, the door switches or the internal fuse need replacing. Those parts sit inside the cabinet next to the high-voltage circuit, so **call a technician**. Don't try to reach them yourself.

## 4. Outlet, extension cord or circuit problem

Microwaves draw a lot of current. On a loose outlet, a light-duty extension cord or a busy circuit, the voltage can drop and the microwave may heat weakly or reset.

**Fix:**

- Plug the microwave **directly into a wall outlet**. Avoid extension cords and power strips unless your manual says otherwise.
- Test the outlet with another appliance or a plug-in outlet tester. If it's dead or loose, see our guide to an [outlet that's not working](/electrical/outlet-not-working/).
- Don't run the microwave at the same time as a toaster, kettle or air fryer on the same circuit. If the breaker trips, see [why a breaker keeps tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/).
- Over-the-range and built-in microwaves are often on their own circuit. If yours stopped working, check the breaker panel.

## 5. Overheating, blocked vents or the wrong cookware

Microwaves have built-in protection that can shut them off if they overheat. Blocked vents, a long run time or a cabinet that's too tight can trigger it. Some containers also absorb microwave energy themselves, so the dish gets hot but the food doesn't.

**Fix:**

- Make sure the vents on the sides, top or back aren't blocked. Leave the clearance your manual asks for.
- Let the microwave cool for 30 minutes if it stopped during a long cook, then try the water test again.
- Use containers marked microwave-safe. Never use metal or dishes with metal trim, which can cause sparks.

## 6. Failed high-voltage parts (magnetron, diode, capacitor)

The heat in a microwave comes from the **magnetron**, which is powered by a **high-voltage transformer, capacitor and diode**. If any of these fail, the microwave can look completely normal, with light, fan, turntable and timer all working, but produce no heat. A loud hum or buzz, a burning smell, or a pop just before it stopped heating are common signs.

The capacitor can store a **dangerous charge even when the microwave is unplugged**, and it may stay charged for a long time. Professionals discharge and test it with the right tools before touching anything. (For background, here's how a [capacitor stores and releases energy](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/capacitor-working-principle/).)

**Fix:**

- **Do not open the cabinet** or try to test these parts.
- Get a quote from a qualified appliance technician, or replace the microwave.
- If it's still under warranty, contact the manufacturer first.

## Microwave won't turn on at all?

If the display is blank and nothing happens:

- Check that it's plugged in firmly and that the outlet works (cause 4).
- Check the breaker panel and any GFCI outlets on the kitchen counter. A tripped GFCI can cut power to other outlets on the same circuit.
- Unplug it for a minute, then plug it back in.
- If the outlet works and the microwave is still dead, an internal fuse, door switch or control board has likely failed. These are behind the cabinet, so call a technician or replace the unit.

## Repair or replace?

| Situation | Usual best choice |
|---|---|
| Demo mode, settings, door cleaning, outlet | Fix it yourself |
| Countertop model, no heat, out of warranty | Often replace, because the repair can cost close to a new unit |
| Over-the-range or built-in model, no heat | Get a repair quote first |
| Rusted interior, damaged door or door glass | Replace |

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a qualified technician, or replace the microwave, if:

- It runs normally but the water test stays cold after you've ruled out demo mode and power level.
- It hums or buzzes loudly, sparks, or smells of burning.
- It won't start with the door firmly closed and a working outlet.
- The door is damaged, bent or won't seal.

Never open the cabinet to test or replace the magnetron, capacitor, diode, transformer, fuse or door switches yourself.
