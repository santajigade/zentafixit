Water around the base of a toilet can come from a few different places. It might seep out from under the toilet itself, or it might start higher up and run down the porcelain before it pools on the floor.

The usual suspects are **a failed wax ring, a loose toilet, condensation on the tank, a leaking supply line, or loose tank-to-bowl bolts**. A few minutes with towels and food coloring will usually tell you which one you have.

> **Safety first:** Water from under the toilet may be dirty. Wear gloves, clean up with disinfectant, and wash your hands afterward. Turn off the water at the shut-off valve before taking anything apart.

## How to find the leak: dry and dye test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/toilet-leaking-at-base-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: dry everything and add dye to the tank; if the tank is sweating all over it's condensation; if drips are at the supply line it's the line or valve; if color appears under the tank it's the tank bolts or gasket; if the base gets wet after a flush it's the wax ring seal" width="640" height="510" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Work from the top down to find where the water starts.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Dry everything.** Wipe the tank, bowl, supply line, valve and floor with towels until they're completely dry.
2. **Add dye to the tank.** Put a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet in the tank water. Don't flush yet.
3. **Lay paper towels** around the base and under the supply line. They show even a small drip.
4. **Wait 15 to 30 minutes and look.** Feel the outside of the tank and run a dry paper towel along the supply line nuts, the valve, and the bolts under the tank. Colored water under the tank points to the tank bolts or a crack.
5. **Flush a few times and watch the base.** Water that seeps out from under the toilet right after flushing points to the wax ring or a loose toilet.

## 1. Condensation on the tank

When cold water fills the tank on a warm, humid day, moisture in the air forms droplets on the outside of the porcelain. They run down and drip onto the floor, which looks a lot like a leak. It's most common in summer and in bathrooms without good ventilation.

**Fix:**

- Run the bathroom exhaust fan during and after showers, or use a dehumidifier.
- Keep the bathroom door open when you can to let humid air out.
- Fit a tank liner kit (foam panels that insulate the inside of the tank).
- Ask a plumber about an anti-sweat (mixing) valve, which adds a little warm water to the tank supply.

## 2. A leaking supply line or shut-off valve

The flexible supply line runs from the shut-off valve to the bottom of the tank. Its nuts can loosen, and older lines can crack or corrode. Water then drips down the line or the wall and puddles near the base.

**Fix:**

1. Check both ends of the line with a dry paper towel: the nut on the valve and the nut under the tank.
2. Snug a loose nut gently. Plastic nuts on the tank end usually need only hand-tight plus a small turn. Overtightening can crack them.
3. If the line is kinked, bulging, rusty or more than a few years old, replace it. Turn off the water, flush to empty the tank, unscrew the old line and fit a new braided line of the right length.
4. If water seeps from around the valve handle, try a gentle snug of the packing nut behind the handle. If it still leaks or won't turn, have the valve replaced.

## 3. Leaking tank-to-bowl bolts or gasket

The tank sits on the bowl with a large rubber gasket and is held down by two or three bolts, each with its own rubber washer. If the washers wear out or the bolts loosen, water can drip from under the tank, especially just after a flush. The dye test shows colored drops on the bolts or the back of the bowl.

**Fix:**

1. Look under the tank with a flashlight. Note which bolt is wet.
2. Turn the water off and flush to empty the tank.
3. Snug each nut a little at a time, alternating sides so the tank sits level. Stop as soon as it feels firm. **Overtightening can crack the tank.**
4. If it still drips, replace the gasket, bolts and washers with a tank-to-bowl kit that fits your toilet. That means lifting the tank off, which is a manageable job with a helper.

## 4. A loose or rocking toilet

The toilet is held to the floor flange by two closet bolts. If the nuts loosen or the floor is uneven, the toilet rocks slightly each time you sit down. That movement breaks the seal of the wax ring and lets water out when you flush.

**Fix:**

1. Pop off the plastic caps at the base and tighten each nut a little at a time, alternating sides. Firm is enough. Too much force can crack the base.
2. If the toilet still rocks because the floor isn't level, slide plastic toilet shims under the low spots, then tighten again and trim the shims.
3. If it rocked for a while before you tightened it, the wax ring may already be damaged. Flush a few times and watch the base. If water still appears, replace the ring (step 5).

## 5. A failed wax ring

A wax ring seals the toilet's outlet to the drain flange in the floor. Over time it can compress, dry out or get squashed out of place, especially if the toilet has rocked. Water then seeps out from under the toilet during flushes. A sewer smell in the bathroom is another common sign.

**Fix:**

1. **Turn off the water** at the shut-off valve, flush and hold the handle down to drain as much as possible. Sponge out the rest of the tank and bowl.
2. **Disconnect the supply line** from the bottom of the tank.
3. **Remove the caps and nuts** from the closet bolts at the base.
4. **Rock the toilet gently to break the seal**, then lift it straight up with a helper and set it on an old towel or cardboard. It's heavy and the porcelain chips easily.
5. **Stuff a rag in the drain opening** to block sewer gas and stop anything from falling in.
6. **Scrape off the old wax** from the flange and the bottom of the toilet with a putty knife.
7. **Check the flange.** If it's cracked, broken or rusted through, it needs repairing before a new ring will seal. Repair rings and kits exist, but this is a common point to call a plumber.
8. **Fit new closet bolts and a new wax ring** (or a wax-free seal). Remove the rag.
9. **Lower the toilet straight down** over the bolts, then press down firmly with your body weight to set the ring. Don't rock it or lift it again, or you'll need another new ring.
10. **Tighten the nuts** a little at a time, alternating sides, until firm. Reconnect the supply line, turn the water on and flush several times while you check for leaks.

If you caulk around the base afterward, many plumbers leave a small gap at the back so any future leak shows up quickly instead of staying hidden.

## 6. A cracked tank or bowl

A hairline crack in the porcelain can let water seep out slowly. Cracks can be hard to see, so look closely with a flashlight, especially if the tank, bolts, supply line and base all test dry but water keeps appearing.

**Fix:** A crack in the tank or bowl can't be reliably repaired. Replace the cracked part or the whole toilet. A crack below the water line can get worse suddenly, so shut off the water and don't use the toilet until it's replaced.

## Floor soft or stained around the toilet?

A spongy floor, loose tiles, or dark stains around the base mean water has been getting under the toilet for a while. The subfloor may be soaked or rotting, and the ceiling below may show stains too.

- **Stop using the toilet** and turn off its water until it's fixed.
- **Pull the toilet** and check the flange and subfloor. Soft or crumbling wood needs to be cut out and replaced before a new wax ring will hold.
- **Look for mold** and dry the area fully. A fan and dehumidifier help.

Subfloor repair is usually a job for a plumber or a flooring contractor. If you aren't sure how long a slow drip has been running, this [water leak calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/ecology/water-leak/) can give you a rough idea of how much water it has been wasting.

## When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if:

- The flange is broken or the floor around the toilet is soft.
- The shut-off valve leaks or won't turn.
- You've replaced the wax ring and it still leaks.
- The tank or bowl is cracked and you'd rather not install a new toilet yourself.
- Water is showing up on the ceiling below the bathroom.

If the toilet also keeps running or refilling on its own, see [toilet keeps running](/plumbing/toilet-keeps-running/). For flushing problems, see [toilet won't flush](/plumbing/toilet-wont-flush/).
