When a dryer takes two or three cycles to finish a load, it's almost never a mystery part failing. A dryer works by heating air, pushing it through the tumbling clothes and blowing the wet air outside. If any part of that path slows down, the moisture has nowhere to go and the clothes stay damp.

The most common causes are a **clogged lint screen**, a **blocked or crushed vent**, and **clothes that are too wet or too crowded** when they go in. Heating problems on electric or gas dryers come after those. Start with the free checks.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the dryer before moving it or removing any panel. Electric dryers run on 240 V. On a gas dryer, also close the gas shutoff valve before pulling it out. **If you smell gas, don't touch any switches or plugs, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.** Lint is very flammable, and a lint-packed vent is a real fire risk, so don't keep running a slow dryer and hope.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Clean the lint screen** and start a timed dry on the highest heat setting with a normal load of damp towels.
2. **After 5 minutes, go outside** and hold your hand near the vent hood. Watch the flap and feel the air.
3. **Weak or no airflow, flap barely moving:** the lint screen or vent is restricted (causes 1 and 2).
4. **Strong, warm airflow:** the dryer is heating and venting. Look at the load, the washer and the cycle (causes 3, 4 and 5).
5. **Strong airflow but cool or barely warm:** the heat source is weak (causes 6, 7 and 8).

## 1. A clogged lint screen

The lint screen is the first place air gets choked. Even a thin layer of lint slows drying, and fabric softener sheets can coat the mesh with a film you can't see.

**Fix:**

- Pull the lint off **before every load**, not just when it looks full.
- Hold the screen under running water. If water beads up and sits on top, wash it with **warm soapy water and a soft brush**, rinse and let it dry fully before putting it back.
- Vacuum the slot the screen sits in with a crevice tool. Lint collects below the screen too.
- Check the screen for tears. A torn screen lets lint straight into the vent and should be replaced.

## 2. A blocked or crushed vent

This is the most common cause of slow drying and the most important one to fix. Lint builds up inside the duct over time, the flexible hose behind the dryer gets crushed when the dryer is pushed back, or the outdoor flap sticks shut. Bird nests and snow can block the hood too.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the dryer (and close the gas valve on a gas model), then pull it out gently.
2. Check the **transition hose** between the dryer and the wall. Straighten kinks and replace crushed or torn hose. Foil or plastic hose should be replaced with semi-rigid or rigid metal duct, following your dryer manual.
3. Disconnect the hose and brush out the duct from both ends with a vent brush kit.
4. Clean the **outdoor hood** and make sure the flap opens freely.
5. Reconnect, push the dryer back without crushing the hose, and repeat the airflow test.

Our step-by-step [dryer vent cleaning guide](/home-safety/dryer-vent-cleaning/) covers each step and which duct types are safe. Very long or complicated duct runs, or ones through the roof, are worth handing to a professional vent cleaner.

## 3. Clothes are too wet from the washer

If the washer isn't spinning water out properly, the dryer has to evaporate far more water. Clothes that drip when you lift them out are the giveaway.

**Fix:**

- Check that the washer actually finishes its spin. A drain problem or an unbalanced load can make it skip or shorten the spin.
- Pick a **higher spin speed** if your washer offers one, especially for towels and jeans.
- If clothes are sopping wet every time, the washer needs attention, not the dryer.

## 4. An overloaded or tangled load

Clothes need room to tumble so air can move between them. An overstuffed drum, or a load where sheets wrap everything into a ball, leaves damp patches in the middle.

**Fix:**

- Leave room for clothes to tumble freely. Many manuals suggest filling the drum no more than about **three quarters full**, so check yours.
- Dry sheets with a few smaller items, and untangle them halfway through if they ball up.
- Dry heavy items like towels and jeans separately from light ones, so the light items aren't overdried while the heavy ones stay wet.

## 5. Wrong cycle or dirty moisture sensors

Auto or sensor dry cycles stop when the dryer thinks the clothes are dry. Many dryers check this with **two metal sensor bars** inside the drum, usually near the lint screen. Residue from fabric softener and dryer sheets can coat them so the dryer ends the cycle too early.

**Fix:**

- Make sure you're not on a low-heat, delicate or air-only setting by mistake. Try a timed cycle on high.
- Find the sensor bars (check your manual) and wipe them with a soft cloth. Many manufacturers suggest a little rubbing alcohol on the cloth. Unplug the dryer first.
- Set the dryness level higher (for example "more dry") if your model has that option.
- If timed cycles work but sensor cycles never do, the sensor or control may need a technician.

## 6. Electric dryer: weak heat from the breaker or supply

An electric dryer heats with a 240 V element, while the drum motor runs on 120 V. If one half of the double-pole breaker trips, the drum can keep turning with little or no heat. Some apartments have 208 V service instead of 240 V, which gives noticeably less heat from a dryer designed for 240 V.

**Fix:**

- At the panel, push the dryer breaker **fully to OFF**, then back to ON. A half-tripped breaker can look like it's on.
- If the breaker keeps tripping, stop and call an electrician.
- If you're not sure what voltage your building has, check the dryer's rating label and ask your landlord or an electrician.
- If the breaker is fine and the dryer gives no heat at all, jump to the no-heat section below.

## 7. Gas dryer: burner keeps shutting off

A gas dryer that heats at the start of a cycle and then runs cool often has a burner that lights once and won't relight. Worn **gas valve coils** are a common cause of this, along with a weak igniter or a faulty flame sensor.

**Fix:**

- Make sure the gas shutoff valve behind the dryer is fully open (handle in line with the pipe).
- During a cycle, many models let you watch the burner through a small window or opening (check your manual). If the flame goes out and the igniter doesn't glow again when the dryer cools, the valve coils or igniter are the likely culprits.
- Gas parts should be replaced by a qualified appliance technician. **If you smell gas at any point, leave and call your gas utility from outside.**

## 8. Worn heating element, thermostat or lint inside the cabinet

On older dryers, heat can drop off slowly. A cycling thermostat may shut the heat off too early, or lint can pack around the blower wheel and inside the cabinet, restricting air and creating a fire hazard you can't see from the front.

**Fix:**

- If airflow outside is strong but the air is only lukewarm, a technician can test the thermostats and element.
- Have the cabinet and blower housing cleaned out if the dryer is several years old and has never been opened, or if you've had a vent blockage before.
- Don't bypass a thermostat or thermal fuse to "test" it in use. They protect against overheating and fire.

## No heat at all?

If the drum turns but the air is cold from the start of every cycle, this is a no-heat problem rather than a slow-drying one. Check the breaker (cause 6) and the gas valve, then follow the [dryer not heating](/appliances/dryer-not-heating/) guide, which covers thermal fuses, elements and igniters.

If drying is slow and you also notice a **burning smell**, a dryer that's **hot to the touch on the outside**, or lint coming out around the door, stop using it until the vent and cabinet are cleaned.

Long cycles also cost you on every load. To see what the extra running time adds to your bill, try this [electricity cost calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/everyday-life/electricity-cost/) with your dryer's wattage from its rating label.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician or a dryer vent professional if:

- You smell gas, or a gas dryer's burner won't stay lit. Leave gas work to a qualified technician.
- You smell burning, see scorch marks, or the dryer shuts off on its own when hot.
- The vent run is long, goes through the roof, or you can't reach the blockage.
- Airflow is strong, loads are normal and the dryer still only gets lukewarm.
- The breaker trips repeatedly, which needs an electrician.

Most slow dryers are fixed with a clean lint screen and a clear vent. Do those first, and keep doing them: it's the cheapest way to get faster drying and lower fire risk.
