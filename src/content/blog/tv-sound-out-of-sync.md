When TV sound is out of sync, you see lips move a moment before or after you hear the words. Even a small gap is distracting, and it can come from the app, the TV, a soundbar or receiver, or a wireless speaker.

The usual causes are **picture processing that delays the video**, **audio processing or delay settings on a soundbar or receiver**, **surround formats that take extra time to decode**, and **Bluetooth lag**. A glitch in one app or stream is also common, and that's the easiest to fix.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Try a different source.** Switch from your streaming app to live TV, another app, or a game console. If only one source is off, start with causes 1 and 2.
2. **Note which way it's off.** Do you hear words before lips move (sound early) or after (sound late)? Sound early usually points to picture processing (cause 3). Sound late usually points to the soundbar, the audio format or Bluetooth (causes 4, 5 and 7).
3. **Switch to the TV's own speakers.** If sync is fine on the TV speakers but off through a soundbar or receiver, the problem is in the audio chain (causes 4 to 6 and 8).
4. **Turn off Bluetooth audio** if you're using headphones or a wireless speaker, and listen on the TV speakers. If sync returns, see cause 7.

## 1. It's only one app, show or channel

If live TV and other apps look fine, the problem is in that one app, that show's audio track, or the stream itself. A slow or unstable internet connection can make a stream stall and lose sync, and some apps get out of step after a long viewing session.

**Fix:**

- Pause for a few seconds and play again, or skip back 10 seconds. This often resets the sync.
- Try a different show in the same app. If only one title is off, it may be a problem with that file, so try a different audio track or language in the player menu.
- Force close the app and reopen it. If your TV or streaming device lets you, clear the app's cache or reinstall it.
- If the picture also stalls or drops in quality, the stream is struggling. Our guide to [streaming that keeps buffering](/tv-audio/streaming-keeps-buffering/) covers Wi-Fi and internet fixes.
- On cable or satellite, if just one channel is off, it's likely on the provider's end. Change channels and come back, then report it if it persists.

## 2. A glitch in the app, TV or streaming device

TVs, streaming sticks and soundbars are small computers. After days of standby they can get into a state where audio and video drift apart.

**Fix:**

1. Close the app and reopen it.
2. Unplug the TV, the streaming device and the soundbar or receiver from the wall for 60 seconds. A remote "off" is often just standby.
3. Plug the soundbar or receiver in first, then the TV, then the streaming device, and test again.

## 3. Picture processing is delaying the video

Modern TVs can spend time "improving" the picture before showing it: motion smoothing, noise reduction, upscaling and similar features. While the picture waits, the sound may go straight out, so you hear words a beat early. TVs try to compensate, but it doesn't always work, especially with an external soundbar.

**Fix:**

- Turn on **game mode** (sometimes called low-latency mode). It switches off most processing, so the picture shows sooner.
- Or, in your normal picture mode, turn off **motion smoothing** (it has many brand names, so look for words like motion, clarity or smooth) and extra noise reduction.
- Test the same scene before and after. If game mode fixes it but you prefer the other picture mode, add a small audio delay instead (cause 4).

## 4. Soundbar or receiver audio delay

Most TVs, soundbars and AV receivers have a setting to delay the sound so it lines up with the picture. If it's set wrong, or both the TV and the soundbar are adding delay, the sound ends up late. A new soundbar often needs this adjusted once.

Look for a setting called something like **Audio Delay**, **AV Sync** or **Lip Sync**. Where it lives varies by brand and model, so check your manual.

**Fix:**

1. On the TV, set any audio delay or digital audio delay to zero or its default.
2. On the soundbar or receiver, find the AV sync or lip sync setting.
3. Play a clip with a person talking close to the camera, such as a news anchor.
4. Adjust in small steps. If sound is late, reduce the delay. If sound is early, add delay.
5. Some devices have an **automatic lip sync** option. If yours does, try it both on and off and keep whichever looks right.

## 5. The audio format (try PCM)

Surround formats like Dolby Digital, Dolby Atmos and DTS have to be decoded by the soundbar or receiver, and that takes time. Some setups handle it smoothly. Others fall slightly behind, or the TV doesn't account for the delay.

**Fix:**

- In the TV's sound settings, find the digital audio output or audio format option and set it to **PCM** (sometimes listed as stereo) as a test.
- If sync becomes correct, the decoding step was causing the lag. You can leave it on PCM, or switch back to Auto or Bitstream and fine-tune with the delay setting in cause 4.
- PCM over ARC is usually stereo, so you may lose true surround or Atmos. That trade-off is normal.

## 6. HDMI ARC or eARC setup

With a soundbar or receiver on HDMI ARC or eARC, the TV sends sound back down the cable. eARC is newer and is designed to pass lip sync information between devices, so an eARC TV and eARC soundbar often line up better than older ARC.

**Fix:**

- Make sure the cable goes into the TV port labeled ARC or eARC and the soundbar port labeled ARC, eARC or TV.
- If both the TV and soundbar support eARC, turn on eARC in the TV's sound settings (check your manual for the name).
- Make sure HDMI-CEC is on. It's the feature that lets the devices talk to each other, and it goes by different brand names.
- If you're stuck, try an optical cable as a test. If sync is fine on optical, the problem is in the HDMI setup. Our guide to [HDMI ARC vs eARC](/tv-audio/hdmi-arc-vs-earc/) explains ports, cables and settings in detail.

## 7. Bluetooth headphones or speakers

Bluetooth audio always adds some delay because the sound has to be compressed, sent and decoded. With some headphones and speakers the gap is small. With others it's obvious, especially on older devices.

**Fix:**

- Look in the TV's Bluetooth or sound settings for an audio delay or AV sync option for Bluetooth devices, and adjust it if available.
- Unpair and pair again, and keep the headphones or speaker close to the TV with a clear path.
- Turn off other Bluetooth devices nearby that might be competing for the connection.
- If the lag can't be fixed, use wired headphones, the TV's headphone jack, a wired soundbar, or headphones designed for TV use with their own transmitter.

## 8. Loose or worn HDMI cables

A cable doesn't add delay on its own, but a poor connection can cause dropouts, format changes and repeated HDMI handshakes. Each time the link resets, sync can drift.

**Fix:**

- With the devices turned off, unplug and firmly reseat both ends of each HDMI cable.
- Remove adapters, switches or extension cables between devices and test with a direct connection.
- Try a known good cable. For eARC, use an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable or a High Speed HDMI cable with Ethernet.
- Try a different HDMI input on the TV for your streaming device.

## 9. Outdated firmware

Sync problems are sometimes caused by software bugs that the maker fixes in an update. This is common after a new soundbar is paired with an older TV, or the other way round.

**Fix:**

- Check for a software or firmware update on the TV, in its settings menu.
- Update the streaming device and the app that's out of sync.
- Update the soundbar or receiver. Some update over Wi-Fi or a phone app, others with a USB stick. Your manual or the maker's support site will say how.
- Restart everything after updating, then test again.

## Still out of sync on everything?

- **Reset the sound settings.** Many TVs and soundbars have an option to reset audio or sound settings to defaults. Try that before a full factory reset.
- **Take the soundbar or receiver out of the chain.** If sync is fine on the TV speakers with no soundbar connected, the audio device or its settings are the issue.
- **Connect the streaming device straight to the soundbar or receiver**, if it has an HDMI input, and run HDMI out to the TV. This can avoid ARC delays.
- **Factory reset** the TV or soundbar as a last step. You'll need to sign back in to apps and redo settings.

## When to call a pro

TV sync problems aren't dangerous, so most people solve them without help. Contact the maker's support, or a home theater installer, if:

- Sync is off on every source and every speaker, including the TV's own speakers, after updates and a factory reset. That can point to a hardware fault.
- A brand-new TV or soundbar has the problem out of the box and none of the settings help. It may be covered under warranty.
- You have a multi-room or in-wall setup with switches, extenders or long HDMI runs, and you can't tell which part is adding the delay.
