When a smart lock won't lock or unlock, the electronics are usually fine. Smart locks use a small motor to turn the same deadbolt you'd turn by hand, and that motor has much less strength than your wrist. Anything that makes the bolt stiff, or anything that starves the motor of power, stops it.

The most common causes are **weak batteries**, **a door that's out of alignment with the strike plate**, **a lock that needs recalibrating**, and **a lost Bluetooth or Wi-Fi connection**. These fixes apply to most brands, including August, Yale, Schlage and Kwikset smart locks.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Lock it by hand with the door closed** using the inside thumbturn. If it's stiff or you have to push or pull the door to make it turn, the motor can't do it either (fix 2).
2. **Open the door and lock it from the app or keypad.** If it works smoothly when open, the lock is fine and the door is the problem (fix 2).
3. **If it struggles even with the door open**, replace the batteries (fix 1), then recalibrate (fix 3), then check the installation (fix 7).
4. **If it works at the door but not remotely**, it's a connection or settings problem (fixes 4 and 5).

## 1. Replace the batteries

Smart lock motors need a burst of current to move the bolt. As batteries age, the voltage drops under that load, so the lock may still light up and beep but move slowly, stop halfway or report a jam. The low battery warning doesn't always come early enough.

**Fix:**

- Replace **all** the batteries at once with fresh ones of the type the manual specifies. Don't mix old and new, or different brands.
- Many locks are designed for 1.5 V alkaline cells. Rechargeable NiMH batteries run at a lower voltage and can cause weak operation or wrong battery readings. Use them only if your manual allows it.
- Check the battery contacts for corrosion and make sure each battery sits firmly.
- If your lock has a separate keypad, it has its own batteries. Change those too.

## 2. Check door alignment and the strike plate

This is the most common cause of a smart lock that jams or stops short. Doors sag on their hinges and swell or shrink with humidity and temperature, so a bolt that lined up perfectly in spring can rub on the strike plate in summer. A strike hole that isn't deep enough also stops the bolt before it fully extends.

**Fix:**

1. **Find where it rubs.** Rub pencil lead or lipstick on the end of the bolt, close the door and turn the thumbturn by hand. The mark on the strike plate or frame shows where it hits.
2. **Tighten the hinges.** Tighten all hinge screws. If the door has sagged, replacing one screw in the top hinge with a longer screw that reaches the wall framing can pull the door back up.
3. **Adjust the strike plate.** Loosen its screws and shift it slightly toward the mark, or file the edge of the opening a little where the bolt hits.
4. **Check the hole depth.** The bolt should extend fully into the frame without hitting the bottom. If it's too shallow, the hole behind the strike plate needs drilling or chiseling deeper.
5. **Check the weatherstripping.** Thick new weatherstripping can push the door away from the frame. You shouldn't need to push or pull the door for the bolt to turn.

After any adjustment, recalibrate the lock (fix 3).

## 3. Recalibrate the lock

Many smart locks learn how far the bolt travels and which way the door is handed (whether it locks by turning left or right). If that setup is wrong, or if the door or lock was adjusted after setup, the lock may stop short, report a jam, or show the wrong locked or unlocked state.

**Fix:**

- Look in the lock's settings in its app for a calibration, door handing or lock setup option, and follow the steps.
- Some locks calibrate automatically after a battery change or a reset. Check your manual.
- If the app shows "unlocked" when the bolt is extended (or the reverse), the handing or calibration is wrong.

## 4. Fix the Bluetooth or Wi-Fi connection

Many smart locks talk to your phone over **Bluetooth**, which only reaches a short distance. Remote control from away needs a **Wi-Fi bridge or hub** (a small plug-in device near the door), or a lock with built-in Wi-Fi. If the lock is "offline" in the app, it's usually this link that's broken, not the lock.

**Fix:**

- Stand close to the door with Bluetooth turned on and the lock's app allowed to use it. If it works up close, range is the issue.
- Plug the bridge or hub into an outlet closer to the lock, ideally in the same room or the next room, and check that its light shows it's online.
- Check that the bridge or Wi-Fi lock is on a network it supports. Many use 2.4 GHz only. If you changed your router or password, reconnect it in the app.
- A metal door or thick walls between the bridge and the lock cut the range. Try a different outlet.
- If the bridge keeps dropping, see our guide to [mesh Wi-Fi placement](/networking/mesh-wifi-placement/) to strengthen the signal near the door.

## 5. Adjust auto-lock and door sensor settings

Auto-lock relocks the door after a set time, and some locks use a **door position sensor** to know when the door is closed. If auto-lock fires while the door is still open, the bolt sticks out and the door can't close. If the sensor is out of place, the lock may think the door is open and refuse to lock.

**Fix:**

- In the app, check the auto-lock timer. Lengthen it, or turn it off while you test.
- If your lock uses a door sensor (often a small magnet on the frame), make sure the magnet is still in place and lined up with the lock, and recalibrate the sensor in the app.
- Check for "auto-unlock" or geofence features that unlock as you arrive. Turn them off if the lock unlocks when you don't expect it.
- Review the activity log in the app. It shows who or what locked or unlocked the door, which helps you spot a setting or a shared user.

## 6. Fix keypad problems

A keypad that doesn't light up, or rejects a code that used to work, usually has a simple cause.

**Fix:**

- **No lights:** replace the batteries. On locks with a separate keypad, change the keypad batteries.
- **Code rejected:** check the code in the app and whether it has a schedule or expiry. Guest codes often do.
- **Locked out after wrong codes:** many keypads disable themselves for a short time after several wrong entries. Wait and try again.
- **Sticky or unresponsive buttons:** wipe the keypad with a damp cloth and let it dry. Moisture or ice on an outdoor keypad can affect it, so check your lock's weather rating.

## 7. Check the installation

If the lock struggles even with the door open and fresh batteries, the parts inside the door may be binding.

**Fix:**

- Check that the mounting screws aren't overtightened. Too much pressure can squeeze the bolt mechanism and make it stiff.
- Check that the tailpiece (the flat bar that runs through the door) is seated in the right adapter or slot, as shown in your manual.
- With the smart lock removed from the inside, the bolt should turn freely by hand. If it doesn't, the deadbolt itself or the bore in the door is the problem.

## 8. Update the firmware and app

Lock makers fix bugs, battery reporting and connection problems through updates. Updates often install through the app or the bridge.

**Fix:**

- Update the lock's app on your phone.
- In the app, check for a firmware update for the lock and bridge. Keep your phone close to the lock during the update and don't interrupt it.
- Put in fresh batteries before a firmware update so the lock doesn't lose power partway through.

## Locked out with a dead lock?

- **Use the physical key** if your lock has a keyway. Keep a key somewhere you can reach it, not inside the house.
- **Power the keypad briefly.** Some keypad locks have contacts for a 9 V battery on the outside so you can enter a code. Check your manual to see whether yours does.
- **Try another entrance** and replace the batteries from the inside.
- If you can't get in any other way, call a locksmith.

## When to call a locksmith

Call a locksmith or a door installer if:

- The door has sagged or warped badly enough that hinge and strike adjustments don't fix it.
- The deadbolt is stiff even with the smart lock removed.
- The strike hole needs deepening or moving and you don't have the tools.
- You're locked out with no key and no working keypad.

For electronic faults, such as a lock that won't power on with fresh batteries or a motor that runs but doesn't move the bolt, contact the lock maker. Many smart locks have a warranty that covers the motor and electronics.
