An iPhone gets warm when it works hard: charging, gaming, streaming, filming or navigating. That is normal. It is **overheating** when it gets too hot to hold comfortably, gets hot while sitting idle, or shows a warning that it needs to cool down.

The usual causes are **charging while you use it**, **heat from the sun or a hot car**, **a thick case**, **an iOS update still finishing in the background** and **an app running when it shouldn't**. Weak cellular signal and an aging battery can add to it.

> **Safety first:** If the iPhone is bulging, the screen is lifting, or you smell something sweet or chemical, stop using it, don't charge it and don't press on it. A swollen lithium-ion battery can catch fire if it is punctured. Keep it away from anything flammable and contact Apple or an authorized service provider.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Note when it gets hot:** while charging, while using a certain app, in the car, or while sitting idle.
2. **Unplug it and take it out of its case.** If it cools noticeably, charging or the case was part of the problem.
3. **Open Settings > Battery** and look at the list of apps by battery use. One app at the top with a large share of use you don't recognize is a strong clue.
4. **Think about recent changes:** an iOS update, a new phone set up from a backup, a new charger or a new case.
5. **Look at the phone from the side.** A gap between the screen and the frame, or a back that rocks on a table, means the battery may be swelling.

## 1. Charging while you use it

Charging makes heat, and so does a busy processor. Gaming, streaming or video calling while charging puts both together. Fast charging and wireless charging also run warmer than slow charging. When the battery gets warm, iOS may pause charging above 80% and show **Charging On Hold** until it cools.

**Fix:**

- Avoid heavy use while charging. Let it charge, then play.
- Charge on a **hard, flat surface**, not on a bed, pillow or couch.
- Take off thick or leather cases while charging, especially on a wireless pad.
- Let a held charge resume on its own. Our guide to an [iPhone not charging](/phones-tablets/iphone-not-charging/) covers charging pauses in more detail.

## 2. Sun, hot cars and pockets

Direct sun heats a phone fast, and a closed car can get far hotter than the air outside. A phone mounted on a dashboard in the sun while running navigation and charging is one of the most common ways to trigger a temperature warning. Apple says iPhone works best in ambient temperatures between **32°F and 95°F**.

**Fix:**

- Keep the phone **out of direct sun**, especially in the car. Use a vent mount (with the AC on) or a shaded spot instead of the dashboard.
- Never leave it in a parked car on a warm day.
- Don't keep it in a tight pocket against your body during a workout in the heat.

## 3. A thick case trapping heat

Rugged, battery and leather cases slow how fast heat leaves the phone. That matters most when the phone is already working hard.

**Fix:**

- Remove the case when the phone feels hot, and while charging or gaming for long periods.
- A **battery case** adds its own heat while it charges the phone. Take it off if overheating is a regular problem.

## 4. A new update or setup still finishing

After a major iOS update, or when you set up a new iPhone or restore it from a backup, the phone does a lot of background work: downloading apps and photos, indexing for search and analyzing your photo library. It can stay warm and use more battery for a day or two.

**Fix:**

- Keep it on **Wi-Fi** and **plugged in overnight** so it can finish.
- Give it a couple of days before deciding something is wrong.
- Make sure iOS and your apps are up to date: **Settings > General > Software Update**, and the App Store's account page for app updates. Later updates often fix heat or battery bugs.

## 5. An app busy in the background

A buggy app, a stuck upload, a navigation or fitness app that keeps using location, or a video still exporting can keep the processor busy while the screen is off.

**Fix:**

1. Open **Settings > Battery** and check the apps at the top of the list. Tap the list for activity and screen time details where your iOS version shows them.
2. **Close** the app (swipe up from the bottom, pause, then swipe the app's card up) and see if the phone cools.
3. **Update or reinstall** the app. If one app keeps doing this, contact its developer.
4. Turn off **Background App Refresh** for apps that don't need it in **Settings > General > Background App Refresh**.
5. **Restart** the iPhone to clear stuck processes.

## 6. Weak cellular signal

When signal is weak, the cellular radio works harder to stay connected, which uses power and makes heat. You'll notice it on long drives, in basements and in buildings with poor reception.

**Fix:**

- Use **Wi-Fi** where you can, and turn on **Wi-Fi Calling** in the Phone settings if your carrier supports it.
- In an area with no usable signal, turning on **Airplane Mode** for a while stops the radio from searching.

## 7. The charger, cable or wireless pad

Damaged cables and cheap uncertified chargers can charge poorly and run hot. Wireless pads get warm, and a phone that sits slightly off-center on a pad wastes more energy as heat.

**Fix:**

- Use the Apple charger and cable or a **certified** one (MFi for Lightning cables, and a reputable USB-C charger). Look for the **Qi or Qi2** mark on wireless chargers, or use MagSafe on models that have it.
- Stop using any cable with fraying, bent plugs or burn marks.
- **Center the phone** on a wireless pad and remove anything between the phone and the pad.

## 8. An aging battery

All lithium-ion batteries wear out. An old battery can run warmer and drain faster under the same load. Heat is one of the main things that speeds up that wear, so a phone that overheats often ages faster too.

**Fix:**

- Open **Settings > Battery > Battery Health** (on some models and iOS versions it is called **Battery Health & Charging**) and look for a **service** message under Maximum Capacity.
- If service is recommended, a battery replacement from Apple or an authorized service provider usually helps.
- Leave **Optimized Battery Charging** or a charge limit on if your model has one. It reduces the time the battery spends full and warm.

## "iPhone needs to cool down" warning

If the iPhone gets too hot, it can show a temperature screen saying it needs to cool down before you can use it. Before that, you may notice it charging slowly or not at all, the screen dimming, the camera flash turning off, or apps running slower. These are protections, not faults.

- **Turn it off** or leave it alone, unplugged, in a shaded and cooler place.
- Don't put it in a fridge, freezer or water, or in front of a fan blowing cold air from an air conditioner. Fast cooling can cause condensation inside.
- Emergency calls may still be available from the warning screen.
- It should return to normal once it cools. If the warning keeps coming back in normal room temperatures, contact Apple Support.

## Swollen battery? Stop using the iPhone

A swollen battery pushes the screen away from the frame or bulges the back. It is a fire risk.

- **Stop using and charging it** right away.
- Don't press, bend or try to open it.
- Keep it on a non-flammable surface away from heat, and take it to Apple or an authorized service provider.

## When to contact Apple Support

Contact Apple Support or visit an Apple Store or authorized service provider if:

- The phone gets **hot while idle** and nothing stands out in battery usage, even after a restart and update.
- The temperature warning appears **in normal room temperatures**.
- Battery Health shows a **service message**.
- The phone is **bulging**, the screen is lifting, or it smells unusual.

Most overheating is a hot environment plus a busy phone. Cool it gently, change the habit that heats it, and get the battery checked if the heat keeps coming back. Android owners can see our [Android phone overheating](/phones-tablets/android-phone-overheating/) guide.
