When a freezer stops freezing, ice cream turns soft, meat feels spongy and frost may appear in odd places. A freezer should hold **0°F**, which is the FDA's recommended temperature. Anything much warmer and food starts to thaw.

Most of the time the cause is simple: **a wrong setting, a door that isn't sealing, blocked vents or dirty condenser coils**. If those check out, look for **frost on the back wall (a defrost problem) or a fan that has stopped**. Here's how to find which.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the refrigerator before you remove any panel, touch the condenser coils or fans, or work in the machine compartment. Fan blades can start without warning and some parts carry 120 V. Never chip ice with a knife or ice pick: you can puncture a refrigerant line. Anything involving refrigerant is a job for a certified technician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/freezer-not-freezing-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: measure the freezer temperature, check the setting, door seal and vents, then listen at the back; if the compressor and condenser fan are silent or clicking check causes 7 and 8; if they run, look at the freezer back wall: heavy frost points to cause 5, no frost and no airflow to cause 6, airflow but still warm to causes 1 to 4 and 9" width="640" height="560" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Two listening checks and one look at the back wall narrow it down.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Measure it.** Put an appliance thermometer between frozen packages and read it after several hours or overnight. Above 0°F means there's a real problem.
2. **Check the basics.** Confirm the setting, close the door on a sheet of paper to test the seal, and make sure nothing blocks the vents (causes 1 to 3).
3. **Listen at the back.** With the fridge running, you should hear the compressor hum and, on most models, a small fan at the bottom back. Silence or a click every few minutes points to causes 7 and 8.
4. **Look at the freezer back wall.** Heavy frost or ice on the panel points to a defrost problem (cause 5).
5. **Press the door switch** (the button or lever the door pushes in) and listen inside the freezer. On many models the fan only runs with the door closed or the switch pressed. No fan sound points to cause 6.

## 1. Wrong temperature setting

Settings get bumped when you load groceries, and many dials use a 1 to 9 scale where a higher number is usually colder (check your manual). Some models also have a demo or showroom mode that runs the lights but not the cooling.

**Fix:**

- Set the freezer to **0°F** (or the coldest middle setting on a number dial) and wait 24 hours before judging.
- If your display shows Celsius, 0°F is about -18°C. A [Fahrenheit to Celsius converter](https://zentacalculator.com/conversion/fahrenheit-to-celsius/) helps if your manual lists only one.
- If the display works but nothing gets cold, check your manual for how to turn off demo mode.
- A new fridge, or one just restarted, can take up to 24 hours to reach temperature. A big load of room-temperature food also slows it down.

## 2. Door not closing or a worn gasket

Warm, humid kitchen air sneaks in through a gap and turns into frost on food, around the door and on the walls. The freezer can't keep up.

**Fix:**

1. Close the door on a dollar bill or a sheet of paper at several spots. If it slides out easily, the seal is weak there.
2. Clean the gasket with warm soapy water. Sticky or dirty gaskets don't seat well.
3. Check that nothing (an ice bin, a tray, a bag) holds the door open a fraction of an inch.
4. Make sure the fridge leans back slightly so the doors swing shut. Your manual explains how to adjust the front feet.
5. Replace a torn, flattened or cracked gasket with the part for your model number.

## 3. Overpacked freezer or blocked vents

Cold air enters the freezer through vents, usually on the back wall or ceiling, and returns to the coil through another opening. A bag of ice or a stack of boxes against them can stop the air from moving.

**Fix:**

- Pull items an inch or two away from the back wall and any vents.
- Don't pack the freezer so tightly that air can't circulate. A full freezer is good, a crammed one is not.
- If the vents themselves have ice in them, see cause 5.

## 4. Dirty condenser coils or no room to breathe

The condenser coils release the heat pulled out of the freezer. When they are coated in dust and pet hair, or the fridge is pushed tight into a cabinet, the system runs longer and cools less. Room temperature matters too: some models aren't designed for a very hot or very cold garage.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the refrigerator.
2. Find the coils: behind the front kick plate at the bottom, or on the back on older models.
3. Clean them with a coil brush and a vacuum. Once or twice a year is a good habit, more often with pets.
4. Leave the clearance your manual lists around the sides, top and back.
5. If it lives in a garage, check your manual for the rated room temperature range. On many single-control models, a cold garage makes the compressor run less, so the freezer warms up.

## 5. Frost buildup on the evaporator coil (defrost problem)

Frost-free freezers melt frost off the evaporator coil a few times a day with a small heater. If the defrost heater, defrost thermostat or control fails, frost keeps building until it blocks the airflow. The freezer warms up, often along with the fridge.

**Signs:** a thick white layer of frost or ice on the freezer back panel, ice on the freezer floor, or a fan that sounds like it's scraping.

**Fix:**

1. Move food into coolers with ice, or into another freezer.
2. Unplug the fridge, prop the doors open and lay towels on the floor.
3. Let all the ice melt, often 24 hours or more. A hair dryer on low, held well away from plastic parts, speeds it up. Never chip at ice.
4. Plug it back in. If it freezes again but frost returns in days or weeks, a defrost part has failed. A technician can test the heater, thermostat and control and replace the faulty one.
5. If the ice is mostly on the freezer floor, the defrost drain may be clogged. Clearing it with warm water after a defrost often fixes it.

## 6. Evaporator fan not running

The evaporator fan sits behind the freezer back panel and pushes cold air from the coil around the freezer and into the fridge. If it stops, the freezer only cools near the coil. A squealing or chirping fan is often close to failing.

**Fix:**

1. Press the door switch and listen inside the freezer for the fan.
2. No sound? Check that ice isn't jamming the blade (see cause 5).
3. If there's no ice and the fan still won't run, the fan motor or door switch is likely bad. Replacing the motor means unplugging the fridge and removing the back panel. If you're not comfortable, call a technician.

## 7. Condenser fan not running

Most fridges with coils underneath have a small condenser fan next to the compressor. If it stops, the compressor overheats and shuts off, so cooling is weak or comes and goes. Older fridges with coils on the back usually don't have one.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the fridge and remove the rear lower access panel.
2. Clear any dust or debris from the blade and spin it by hand. It should turn freely.
3. Plug it in and watch from a safe distance. If the compressor runs but this fan doesn't, the fan motor likely needs replacing.

## 8. Compressor or start relay problem

The compressor is the black dome in the machine compartment. If it hums, clicks off after a few seconds and tries again every few minutes, the start relay or the compressor itself may be failing. The freezer slowly warms.

**Fix:** Clean the coils and check the condenser fan first (causes 4 and 7), since an overheating compressor can act the same way. Beyond that, have a technician test the relay and compressor. A relay is often an inexpensive part. A failed compressor is a major repair.

## 9. Sealed system problem (refrigerant)

The refrigerant runs in a sealed loop of tubing. A leak or blockage means the coil never gets fully cold. One clue is frost on only part of the evaporator coil while the compressor runs nonstop.

**Fix:** This is not a DIY repair. Refrigerant work needs a certified appliance technician with the right equipment.

## Freezer and fridge both warm and silent?

- **Check power.** Plug a lamp into the same outlet. If it's dead, check the breaker and any GFCI outlet that may protect the circuit.
- **Check the controls.** Make sure the fridge isn't switched off from its control panel and the setting isn't on its warmest stop or "off".
- **Lights on, no cooling at all?** That points to the control board, a failed start relay, the compressor or demo mode (see causes 1 and 8).
- If the fridge side is fine and only the freezer is warm, start at causes 3, 5 and 6. If it's the other way around, see our guide to a [refrigerator not cooling while the freezer works](/appliances/refrigerator-not-cooling-freezer-works/).

## Keep your food safe while you fix it

Keep the door closed as much as you can. USDA guidance is that a full freezer holds its temperature for about **48 hours** and a half full one about **24 hours** with the door shut. Food that still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below can be refrozen, though quality may suffer. Throw out perishable food that has been above 40°F for more than 2 hours. See our [power outage checklist](/home-safety/power-outage-checklist/) for more food tips. When in doubt, throw it out.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a technician if:

- Frost keeps coming back after a full manual defrost.
- The compressor clicks on and off, hums without starting, or runs nonstop while the freezer stays warm.
- You see oily spots on tubing, hear hissing, or see frost on only part of the coil.
- You'd need to remove panels and replace a fan motor or defrost part and aren't comfortable doing it.

Have the model and serial number ready from the label inside the fridge, so the technician can bring the right parts.
