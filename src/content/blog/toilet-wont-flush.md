A toilet flush is simple: the handle lifts a flapper, a tank full of water rushes into the bowl, and that rush starts a siphon that pulls the bowl empty. When the toilet won't flush, or flushes weakly, one of those three steps isn't happening.

The most common causes are **a slack or broken chain, too little water in the tank, a flapper that closes too soon, and a partial clog**. Mineral buildup in the rim holes and drain or vent problems come next. Start in the tank, since most fixes there take minutes and cost little.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/toilet-wont-flush-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: lift the tank lid and press the handle; if the flapper doesn't lift, fix the chain or handle; if the tank is low, open the valve and raise the water level; if the tank side works, pour a bucket of water into the bowl; if the bowl still drains slowly it is a clog, if the bucket flush is strong check the flapper and rim holes" width="640" height="460" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The bucket test tells you whether the problem is in the tank or the drain.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Take the tank lid off** and set it somewhere flat and safe. It's heavy porcelain and cracks easily.
2. **Press the handle and watch.** Does the flapper lift? Does it stay up for a second or two before dropping?
3. **Check the water level** once the tank refills. It should reach the fill line marked inside the tank, or about 1 inch below the top of the overflow tube if there's no line.
4. **Do the bucket test.** Pour 1 to 2 gallons of water quickly into the bowl.
    - **Bowl empties with a strong whoosh?** The drain is fine. The problem is in the tank or the rim holes (causes 1, 2, 3, 5).
    - **Water rises and drains slowly?** Something is blocking the toilet or the drain (causes 4, 6, 7).

## 1. Not enough water in the tank

A tank that only fills partway can't push enough water into the bowl to start the siphon. The cause is usually a **shutoff valve that's partly closed** or a **fill valve set too low**.

**Fix:**

- Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet counterclockwise until it's fully open.
- Raise the fill level. On most modern fill valves you turn an adjustment screw or slide a clip on the float up the shaft. On older ball floats, gently bend the float arm up a little.
- If the tank fills very slowly, the fill valve may be clogged with debris. Shut the water off, remove the valve cap (check the maker's instructions) and flush it out, or replace the fill valve. They cost roughly $10 to $25.

## 2. The chain or handle is disconnected

If the handle feels loose or flops with no resistance, the chain has slipped off the lift arm, the clip has broken, or the handle nut inside the tank has worked loose.

**Fix:**

- Reattach the chain to the lift arm. Leave a little slack, about 1/2 inch, when the flapper is closed.
- If the chain is too long, it can tangle under the flapper or not lift it fully. Move the clip to a closer link and cut off extra chain.
- Tighten the handle nut inside the tank. On most toilets it turns the opposite way to a normal nut (clockwise to loosen), so check gently.
- Replace a cracked handle or lift arm. A universal handle costs a few dollars.

## 3. The flapper closes too soon

For a full flush, the flapper should float up and stay open until most of the tank has emptied. If it drops shut after a second, you get a short, weak flush. Too much slack in the chain, a waterlogged flapper or the wrong flapper for your toilet can all cause this.

**Fix:**

- Hold the handle down for a couple of seconds while you flush. If the flush is much stronger, the flapper is closing too early.
- Take up a little chain slack so the flapper lifts higher.
- Many replacement flappers have an adjustable dial or float that sets how long they stay open. Turn it toward a longer, fuller flush.
- Replace a warped or worn flapper with one that matches your flush valve size (most are 2 or 3 inches across). Take the old one to the store if you're unsure.

## 4. A partial clog in the toilet

If the water in the bowl rises high and then drains slowly, something is stuck in the toilet trap: too much paper, wipes, a toy or a cosmetic item. Even "flushable" wipes can catch in the trap.

**Fix:**

1. Stop flushing so the bowl doesn't overflow. If the water keeps rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper closed, then turn off the shutoff valve.
2. Use a **flange plunger** (the kind with a soft rubber sleeve under the cup). Seat it firmly over the hole, keep it covered with water, and push and pull steadily 10 to 15 times.
3. If plunging doesn't clear it, use a **toilet auger**. Feed the cable into the bowl, crank it through the trap, then pull it back out slowly. A toilet auger has a rubber sleeve that protects the porcelain.
4. If you know a hard object went down, use the auger to try to snag it rather than pushing it deeper.

## 5. Clogged rim holes and siphon jet

Small holes under the rim and a larger siphon jet at the bottom of the bowl deliver water for the swirl and the siphon. In hard water areas they slowly fill with mineral scale, so the flush gets weaker over months or years.

**Fix:**

- Use a small mirror to look under the rim. White or brown crust around the holes means they're blocked.
- Turn off the water and flush to empty the tank. Pour about a quart of white vinegar slowly into the overflow tube so it runs out through the rim holes, and leave it for several hours or overnight.
- Gently clear each hole with a stiff wire or small brush, then flush a few times.
- Never mix vinegar or other cleaners with bleach or bleach-based toilet cleaners.

## 6. A blocked drain line

If the toilet clears for a moment and then backs up again, or other fixtures are slow too, the blockage may be past the toilet in the drain line. Signs include **gurgling in the tub or sink when you flush** and water backing up into a tub or floor drain.

**Fix:**

- Check whether only one bathroom is affected or the whole house. One bathroom points to a branch line. The whole house points to the main sewer line.
- Stop running water until it's cleared, to avoid sewage backing up into the house.
- A main line blockage needs a plumber with a drain machine or camera. Tree roots and collapsed pipes are common in older homes.

## 7. A blocked plumbing vent

Your drains need air from a vent pipe that runs through the roof. If leaves, a bird nest or ice block it, the toilet may flush weakly, gurgle or leave the bowl water level swinging up and down.

**Fix:**

- Look for gurgling in nearby drains and a slow, weak flush that the tank fixes didn't solve.
- Clearing a roof vent means working on a ladder and roof. Unless you're comfortable and safe on a roof, leave this to a plumber or roofer.

## 8. An older low-flow toilet

Some early low-flow toilets simply flush weakly by design, even when everything is working. If the tank is full, the flapper works, the rim holes are clear and the drain is open, the toilet itself may be the limit.

**Fix:** A modern high-efficiency toilet with a well-reviewed flush design may solve it. Check the label inside the tank or behind the seat for the gallons per flush and model, and compare before you buy.

## Toilet won't flush at all?

- **No water in the tank:** the shutoff valve is closed, the house water is off, or the fill valve has failed. Check the valve and test another faucet.
- **Handle does nothing:** the chain or lift arm has come off (cause 2).
- **Bowl is full and won't go down:** don't flush again. Plunge or auger it (cause 4).
- **You need to flush now:** pour a bucket of water quickly into the bowl.

## When to call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber if:

- Plunging and a toilet auger don't clear the clog, or a hard object is stuck.
- Several fixtures are slow or gurgling, or sewage comes up in a tub, shower or floor drain.
- Water leaks from the base of the toilet when you flush. The wax ring seal may have failed.
- The tank or bowl is cracked.

If water is leaking from the tank or a valve instead, see our guide on a [toilet that keeps running](/plumbing/toilet-keeps-running/), which covers the flapper and fill valve in more detail.
