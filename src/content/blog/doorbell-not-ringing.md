A wired doorbell is a simple circuit: a **transformer** lowers 120 V house power to a safe low voltage, the **button** completes the circuit when you press it, and the **chime** makes the sound. When the bell goes silent, one of those three parts, or a wire between them, has failed.

The usual causes are **a worn-out button**, **a loose wire**, **a sticky chime plunger**, and **a dead transformer**. You can test each one in a few minutes with basic tools, and most fixes cost little.

> **Safety first:** The button and chime wires are low voltage, but the transformer is wired to 120 V. Turn off the breaker for the transformer and confirm it's dead with a non-contact voltage tester before you touch its line side or replace it. If the transformer is inside the main panel, leave it to an electrician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Press each button** (front and back) and listen at the chime. Note any hum, buzz or click.
2. **Jump the button.** Unscrew the front button and touch its two wires together for a second. If the chime rings, the button is the problem.
3. **Open the chime cover** (it usually lifts or slides off). Check for loose wires and plungers that stick.
4. **Test at the chime.** With a multimeter on AC volts, touch the TRANS terminal and the FRONT terminal while someone holds the button. A reading means power is getting to the chime.
5. **Test the transformer.** If you got no reading, measure across the transformer's low-voltage screws.

## 1. A worn-out or dirty button

Doorbell buttons live outdoors, so rain, heat and corrosion wear out their contacts. They are a very common failure, and the easiest to fix.

**Fix:**

1. Pry or unscrew the button from the wall. Many pop off with a flat screwdriver at the edge.
2. Look at the two wires behind it. If they're corroded or loose, sand them lightly and tighten them under the screws.
3. Touch the two wires together for a second. If the chime rings, the button is bad.
4. Connect the two wires to a new button (either wire on either screw). Wrap the wires around the back of the button or tape them so they don't slip into the wall.

If you have a lighted button, its tiny bulb or LED can fail too, and some lighted buttons don't work with certain chimes or video doorbells. Check the packaging before you buy.

## 2. Loose or broken wires at the button

If the front door works but the back door doesn't (or the reverse), the problem is in that button or its wiring. Doorbell wire is thin and easy to break, especially where it bends behind the button or inside the chime.

**Fix:**

- Check both ends of the wire for that door: the button and the FRONT or REAR terminal on the chime.
- Strip about 1/2 inch of fresh wire if the end is broken or green with corrosion, and reattach it.
- If both ends are good but jumping the wires at the button does nothing, the wire may be broken inside the wall. You can run new doorbell wire, use a wireless button, or have a pro trace it.

## 3. A sticky or dirty chime

A mechanical chime has small plungers that a magnet coil pushes against metal bars. Dust, grease and cooking residue can make the plungers stick, so you hear a hum or click instead of a ding.

A sticky one hums instead of ringing.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

- Remove the chime cover. Gently push each plunger with your finger. It should slide and spring back freely.
- Wipe the plungers and the tubes with a dry cloth or cotton swab. If they're gummy, a little rubbing alcohol on a swab helps. Don't oil them, which attracts dust.
- Make sure the chime is mounted level and the tone bars aren't bent or touching anything.
- Tighten the three terminal screws: FRONT, TRANS and REAR.

## 4. Loose wires at the chime

Chime terminals can work loose over the years, and a single loose wire there stops every button.

**Fix:**

- With the chime cover off, gently tug each wire. Retighten any that move.
- Check that the transformer wire is on TRANS, the front button wire on FRONT, and the back button wire on REAR.
- Test with a multimeter on AC volts from TRANS to FRONT while someone holds the front button. If you see voltage but hear nothing, replace the chime. If you see zero, the problem is the transformer or wiring before the chime.

## 5. A dead or tripped transformer

The transformer lowers 120 V to the low voltage the doorbell needs. Most are rated somewhere between about 10 and 24 V AC (check the label). They can burn out with age, after a power surge, or if the button wires short together. Some have a small reset button or a fuse, and the breaker that feeds them can trip.

**Fix:**

1. Find the transformer. It's usually a small box on or near the main panel, a junction box, the furnace, or in an attic, closet, basement or garage.
2. Check that its breaker is on.
3. Set a multimeter to AC volts and touch the two low-voltage screw terminals. Compare the reading with the voltage on its label.
4. If you read zero with the breaker on, the transformer is dead.
5. To replace it, turn off the breaker, confirm the power is off with a non-contact tester, and install a new transformer with the same voltage and an equal or higher VA rating (or the rating your video doorbell asks for). If the 120 V side is in a box you're not comfortable working in, call an electrician.

A transformer that's hot, buzzing loudly, or smells burnt should be replaced. Find out why first: a pinched wire or stuck button can short the low-voltage side.

## 6. A video doorbell changed the setup

When you replace a plain button with a video doorbell, it draws power through the same wires, and the chime may stop ringing or hum constantly.

**Fix:**

- In the doorbell's app, look for a chime setting and choose the type you have: **mechanical** (the box on the wall with bars) or **digital** (plays a tune through a speaker), or **none**.
- Many brands include a small power kit or chime kit that must be installed inside the chime box. Check that it's on the right terminals, following the brand's instructions.
- Check that your transformer meets the doorbell's voltage and VA requirement. An undersized transformer causes a silent chime, constant reboots, or a doorbell that keeps going offline. See [video doorbell keeps going offline](/smart-home/video-doorbell-offline/).
- Some digital chimes aren't compatible with video doorbells. The doorbell maker's support pages usually list them.

## 7. Wireless doorbell: batteries, range or pairing

If your doorbell has no wires, the button sends a radio signal to a plug-in or battery receiver.

**Fix:**

- Replace the battery in the button and, if it has one, in the receiver.
- Move the receiver closer to the door, or away from large metal objects, appliances and thick masonry walls.
- Re-pair the button and receiver following the manual. A power outage or new batteries can sometimes clear the pairing.
- Check that the receiver's volume isn't turned down or muted.

## Still silent after all that?

If the button, chime and transformer all test good but the bell still doesn't ring, the problem is a break in a wire inside the wall. Your options:

- **Run new wire** on the surface or through the wall if you can reach both ends.
- **Switch to a wireless doorbell kit.** The receiver plugs into an outlet, so the old wiring no longer matters.
- **Have an electrician or handyman trace the wire** if you want to keep the wired chime or install a wired video doorbell.

## When to call an electrician

Call a licensed electrician if:

- The transformer is inside the main panel or in a box you can't safely open.
- The transformer keeps burning out, which suggests a short somewhere.
- You smell burning or see scorch marks at the transformer or chime.
- You need a new circuit or a larger transformer for a video doorbell.

Most doorbell fixes are cheap and quick: a new button or a cleaned chime solves the problem more often than anything else.
