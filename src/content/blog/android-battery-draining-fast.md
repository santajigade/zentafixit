When an Android battery drains fast, something is using more power than it should: the screen, an app working in the background, the radio hunting for signal, or a battery that has simply aged. Most of these are quick to find, because Android tracks which apps and parts of the phone use the most power.

The most common causes are **a bright screen, one misbehaving app, weak cellular signal and a worn battery**. A recent system update can also cause a day or two of heavy drain. Start with the battery usage screen, then work through the fixes below.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Charge the phone, then restart it.** A restart clears stuck processes, which is sometimes the whole fix.
2. **Use it normally for a few hours**, then open **Settings, Battery** and find the battery usage list (on some phones it's under a "Battery usage" or "Usage since last full charge" option).
3. **Read the top of the list.**
    - **One app is far ahead of the rest?** Go to cause 2.
    - **Screen is the biggest user?** Go to cause 1.
    - **Drain is high while the phone sat unused?** Look at signal, sync and location (causes 3, 4, 5).
4. **Try safe mode.** Safe mode turns off apps you installed. On many phones you press and hold the power button, then press and hold "Power off" until a safe mode prompt appears. Check your maker's support page if that doesn't work. If the drain stops in safe mode, an app is to blame. If it doesn't, look at the battery (cause 8).

## 1. Screen brightness and display settings

The screen is often the single biggest power user. High brightness, a long screen timeout, a high refresh rate and an always-on display all add up.

**Fix:**

- Turn on **adaptive brightness** (or auto brightness) and drag the slider lower.
- Set the **screen timeout** to 30 seconds or 1 minute.
- If your phone has a high refresh rate option (often 90 or 120 Hz), try the standard or adaptive setting.
- Turn off the **always-on display**, or set it to show only when you tap the screen.
- Use **dark mode** if your phone has an OLED screen.

## 2. An app running in the background

One app stuck in a loop, constantly syncing or using location can drain a battery faster than everything else combined. Social media, messaging, navigation, streaming and some games are common culprits.

**Fix:**

1. In the battery usage list, tap the app that's using the most.
2. Choose a more restrictive battery option. Names vary, such as "Restricted", "Optimized" or putting the app in "Sleeping apps".
3. Check the Play Store for an update to that app. Developers often fix battery bugs.
4. If it keeps draining, clear its cache (Settings, Apps, the app, Storage), or uninstall and reinstall it.
5. Remove apps you don't use. Some run background services even when you never open them.

## 3. Weak cellular signal

When signal is weak, the phone boosts its radio power and keeps searching for a better tower. In a basement, a rural area or a building with poor coverage, this can drain the battery even while the phone sits in your pocket.

**Fix:**

- If you're on Wi-Fi with weak cell signal, turn on **Wi-Fi calling** in your phone or carrier settings if your carrier supports it.
- In places with no coverage at all, turn on **airplane mode** and switch Wi-Fi back on if you need it.
- If 5G is patchy where you live, try a setting that prefers LTE or uses 5G only when needed. Not all phones and carriers offer this.

## 4. Location, Bluetooth and scanning

Apps that use precise location all the time keep the GPS and radios busy. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning, which help location accuracy, also run in the background on many phones.

**Fix:**

- Go to **Settings, Location, App location permissions** (or similar) and change apps that don't need it to "Allow only while using the app" or "Don't allow".
- Turn off Wi-Fi scanning and Bluetooth scanning in the location settings if you don't need them.
- Turn Bluetooth off when you aren't using a watch, earbuds or car connection.

## 5. Sync, notifications and widgets

Email, cloud photo backup, contacts and calendar accounts all check for updates. Several accounts syncing often, plus live widgets, add steady background drain.

**Fix:**

- In **Settings, Accounts** (or Passwords and accounts), turn off sync for accounts or items you don't need.
- Set photo backup to run only on Wi-Fi or only while charging, if your backup app offers it.
- Remove widgets you don't look at, especially ones that refresh live data.
- Turn off notifications for apps that don't need them.

## 6. A recent update still settling

After a major Android or security update, the phone often reorganizes apps and rebuilds its search index in the background. Battery life can be worse for a day or two, then return to normal.

**Fix:**

- Keep the phone charged and give it a couple of days.
- Update all apps in the Play Store, since older app versions sometimes clash with a new system update.
- If drain stays high after a few days, restart the phone and check the battery usage list again.

## 7. Heat and charging habits

Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity faster when they get hot, and a hot phone also uses more power. Gaming while charging, leaving the phone in a car or the sun, and thick cases all raise the temperature.

**Fix:**

- Avoid using heavy apps while the phone charges.
- Use the original charger or a certified one that matches your phone.
- Turn on your phone's battery protection or adaptive charging option if it has one.
- See our guide on an [Android phone overheating](/phones-tablets/android-phone-overheating/) if it runs hot often.

## 8. A worn-out battery

Every phone battery slowly holds less charge as it goes through charge cycles. An older phone that used to last all day may now fade by mid-afternoon even with sensible settings. Signs include the percentage dropping quickly, or the phone shutting off with charge still showing.

**Fix:**

- Check battery health if your phone shows it in Settings, or use your maker's diagnostics app if it has one.
- If the battery tests poor, get it replaced by the maker or an authorized repair shop. Most modern phones have sealed batteries that are not meant to be swapped at home.
- Compare the repair cost with the phone's age. On an older phone, it may still be worth it if the rest of the phone works well.

## 9. Too many power-hungry features at once

Hotspot, screen mirroring, high-resolution video recording and live navigation all use a lot of power. Running a few of them together drains a battery fast even when everything is healthy.

**Fix:** Turn on **battery saver** or power saving mode when you need the battery to last, and switch off hotspot and other features as soon as you're done with them.

## Phone hot, swollen or draining in minutes?

- **Back is bulging, the screen is lifting, or the case no longer fits flat:** stop using and charging the phone. A swollen battery can be a fire risk. Don't press on it or try to open it. Take it to the maker or a repair shop.
- **Very hot with light use:** turn it off, let it cool away from soft surfaces, then check for a rogue app in safe mode.
- **Drops from full to empty in minutes:** back up your data and get the battery tested.

## When to call a pro

Contact the phone maker, your carrier or an authorized repair shop if:

- The battery is swollen, smells odd or the phone gets very hot.
- Drain stays high in safe mode after you've tried the settings above.
- The phone shuts off at 20 or 30 percent, or won't hold a charge at all.
- The phone is under warranty. Opening it yourself can void that warranty.
