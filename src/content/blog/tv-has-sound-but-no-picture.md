A TV that plays sound with a black screen is clearly on, so the good news is that the power supply and the speakers are working. Something between the picture source and the screen has stopped. Often it's **a screen off or power saving setting**, **the wrong input or a sleeping device**, or **a loose or faulty HDMI cable**.

If those check out, the problem may be **a frozen software state** you can clear with a power cycle or update, or a hardware fault: **a failed backlight**, the **T-con board** or **main board**, or the panel itself. One button press and one flashlight tell you which side you're on.

> **Safety first:** Never take the back off a TV. The power supply inside uses capacitors that can hold a dangerous high voltage charge even after the TV is unplugged. If you smell burning, see smoke or heard a pop before the picture went, unplug the TV and leave it unplugged.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Unplug the TV from the wall for 60 seconds**, plug it back in and turn it on.
2. **Press Home, Menu or Settings on the remote.** These screens come from the TV itself, not from your cable box or streaming stick.
    - **Menu appears?** The screen works. The problem is the input, cable or source device (causes 2 and 3).
    - **Still black?** Carry on to step 3.
3. **Check for a screen off setting** (cause 1). Pressing almost any button usually brings the picture back if this is it.
4. **Do the flashlight test** (cause 7). A faint image in the beam means the backlight is out. Nothing at all points to a setting, software or a board (causes 4 to 6 and 8).

## 1. Turn off screen off or picture off mode

Many smart TVs have a **screen off**, **picture off** or **audio only** option that blanks the screen on purpose so you can listen to music or a game with less power. It's easy to switch on by accident from a quick settings menu or an eco button.

**Fix:**

- Press a button such as volume, Home or the power button briefly. On many TVs that wakes the screen.
- Look for the option under the power, eco, energy saving or picture settings and turn it off. The name and location vary, so check your manual.

## 2. Check the input, source device and app

If the TV's own menus show up fine, the screen is working. The black picture is coming from whatever is feeding it, while the sound may still play through the TV or a soundbar.

**Fix:**

- **Press Input or Source** and step through each input. You may be on an input where a device is on but showing nothing.
- **Restart the device.** Cable boxes, consoles and streaming sticks can freeze with audio still playing. Unplug it for 30 seconds.
- **Black only in one app?** The app is the problem, not the TV. Close it, restart the TV, then update or reinstall the app. Try another app to confirm.
- **Black only on protected content,** such as paid streaming or a cable box, can be a copy protection (HDCP) handshake failure. The fixes are in our guide to [a TV that shows No Signal on HDMI](/tv-audio/tv-no-signal-hdmi/).

## 3. Reseat or swap the HDMI cable and port

A cable that's loose, bent or too slow for 4K HDR can carry sound while the video fails, especially one pulled tight behind a wall mount.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the HDMI cable at both ends and push it back in firmly.
2. Try a **different HDMI port** on the TV, then change the input to match.
3. Swap in a **known-good cable**. For 4K at 60Hz with HDR, use one labeled Premium High Speed or Ultra High Speed.
4. Connect the device **straight to the TV**, skipping any soundbar, receiver or splitter, to see if one of them is the weak link.

## 4. Power cycle the TV properly

Smart TVs never fully switch off with the remote. Their software can freeze in a state where audio works and video doesn't. A true power cycle clears it.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the TV from the wall**, not just from a power strip.
2. **Wait about 60 seconds.** Some makers suggest holding the TV's own power button for several seconds while it's unplugged.
3. Plug it back in, wait a few seconds, then turn it on with the button on the TV.

If the TV won't come back on at all after this, see [what to do when a TV won't turn on](/tv-audio/tv-wont-turn-on/).

## 5. Reset picture settings and energy saving

A very dark picture can look like no picture at all in a bright room. Power saving modes, a backlight setting turned right down or a light sensor can all cause it.

**Fix:**

- Change the **picture mode** to a standard preset.
- Turn **energy saving** or **eco** mode off, and turn off any automatic brightness or ambient light option.
- Raise the **backlight** or **brightness** setting. On LCD TVs, the backlight setting controls how bright the screen glows.
- Many TVs also offer a "reset picture settings" option.

## 6. Update or reset the TV software

Software bugs can leave the screen blank after an update or a power cut.

**Fix:**

- If you can see the menu, even faintly, run a **software update** from the support or system settings.
- Can't see anything? Many makers offer the update as a USB download on their support site. Follow their instructions for your model exactly.
- As a last resort, do a **factory reset**. It erases your Wi-Fi, app sign-ins and settings. Some TVs allow a reset with a button combination when the screen is dark, but this varies, so check your manual rather than guessing.

## 7. Do the flashlight test for a failed backlight

LCD and LED TVs light their panel from behind with rows of LEDs. When the backlight or the circuit driving it fails, the TV keeps working, sound and all, but the screen looks black.

**Fix:**

1. **Darken the room**, turn the TV on and press Home or Menu.
2. **Shine a flashlight close to the screen at an angle**, a few inches away.
3. **Look for a faint picture or menu** in the beam.
    - **Faint image:** the backlight, or the power board that feeds it, has failed. That's a repair shop job.
    - **Nothing at all:** the backlight may be fine and the problem is the picture path (cause 8), or a setting you missed.

A backlight that flickered, dimmed or switched off a few seconds after turning on is often a warning that it's failing.

## 8. Suspect the T-con or main board

The **main board** processes the signal and splits off sound and picture. The **timing control (T-con) board** takes the picture and drives the panel. If either fails, sound can keep playing while the screen stays black, flickers, shows vertical lines, shows only half a picture or looks washed out.

**Fix:**

- Note the exact symptoms and any blink pattern on the standby light. The meaning of blink codes varies by brand, so look them up in your manual or on the maker's support site.
- Don't open the TV to reseat cables or swap boards. Have a repair shop diagnose it.

A cracked or damaged **panel** can also show sound with a black or partly black picture. Look for impact marks or spreading black patches. A failed panel is rarely worth replacing.

## OLED TV has sound but no picture?

OLED TVs have no backlight. Each pixel makes its own light, so the flashlight test won't show anything useful and a black screen with sound points to settings, software, the panel's driver electronics or the main board.

- **Work through causes 1 to 6 first.** Screen off modes and app or input problems are just as common on OLED.
- **Give it a few minutes.** Many OLED TVs run a panel maintenance cycle in standby after being switched off. Avoid unplugging it during one; your manual explains how yours behaves.
- **Dimming of still images** is a normal OLED protection feature. If the screen fades during a paused or static picture and comes back when the image moves, that's not a fault.
- If the screen stays black after a power cycle and update, contact the maker or a repair shop.

## Still no picture after all of this?

If the TV's own menu won't appear, a power cycle and picture reset changed nothing, and a flashlight shows nothing on an LCD TV, the fault is most likely a board, the backlight driver or the panel. Note any light pattern and stop there. Opening the back can expose you to stored high voltage and can void your warranty.

## When to get it repaired

Contact the TV maker or a TV repair shop if:

- The flashlight test shows a faint image (backlight or power board failure).
- You see lines, half a picture or a black screen even in the TV's own menus.
- The standby light blinks a pattern your manual lists as a fault.
- The screen is cracked, or the problem started with a pop, smell or storm.

**Check your warranty first,** including any extended cover from your credit card or retailer. Outside warranty, ask for a written quote. Backlight and board repairs can cost a good share of a new TV, so compare before you decide, and recycle the old set at an e-waste drop-off if repair doesn't make sense.
