A running toilet means water is leaving the tank, so the fill valve keeps switching on to replace it. You may hear a constant trickle, a hiss, or a short refill every so often when nobody has used it.

Almost every case comes down to **a flapper that doesn't seal, a chain that's the wrong length, or a water level set too high**. All three are easy fixes with parts from any hardware store.

## How to tell where the water is going: dye test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/toilet-keeps-running-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: lift the tank lid and check whether water spills into the overflow tube; if yes, lower the water level or replace the fill valve; if no, add food coloring and wait 15 to 30 minutes; color in the bowl means a flapper, chain or seat leak" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Two checks tell you which part to fix.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Lift the tank lid** and set it somewhere safe. It's heavy porcelain and breaks easily.
2. **Watch the overflow tube** (the open pipe in the middle). If water is spilling into the top of it, the water level is too high or the fill valve won't shut off. Go to steps 2 and 6.
3. **If not, add a few drops of food coloring** or a dye tablet to the tank water. Don't flush.
4. **Wait 15 to 30 minutes**, then look in the bowl.
    - **Colored water in the bowl?** Water is leaking past the flapper. Go to steps 1, 4 and 5.
    - **Bowl still clear?** The tank is sealing. Check the refill tube and fill valve (steps 3 and 6).
5. **Flush afterward** so the dye doesn't stain the bowl.

## 1. Adjust the flapper chain

The chain links the flush handle to the flapper. Too short, and it holds the flapper slightly open. Too long, and the slack can get trapped under the flapper so it can't close. Both cause running.

**Fix:**

1. With the tank full, look at the chain while the flapper is closed.
2. Leave about **half an inch of slack**, enough that the chain hangs loosely without pulling the flapper.
3. Move the clip to a different link on the handle arm to adjust it.
4. Flush a few times and watch the flapper drop and seal each time. Trim excess chain so it can't fall under the flapper.

## 2. Lower the water level

If the water level is set too high, it spills into the overflow tube and the fill valve never shuts off. The water should usually sit about **1 inch below the top of the overflow tube**, or at the fill line marked inside the tank.

**Fix:**

- **Float cup** (a cylinder that slides up and down the fill valve): turn the adjustment screw or squeeze the clip and slide the cup down a little.
- **Float ball** (a ball on a long arm): turn the screw on top of the valve to lower the ball, or gently bend a metal arm down slightly.
- Flush and let it refill. Repeat until the water stops about an inch below the top of the overflow tube.

## 3. Check the refill tube

The small flexible tube from the fill valve sends some water into the overflow tube to refill the bowl. If its end is pushed down into the overflow tube below the water line, it can siphon water out of the tank continuously.

**Fix:**

- Clip the refill tube to the top of the overflow tube so its end sits **above** the water line. Most tubes come with a small clip for this.
- If the tube is cracked or missing, replace it. Refill tubes come with most new fill valves.

## 4. Replace the flapper

The rubber flapper seals the hole at the bottom of the tank. Over time, chlorine and minerals make it stiff, warped or slimy, so water seeps past it into the bowl. This is the most common cause of a running toilet and of the "phantom flush" refill.

**Fix:**

1. **Turn off the water** at the shut-off valve behind the toilet (turn it clockwise).
2. **Flush** to empty the tank. Sponge out what's left if needed.
3. **Unhook the old flapper** from the ears on the overflow tube (or slide it off a ring) and unclip the chain.
4. **Take it to the store** to match it. Most US toilets use a 2-inch or 3-inch flapper. Some toilets use a different style, so check the model number stamped inside the tank.
5. **Fit the new flapper**, attach the chain with about half an inch of slack, turn the water back on and test with dye again.

## 5. Clean the flush valve seat

If a new flapper still leaks, the problem may be the seat it presses against. Mineral deposits, grit or a rough spot on the rim of the drain opening can stop even a new flapper from sealing.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the water and flush to empty the tank.
2. Run a finger around the rim of the drain opening. Feel for bumps, scale or nicks.
3. Gently scrub the rim with a non-scratch scrub pad until it's smooth.
4. If the seat is cracked or badly pitted, a seat repair kit can cover it, or the flush valve can be replaced. Replacing the flush valve means removing the tank, so many people call a plumber for that part.

## 6. Replace the fill valve

The fill valve refills the tank and should shut off fully when the float reaches the set level. If the water still runs or hisses after you lower the float, or the tank fills very slowly, the valve is worn or clogged with sediment.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the water and flush to empty the tank. Sponge out the rest.
2. Put a towel down, then disconnect the supply line under the tank.
3. Unscrew the locknut under the tank and lift out the old valve.
4. Install a new fill valve following the package instructions. Universal valves adjust to fit most tanks.
5. Reconnect the supply line hand-tight plus a small turn, turn on the water, set the water level and check for drips under the tank.

## 7. Check for a cracked overflow tube

Rarely, the overflow tube itself cracks below the water line. Water then drains into the bowl through the crack, even with a good flapper and the correct water level.

**Fix:** Look closely at the tube for cracks, especially near the base. A cracked overflow tube is part of the flush valve, which means removing the tank to replace it. If you're not comfortable with that, call a plumber.

## Water won't stop running at all?

If water is pouring nonstop, first **lift the float by hand**. If the water stops, the fill valve works and you just need to lower the water level (step 2). If it keeps running:

- **Turn the shut-off valve** behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
- If that valve is stuck or leaks, don't force it. Turn off the **main water shut-off** for the house instead, then call a plumber.
- Once the water is off, replace the fill valve (step 6).

A running toilet can quietly add a lot to your water bill. To see what a constant leak adds up to, try this [water leak calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/ecology/water-leak/).

## When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if:

- The tank or bowl is cracked, or water is leaking onto the floor.
- The shut-off valve behind the toilet won't turn or drips.
- The flush valve or overflow tube needs replacing and you'd rather not remove the tank.
- You've replaced the flapper and fill valve and it still runs.

For most running toilets, though, a new flapper or a quick chain or float adjustment is all it takes.
