A leaking dishwasher usually lets water out in one of three places: **around the door, underneath the unit, or under the sink** where its hoses connect. Where you find the water is the best clue to the cause.

The most common causes are **too many suds from the wrong detergent, a worn or dirty door gasket, and a loose drain hose or supply connection**. Most of these you can check yourself in under an hour.

> **Safety first:** Water and electricity don't mix. Before you remove the kick plate or touch anything under the dishwasher, unplug it or turn off its circuit breaker, and turn off its water supply valve (usually under the kitchen sink). Mop up standing water before you work.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Dry the floor and cabinet** completely with towels, including inside the sink cabinet.
2. **Run a short cycle** and stay close by. Watch the door, the floor and the cabinet under the sink.
3. **Note where water appears first.**
    - **Door edge, often with foam:** causes 1 to 4.
    - **Sink cabinet:** cause 5.
    - **Under the front of the unit, door dry:** causes 6 to 8.
4. **Check when it leaks.** A leak while filling points to the supply side (cause 6). A leak while draining points to the drain hose (cause 5).

## 1. Wrong detergent or too many suds

Regular dish soap, laundry detergent or too much rinse aid can make far more foam than a dishwasher is built for. The suds push past the door seal and spill onto the floor. Even a small squirt of hand soap on pre-rinsed dishes can do it.

**Fix:**

- Use only detergent made for automatic dishwashers, in the amount on the package.
- If you see foam inside, cancel the cycle and soak up the suds with towels. Then run a rinse cycle with no detergent, and repeat until the foam is gone.
- Rinse dishes in plain water, not soapy water, before loading.

## 2. Worn or dirty door gasket

The rubber gasket around the door or tub opening seals in the water. Food bits and grease stop it from sealing, and an old gasket can harden, crack or pull loose.

**Fix:**

- Wipe the gasket with warm water and a cloth all the way around, including the bottom corners.
- Look for cracks, flat spots, tears or sections that have come out of their channel.
- Replacement gaskets press into a channel on most models. Order one by your model number (on a label on the door edge or tub frame).

## 3. Dishwasher not level

If the dishwasher tilts forward or to one side, water collects against the door and can seep out at a low corner. It also can make the door close unevenly so the seal doesn't press evenly.

**Fix:**

1. Open the door and place a level on the bottom door rail or the tub edge, front to back and side to side.
2. Adjust the front leveling legs (behind the kick plate) with a wrench, following your manual.
3. Check that the unit is still secured to the counter or cabinet with its mounting brackets so it doesn't move.

## 4. Clogged filter, bent door or blocked spray arm

A clogged filter can let water build up in the tub, and a spray arm hitting a tall item can blast water straight at the door seal. A door that doesn't close fully, because of a bent hinge or a dish sticking out, can leak too.

**Fix:**

- Clean the filter in the bottom of the tub. Our guide to a [dishwasher that won't drain](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/) shows how.
- Spin the spray arms by hand after loading to make sure nothing blocks them.
- Close the door and check that it latches firmly and evenly. Have a bent hinge or broken latch replaced.

## 5. Loose or damaged drain hose

The drain hose runs from the dishwasher to the sink drain, garbage disposal or an air gap on the sink. A loose clamp, a cracked hose, or a clog that backs water up can all leak, usually during the drain part of the cycle.

**Fix:**

1. With the power and water off, check the hose clamp at the sink drain or disposal and tighten it.
2. Look along the hose for cracks or wet spots, and feel underneath for drips.
3. Replace a damaged hose with one made for your model.
4. If there's an air gap on the sink and water spills from it, clean it. A clogged air gap or drain backs up water.

## 6. Leaking water inlet valve or supply line

The water supply line runs from a valve under the sink to the inlet valve, usually behind the kick plate at the front bottom of the dishwasher. A loose fitting, worn line or cracked valve drips while the dishwasher fills.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the power and the water supply valve. Remove the kick plate.
2. Shine a flashlight on the supply line fitting and the inlet valve. Look for drips, mineral crust or rust.
3. Snug a loose fitting gently. Don't overtighten, or you can crack it.
4. Replace a worn supply line or a cracked inlet valve. Many people hire a pro for the valve because it means working with the supply and wiring.

## 7. Pump or motor seal leak

The pump that circulates and drains water sits under the tub. When its seal wears out, water drips straight down under the unit, often near the middle. You may also hear grinding or humming.

**Fix:** Pump and seal repairs mean pulling the dishwasher out and working on the base. Call an appliance technician unless you have experience. On an older dishwasher, compare the repair quote with the cost of a new one.

## 8. Cracked tub or overfilling

A crack or rust hole in the tub, or a fill that doesn't stop (for example, a stuck float switch or inlet valve), can put water on the floor even with a good door seal. Many dishwashers have a float in the bottom of the tub that should move freely.

**Fix:**

- Look inside the tub for cracks or rust spots, especially in the bottom corners.
- Find the float in the tub and make sure it moves up and down freely. Clean any debris around it.
- If water keeps rising past its normal level, turn off the water valve and call a technician.

## Large leak or water under the floor?

- Turn off the dishwasher's water supply valve and the power right away.
- Mop up the water and use fans to dry the area.
- Check for soft flooring, swollen cabinet panels or stains on the ceiling below if your kitchen is above a finished room.
- Some newer dishwashers have a leak sensor in the base that stops the cycle or shows an error. Check your manual for what that error means for your model.

Water left under a floor can lead to rot and mold, so act quickly and have the damage looked at if you can't dry it fully.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician or a plumber if:

- The leak comes from the pump, motor or inside the tub.
- You can't find the source after the cycle test.
- The water supply valve won't shut off or leaks itself.
- Water reached the wiring, or you see scorch marks or smell burning.
- The floor or subfloor is wet or damaged.
