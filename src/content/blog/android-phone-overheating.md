An Android phone that runs hot is usually working hard, charging fast, or sitting somewhere it can't shed heat. The most common causes are **charging while you use it**, **heavy games and video**, **a thick case**, **direct sun or a hot car**, and **an app stuck running in the background**.

Most of these are quick to fix. The one exception is a **swollen battery**: if the back or screen is lifting, stop using the phone and skip to that section.

> **Safety first:** Never cool a phone in the fridge or freezer, and don't charge it under a pillow or on a bed. If the phone is swollen, smells odd, hisses or is too hot to touch, unplug it, put it on a hard, non-flammable surface away from anything that can burn, and don't charge it again.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Unplug it and take off the case.** Put the phone on a table indoors, out of the sun.
2. **Close all open apps** from the recent apps screen and let it sit for 10 to 15 minutes.
3. **Notice when the heat comes back.**
    - **Only while charging?** See causes 1 and 7.
    - **Only during games, video, camera or maps?** See causes 2 and 8.
    - **Even while idle in your pocket or on a desk?** See causes 5 and 6.
4. **Look at the phone from the side.** If the screen or back panel is lifting or the phone rocks on a flat table, go straight to the swollen battery section.

## 1. Charging while you use it

Charging makes heat, and so does a busy processor. Doing both at once, such as gaming or streaming while plugged in, is the single most common reason a phone gets uncomfortably hot. Many phones will slow charging or pause it until they cool.

**Fix:**

- Let the phone charge without heavy use, or play and stream after it's charged.
- Charge it on a hard surface like a desk, not on a bed, sofa or under a pillow where heat gets trapped.
- If your phone has a battery protection or adaptive charging option in the battery settings, turn it on. It can reduce time spent at full charge, which is easier on the battery.

## 2. Heavy apps, games and video

3D games, long video calls, 4K video recording, the camera and GPS navigation all push the processor and screen hard. Some warmth is normal, and the phone may lower brightness or performance to protect itself.

**Fix:**

- Take short breaks during long gaming sessions.
- Lower screen brightness, or let adaptive brightness handle it.
- In games, lower the graphics quality or frame rate setting if the game offers one.
- Close apps you're not using. Several apps running at once add up.
- For navigation in the car, mount the phone where it gets airflow from a vent, not on the dashboard in direct sun.

## 3. A thick case trapping heat

Rugged, leather and wallet cases act like a jacket. They hold heat against the phone, especially while charging or gaming.

**Fix:**

- Take the case off while charging or during long gaming sessions.
- If the phone runs cooler without it, consider a thinner case.
- Remove anything stuck to the back, like a metal ring or plate, before wireless charging. Metal on a wireless charger can get hot.

## 4. Sun, hot cars and pockets

Phones are built to work in normal room temperatures. A parked car in summer can get far hotter than the air outside, and direct sun on a dark phone heats it fast. Most phones show a temperature warning and limit use until they cool.

**Fix:**

- Never leave a phone on the dashboard or seat of a parked car in warm weather.
- Keep it in the shade at the beach or pool, and out of a tight back pocket on a hot day.
- If it shows a heat warning, turn it off and let it cool in a shaded room. Don't use a fridge, freezer or ice pack.

## 5. A rogue app running in the background

If the phone gets hot while you're not using it, an app is often stuck doing something in the background: syncing, using location, or crashing and restarting. Android's battery usage screen shows which apps use the most power.

**Fix:**

1. Open **Settings** and go to **Battery** (on some phones it's under **Battery and device care** or **Device care**). Look for **Battery usage** or **App battery usage**.
2. Look for an app near the top that you haven't been using much.
3. Tap it and choose **Force stop**, then check its background or battery setting and restrict it if the option is there.
4. If it keeps happening, update the app from the Play Store, clear its cache (in the app's storage settings), or uninstall it.
5. To test whether any downloaded app is to blame, restart in **safe mode**, which runs only the built-in apps. On many phones you press and hold the power button, then press and hold **Power off** on the screen until you see the safe mode option. Check your phone's manual if that doesn't work. If the heat stops in safe mode, a downloaded app is the cause.

## 6. A new update still settling in

After a big Android update, the phone may spend a day or two optimizing apps and re-indexing photos and files in the background. That can make it warmer than usual for a while.

**Fix:**

- Leave it on Wi-Fi and charging overnight so the work finishes.
- If it's still hot after a few days, check for a follow-up system update in **Settings** under **System** or **Software update**, and update your apps from the Play Store.
- Restart the phone. A simple restart clears stuck processes.

## 7. Fast charging and the wrong charger

Fast charging pushes more power into the battery, which makes more heat. Cheap uncertified chargers and damaged cables can also run hot and charge poorly.

**Fix:**

- Use the charger that came with the phone, or a certified charger and cable from a reputable brand.
- Replace any cable with a frayed jacket, bent plug or loose fit.
- If heat during charging bothers you, many phones let you turn off fast charging in the battery or charging settings. Charging will be slower but cooler.
- Wireless charging is usually warmer than wired. Line the phone up properly on the pad, and remove the case if it's thick.

## 8. Weak signal and constant syncing

When cell signal is weak, the phone boosts its radio power to stay connected, and that makes heat and drains the battery. Constant photo backup and email syncing add more.

**Fix:**

- Use Wi-Fi when you have it.
- In areas with no signal, turn on airplane mode, then turn Wi-Fi back on if you need it.
- Pause photo backup until you're on Wi-Fi and charging.

## Swollen battery? Stop using the phone

Lithium-ion batteries can swell as they age or after damage. Signs include a screen lifting at one edge, a back panel separating, a gap along the frame, or a phone that rocks on a flat table. A swollen battery can catch fire.

- **Stop charging and stop using it.** Power it off if you can do so without pressing on the bulge.
- **Don't press, puncture or try to flatten it,** and don't try to pry the battery out yourself.
- **Keep it on a hard, non-flammable surface,** away from heat, bedding and paper.
- **Take it to the manufacturer or a reputable repair shop** for a battery replacement. Don't throw it in the trash. Ask the shop or your local recycling center how to dispose of it safely.

## When to call a pro

Get the phone checked by the manufacturer, your carrier or a reputable repair shop if:

- The battery or case is bulging, or you smell burning or see smoke.
- It gets very hot while idle, even in safe mode and after a restart.
- It shuts down from heat in a cool room with nothing running.
- The charging port gets hot with several different good chargers.
- The battery drains much faster than it used to, along with the heat. The battery may need replacing.

Back up your photos and data before you hand the phone in, if it's safe to use long enough to do so.
