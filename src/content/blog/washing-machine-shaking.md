A washing machine shakes when the drum spins with its weight off-center or when something meant to hold it steady has slipped or worn out. During a fast spin, a small imbalance turns into a big force, and the washer bangs, rocks or walks across the floor.

The most common causes are an **unbalanced load**, **uneven or loose leveling feet**, and, on a new front loader, **shipping bolts that were never removed**. On older washers, **worn suspension springs, rods or shock absorbers** are next. A **soft floor** can make any of these worse.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the washer before you tilt it, adjust it or remove any panel. Washers are heavy, so get a helper to move or tip one, and never tip a washer with water in the tub. Turn off the water valves if you need to pull it out far enough to stretch the hoses. Stop any cycle that is banging hard against walls or other appliances.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Note when it shakes:** every load, or only with bulky items, small loads or one heavy item.
2. **Unplug it, then push down on each top corner** in turn. If it rocks, a foot isn't touching the floor.
3. **Put a level** on top, front to back and then side to side.
4. **On a new front loader, look at the back panel.** Bolts or plastic spacers in large holes mean the shipping bolts are still in.
5. **With the washer empty and unplugged, push the tub or drum** down and let go. It should move a little and settle. If it bounces several times, sags to one side or bangs the cabinet, the suspension is worn (causes 4 and 5).

## 1. An unbalanced load

This is the most common cause. When clothes clump on one side of the drum, the spin throws the whole washer around. Single heavy items, such as a blanket, a rug, a bath mat or a pair of jeans in a small load, are the usual culprits. Mixing very heavy and very light items does the same.

**Fix:**

- **Stop or pause the cycle** and spread the clothes evenly around the drum.
- Wash bulky items with **two or three similar items** to balance them, or wash comforters in a washer rated for them.
- Don't **overload**. Clothes need room to move. On a top loader, load around the drum, not in one pile.
- Most newer washers detect imbalance, try to redistribute and slow the spin. If yours keeps stopping with an unbalanced warning, check your manual for what the code means.

## 2. Uneven or loose leveling feet

Most washers have **adjustable feet** with a lock nut. If one foot isn't touching the floor, or the feet have turned loose from vibration, the washer rocks and then walks. Some top loaders also have **self-leveling rear legs**.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug** the washer and pull it out a little if you need room to reach the feet.
2. **Loosen the lock nut** on each front foot with a wrench or pliers.
3. Turn the feet in or out until the washer is **level side to side and front to back**, and it no longer rocks when you push each corner. Keep the feet as short as practical.
4. **Tighten the lock nuts** up against the cabinet so the feet can't turn.
5. If your washer has self-leveling rear legs, your manual will usually explain how to tilt the washer forward a few inches and set it down so they adjust. Get a helper for this.

## 3. Shipping bolts still in place

Front-loading washers ship with **transit bolts** that lock the drum so it doesn't get damaged on the truck. If they aren't removed, the drum can't float on its suspension, and the washer shakes violently on its first spins.

**Fix:**

- **Unplug** the washer and look at the back panel for several bolts, often with plastic spacers.
- Remove them as the installation guide describes, and fit the **plastic caps** that came with the washer over the holes.
- **Keep the bolts.** You'll need them if you move the washer.

## 4. Worn suspension springs or rods (top loaders)

Many top loaders hang the tub from **suspension rods or springs** at the corners. As they wear, the tub sags, swings and bangs the cabinet, especially on spin.

**Fix:**

- With the washer **unplugged and empty**, look for a tub that sits lower on one side or bounces freely when pushed.
- Suspension rods are usually replaced **as a set**, not one at a time. Find the right part with your washer's **model number**, which is usually on a label under the lid or on the back.
- Replacing them means removing the top or cabinet. If you're not comfortable with that, call an appliance technician.

## 5. Worn shock absorbers or dampers (front loaders)

Front loaders use **shock absorbers or dampers** under the drum and **springs** above it. Worn shocks let the drum move too much, and you'll often hear banging or knocking on spin. You may also find bits of foam or a broken mount inside the cabinet.

**Fix:**

- With the washer unplugged, push down on the drum. It should move a little and return smoothly. If it drops easily and bounces, the shocks are likely worn.
- Shocks are often replaced **in pairs or as a set**. Order by model number.
- This job usually means removing a front or back panel. An appliance technician can do it if you'd rather not.

## 6. Worn drum bearings

The bearings let the drum spin smoothly. When they wear out, you'll hear a **loud rumble or roar** that gets louder as the drum spins faster, and the washer may vibrate more. Bearings often fail after a seal lets water reach them, so you may also see leaks or rust streaks.

**Fix:**

- With the washer unplugged and empty, **rock the drum** up and down by hand. Noticeable play or a grinding feel points to worn bearings.
- Bearing replacement is a big job on most washers and the parts and labor can cost more than the washer is worth. Get a quote from an appliance technician and compare it with replacing the washer.

## 7. A soft or bouncy floor

Wood-framed floors, especially upstairs laundry rooms or older floors, can flex during spin. The floor bounces with the washer and makes normal vibration feel much worse.

**Fix:**

- Place the washer as close to a **wall or over a joist** as practical, where the floor is stiffest.
- Use **anti-vibration pads** or a solid, flat platform under the feet.
- If the floor itself is sagging or soft, have a contractor look at it. Don't ignore a floor that feels weak.

## 8. A pedestal, stacking kit or loose cabinet parts

Washers on pedestals or in stacked pairs have more places to loosen. A pedestal that isn't bolted to the washer, a loose stacking kit or loose cabinet screws can rattle and shake.

**Fix:**

- Check that the **pedestal** is attached as its instructions describe and its own feet are level and locked.
- On a stacked pair, check the **stacking kit** brackets are secure. Check your manual for the right kit.
- Tighten loose **cabinet screws** and look for a loose **counterweight** (a heavy block on the drum) on front loaders. A loose counterweight bangs on every spin and needs to be refitted or replaced.

## Washer won't spin or stops mid-cycle?

If the washer shakes, then stops spinning and leaves clothes wet, it may be detecting an unbalanced load and protecting itself. Redistribute the load and run a **drain and spin** cycle. If it won't spin even with a balanced load, see our guide to a [washer not spinning](/appliances/washer-not-spinning/).

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a technician if:

- The washer bangs hard even with **balanced loads** and level, locked feet.
- The tub or drum **sags, bounces** or hits the cabinet.
- You hear a **loud roar or grinding** that gets worse with spin speed.
- You see **leaks, rust or broken parts** inside the cabinet.
- You'd rather not remove panels or tip the washer.

Most shaking washers need nothing more than an even load and four level, locked feet. Check those first, and save the technician for worn parts.
