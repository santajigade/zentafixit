When an iPhone won't charge, the fault is usually outside the phone: a **worn cable or adapter**, **lint packed into the charging port**, or a **setting that pauses charging** at 80%. Heat, liquid in the port and software glitches are the next most common causes. A failed battery or port is less likely than you'd think.

First, check which connector your phone has. **iPhone 15 and later use USB-C. iPhone 14 and earlier use Lightning.** The fixes are the same, but you need the right cable.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Plug in with a different cable and adapter**, straight into a wall outlet. If it charges, your old cable or adapter is the problem (fix 1).
2. **Look at the screen.** A message such as "Charging On Hold," "Liquid Detected" or "This accessory may not be supported" points straight to a fix (fixes 1, 4 and 7).
3. **Check the battery icon.** If it charges to 80% then stops, see fix 3. If it doesn't show a lightning bolt at all, check the port (fix 2).
4. **Try wireless charging** if your iPhone supports it. If wireless works but the cable doesn't, the port is the likely problem.

## 1. Try a different cable and power adapter

Cables wear out at the ends, often where the cable bends. Cheap uncertified cables and adapters can also stop working or trigger a "This accessory may not be supported" alert.

**Fix:**

- Use the cable that came with the phone, or one from Apple or a reputable brand. For Lightning, look for Apple's **MFi ("Made for iPhone")** certification on the packaging.
- Plug the adapter into a **wall outlet**, not a power strip, a car socket or a sleeping computer.
- Check the cable for fraying, kinks or bent connectors, and the adapter for damaged prongs.
- For faster charging, Apple recommends a USB-C power adapter rated **20 W or more**. A small 5 W cube works but is slow.

## 2. Clean lint out of the charging port

Pocket lint builds up in the port and gets packed down each time you plug in. Eventually the cable can't seat fully, so it feels loose or only charges at an angle.

**Fix:**

1. **Turn the iPhone off.**
2. Shine a flashlight into the port and look for gray or dark fluff at the back.
3. Gently loosen it with a **wooden or plastic toothpick**. Scrape along the inside walls and lift the lint out. Don't press hard on the contacts.
4. Plug the cable in. It should click in firmly.

**Never use** a metal pin, paper clip or SIM tool, and don't spray liquid or cleaner into the port. If the lint is packed tight, an Apple Store or authorized service provider can clean it.

## 3. Check Optimized Battery Charging and the 80% limit

If your iPhone always stops at 80%, it's probably a battery-saving feature, not a fault.

- **Optimized Battery Charging** learns your routine and holds the charge at 80% until shortly before you usually unplug, such as overnight. When it's holding, a Lock Screen notification usually lets you charge now.
- **Charge limits:** iPhone 15 and later models can set a fixed charging limit, such as 80%.

**Fix:** Open **Settings > Battery**, then **Charging** or **Battery Health & Charging**, depending on your model and iOS version. Turn off Optimized Battery Charging or raise the limit if you need a full charge today. Many people leave these on to slow battery wear.

## 4. Let it cool down if charging is on hold

An iPhone stops or slows charging when its battery is too warm. You may see a **"Charging On Hold"** message, or it may simply pause above 80%. Apple says the iPhone works best between 32°F and 95°F ambient temperature.

**Fix:**

- Unplug it and move it to a cooler spot, out of direct sun and off pillows or car dashboards.
- Take it out of a thick case while it charges.
- Close games and navigation apps that heat it up.
- Never cool it in a fridge or freezer. Let it warm or cool gradually, and it will resume charging on its own.

## 5. Force restart your iPhone

A software glitch can stop the phone from recognizing the charger. A force restart clears it without erasing anything.

**Fix (iPhone 8 and later, including all models with Face ID):**

1. Press and quickly release **volume up**.
2. Press and quickly release **volume down**.
3. Press and hold the **side button** until the Apple logo appears, then release.

Plug it back in after it starts up.

## 6. Fix wireless and MagSafe charging

Wireless (Qi) charging works on iPhone 8 and later. MagSafe is on most iPhone 12 and later models, but not the iPhone SE or iPhone 16e. Wireless charging is sensitive to position and anything between the phone and the charger.

**Fix:**

- **Center the iPhone** on the pad. Off-center phones often charge slowly or not at all.
- **Remove thick cases, metal plates, magnetic mounts, grips and wallets with cards.** These can block charging. Use only cases made for wireless or MagSafe charging.
- Check the pad's own cable and power adapter. Many pads need a specific adapter wattage, so check the pad's manual.
- If cable charging works but wireless doesn't, try another pad before blaming the phone.

## 7. Deal with a liquid detected alert

Recent iPhones can detect liquid in the Lightning or USB-C port and show an alert that **charging is not available**. This protects the port and the phone from damage.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the cable** from the phone.
2. **Tap the iPhone gently** against your hand with the port facing down to remove extra liquid.
3. Leave it **somewhere dry with some airflow**. Try charging again after at least 30 minutes.
4. If the alert comes back, there's still liquid inside. Leave it to dry for up to a day.

**Don't** use a hair dryer or other heat source, compressed air, or cotton swabs or paper towels in the port, and **don't put it in rice**. While you wait, wireless charging is usually an option if your model supports it.

## 8. Update iOS

Apple fixes charging and battery bugs in iOS updates.

**Fix:** Go to **Settings > General > Software Update** and install any available update. Plug in to a charger if you can, and stay on Wi-Fi. If the phone has too little charge to update, try again after it has charged for a while.

## 9. Check battery health

Batteries wear out over time. A worn battery may charge slowly, drain fast, or shut down suddenly.

**Fix:**

- Open **Settings > Battery > Battery Health** (on some models and iOS versions, **Battery Health & Charging**).
- Look at **Maximum Capacity** and any message under it. If you see a **service** message, the battery needs replacing.
- Heat and age are the main reasons lithium-ion batteries wear out. Here's a deeper look at the [common causes of battery failure](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/causes-of-battery-failure/).

A battery replacement from Apple or an authorized service provider usually brings back normal charging and battery life.

## iPhone won't turn on or charge at all?

- **Leave it on a known-good charger for up to an hour.** A fully drained battery may take a while to show anything, sometimes just a red battery icon with a cable.
- **Force restart while plugged in** (fix 5).
- **Try a different wall outlet, cable and adapter**, and wireless charging if supported.
- If the screen stays black but the phone vibrates or makes sounds, the display may be the problem, not charging.

## When to contact Apple Support

Stop charging and contact Apple Support, visit an Apple Store, or go to an Apple Authorized Service Provider if:

- The phone is **swollen**, the screen is lifting, or the back is bulging. Don't charge it or press on it.
- The phone or cable gets **very hot**, smells of burning, or the port is scorched.
- The port is loose or damaged, or the phone was dropped in water and still shows a liquid alert after a day.
- Nothing above works with a known-good cable and adapter.

You can start a repair or chat with Apple through the **Apple Support** app or Apple's website. Check whether your phone is covered by warranty or AppleCare+ first.
