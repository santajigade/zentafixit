When an Android phone won't charge, you plug it in and nothing happens, the charging icon flickers on and off, or the percentage barely moves. It can feel like the phone is dying, but the cause is usually small.

Most cases come down to **a worn-out cable or charger, lint packed into the USB-C port, moisture in the port, or a battery protection setting** that stops charging early. Work through the fixes below in order.

> **Safety first:** Stop using the phone and charger if either gets very hot, smells of burning, or the battery looks swollen (the screen or back lifting). Never push metal into the charging port, and don't charge a phone that's wet.

## How to tell what's wrong: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/android-not-charging-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: try a known good cable and charger; if the phone charges, replace the old one; if not, check the port for lint or moisture; if it is clean and dry, restart and test in safe mode; if it still won't charge, the port or battery needs repair" width="640" height="430" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Swap the easy parts first, then look at the phone.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Swap the cable and charger** for ones you know work, ideally ones that charge another phone fine.
2. **Use a wall outlet**, not a laptop USB port, car charger or power strip.
3. **Look at the port** with a flashlight. Lint, dirt or a green or white crust means the port needs cleaning.
4. **Restart the phone** and plug it in again.
5. **Still nothing?** Try wireless charging if your phone supports it. If wireless works but the cable doesn't, the port is the likely problem.

## 1. Try a different cable, charger and outlet

Cables wear out at the ends from bending, and chargers can fail without any visible sign. A cable that works for another device may still have a damaged wire that stops fast charging or charging at all.

**Fix:**

- Try a different USB-C cable, then a different charger.
- Plug into a different wall outlet. If you use a power strip, plug straight into the wall.
- Look for frays, kinks or a bent plug, and throw away any damaged cable.
- Choose cables and chargers from reputable brands. Cheap, uncertified ones are a common cause of problems.

## 2. Clean lint out of the USB-C port

Pocket lint gets pressed into the port every time you plug in. Over time it packs down so the plug can't seat fully, and the phone charges only at an angle or not at all.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the phone off.
2. Shine a flashlight into the port and look for lint or debris at the back.
3. Gently loosen it with a wooden or plastic toothpick, working around the center tab. Don't press hard on the tab.
4. Blow out the loose lint with a puff of air.
5. Never use a metal pin, paper clip or liquid cleaner.

If the plug now clicks firmly into place, try charging again.

## 3. Let a wet port dry out

Many Android phones, including Samsung Galaxy models, detect moisture in the port and block wired charging until it's dry. You'll see a warning on the screen.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the cable and turn the phone off if you can.
- Gently shake out water with the port facing down, and wipe the outside dry.
- Let it air-dry in a cool, dry place. This can take a few hours.
- Don't use a hair dryer, heater, or put the phone in rice. Heat can damage it and rice can leave dust in the port.
- If your phone supports wireless charging, you can usually use that while the port dries. Check your manual.

## 4. Restart the phone and check for apps

A software glitch can stop the phone from recognizing the charger. A misbehaving app can also keep the phone so busy that it uses power as fast as it charges.

**Fix:**

- Restart the phone, then try charging.
- Install any pending system updates in **Settings**.
- Try **safe mode**, which turns off downloaded apps. The way to enter it differs by phone, so check your maker's help pages. If it charges normally in safe mode, a recently installed app is the likely cause.

If the battery also drains quickly, see our guide on an [Android battery draining fast](/phones-tablets/android-battery-draining-fast/).

## 5. Check battery protection settings

Many Android phones can stop charging at 80 or 85 percent to slow down battery wear. Others pause charging overnight and finish just before your alarm. Both are normal features, not faults.

**Fix:**

- Open **Settings** and look under **Battery** for options such as battery protection, adaptive charging or charging optimization. The names differ by maker.
- Turn the limit off if you need a full charge, or leave it on to help the battery last longer.

## 6. Charging slowly? Use the right charger

A phone charges slowly on a low-power charger, a laptop USB port, a long thin cable, or while you're using it heavily. A hot phone also slows or pauses charging to protect the battery.

**Fix:**

- Use a USB-C charger that supports **USB Power Delivery (PD)**, and check your phone maker's site for the fast charging standard it uses.
- Avoid gaming, navigation or video calls while charging if you need a quick top-up.
- Take the phone out of a thick case and keep it out of direct sun or a hot car while charging.
- Turn on airplane mode for the fastest possible charge.

## 7. Wireless charging not working

Wireless charging is sensitive to position and to anything between the phone and the pad.

**Fix:**

- Center the phone on the pad. Many pads have a sweet spot, and small shifts matter.
- Remove thick cases, metal plates, magnetic mounts, credit cards or pop sockets from the back.
- Make sure the pad has its own power adapter with enough output. Check the pad's manual.
- Confirm your phone supports wireless charging. Not all Android models do.

## 8. A worn battery or damaged port

If nothing above helps, the charging port may be loose or damaged inside, or the battery may be worn out. Phones that were dropped or got wet are more likely to have this problem.

**Fix:**

- If the cable feels loose or wiggles even after cleaning, the port may be worn.
- Check battery health in **Settings** if your phone shows it, or use your maker's diagnostics app.
- Contact the maker or an authorized repair shop. Port and battery replacements are common repairs.

## Phone won't charge or turn on at all?

- **Leave it on the charger for 30 minutes.** A fully drained battery can take a while before the phone shows any sign of life.
- **Force a restart.** On many Android phones you hold the power button, or power and volume down together, for 10 to 20 seconds. Check your maker's instructions.
- **Try a different charger and cable** one more time, plugged into the wall.
- **No response at all** after that usually means a hardware problem. Take it to the maker or a repair shop.

## When to call a pro

Take the phone to the maker or an authorized repair shop if:

- The battery is swollen, or the phone gets very hot while charging.
- The port is loose, damaged or corroded.
- It won't charge with any cable, charger or wireless pad.
- It got wet and still won't charge after drying.

Don't try to replace a phone battery yourself unless you have the right tools and parts. Damaged lithium-ion batteries can catch fire.
