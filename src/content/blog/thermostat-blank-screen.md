A thermostat screen goes blank when the thermostat loses power or, on many smart models, when the display simply goes to sleep. Thermostats get power in one of two ways: from batteries, or from a small 24-volt transformer inside your furnace or air handler. Anything that cuts either source turns the screen off.

The most common causes are **dead batteries, a tripped breaker or furnace switch, a furnace door that isn't closed, and a condensate float switch that has shut the system off**. A blown fuse on the furnace control board and loose wires come next. Work through them in order, since the first few take a minute each.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Replace the batteries if your thermostat uses them. > **Safety first:** Turn off power at the breaker or furnace service switch before removing the furnace door or touching wires. The thermostat wiring is low voltage, but the furnace and air handler have 120 V or 240 V wiring inside. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas company or 911 from outside.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/thermostat-blank-screen-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: does the thermostat use batteries; if yes replace them; if no, does the furnace or air handler have power; if no, check the breaker, service switch and door; if it has power, check the condensate float switch; if that is dry, check the control board fuse and the thermostat wiring" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Start with the thermostat, then follow the power back to the furnace.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Tap the screen or press a button.** Many smart thermostats dim or switch off the display to save power. If it wakes, nothing is wrong (cause 2).
2. **Pull the thermostat off its wall plate** (most lift or slide off) and look for batteries. If it has them, replace them (cause 1).
3. **Check the HVAC system.** Is the furnace or air handler running, or can you hear it? Is its breaker on and its service switch on?
    - **System has no power?** Causes 3 and 4.
    - **System has power but the thermostat is blank?** Causes 5, 6 and 7.
4. **Look at the AC drain pan** under the indoor coil or air handler. Standing water points to a tripped float switch (cause 5).

## 1. Dead or weak batteries

Battery-powered thermostats go blank when the batteries die, often after a low-battery warning you may have missed. Some thermostats that run on system power also use batteries as a backup to keep settings and the display working.

**Fix:**

- Remove the thermostat from its plate and replace the batteries with fresh ones of the type marked in the compartment, usually AA or AAA alkaline.
- Make sure each battery faces the right way and the contacts are clean.
- Many thermostats need the batteries replaced about once a year. Check your manual.

## 2. The display is just asleep

Many smart thermostats turn off or dim the screen after a short time to save power, then wake when you walk up or touch them. Some have a display setting that keeps the screen dark until you tap it.

**Fix:**

- Tap the screen, press a button or wave a hand in front of it.
- Check the display or screen settings in the thermostat or its app and choose a brighter or always-on option if you prefer.
- If it wakes for a second and goes dark again, it may be low on power. See cause 7.

## 3. The HVAC system has no power

Most non-battery thermostats get their power from a transformer inside the furnace or air handler. If that equipment loses power, the thermostat goes blank too. The usual reasons are a **tripped breaker** or a **service switch** that someone turned off. The service switch often looks like a light switch on or near the furnace.

**Fix:**

- Check the breaker for the furnace or air handler. If it's tripped, push it fully to OFF, then back to ON.
- Find the service switch near the furnace and make sure it's on.
- If the breaker trips again, stop resetting it and call an HVAC technician or electrician. See our guide on a [breaker that keeps tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/).

## 4. The furnace door switch is open

Furnaces have a safety switch that cuts power when the blower door is removed. If the door isn't fully seated after a filter change or a look inside, the furnace and the thermostat both stay off.

**Fix:**

- Push the furnace door firmly into place until it sits flat and the switch is pressed. Some doors have to hook in at the bottom first.
- Make sure no wire or filter edge is stopping the door from closing.
- Wait a minute or two. The thermostat may take a moment to start up.

## 5. A condensate float switch cut the power

Air conditioners and high-efficiency furnaces make water that runs down a drain line. Many systems have a **float switch** in the drain pan or line that cuts low-voltage power if the drain clogs and water rises. On many setups this also cuts power to the thermostat, so the screen goes blank with water nearby.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off power to the system at the breaker or service switch.
2. Remove standing water from the drain pan with a wet/dry vacuum or towels.
3. Clear the drain line. A common method is to hold a wet/dry vacuum hose over the outside end of the line for a minute or two to pull the clog out. If you can't reach the end or it stays blocked, ask a technician to clear it.
4. Once the water is gone, the float drops and power returns. Turn the system back on.

If the pan keeps filling, or there's water on the ceiling or floor, call an HVAC technician.

## 6. A blown fuse on the furnace control board

Many furnace and air handler control boards have a small fuse, often an automotive-style blade fuse, that protects the 24-volt circuit. A short in the thermostat wiring, for example a wire nicked while hanging a picture or installing a new thermostat, can blow it.

**Fix:**

- With power off, remove the furnace door and look for the fuse on the control board. A blown blade fuse has a broken metal strip inside.
- Replace it only with a fuse of the same rating printed on it or on the board.
- If the new fuse blows right away, there's a short in the wiring or a failed part. Stop and call an HVAC technician.

## 7. A loose wire or no C-wire

At the thermostat, a loose **R wire** (power) or **C wire** (common) can cut power. Smart thermostats installed without a C-wire sometimes try to borrow power from the heating or cooling wires, which can leave them flat and going blank, especially when the system has been idle.

**Fix:**

- Turn off power to the HVAC system, remove the thermostat and check that each wire is firmly seated in its terminal with no bare copper touching another wire.
- If you're comfortable with a multimeter, set it to AC volts and measure between R and C with the power on. You should see roughly 24 V. No reading means the problem is back at the furnace.
- If you don't have a C-wire, many smart thermostats offer an adapter, or a technician can run one. See our guide to [thermostat wire colors](/hvac/thermostat-wire-colors/).

## 8. A failed thermostat or transformer

If the thermostat has power at its terminals but stays blank, the thermostat itself may have failed. If there's no 24 V at the furnace transformer with the furnace powered, the transformer may have failed.

**Fix:**

- Try fresh batteries and a careful reseat on the wall plate first.
- Replacing a basic thermostat is a common DIY job if you label the wires first.
- Testing or replacing a transformer means working near line voltage inside the furnace. Leave it to an HVAC technician unless you're experienced.

## Screen still blank and no heat or cooling?

- **Check all the power points again:** breaker, service switch, furnace door and float switch.
- **Look for blinking lights** on the furnace control board through the sight glass. No light at all usually means no power reaching the board.
- **In cold weather,** keep pipes from freezing with safe space heaters and call for service, since the problem is likely at the furnace, not the thermostat.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

- The breaker trips again or the control board fuse keeps blowing.
- There's water in the drain pan you can't clear, or water damage near the unit.
- You measure no 24 V at the transformer, or you aren't comfortable working inside the furnace.
- You smell burning or gas, or see scorch marks on wires or the board.

If the screen works again but the thermostat won't get online, see our guide on a [smart thermostat not connecting to Wi-Fi](/smart-home/smart-thermostat-not-connecting-to-wifi/).
