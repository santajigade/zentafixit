When a washer won't spin, the cycle stops with soaking wet clothes, or the drum turns so slowly that the laundry drips all the way to the dryer. In most cases the washer is protecting itself, not broken. It refuses to spin when something about the load, the water or the lid doesn't look right.

The most common causes are **an unbalanced load, water that hasn't drained, a lid switch or door lock that isn't registering, or a low or no-spin setting**. Less often a **worn belt, motor coupling or other drive part** has failed. Here's how to find which, starting with the free checks.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the washer before you reach into the drum, remove a panel or tip the machine. The water inside may be hot after a warm or hot cycle, so let it cool before you drain it. Never force a locked front-load door or pry at the lock. A washer full of wet laundry is very heavy, so get help before you move it.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/washer-not-spinning-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: unplug the washer for a minute, rebalance the load and run Drain and Spin. If water is still in the tub, it is a drain problem, so check the hose, filter and pump. If it drains but the drum stays still or spins slowly, check the load, lid lock, cycle, suds and drive parts" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Whether the water drains splits the problem in two.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Reset the controls.** Unplug the washer for about a minute, then plug it back in.
2. **Rebalance the load.** Spread wet clothes evenly around the drum, or take out a single heavy item.
3. **Run Drain and Spin** (or Rinse and Spin) with the lid or door firmly closed, and watch and listen.
4. **Read the result.**
    - **Water still in the tub?** It's a drain problem (cause 2).
    - **Drains but won't spin, or spins slowly?** Work through causes 1 and 3 to 9.
    - **Shows an error code?** Look it up in your manual before you take anything apart.

## 1. An unbalanced load

This is the most common cause. A single heavy item, like a bath mat, a pair of jeans in a small load or a comforter, can bunch up on one side of the drum. Most washers sense the wobble and try to rebalance, slow the spin or stop it completely.

**Fix:**

- Open the lid or door (once it unlocks) and spread the clothes evenly around the drum.
- Wash a heavy item with two or three similar items, such as towels, so the weight is spread out.
- Untangle sheets and towels that have twisted into a rope, a common problem in top loaders with an agitator.
- For bulky bedding, follow your manual. Some washers have a dedicated bulky or bedding cycle, and some say to wash one large item at a time.

## 2. Water is still in the tub

A washer won't spin with water standing in it. If the drain is slow or blocked, the spin never starts, or the clothes come out dripping. A kinked drain hose, a clogged pump filter or a sock stuck in the drain pump are the usual reasons.

**Fix:**

- Check that the drain hose behind the washer isn't kinked or crushed against the wall.
- On a front loader, clean the drain pump filter behind the small panel at the bottom front.
- If the pump hums but no water moves, or water backs up out of the standpipe, follow our full guide to a [washer that won't drain](/appliances/washer-wont-drain/). It covers safely emptying the tub, the filter, the pump and the house drain.

## 3. A lid switch or door lock fault

Washers won't spin unless they sense that the lid or door is closed, and most front loaders and many newer top loaders lock it during the spin. If the lid switch or door lock doesn't register, the washer may fill and wash normally, then skip the spin, and a lock light may blink.

**Fix:**

- Close the lid or door firmly. Look for a sock or a fold of the door seal caught in the latch.
- On a top loader, check the small plastic tab or strike on the lid that presses into the cabinet. If it's broken, bent or loose, the switch can't sense the lid.
- If the lock light keeps blinking or the door won't lock at all, the lid switch or door lock assembly may have failed. These are common replacement parts, but test procedures vary by model, so check your manual or call a technician.
- Don't bypass or tape over a lid switch. It stops you reaching into a spinning drum.

## 4. An overloaded drum

A drum that's packed too tight can't spread the weight evenly, so it behaves like an unbalanced load. Overloading also strains the belt, clutch and motor.

**Fix:**

- Take out part of the load and run Drain and Spin again.
- Leave room for clothes to tumble or move freely. Your manual shows the maximum load for your washer.

## 5. The wrong cycle or spin setting

Delicate, hand wash and wool cycles spin slowly on purpose, so clothes come out wetter than usual. Many washers also let you pick a low spin speed, a **No Spin** option or a rinse hold setting that pauses the cycle with water in the tub. A setting left over from the last load is easy to miss.

**Fix:**

- Check the spin speed and option buttons on the console, not just the cycle knob.
- Pick a normal or cotton cycle with a medium or high spin and run it again.
- If you used a rinse hold option, run Drain and Spin. Check your manual for your model's options.

## 6. Too many suds

High-efficiency (HE) washers use very little water. Too much detergent, or regular detergent in an HE machine, makes a thick layer of suds. Many washers detect this and slow the spin or add extra rinses, and some show a suds code.

**Fix:**

- Run a rinse cycle with no detergent to clear the suds. You may need to run it twice.
- Use only HE detergent in an HE washer, and use the amount your manual suggests, not the full cap.

## 7. The washer isn't level

A washer that rocks on its feet shakes much more in the spin. The washer may read that as an unbalanced load and cut the spin short, even when the clothes are spread out.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the washer and push down on each corner. If it rocks, one foot is short.
2. Put a level on top, front to back and side to side.
3. Raise or lower the front feet until it sits flat, then tighten the lock nuts against the cabinet so the feet can't creep. Some washers have self-adjusting rear feet, so check your manual.

## 8. A worn drive belt or motor coupling

If you hear the motor running or humming but the drum doesn't turn, or turns weakly, the part that links the motor to the drum may have failed. Belt-drive washers (some top loaders and some front loaders) use a rubber belt that can stretch, glaze, slip or break. A burning rubber smell during spin is a common sign. Some older direct-drive top loaders use a plastic and rubber **motor coupling** between the motor and transmission that can wear out or break, often after repeated overloading.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the washer and look for pieces of rubber or plastic under the machine.
- Checking a belt or coupling means removing a cabinet panel, and on some top loaders tipping the washer. The steps differ a lot between models, so follow your manual or service sheet.
- If you're not comfortable taking panels off, call a technician. These are usually affordable parts.
- Many newer washers are direct drive, with the motor mounted on the drum or transmission and no belt at all. If yours has no belt, skip to cause 9.

## 9. Shifter, clutch, motor or control board

These are the less common, more expensive causes. Some newer top loaders use a **shifter** (also called an actuator) to switch between agitating and spinning. If it fails, the washer may agitate but not spin. Some belt-drive top loaders have a **clutch** that brings the basket up to speed, and a worn one can leave the spin weak and slow. A failed **motor**, motor control or main **control board** can stop the spin completely.

**Fix:**

- Note any error code and look it up with your exact model number. Our guide to [reading appliance error codes](/appliances/how-to-read-appliance-error-codes/) shows where to find the model tag, manual and tech sheet.
- Rule out causes 1 to 8 first. Lid switch, drain and load problems often look like a motor fault.
- Diagnosing these parts usually needs a multimeter, the wiring diagram and the model's test mode. That's a job for an appliance technician.

## Drum won't turn at all?

- **No power or display:** check that the washer is plugged in firmly and that its breaker hasn't tripped. Many washers have their own circuit.
- **Child lock or control lock:** a lock icon on the display can stop the cycle from starting. Your manual shows how to turn it off.
- **Something jammed:** unplug the washer, empty the drum and try turning it slowly by hand. If it feels stuck or grinds, an item may be caught between the drum and the outer tub. Don't force it.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a technician if:

- The washer drains fully, the load is balanced, the lid locks and it still won't spin.
- The motor hums or you smell burning rubber or hot electrical parts.
- The door lock or lid switch has failed, or a lock code keeps coming back.
- The fix means tipping the washer, removing the cabinet or testing a motor or control board.
- The washer trips a breaker, sparks or shows burnt wiring.
