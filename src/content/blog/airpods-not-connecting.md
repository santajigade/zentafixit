When AirPods or other true wireless earbuds won't connect, the problem is usually simple. The most common causes are a **flat case or earbuds**, **earbuds not seated properly in the case**, a connection to **another phone, tablet or laptop**, or a **stale saved pairing** on your phone.

These fixes work for AirPods and for other brands of wireless earbuds on iPhone, Android and Windows. Work through them in order. The first five solve most problems. (If you're having trouble with headphones, speakers or a car system too, see our general guide on [why Bluetooth won't connect](/phones-tablets/bluetooth-wont-connect/).)

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Put both earbuds in the case** and check that the case light comes on when you open the lid.
2. **Open your phone's Bluetooth settings** and hold the open case right next to the phone.
3. **Watch the list of devices.**
    - **They never appear?** The earbuds aren't discoverable. See causes 1, 2, 6 and 7.
    - **They appear but won't connect, or connect then switch away?** See causes 3, 4 and 5.
    - **Only one earbud plays?** Jump to "Only one earbud works" below.
4. **Try them with another phone or computer.** If they won't pair with anything after a reset, the earbuds or case may be faulty.

## 1. Charge the case and both earbuds

Earbuds charge from the case, and the case charges from a cable or charging pad. If either is flat, the buds can't turn on or advertise themselves to your phone. A very low battery can also cause failed pairing before anything shuts off completely.

**Fix:**

- Put both earbuds in the case, close the lid and charge the case for at least 15 to 30 minutes.
- Check the case light. On AirPods, green usually means charged and amber means less than a full charge. Other brands use different colors, so check your manual.
- If the light doesn't come on at all, try a different cable, charger and outlet. If you use a wireless charging pad, try a cable instead.
- On iPhone, you can see battery levels by opening the lid next to the phone (a card pops up) or in the Batteries widget.

## 2. Seat the earbuds properly in the case

The case only charges and controls the buds through small metal contacts. If a bud is tilted, sitting on debris, or held up by a tight case cover, it won't charge and may not wake up or reset.

**Fix:**

- Take each bud out and drop it back in. Most cases use magnets, so a seated bud clicks into place.
- Remove any ear tips or foam covers that don't fit the case properly, and check the wells for lint or grit.
- If you added a third-party protective cover, take it off and test without it.
- Close the lid, wait a few seconds and open it again.

## 3. Turn Bluetooth off and on, then restart

Bluetooth on your phone can get stuck after an update, after sleep or after a lot of switching between devices. Cycling it, and restarting the phone, clears that.

**Fix:**

- **iPhone:** open **Settings > Bluetooth**, turn it off, wait 10 seconds and turn it on. Use Settings rather than Control Center, which only disconnects accessories.
- **Android:** open **Settings** and find **Bluetooth** (often under **Connected devices** or **Connections**). Turn it off and on.
- Put the buds back in the case, close the lid for about 30 seconds, then open it next to the phone.
- If that doesn't help, restart the phone fully and try again.

## 4. Check if they're connected to another device

Most earbuds reconnect to the last device they used. If your tablet, laptop or a family member's phone grabs them first, your phone can't connect. AirPods can also switch automatically between Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account, so they may jump to your iPad or Mac.

**Fix:**

- Turn off Bluetooth on nearby devices that have used the earbuds, or disconnect the earbuds there.
- Some earbuds support **multipoint** (two devices at once). They may already be connected to two other devices. Check the manual.
- For AirPods, if they keep jumping to another Apple device, look in the AirPods settings on your iPhone for the option that controls automatic connecting to that iPhone.
- Then connect from your phone's Bluetooth list.

## 5. Forget the earbuds and pair them again

Your phone keeps a security key for every paired device. If the earbuds were reset, updated or paired with something else, that key may no longer match, and connections fail or never start. Forgetting them and pairing again creates a fresh pairing.

**Fix:**

1. **iPhone:** go to **Settings > Bluetooth**, tap the **info (i)** button next to the earbuds and tap **Forget This Device**, then confirm.
2. **Android:** in the Bluetooth or Connected devices screen, tap the **gear** or the earbuds' name and choose **Forget** or **Unpair**.
3. Put the earbuds in **pairing mode**. For AirPods, open the lid next to the iPhone and follow the setup card. If no card appears, hold the case button until the light flashes white. For other brands, this is usually a long press on the case button or on both buds. Check the manual.
4. Select the earbuds from your phone's Bluetooth list.

## 6. Reset the earbuds

If re-pairing doesn't work, reset the earbuds themselves. This clears their own list of paired devices and settings, so you'll need to pair them again with every device afterward.

**Fix for AirPods:**

1. Put both AirPods in the case and close the lid. Wait about 30 seconds.
2. On your iPhone, forget them (**Settings > Bluetooth**, tap **info (i)**, **Forget This Device**).
3. Open the lid. Press and hold the button on the back of the case for about **15 seconds**, until the status light flashes **amber, then white**.
4. With the lid open, hold the case next to your iPhone and follow the setup steps on screen.

Some newer AirPods cases have no physical button and over-ear models work differently. Check Apple's support site for your exact model.

**Fix for other earbuds:** reset methods vary a lot between brands and models. Common ones are a long press on the case button, or a long press on the touch areas of both buds while they sit in the open case. Check the manual or the maker's app before you try, so you know what the light should do.

## 7. Clean the charging contacts

Earwax, skin oil and pocket lint build up on the metal contacts on the buds and inside the case wells. Dirty contacts stop the buds charging, so one or both may be flat even when the case is full.

**Fix:**

- Wipe the buds with a soft, dry, lint-free cloth, paying attention to the metal contacts.
- Clean the case wells and contacts gently with a **dry cotton swab** or a **soft, clean, dry brush**.
- Don't use sharp objects, and don't get liquid into the case wells, the charging port or the speaker mesh. Check the maker's cleaning advice before using any cleaning solution.
- Seat the buds again and charge the case for 15 to 30 minutes.

## 8. Update your phone and the earbud firmware

Software bugs on either side can cause pairing problems that updates fix.

**Fix:**

- **iPhone:** **Settings > General > Software Update**.
- **Android:** look in **Settings** for **System update** or **Software update** (the location varies by brand).
- **AirPods** update their firmware automatically. Leave them in the case, charging, near your iPhone while it's connected to Wi-Fi.
- **Other earbuds** usually update through the maker's companion app. Install it, connect the buds and check for updates.

## Only one earbud works?

- **Charge both buds.** Put them both in the case, close the lid and check each bud's battery level. A flat bud is the most common cause.
- **Clean the contacts** on the silent bud and in its case well (cause 7), then reseat it.
- **Check the audio balance.** On iPhone, go to **Settings > Accessibility > Audio & Visual** and make sure the **Balance** slider is in the center. Android has a similar balance setting under Accessibility on many phones.
- **Check for blocked mesh.** Earwax can block the speaker opening and make a working bud sound dead. Clean it gently with a dry brush.
- **Reset the earbuds** (cause 6). This often brings back a bud that lost sync with its partner.
- If one bud still won't connect after a reset, it may be faulty. Many makers sell single replacement buds.

## Android and Windows notes

**Android:**

- Many Android phones show a pop-up for supported earbuds when you open the case nearby. If yours doesn't, pair from the Bluetooth screen as usual.
- AirPods work on Android as regular Bluetooth earbuds. Open the lid, hold the case button until the light flashes white, then choose them in the Bluetooth list.
- Install the earbud maker's app if it has one. It often handles pairing, firmware updates and resets.

**Windows 11:**

- Open **Settings > Bluetooth & devices** and make sure Bluetooth is on.
- Choose **Add device**, then **Bluetooth**, with the earbuds in pairing mode next to the PC.
- To remove an old pairing, find the earbuds under **Bluetooth & devices**, open the menu next to them and choose **Remove device**, then pair again.
- If the earbuds connect but play no sound, check that they're selected as the output device in the sound settings.

## When to call a pro

Contact the earbud maker, the store or a repair shop if:

- The case won't charge or show any light with several cables and chargers.
- The earbuds won't pair with **any** phone or computer, even after a reset.
- One bud never charges, even after cleaning the contacts.
- The case or buds got wet, were crushed, or feel hot or swollen. Stop using them and don't charge them.

Earbud batteries can't usually be replaced at home. Check your warranty first, since a faulty case or bud is often covered.
