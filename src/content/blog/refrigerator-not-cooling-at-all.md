When the fresh food section and the freezer are both warm, the problem is not airflow between them. Something has stopped the whole cooling system: the **compressor** is not running, or it runs but the heat has nowhere to go.

The most common causes, simplest first, are **no power, controls set to off or demo mode, dirty condenser coils, a stopped condenser fan, or a failed compressor start relay**. Here's how to find which. If only the fridge is warm and the freezer is still cold, see [refrigerator not cooling but freezer works](/appliances/refrigerator-not-cooling-freezer-works/) instead.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the refrigerator before you remove the kick plate or the back access panel, clean the coils or touch the fan, relay or wiring. Pull it out slowly and get help if it's heavy, so you don't tear the water line or tip it. Never cut, bend or puncture the tubing. Refrigerant work is for a licensed, EPA-certified technician only.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Measure it.** Put an appliance thermometer in the fridge and another in the freezer. After several hours, readings well above 40°F and 0°F confirm the whole system is warm.
2. **Open the door.** Does the inside light come on? If not, start with power (cause 1).
3. **Look at the controls.** Check that cooling isn't set to off and that the display doesn't show a demo, showroom or off message (cause 2).
4. **Listen near the bottom back** with the doors closed.
    - **Silent:** see causes 2, 6 and 8, and the silent section near the end.
    - **Click, short hum, quiet, then another click a few minutes later:** see causes 6 and 7.
    - **Running, but nothing gets cold:** see causes 3, 4, 5 and 9.

## 1. Power, outlet or tripped breaker

If the light is off and the display is blank, the fridge may have no power at all.

**Fix:**

- Check that the plug is pushed fully into the outlet. It can work loose when the fridge is pushed back.
- Plug a lamp or phone charger into the same outlet. If that doesn't work either, check the breaker panel and any GFCI outlet that might feed the kitchen circuit, then see [outlet not working](/electrical/outlet-not-working/).
- Plug the fridge directly into a wall outlet. Most manufacturers say not to use an extension cord or power strip, which can drop the voltage the compressor needs to start.

## 2. Controls off or demo mode

Many refrigerators with digital controls have a **demo or showroom mode** for store displays. The lights, display and sometimes fans work, but the compressor never runs. It can get switched on by accident. Older fridges with a dial can also be turned all the way to off.

**Fix:**

- Set the fridge and freezer controls to the recommended setting. Aim for **37 to 40°F** in the fridge and **0°F** in the freezer.
- Look for a demo, showroom or cooling off message on the display. How to turn it off differs by brand and model, so follow your manual exactly. Don't guess at button combinations.
- After you change anything, give it several hours and check with the thermometer.

## 3. Just plugged in or recently moved

A refrigerator that was just delivered, moved or turned back on will feel warm for a while. Many manufacturers say it can take up to 24 hours to reach the set temperature, and longer if you load it with warm food.

**Fix:**

- Wait the full time your manual gives before you decide something is wrong. Keep the doors closed as much as you can.
- If the fridge was transported lying down, check your manual for how long it should stand upright before you plug it in.
- Leave the clearance your manual asks for around the fridge so heat can escape.

## 4. Dirty condenser coils

The condenser coils release the heat the fridge pulls out of the food. On many models they sit underneath, behind the kick plate at the bottom front. On others they are on the back. When they are coated in dust and pet hair, the compressor can run nonstop and still not keep up, and both sections warm up.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the fridge.**
2. Unclip the kick plate, or pull the fridge out to reach coils on the back.
3. Clean the coils with a long, thin coil brush and a vacuum. Work gently so you don't bend the fins or tubing.
4. Plug it back in and check the temperature after several hours. Cleaning the coils regularly, more often with pets, helps prevent this.

## 5. Condenser fan not running

Fridges with coils underneath usually have a **condenser fan** near the compressor that pulls air through the coils whenever the compressor runs. If it stops, heat builds up and the compressor can overheat and shut itself off.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the fridge** and remove the lower back access panel. Check your manual if you can't find it.
2. Clear any dust or debris jamming the blade.
3. Turn the blade by hand. It should spin freely. A stiff or wobbly fan has usually failed.
4. Put the panel back, plug the fridge in and feel for warm air coming out at the kick plate or back once the compressor is running. No airflow while the compressor hums means the fan motor likely needs replacing.

Fan motors are model-specific, so order by the model number on the label inside the fridge, or have a technician fit it.

## 6. Compressor start relay or overload

The **start relay** plugs onto the side of the compressor and gives it the boost it needs to start. On many models an **overload protector** sits with it and cuts power if the compressor draws too much current or gets too hot. When the relay fails, the compressor can't start, so the overload trips again and again, or the compressor just sits silent.

**Fix:**

- A failed relay is a common reason a fridge stops cooling completely, and it's usually an inexpensive part.
- Relays are matched to the compressor, so order by the fridge's model number. With the fridge unplugged and the maker's instructions, it can be a DIY job. Otherwise, call an appliance technician.
- If a new relay doesn't fix it, the compressor itself may be failing. That is a sealed system repair (cause 9).

## 7. Compressor clicking on and off

A single click when the fridge starts or stops a cooling cycle is normal. A pattern of **click, short hum, click, then quiet for a few minutes** while nothing gets cold is not. It means the compressor is trying to start and failing.

**Fix:**

- Plug the fridge straight into the wall, with no extension cord or power strip (cause 1).
- Clean the condenser coils and check the condenser fan (causes 4 and 5). An overheated compressor can trip its overload too.
- If the clicking continues, the start relay (cause 6) is the usual suspect, followed by a failing compressor. Have a technician test it if you're not sure.

## 8. Control board or temperature control

Newer refrigerators use an electronic **control board** that switches the compressor and fans on and off. Some use a variable speed compressor with its own drive board. Older models use a mechanical temperature control behind the dial. If any of these fails, the light can work while nothing cools.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the fridge for a few minutes, then plug it back in. This can reset a control that has locked up.
- Some models show a code or have a self-test mode. Use your manual or the tech sheet (often tucked behind the kick plate or on the back) to see what your model offers. Don't guess what a code means.
- Testing a board needs the wiring diagram and a multimeter. Most people should leave it to a technician.

## 9. Sealed system leak (pro only)

The compressor, coils and tubing form a **sealed system** that holds the refrigerant. If refrigerant leaks out, or the compressor runs but no longer pumps, the fridge can run nonstop and never get cold. Signs include a compressor that runs constantly with clean coils and a working fan, no frost at all on the freezer coil, and sometimes oily residue near tubing joints.

**Fix:** There is no safe DIY fix. Leave the tubing alone and don't use refrigerant "recharge" kits. Call a licensed appliance or refrigeration technician. On an older fridge, ask for an estimate first, since a sealed system repair can cost close to the price of a new refrigerator.

## Light on but completely silent?

- **It may be defrosting.** Frost-free fridges stop the compressor during a defrost cycle. You may hear sizzling or dripping. Wait an hour or so and listen again.
- **It may have a variable speed compressor.** These can run very quietly. Feel for warm air at the kick plate and trust the thermometer.
- **Recheck demo mode and the controls** (cause 2).
- **Still silent for hours and warm inside?** The start relay, overload, control board or compressor is the likely cause. Unplug it and call a technician.

## Keep your food safe while you work

Move milk, meat, fish and leftovers into coolers with ice as soon as you know the fridge is warm, and keep the doors closed while you work. USDA guidance is to throw out perishable food that has been above 40°F for more than 2 hours.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a qualified appliance technician if:

- The compressor clicks on and off, stays silent for hours, or runs nonstop after you've cleaned the coils and checked the fan.
- A new start relay didn't help, or you're not comfortable replacing one.
- You suspect the control board or see a code you can't look up in the manual.
- You hear hissing, see oily residue on the tubing or smell something unusual.
- You smell burning or see scorch marks at the plug or outlet. Unplug the fridge and leave it unplugged.

Any repair that involves refrigerant or the compressor needs a licensed, EPA-certified technician.
