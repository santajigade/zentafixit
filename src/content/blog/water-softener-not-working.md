A water softener removes calcium and magnesium (the minerals that make water "hard") using a tank of resin beads. Every so often it **regenerates**: it rinses the resin with salty brine from the salt tank, which recharges the beads. When that cycle stops working, hard water comes straight back: spots on dishes, scale on faucets, soap that won't lather.

The usual causes are simple: **the salt ran out**, **a salt bridge is blocking the brine**, **the bypass valve was left open**, or **the clock is wrong after a power outage**. Here's how to find which one.

> **Safety first:** A softener is a low-risk fix, but unplug it before you take off the control cover, and keep the area dry. Salt bags are heavy, so lift with your legs. Never use a metal tool to break up salt, as it can crack the plastic tank.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/water-softener-not-working-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: test a softened tap with a hardness strip, check bypass, salt and clock, then ask whether the salt level drops over a few weeks; if not, check the salt bridge, clock and brine line; if yes, check the bypass, hardness setting and resin" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Whether the salt is being used splits the problem in two.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Test the water.** Use a hardness test strip on a cold tap inside the house. For comparison, test an outdoor spigot too, since those are often plumbed before the softener (not always, so check your pipes).
2. **Look at the control head.** Is the display on, and does it show the right time of day? Note any blinking or error message and look it up in your manual.
3. **Look in the brine tank.** Is there loose salt, and does it sit above the water?
4. **Run a manual regeneration** (most models have a "Regen" or "Recharge" button, see your manual). Test again after it finishes.
    - **Salt level never drops over a few weeks?** Salt isn't being used: check causes 2, 4 and 6.
    - **Salt drops, but the water stays hard?** Check causes 3, 5 and 7.

## 1. The salt is low or empty

This is the most common reason a softener stops working. With no salt, the brine is just water, and the resin can't be recharged. Hard water returns within days.

**Fix:**

- Refill with softener salt. Pellets or evaporated salt crystals are cleaner than rock salt, which leaves more insoluble grit in the tank.
- Keep the tank about a quarter to half full, with salt a few inches above the water line, unless your manual says otherwise.
- Wait a couple of hours for brine to form, then run a manual regeneration.
- If you switch to potassium chloride instead of sodium chloride, check the manual. Some models need a different salt setting.

## 2. A salt bridge or salt mush

A **salt bridge** is a hard crust that forms across the tank, often in humid basements or garages. The tank looks full, but there's an empty gap underneath, so the salt never touches the water. **Salt mush** is the opposite: a thick sludge at the bottom that clogs the brine pickup.

**Fix:**

1. Push a broom handle or wooden dowel gently down through the salt. If it hits a hard layer, you've found the bridge.
2. Tap around the crust to break it into pieces. Don't use metal tools or hammer hard.
3. If there's sludge at the bottom, scoop it out or use a wet/dry vacuum, then rinse the tank and refill.
4. Use good quality pellets, and avoid filling right to the top, to lower the chance it happens again.

## 3. The bypass valve is open

Every softener has a **bypass valve** where it joins the house plumbing. In bypass, water skips the softener completely. It's easy to bump, and plumbers often set it to bypass during other work and forget to switch it back.

**Fix:**

- Find the valve on the pipes behind the control head. It may be a push-through button, a knob or a lever.
- Set it to **service** (sometimes marked "soft" or "in service"). Your manual shows which position is which.
- Some homes have a three-valve loop of separate shutoffs instead. In that case, the inlet and outlet valves should be open and the middle bypass valve closed.
- Open a cold tap for a few minutes, then test again with a strip.

## 4. The clock is wrong after a power outage

Many softeners regenerate in the early morning hours, when nobody uses water. After a power outage, older timer models may stop or show the wrong time, so regeneration happens at the wrong hour or not at all. Some newer models keep the time with a battery or memory backup, but it's worth checking.

**Fix:**

- Set the time of day again (see your manual for the buttons or dial).
- Check the regeneration schedule. On timer models, make sure the days between regenerations match your household's water use.
- If the display stays blank, check that it's plugged in and that the outlet has power. Try another device in the same outlet.
- Run a manual regeneration so you're not waiting for the next scheduled one.

## 5. The hardness setting is too low

Metered (demand) softeners decide when to regenerate based on your water hardness, usually entered in **grains per gallon (gpg)**. If the setting is lower than your real hardness, the resin runs out before the next regeneration, and hard water slips through near the end of each cycle.

**Fix:**

- Find your real hardness: test untreated water with a strip, or ask your water utility for its water quality report. If the result is in mg/L (ppm), divide by 17.1 to get grains per gallon.
- Enter that number in the softener's hardness setting.
- If your water has iron, many manufacturers suggest raising the hardness setting to compensate. Your manual will give the exact rule.
- If more people moved in, you may also need more frequent regeneration.

## 6. A clogged brine line or injector

During regeneration the softener sucks brine out of the salt tank through a small tube and a tiny nozzle called the **injector** (or venturi). If it's blocked by sediment or salt grit, the brine isn't drawn up. Signs include water rising high in the brine tank, or salt that never goes down even with no bridge.

**Fix:**

- Check the brine line between the tanks for kinks or a loose fitting.
- Clean out the bottom of the brine tank if there's sludge (see cause 2).
- The injector usually sits in the control head and can often be removed and rinsed. Follow your manual exactly, and put the softener in bypass and relieve pressure first.
- If you're not comfortable opening the control head, this is a good job for a water treatment technician.

## 7. Worn-out or fouled resin

Resin beads last many years, but not forever. Chlorine in city water slowly breaks them down, and iron or sediment can coat them. Old resin holds less hardness, so the softener regenerates on time but hard water comes back quickly.

**Fix:**

- If causes 1 to 6 all check out and the softener is over 10 years old, worn resin is a strong suspect.
- If you have iron in your water, ask about a resin cleaner made for softeners and use it as the label directs.
- Resin can be replaced. On many units this is cheaper than a new softener, but it means emptying the heavy resin tank. A pro can tell you whether a rebed or a new unit makes more sense.
- If the unit also has failing parts in the control head, replacement may be the better value.

A clogged resin bed or sediment can also cut flow through the house. If you've noticed weak taps as well as hard water, see our guide to [low water pressure](/plumbing/low-water-pressure/).

## Softener won't regenerate at all?

- **No display or power:** check the plug, the outlet and any switch or GFCI that feeds it.
- **Display shows an error:** look the code up in your manual. Don't guess, as codes differ by brand.
- **Motor hums but nothing turns, or it stops partway through a cycle:** the drive motor, gears or valve may be failing. Set the bypass valve to bypass so you still have water, then call for service.
- **Water won't stop running to the drain:** put it in bypass and call a technician. A stuck valve can waste a lot of water.

## When to call a plumber

Call a plumber or water treatment technician if:

- The softener leaks from the tanks, the control head or the connections.
- It runs water to the drain nonstop or gets stuck in a cycle.
- Salt, settings and the bypass are all correct and the water is still hard.
- You want the resin replaced or the control valve rebuilt.
- You're replacing the whole unit and need it sized for your hardness and household.
