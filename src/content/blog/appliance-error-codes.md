Modern appliances watch themselves. When a washer can't drain, a dishwasher can't heat water, or a fridge sensor reads out of range, the control board stores an **error code** and shows it on the display, as **blinking lights** or as a **series of beeps**.

The catch is that codes aren't universal. The same letters can mean different things on two brands, or even two models from the same brand. The way to get the real meaning is always the same: **write down the exact code**, **find your model number**, then check the **manual or the technician's sheet** hidden inside many appliances.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before removing any panel, kick plate or cover. For gas appliances, don't loosen gas fittings. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility from outside. Water and electricity mix badly, so dry up any leak before touching cords or outlets.

## How to look up any error code: quick steps

1. **Record the code** exactly as shown, plus what the appliance was doing.
2. **Find the model number** on the appliance's tag and take a photo of it.
3. **Check the owner's manual** for a troubleshooting or error code table.
4. **Look for the tech sheet** inside the appliance if the manual doesn't list it.
5. **Fix the simple cause** the code points to, then **power cycle** once.

## 1. Write down the exact code

Small details matter. A code can show on a digital display, as a **number of flashes** on an indicator light, or as a **beep pattern**. Some appliances flash two lights together to make a code.

**How:**

- Write it exactly, including letters, numbers and any dash or space. Watch for look-alikes such as the letter O and zero, the letter S and 5, or a lowercase b and 6 on segment displays.
- For blinking lights, count the flashes between pauses, the same way you would for [furnace blinking light codes](/hvac/furnace-blinking-light-codes/). A short phone video helps.
- Note **what was happening**: which cycle or setting, how far into the cycle, and whether you heard noises or saw water.
- Take a photo of the display. It's useful if you end up calling for service.

## 2. Find the model number tag

The model number (and serial number) is on a sticker or metal plate. You need the **full model number**, not just the brand or series name, because codes and parts often differ between versions.

Common places to look:

| Appliance | Where the tag often is |
| --- | --- |
| Top-load washer | Under the lid, on the back of the control panel or on the cabinet back |
| Front-load washer | Inside the door opening or on the frame around the door |
| Dryer | Inside the door opening or on the frame behind the door |
| Refrigerator | On an inside side wall or ceiling of the fresh food section, or behind a crisper drawer |
| Dishwasher | On the edge of the door or the side of the tub opening |
| Range or wall oven | Behind the storage or warming drawer, or on the frame behind the oven door |
| Over-the-range microwave | Inside the door frame or behind the door |
| Water heater | On the rating plate on the side of the tank |

If the tag is worn off, check your purchase receipt, the registration card or the box. Some brands also print the model on the back of the appliance.

## 3. Check the owner's manual

Most owner's manuals have a **troubleshooting** or **error code** section listing the codes a user can deal with.

**How:**

- Look for the paper manual in a kitchen drawer or the original packaging.
- Search the maker's website for your **exact model number**. Most offer free PDF manuals.
- Use the manual for your model, not a similar one. A code from a different model's chart can send you down the wrong path.

Owner's manuals often list only the codes you can fix yourself, such as door not latched, filter clogged or drain blocked. Deeper fault codes may only appear in the tech sheet.

## 4. Look for the tech sheet inside the appliance

Many appliances come with a **technical sheet** (tech sheet or service sheet) meant for repair technicians. It's usually folded into a small plastic bag and tucked somewhere inside the appliance. It can list error codes, a **diagnostic or service mode**, test steps and a wiring diagram.

**Where it's often found:**

- **Refrigerators:** behind the front kick plate or grille at the bottom, or on the back.
- **Ranges and ovens:** under the cooktop, behind the storage drawer or on the back panel.
- **Dishwashers:** behind the lower kick plate or inside the door panel.
- **Washers and dryers:** inside the control console, under the top panel or on the back.

**How:** Unplug the appliance first. Remove only the panels that come off with a screwdriver or a gentle pull, take a photo of the sheet and put it back where you found it. Don't run service or diagnostic modes unless the sheet explains them clearly. Some tests run motors, pumps or heaters directly.

## 5. Reset it with a power cycle

Many codes are stored until the control board restarts. A **power cycle** clears temporary glitches, such as a code from a brief power blip.

**How:**

1. **Unplug** the appliance, or switch off its **breaker** if it's hardwired or you can't reach the plug.
2. **Wait a few minutes.** This lets the control board fully discharge.
3. **Restore power** and start a short cycle while you watch.

Some models also have a **button sequence** to clear codes. It's listed in the manual or tech sheet, so don't guess. For gas water heaters and other gas appliances, follow the reset or relight steps on the appliance label exactly, and don't keep resetting a gas appliance that shuts itself off.

## 6. Fix the cause, not just the code

Codes usually point to a general area. Even without the exact meaning, the categories below come up across many brands:

- **Water supply:** taps closed, kinked hoses, clogged inlet screens.
- **Draining:** blocked filters, kinked drain hoses or clogs. See [washer won't drain](/appliances/washer-wont-drain/).
- **Door or lid lock:** something stopping the door from latching.
- **Temperature sensors:** many appliances use small thermistors whose resistance changes with temperature. A loose connection or failed sensor can trigger a code. (Here's how [NTC and PTC thermistors work](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/ntc-ptc-thermistor-working/).)
- **Heating, motors and control boards:** usually a repair job.

Once you know the meaning from your manual, check the simple causes first: hoses, filters, latches, airflow, power supply.

## 7. If the code keeps coming back

A code that returns after a reset means the fault is still there. Resetting again rarely helps.

**How:**

- Re-check the simple causes from your manual, then try one more power cycle.
- Note when it appears (same point in the cycle every time, or at random). Patterns help a technician.
- Check whether the appliance is **still under warranty**. Opening sealed panels or replacing parts yourself can affect coverage, so check the warranty terms first.
- Contact the **manufacturer's support line** with the model number, serial number and exact code. Some can tell you what it means and whether it's a known issue.
- Check for **recalls** on the manufacturer's site or at the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission site, using your model number.

## Can't find the code anywhere?

- **Double-check the characters.** A misread letter or digit is the most common reason a code doesn't match any chart.
- **Confirm the full model number**, including any suffix letters or numbers at the end.
- **Ask the manufacturer.** Support teams have the full service documentation.
- **Be careful with forums and video sites.** They can be helpful, but a code from a different model may mean something else on yours.

## When to call an appliance repair technician

Call a qualified appliance technician (or the manufacturer's service) if:

- The code points to heating elements, motors, control boards or sealed refrigeration parts.
- The code returns after you've fixed the simple causes and power cycled it.
- You see water leaking, smell burning, or the breaker trips.
- It's a gas appliance and the code relates to ignition, flame or gas valves.
- The appliance is under warranty and the fix needs panels removed.

Give them the **model number**, the **exact code** and **when it appears**. It helps them bring the right part on the first visit.
