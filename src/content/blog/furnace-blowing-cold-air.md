When a furnace blows cold air, the blower is running but the burners are **not lit, or not staying lit**. Sometimes nothing is wrong at all: the fan is set to run all the time, or you're feeling the cool air at the very start of a cycle.

The most common real causes are **the thermostat fan set to ON, a clogged air filter, a dirty flame sensor, a closed gas valve and, on high-efficiency furnaces, a blocked condensate drain**. Start with the free checks and work down.

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas (a rotten egg smell), don't touch any switches. Get everyone out of the house, then call your gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. Turn off the furnace power switch before opening any panel. Never bypass or jump a safety switch, and keep working carbon monoxide alarms in the home.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the thermostat.** Mode on HEAT, set point 3 to 5°F above room temperature, fan on AUTO.
2. **Time the cold air.** Hold your hand at a vent for a few minutes after the heat kicks on. Does it turn warm, start warm and then go cold, or stay cold?
3. **Look through the window in the lower door** (or the burner viewport if your furnace has one). Do you see a steady flame, a flame that lights and goes out, or nothing?
4. **Note any blinking status light** and compare it to the chart on the inside of the furnace door.
5. **Sort it out:**
    - **Cool only between cycles:** cause 1.
    - **Cool for a minute, then warm:** cause 2.
    - **Warm, then cold:** cause 3.
    - **Never warm, no steady flame:** causes 4 to 7.
    - **Warm at the furnace, cold in one room:** cause 8.

## 1. Thermostat fan is set to ON instead of AUTO

On **ON**, the blower runs nonstop, even when the burners are off. Between heating cycles it pushes room-temperature air through the ducts, which feels cool on your skin. On **AUTO**, the blower runs only while the furnace is heating.

**Fix:**

- Set the fan to **AUTO**.
- Confirm the mode is **HEAT**, not COOL, OFF or a schedule that is holding a low temperature.
- If you just installed a new thermostat, check that it is set up for a gas furnace, not a heat pump or electric heat. The wrong system type can make the fan run without the burners. Check the thermostat's installation guide.

## 2. Cool air at the start or end of a cycle (normal)

A furnace starts in steps: the inducer fan clears the burner area, the burners light, and the blower starts once the heat exchanger has warmed up. Air sitting in cold ductwork, especially in an attic or crawlspace, can still come out cool for a short time. At the end of a cycle the burners shut off first and the blower runs a little longer to pull out the leftover heat, so the air cools down before it stops.

**Fix:**

- Nothing to fix if the air turns warm within a minute or two and the house reaches the set temperature.
- If it never warms up, move on to the causes below.

## 3. Dirty air filter tripping the high limit

A clogged filter cuts airflow. Heat builds up in the furnace, and a **high-limit switch** shuts the burners off to protect the heat exchanger. The blower keeps running to cool things down, so you get warm air that turns **cold after a few minutes**. After repeated trips, some furnaces lock out.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the furnace power switch.
2. Slide out the filter and note the size printed on the frame.
3. Replace it if it's grey or clogged, with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
4. Make sure supply vents and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
5. Turn the power back on. Check the filter monthly during heating season.

If it still overheats with a clean filter and open vents, a technician should check the blower and the limit switch. Never bypass a limit switch.

## 4. Ignition or flame sensor problem

Modern furnaces light the burners with a **hot surface igniter** or a spark. A thin metal rod called the **flame sensor** then confirms there's a flame. If the sensor is coated with residue, the burners light and **go out within seconds**. The furnace retries a few times, then may lock out, while any air moving through the ducts stays cool.

**Fix:**

- Reset the power once and watch. If you see the flame light and drop out, the flame sensor is the usual suspect.
- Many homeowners clean it: power and gas off, remove the sensor (usually one screw), lightly rub the rod with a fine abrasive pad or emery cloth, wipe it and refit it.
- If the igniter never glows or sparks, it may be cracked or failed. That's a job for a technician.
- Not comfortable working near the burners and gas valve? Call a pro. Our guide to a [furnace not turning on](/hvac/furnace-not-turning-on/) covers ignition lockouts in more detail.

## 5. Pilot light is out (older furnaces)

Older furnaces often have a **standing pilot**: a small flame that stays lit all the time and lights the burners. If it goes out, the blower can still run but the burners never light.

**Fix:**

- Look through the viewport for the small pilot flame.
- If it's out, follow the **relighting instructions on the label** inside the furnace door exactly. Don't guess the steps.
- If it won't stay lit, the thermocouple or pilot assembly may need attention. Call a technician.
- If you smell gas at any point, stop and follow the safety steps above.

## 6. Gas supply is off

If the inducer runs and the igniter glows or clicks but there's no flame, the furnace may not be getting gas.

**Fix:**

- Find the **gas shutoff valve** on the pipe to the furnace. The handle is **in line with the pipe when open** and crosswise when closed.
- Some furnaces also have an ON/OFF control on the gas valve inside the cabinet. Check your manual or the door label.
- Check another gas appliance. If the stove or water heater won't light either, contact your gas company.
- On **propane**, check the tank gauge.

## 7. Condensate drain or pressure switch (high-efficiency)

**High-efficiency furnaces** (usually with white plastic vent pipes) make water that drains through a **trap and hose**. If the drain clogs, water backs up, a **pressure switch** can't close, and the furnace won't light. A blocked intake or exhaust pipe, for example from snow or a nest, can cause the same thing.

**Fix:**

- Power off. Look for water in or under the cabinet, and for sludge or a kink in the trap and hose.
- Many traps can be removed and rinsed. Check your manual first.
- If a **condensate pump** is used, make sure it's plugged in and draining.
- Clear the outside end of the drain line with a wet/dry vacuum if you can reach it.
- Check that the outdoor intake and exhaust pipes are clear of snow, ice and debris.

## 8. Leaky or disconnected ductwork

If the air is warm near the furnace but cold at a far room, the heat may be escaping before it gets there. Ducts in an **attic, garage or crawlspace** can leak, come apart at a joint, or get crushed. A loose return duct can also pull in cold air from those spaces.

**Fix:**

- Check that the vents in the cold room are open.
- Look at the ducts you can safely reach for gaps, loose joints or a section that has fallen off.
- Seal small gaps at joints with foil tape or duct mastic, not cloth duct tape.
- For a disconnected or crushed run, or ducts in a tight attic, call an HVAC technician.

## 9. You have a heat pump in defrost

Some homes have a **heat pump** with a furnace as backup, often called a dual fuel system. If an outdoor unit runs in winter, a heat pump is doing some of the heating. In cold, damp weather it briefly runs a **defrost cycle**, and the air can feel cooler for a few minutes.

**Fix:** If this sounds like your system, see our guide to a [heat pump blowing cold air](/hvac/heat-pump-blowing-cold-air/). It covers defrost, aux heat and thermostat settings for heat pumps.

## Blowing cold air all the time?

- **Fan on AUTO and still cold nonstop:** reset the power once. If it's cold again, look at the status light and match the blink pattern to the chart inside the door.
- **Blower runs but the furnace never tries to light:** check the gas valve, the condensate drain and any blinking code.
- **Locks out again after a reset:** don't keep resetting it. Repeated failed starts point to a real fault such as the flame sensor, igniter, venting or a limit switch.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed technician if:

- You **smell gas** (after leaving the house and calling the gas company from outside).
- Your **carbon monoxide alarm** goes off. Get out and call 911.
- The burners light and go out after you've cleaned the flame sensor, or the igniter never glows.
- It keeps tripping the high limit with a clean filter and open vents.
- The pilot won't stay lit, or the same error code comes back.
- You see soot, scorch marks or rust around the burners or vent pipe.

An annual tune-up before winter keeps the flame sensor clean, checks the venting and drain, and catches weak parts before they leave you with cold air on the first cold night.
