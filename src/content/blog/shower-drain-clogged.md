A clogged shower drain is almost always **hair tangled with soap scum**, caught just below the drain cover. The water drains slower and slower until you're standing in it. Shampoo, conditioner, body oils and hard water minerals make the clog sticky, so it grabs more hair each time.

The good news: most shower clogs sit within the first few inches of the drain, so you can usually clear them yourself without chemicals. The usual fixes are **pulling out the hair at the strainer**, **plunging**, and **using a small drain snake**.

## How to tell where the clog is: quick test

1. **Check other drains.** Run the bathroom sink and flush the toilet. If only the shower is slow, the clog is in the shower drain. If several are slow, skip to the section on several drains.
2. **Look under the cover.** Use a flashlight. Hair visible right below the strainer means a simple fix.
3. **Watch how it drains.** Slow but steady usually means a partial hair clog. Not draining at all, or gurgling, means the clog is tighter or further down.
4. **Notice when it started.** A clog right after a remodel or a dropped object (cap, razor cover) may need the object fished out, not snaked.

## 1. Pull out the hair at the strainer

This is the fix that works most often.

**Fix:**

1. Put on rubber gloves. Remove the drain cover: some unscrew with a screwdriver in the middle or at the edge, some lift or pry off, and some twist out.
2. Pull out any hair you can see with your fingers or needle-nose pliers.
3. Push a **plastic barbed drain strip** down the drain, twist it, and pull it up slowly. Repeat until it comes up clean. They're inexpensive at hardware stores.
4. Scrub the cover and the drain opening with an old toothbrush.
5. Run very hot tap water for a minute to flush loose soap scum.

## 2. Plunge the drain

A plunger pushes and pulls water to loosen a clog that's further down. Use a cup plunger (the flat kind), not a flanged toilet plunger.

**Fix:**

- Remove the drain cover first.
- If it's a tub and shower combo, **block the overflow opening** (the round plate under the faucet) with a wet rag held firmly. Otherwise the pressure escapes through it.
- Run an inch or two of water in the shower so the plunger cup is covered and seals.
- Plunge firmly 10 to 15 times, keeping the seal, then pull the plunger off quickly.
- Repeat a few times. If water starts draining, follow with hot tap water.

## 3. Hot water and dish soap (not boiling)

Hot water and dish soap can soften greasy soap scum that's holding a clog together. It won't remove hair on its own, so use it after step 1.

**Fix:**

- Squeeze a good squirt of dish soap into the drain, then pour in a few quarts of very hot tap water.
- Skip boiling water. It can soften PVC drain pipes and joints, and it can damage acrylic, fiberglass or porcelain surfaces.
- Baking soda followed by vinegar fizzes and may loosen light buildup, but don't expect it to clear a solid clog.

## 4. Clean the tub stopper and linkage

Tub and shower combos often have a stopper that catches hair. There are a few common types:

- **Toe-touch or push-pull stoppers** usually unscrew from the top. Some have a small set screw on the knob.
- **Lift-and-turn stoppers** usually have a small set screw on the knob, or they unscrew from the drain.
- **Trip lever stoppers** are opened with a lever on the overflow plate. Unscrew the two screws on the overflow plate and pull out the linkage. Hair often wraps around the spring or plunger at the bottom.

**Fix:**

- Remove the stopper or linkage, pull off the hair, and scrub off the soap scum.
- While it's out, use the barbed strip or snake in the open drain.
- Reinstall it and test that it still opens and closes. If parts are worn or broken, take the old one to a hardware store to match it. Check the faucet maker's instructions if you're not sure how yours comes apart.

## 5. Use a hand drain snake

A hand drain snake (also called an auger) is a thin coiled cable on a crank. It reaches clogs past the reach of a barbed strip.

**Fix:**

1. Remove the drain cover or stopper. On a tub, you may get a smoother path by feeding the snake through the overflow opening.
2. Feed the cable slowly into the drain until you feel resistance.
3. Lock the cable and turn the crank to grab or break up the clog. Don't force it hard around tight bends.
4. Pull the cable back out slowly, wiping it with a rag as it comes. Expect hair and gunk on the end.
5. Run hot tap water to test. Repeat if it's still slow.

Avoid powered drum augers unless you know how to use one. They can kink and damage the drain if they're forced.

## 6. Soap scum and buildup in the pipe

If the hair is gone but the drain is still slow, or it smells, soap scum and minerals may be coating the pipe or the P-trap. Gurgling can also mean the drain's vent is partly blocked.

**Fix:**

- Flush the drain with very hot tap water and dish soap once a week for a few weeks.
- An enzyme drain cleaner, used as the label says, can help with slow organic buildup over time. It works slowly, so it's maintenance, not a quick fix.
- If the drain keeps gurgling, have a plumber check the vent. A blocked vent makes drains slow and can pull water out of the trap, which lets sewer smell in.

## 7. Fit a hair catcher to stop it coming back

Once the drain runs freely, keep it that way.

**Fix:**

- Fit a **hair catcher** that sits over or inside the drain. Pick one sized to your drain opening.
- Empty it after every few showers.
- Brush long hair before you shower, and rinse pet hair into the trash rather than the drain when washing pets.
- Pull the hair from the strainer every month or two.

## Several drains backing up?

If the shower, the tub, the toilet and the sinks are all slow, or water rises in the shower when you flush the toilet or run the washing machine, the clog is in the **main drain or sewer line**, not the shower. The shower is often one of the lowest drains in the house, so wastewater backs up there first.

- Stop using water in the house as much as you can.
- Don't use chemical drain cleaners. They won't reach the clog and will sit in your pipes.
- Call a plumber. A main line clog usually needs a professional machine at a cleanout, and sometimes a camera inspection to find roots or a broken pipe.

Low flow from the showerhead is a different problem. See [low water pressure in the shower](/plumbing/low-water-pressure-shower/). For sinks, see [clogged sink drain](/plumbing/clogged-sink-drain/).

## When to call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber if:

- Several drains are slow, or water backs up into the shower or tub.
- You smell sewage, or gurgling continues after the clog is gone.
- The snake won't pass a point, or you think something solid fell into the drain.
- You see water leaking through a ceiling below the bathroom.
- The clog keeps coming back within weeks.

Most shower clogs are a 15 minute job with gloves and a plastic drain strip. If that fails, a plumber can clear and inspect the line, and you'll know whether there's a bigger problem.
