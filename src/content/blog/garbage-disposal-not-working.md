A garbage disposal that stops working usually does one of three things: it **hums without spinning**, it **does nothing at all**, or it **runs but leaves water in the sink**. Each points to a different part of the system, so the sound it makes is your best clue.

The most common causes are a **jammed flywheel**, a **tripped reset button**, **no power at the switch or breaker**, and a **clog in the drain below**. Almost all of them can be fixed with a hex key, a flashlight and a bit of patience.

> **Safety first:** Never put your hand inside a disposal, even when it's switched off. Before you reach into the sink opening or work underneath, turn off the wall switch **and** unplug the unit or turn off its breaker, so nobody can start it by accident. Use tongs or pliers, not fingers, and keep a flashlight handy. Don't pour chemical drain cleaners into a disposal.

## How to tell what's wrong: quick test

1. **Run cold water** into the sink and flip the disposal switch on for just a second or two.
2. **Listen.** A hum with no grinding means a jam (cause 1). Total silence means a power or reset problem (causes 2 and 3).
3. **If it runs,** watch the water. Water that sits in the sink points to a clog below (causes 5 and 6). Rattling or clanking means something hard is inside (cause 4).
4. **Switch it off** and cut power at the plug or breaker before you try any fix below.

## 1. Hums but won't spin: a jam

A hum means the motor has power but can't turn. Something is wedged between the spinning flywheel and the grind ring, often a bone, fruit pit, bottle cap or a packed wad of fibrous food. Don't leave it humming. The motor heats up fast and will trip its overload.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the switch off and **unplug the unit or turn off its breaker**.
2. Look under the disposal for a **hex socket in the center of the bottom**. Many units take a 1/4 inch hex key, and some come with a wrench made for it. Check your manual if it doesn't fit.
3. Insert the key and **work it back and forth** until it turns a full circle freely. That breaks the jam loose.
4. Use a flashlight and tongs to **pull out whatever caused the jam** from the top.
5. Wait a few minutes, press the **reset button** (cause 2), restore power and test with cold water running.

If your unit has no hex socket, check the manual. Some describe freeing the flywheel from above with a wooden handle, with the power off.

## 2. The reset button has tripped

Disposals have a thermal overload that shuts the motor off when it overheats, usually after a jam or a long run. When it trips, the unit goes completely silent, as if it had no power.

**Fix:**

- Find the **reset button** on the bottom of the unit or low on its side. It's often red.
- Let the motor **cool for several minutes**, then press the button firmly. You may feel a click.
- If it won't stay in, or trips again right away, there's probably still a jam. Go back to cause 1 and spin the flywheel by hand.

## 3. No power at the switch, outlet or breaker

If reset doesn't help, the disposal may not be getting power at all.

**Fix:**

- **Plug-in units:** check that the plug is fully seated in the outlet under the sink. Try a lamp in that outlet to see if it's live.
- **Breaker:** look for a tripped breaker in your panel. Push it fully to OFF, then back to ON. Some kitchens also have a **GFCI outlet** nearby that feeds the disposal, so check for a tripped one and press RESET.
- **Wall switch:** a worn switch can fail. If the outlet is live but the disposal only works when the switch is jiggled, have the switch replaced.
- **Air switch:** some sinks have a push button on the countertop that works the disposal by air pressure. Check that its small tube is still attached to the control box under the sink.
- **Batch-feed models** only run when the stopper is set in place and turned to the on position. Check the manual for yours.

If the breaker trips again each time you start the disposal, stop resetting it and see the "Still dead" section below.

## 4. Something hard is stuck inside

A loud rattle, clank or grinding sound usually means metal or glass has fallen in: a spoon, a bottle cap, a twist tie or a piece of a broken glass.

**Fix:**

1. Switch off and **unplug the unit or turn off its breaker**.
2. Shine a flashlight into the opening and **remove the object with tongs or long pliers**. Never use your hand.
3. If you can't reach it, turn the hex socket underneath a little to move it into view.
4. If it still rattles once the chamber is empty, a loose mounting ring or a broken part inside may be the cause. Check that the unit is tight on its mount (see the diagram below).

## 5. It runs but the sink won't drain

If the disposal spins normally but water stands in the sink, the clog is almost always in the **discharge tube, P-trap or drain line** below it. Overloading the disposal with peels, rice, pasta or coffee grounds is a common cause.

**Fix:**

1. Run the disposal with **cold water** for a short time. Sometimes that clears a soft blockage.
2. Plunge with a **flat sink plunger**. On a double sink, plug or hold a wet rag over the other drain so the pressure goes down, not up.
3. Put a bucket under the **P-trap**, loosen its slip nuts by hand and clean it out.
4. If the trap is clear, run a **hand snake** through the trap arm into the wall pipe, not through the disposal.

Our [clogged sink drain guide](/plumbing/clogged-sink-drain/) covers the trap and snake steps in more detail.

**Skip the chemicals.** Caustic drain cleaners can harm the disposal's seals and metal parts. If they don't clear the clog, you're left with a sink full of water that can burn your skin and eyes when you plunge or open the trap.

## 6. The dishwasher drain or knockout plug

Most dishwashers drain into the disposal through a small inlet on its side. The two are linked, so a problem in one can show up in the other.

**Fix:**

- **New disposal?** It came with a plastic **knockout plug** sealing the dishwasher inlet. If it wasn't removed during installation, the dishwasher can't drain. With power off, tap it out through the inlet, then fish the loose plug out of the chamber with tongs.
- **Dishwasher water backing into the sink** usually means the disposal or drain below is clogged. Clear it first (cause 5).
- Check that the dishwasher drain hose isn't kinked where it connects to the disposal.

If the dishwasher still won't drain, see our [dishwasher not draining guide](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/).

## 7. It leaks under the sink

Where the water comes from tells you what has failed. Dry the unit with a towel, run some water, and watch with a flashlight.

**Fix:**

- **Top, at the sink flange:** the seal between the sink and the disposal has failed or the mount has loosened. Tightening the mounting ring may help. Otherwise the flange needs resealing with plumber's putty, which means removing the unit.
- **Side, at the dishwasher hose or discharge tube:** tighten the hose clamp or the discharge tube bolts. Replace a worn gasket if needed.
- **Bottom of the body:** this usually means an internal seal has failed. It generally can't be repaired, and the disposal needs replacing. Unplug it until then.

## 8. Bad smell or slow grinding

Food residue builds up on the rubber splash guard and inside the chamber, causing odors. Slow grinding usually means it's being overloaded, it's not getting enough water, or the grind ring is worn on an older unit.

**Fix:**

- With power off, lift or peel back the **splash guard** and scrub its underside with an old toothbrush and dish soap.
- Grind a handful of **ice cubes** with cold water to knock residue off the walls.
- Feed food in **small amounts** with plenty of cold water, and keep the water running a few seconds after the grinding stops.
- Keep **grease, fibrous peels, bones and coffee grounds** out of the disposal.
- If an older unit still grinds poorly after cleaning, it may be worn out.

## Still dead after all of this?

If the flywheel turns freely, the reset button is in, and the outlet or circuit has power, but the motor stays silent or only hums, the motor or its internal switch has likely failed. A disposal that **trips the breaker every time it starts**, even with nothing jammed, may have a shorted motor or wiring fault. Stop resetting the breaker and have the unit and circuit checked.

In most cases a failed motor means replacing the disposal. New units commonly cost from about $100 to $400 or more depending on size and features, plus labor if a plumber installs it. Check that a new unit fits your sink mount and matches the horsepower you need.

## When to call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber if:

- The disposal leaks from the bottom, or the flange keeps leaking after you tighten the mount.
- The sink won't drain after you clean the trap and snake the trap arm, or several drains are slow at once.
- You need the unit replaced and aren't comfortable disconnecting the plumbing.

Call an electrician if the breaker keeps tripping, the switch or outlet is warm, you smell burning, or the unit is hardwired and you aren't comfortable working on it with the power off.
