No hot water is a problem you notice fast, usually in the shower. Luckily, many causes are simple: a tripped breaker, a pilot light that went out or a setting that got bumped.

What to check depends on your heater. On **electric** tanks, start with **the breaker, the high-limit reset button and the heating elements**. On **gas** tanks, check **the pilot or igniter, the gas valve and the thermocouple**. On **tankless** units, start with **the error code and the water flow**.

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas (a rotten egg smell), leave the house now. Don't touch switches or use a phone inside. Call your gas company or 911 from outside. Electric water heaters run on 240 V, so turn off the breaker and confirm power is off with a non-contact voltage tester before removing any access panel. Tank water is hot enough to scald, and the drain valve and pipes can be hot too.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Find out which type you have.** A gas tank has a gas pipe, a flue on top and a control knob near the bottom. An electric tank has a thick cable at the top and one or two access panels on the side, with no flue. A tankless unit is a box on the wall.
2. **Check whether it's all taps or one.** If one faucet or shower has no hot water but others do, the heater is fine. Skip to "Hot water at some taps but not others?"
3. **Electric:** check the breaker panel for a tripped double-pole breaker (causes 1 to 3).
4. **Gas:** look through the small viewing window near the bottom of the tank for the pilot flame, or check the control for a status light (causes 4 and 5).
5. **Tankless:** read the display or blinking light for an error code (cause 6).

## 1. Tripped breaker (electric)

Electric water heaters use a 240 V double-pole breaker. If it trips, the heater gets no power and the tank slowly cools.

**Fix:**

- Find the water heater breaker in the panel. Push it fully to OFF, then back to ON.
- If it trips again right away or within a short time, don't keep resetting it. A shorted element or a wiring fault is likely. See [why a breaker keeps tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/) and call an electrician or plumber.

## 2. High-limit reset button tripped (electric)

Electric tanks have a **high-limit switch** (often called the ECO) that cuts power if the water gets too hot. It has a red reset button, usually behind the upper access panel.

Always turn the breaker off before opening it.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

1. **Turn off the breaker** for the water heater.
2. Remove the upper access panel screws and the panel. Fold back the insulation and remove the plastic safety cover if there is one. Don't touch any wires.
3. Confirm power is off with a non-contact voltage tester.
4. Press the red reset button firmly. You may feel or hear a click.
5. Replace the cover, insulation and panel, then turn the breaker back on. Give the tank an hour or more to heat.

If the button trips again, something is overheating the water, often a stuck thermostat or a shorted element. That needs a repair, not repeated resetting.

## 3. Failed heating element (electric)

Most home electric tanks have **two heating elements**. If the upper one fails, you usually get no hot water at all. If the lower one fails, you get a small amount of hot water that runs out quickly.

**Fix:**

- With the breaker off and power confirmed off, an element can be tested for continuity or resistance with a multimeter after disconnecting its wires. An open reading (no continuity) means it has failed. If you're not confident testing 240 V parts, call a pro.
- Replacing an element means draining the tank or lowering the water level. Never turn the power on until the tank is completely full again. An element powered with no water around it will burn out in seconds.
- Lukewarm water that runs out fast can also come from a broken dip tube, which lets cold water mix with the hot at the top of the tank. A plumber can check it.

## 4. Pilot out or igniter problem (gas)

Older and many standard gas tanks use a small **standing pilot flame** that lights the main burner. If it goes out, the burner can't light. Newer models may use an electronic igniter instead, which needs 120 V power from an outlet or plug.

**Fix:**

- Find the **lighting instructions label** on the tank. Follow it exactly. It usually tells you to turn the control to OFF, wait a set time for gas to clear, then turn it to PILOT, hold it down and press the igniter.
- Many newer gas heaters have a sealed combustion chamber. Don't try to light them with a match unless the label says to.
- On electronic ignition models, make sure the unit is plugged in and the outlet works. Check for a blinking status light and look up its meaning in the manual.
- If the pilot keeps going out, see cause 5.

## 5. Gas valve, gas supply or thermocouple (gas)

The burner won't run if the gas isn't reaching it. Check the simple things first: a control knob turned to PILOT or VACATION, or a closed gas shut-off valve on the supply pipe.

On standing pilot heaters, a **thermocouple** sits in the pilot flame. It produces a small voltage when hot, which holds the gas valve open. If the pilot lights but goes out as soon as you release the knob, the thermocouple is a common cause. (For how thermocouples sense heat, see this [thermistor vs thermocouple explainer](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/thermistor-vs-thermocouple/).)

**Fix:**

- Turn the control knob to ON (not PILOT or VACATION) and set the temperature dial.
- Check the shut-off valve on the gas line to the heater. On a typical lever valve, the handle is in line with the pipe when open.
- If other gas appliances also aren't working, call your gas company.
- A worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot assembly or a faulty gas control valve should be checked by a plumber or gas technician. Don't take apart gas fittings yourself.

## 6. Tankless heater error code or low flow

Tankless heaters only fire when enough water flows through them. They also show an **error code** when something is wrong, from ignition and venting faults to overheating.

**Fix:**

- Look up the exact code in your owner's manual or on the maker's website. Codes differ between brands, so don't guess.
- Make sure the unit has power. Most gas tankless heaters also need 120 V for their controls and fan.
- Open the hot tap wider. A very low flow, like a slow trickle, may not be enough to turn the heater on.
- Clean the inlet water filter screen, if your model has one. Your manual shows where it is.
- In hard water areas, scale builds up inside. Most manufacturers recommend descaling (flushing) on a regular schedule. Many units show a code or warning when it's due.
- Lukewarm with several taps running? You may be asking for more than the unit can make at once.

## 7. Sediment buildup

Minerals in your water settle at the bottom of a tank over time. Sediment takes up space, insulates the water from the gas burner or lower element, and can cause **rumbling or popping** sounds. You may get less hot water, or it may run out sooner.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the power (electric) or set the gas control to PILOT or VACATION (gas).
2. Turn off the cold water supply valve to the heater.
3. Attach a garden hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a floor drain or outside.
4. Open a hot tap in the house to let air in, then open the drain valve. Let the water run until it comes out clear.
5. Close the drain valve, remove the hose, open the cold supply and let the tank fill completely. Run a hot tap until water flows without air.
6. Turn the power or gas back on.

Follow your manual's steps if they differ. Older plastic drain valves can leak after being opened, so keep a cap or bucket handy.

## 8. Thermostat set too low

If the water is warm but not hot, the thermostat may be set too low or may have been bumped.

**Fix:**

- **Gas:** turn the dial on the control valve up a little. Many dials use words or letters rather than degrees.
- **Electric:** the thermostats are behind the access panels. Turn off the breaker before adjusting them, and set the upper and lower thermostats to the same setting.
- **120°F** is a commonly recommended setting. It's hot enough for most homes and lowers the risk of scalding. Wait a few hours, then check the temperature at a tap with a thermometer.
- If you often run out, your tank may be too small for your household. A bigger tank or a different heater type may help. To estimate how long your tank takes to reheat, try this [water heater time calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/other/water-heater-time/).

## Hot water at some taps but not others?

If the kitchen has hot water but one shower doesn't, the water heater is working. The problem is at that fixture.

- **Single-handle shower valves** have an anti-scald limit stop or cartridge. If it's set too low or the cartridge is worn, the shower won't get hot.
- **Faucets** can have clogged aerators or cartridges. Clean or replace them.

## When to call a plumber

Call a licensed plumber, or your gas company where noted, if:

- You smell gas at any time (gas company or 911 first, from outside).
- The breaker or reset button keeps tripping.
- The pilot won't stay lit after relighting.
- Water is leaking from the tank itself, not just a fitting.
- The temperature and pressure relief valve is discharging water.
- The tank is rusty, very old or the water looks rusty from the hot tap only.
