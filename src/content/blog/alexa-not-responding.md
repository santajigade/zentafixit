When Alexa stops responding, the Echo is rarely broken. Something is usually stopping it from **hearing you**, **reaching the internet**, or **playing its answer** where you can hear it.

The most common causes are **the microphone switched off (red light)**, **Wi-Fi or internet trouble**, **a changed wake word**, **background noise or poor placement**, and **volume or Bluetooth output problems**. These fixes work for most Echo speakers and smart displays.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Say the wake word clearly from a few feet away** in a quiet room and watch the light.
    - **Red light already on?** The microphone is off (fix 1).
    - **No light at all?** It isn't hearing the wake word (fixes 3 and 4).
    - **Blue light, then nothing?** It heard you but can't answer (fixes 2 and 6).
2. **Ask something simple**, like "what time is it?" If that works but "turn on the lamp" doesn't, the speaker is fine and the problem is with the smart device or its link (fix 7).
3. **Check other devices.** If your phone and laptop have no internet on Wi-Fi, fix the router first.
4. **Try another Echo** if you have one. If every Echo in the house stops at once, it's your internet or, rarely, an Amazon service outage.

## 1. Turn the microphone back on

Every Echo has a **microphone off button**, and it's easy to press by accident while dusting or adjusting volume. When the mic is off, the light ring or light bar shows **solid red** and Alexa can't hear anything.

**Fix:**

- Press the microphone button once. The red light should go out.
- Say the wake word to test.
- On Echo smart displays with a camera, check the separate camera or privacy switch too. Some models combine the mic and camera into one switch. Check your manual if you're not sure.

## 2. Check Wi-Fi and the internet

Alexa needs a working internet connection for almost everything. If the Echo hears you (light turns blue) but never answers, or says it's having trouble connecting, the link to your router or the internet is the likely problem. An **orange** light ring usually means the Echo is in setup mode or trying to connect.

**Fix:**

1. Check your internet on another device connected to the same Wi-Fi.
2. Restart the router: unplug it (and the modem, if separate) for about 30 seconds, plug the modem in first, wait until it's online, then the router.
3. In the Alexa app, open your Echo's device settings and check its Wi-Fi status. If you changed your router or password, update the Wi-Fi network there.
4. If the Echo is far from the router, move it closer to test. A weak signal causes slow or missing answers.

If your devices show Wi-Fi bars but nothing loads, see our guide to [Wi-Fi connected but no internet](/networking/wifi-connected-no-internet/).

## 3. Check the wake word

You can change the wake word in the Alexa app, and anyone in the house can do it. If someone switched it to a different word, the Echo will ignore "Alexa". Each Echo also has its own wake word setting.

**Fix:**

- Try the other common wake words, such as "Echo", "Amazon" or "Computer".
- In the Alexa app, open the device's settings and look for **Wake Word**. Set it back to the one you use.
- If the TV or a family member's name sounds like the wake word, picking a different one can also stop false triggers.

## 4. Move it away from noise and walls

Echo speakers use several microphones to pick out your voice. Noise from a TV, fan, air purifier, dishwasher or running water, or a speaker pushed into a corner or shelf, makes that much harder.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/alexa-not-responding-placement.svg" alt="Top view of a room comparing a poor speaker spot, in a corner next to a TV and a fan, with a better spot in the open, away from walls and noise sources, with a clear path to where people talk" width="640" height="360" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>An open spot away from noise helps the microphones hear you.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

- Place the Echo in the open, away from walls, corners and the inside of cabinets.
- Keep it away from TVs, speakers, fans and appliances that run for long periods.
- Don't cover the top of the speaker, where the microphones usually are. Wipe off dust with a dry cloth.
- Turn down the music or TV and try again. If it works when the room is quiet, noise was the problem.

## 5. Restart the Echo

A restart clears temporary software glitches, and it's safe: your settings stay saved.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the Echo's power adapter from the wall or from the device.
2. Wait about 30 seconds.
3. Plug it back in and wait until the light ring turns off or shows the normal idle state.
4. Try the wake word again.

## 6. Check the volume and Bluetooth output

Sometimes Alexa is answering, but you can't hear it. The volume may be turned all the way down, or the Echo may be sending its audio to a Bluetooth speaker or another device in another room.

**Fix:**

- Press the volume up button several times. On models with a light ring, the ring shows the volume level as you change it.
- In the Alexa app, check whether the Echo is paired with a Bluetooth speaker or headphones. Disconnect it and test again.
- Check whether the Echo is part of a speaker group or has a preferred speaker set, which can send music to another device.
- If you have several Echos, a different one may be answering. Alexa usually lets the closest one respond, which can be in another room.

## 7. Check the Alexa app and linked devices

If Alexa answers questions but says a device "isn't responding" or can't find it, the speaker works. The problem is the smart device, its own app, or the link between that app and Alexa.

**Fix:**

- Check that the smart device works in its own app. If it doesn't, fix it there first.
- In the Alexa app, look at the device list and see if the device shows as offline or unresponsive.
- Disable and re-enable the device maker's skill in the Alexa app, then ask Alexa to discover devices.
- If you renamed a device, use the new name, and avoid names that sound alike.
- For routines that stopped running, see [smart home routines not working](/smart-home/smart-home-routines-not-working/).

## 8. Update the software and check the power adapter

Echo devices update themselves over Wi-Fi, usually when they're idle. An Echo that has been offline may miss an update that fixes bugs. A low-power phone charger used instead of the original adapter can also cause random restarts and missed wake words.

**Fix:**

- Leave the Echo connected and idle (overnight is ideal) so it can install any pending update. Some models let you ask Alexa to check for software updates.
- Use the power adapter that came with your Echo, or an exact replacement. Check the label on the original for its rating.
- Try a different wall outlet, and avoid loose power strips.

## 9. Factory reset as a last resort

A factory reset erases the Echo's Wi-Fi details and settings, and you'll need to set it up again in the Alexa app. Try every other fix first.

**Fix:**

1. Look up the reset steps for your exact Echo model and generation on Amazon's help pages. The button combination varies between models.
2. Some models also offer a factory reset option in the device's settings in the Alexa app.
3. After the reset, the light should turn orange to show setup mode. Set it up again in the Alexa app and connect it to your Wi-Fi.

## Echo won't turn on or light up at all?

- **Check the outlet.** Plug a lamp or phone charger into the same outlet to confirm it has power.
- **Check the adapter and cable.** Look for damage and make sure the plug is fully seated in the Echo.
- **Try a different adapter** of the correct rating if you have one.
- If it still shows no light at all with a known good outlet and adapter, the Echo has likely failed. Contact Amazon support, especially if it's under warranty.

## When to contact Amazon support

Contact Amazon customer support if:

- The Echo won't power on with the correct adapter on a working outlet.
- It stays orange and won't finish setup on a Wi-Fi network your other devices use without trouble.
- It still doesn't respond after a factory reset.
- The speaker sounds distorted, gets hot, or shows physical damage. Stop using it until it's checked.

Check also whether the problem is limited to one smart device. In that case the device maker's support is the faster route.
