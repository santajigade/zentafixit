Water around a refrigerator is alarming, but the cause is usually simple. Fridges deal with two kinds of water: melt water from the automatic defrost cycle, which runs down a drain to a pan under the fridge, and fresh water from your plumbing, which feeds the ice maker and dispenser. A leak means one of those paths is blocked, cracked or loose.

The most common causes are **a clogged defrost drain**, **a cracked or misplaced drain pan**, **a leaking water supply line or filter**, and **a fridge that isn't level**. Where the water shows up tells you which path to check first.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the refrigerator before you pull it out or work behind it, and pull it straight out slowly so you don't crush the water line. Shut off the water supply valve before touching the water line or filter fittings. Wipe up standing water right away: wet floors are a slip hazard, and water should never reach the cord or outlet.

## How to tell where the water is coming from: quick test

1. **Dry everything.** Wipe the floor, the bottom of the fresh food section and the freezer floor with towels.
2. **Lay a dry paper towel** on the floor under the front of the fridge and another behind it, if you can reach.
3. **Wait a few hours**, then check where water appears first.
    - **Inside the fridge** (under the crisper drawers, on the floor of the fresh food or freezer section): start with causes 1, 5, 6 and 7.
    - **Under the front or behind the fridge:** start with causes 2, 3 and 4.
4. **Look at the timing.** A leak that grows after the ice maker fills points to the water line or ice maker. A slow puddle that appears every day or so points to defrost water.

## 1. A clogged defrost drain

Several times a day, the fridge melts frost off its cooling coil (the evaporator). The melt water runs into a drain hole, usually at the back of the freezer or fresh food section, and down a tube to the drain pan. Food crumbs, mold or ice can block that hole, so the water backs up and pools under the crisper drawers or freezes on the freezer floor.

**Fix:**

1. Find the drain hole. On many models it is at the bottom of the back wall, sometimes behind a removable panel or the crisper drawers. Check your manual if you can't see it.
2. Clear any visible debris from the opening.
3. Use a turkey baster to squirt warm water into the hole. Repeat until it flows freely down to the pan.
4. If it stays blocked, gently push a pipe cleaner or thin flexible tubing into the drain. Never use anything sharp, which can puncture the tube or coil.
5. If the drain is frozen solid, unplug the fridge, move food to a cooler, and leave the doors open for several hours so the ice melts. Keep towels down.

Some fridges have a small rubber valve at the bottom end of the drain tube, near the drain pan, that can clog too. If the drain freezes again within days, the defrost system itself may be failing. Our guide to a [refrigerator not cooling while the freezer works](/appliances/refrigerator-not-cooling-freezer-works/) covers those defrost faults.

## 2. A cracked or misplaced drain pan

The drain pan sits under the fridge, usually behind the front grille (kick plate) or reachable from the back. Defrost water collects there and evaporates with heat from the compressor and condenser. If the pan is cracked, sits crooked after a move, or was knocked out of place during cleaning, water drips straight onto the floor.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the fridge and remove the front grille. Many snap off; some have screws.
- Shine a flashlight in and check the pan is sitting level and centered under the drain tube.
- If you can slide it out (check your manual first), look for cracks and clean out any slime or debris.
- Replace a cracked pan with the part listed for your model number. Don't patch it with tape, because the area gets warm.
- A pan that overflows without a crack is usually getting far more water than normal, which points back to cause 1, 3 or 7.

## 3. A leaking water supply line

If your fridge has an ice maker or water dispenser, a thin water line (usually 1/4 inch) runs from a shutoff valve behind or under the fridge to a water inlet valve at the bottom back of the fridge. Older white plastic lines get brittle and crack, and compression nuts can work loose, especially after the fridge is moved.

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the fridge, shut off the water valve, and pull the fridge out slowly.
2. Dry the line and fittings, turn the water back on, and watch for drips at the valve, along the line and at the fridge inlet.
3. Tighten a dripping compression nut gently by hand plus a little with a wrench. Overtightening can crack plastic fittings.
4. Replace a cracked, kinked or yellowed plastic line. A braided stainless steel line is more durable and less likely to kink.
5. Coil any extra line loosely behind the fridge so it isn't crushed when you push the fridge back.

If the line connects to your pipe with a **saddle valve** (a clamp that pierces the pipe), check it closely. These are prone to slow leaks, and many plumbers recommend replacing them with a proper quarter-turn shutoff valve. If you're not comfortable working on household pipes, call a plumber.

## 4. A loose or wrong water filter

Filters sit inside the fridge, in the front grille or on the back, depending on the model. A filter that isn't fully turned or clicked into place, a cracked housing, or a filter that doesn't match your model can leak at the seal. Leaks often start right after a filter change.

**Fix:**

- Remove the filter and check the O-rings for cuts or twists.
- Reinstall it until it locks fully, following your manual.
- Use the filter your manual lists for your model number. A filter that fits loosely won't seal.
- Run water through the dispenser as your manual directs to purge air. Trapped air can make the dispenser sputter and drip.
- If water drips from the filter housing itself with a correct filter installed, the housing may be cracked and need replacing.

## 5. The fridge isn't level

Most manufacturers want the front of the fridge slightly higher than the back. That tilt lets the doors swing closed on their own and keeps defrost water flowing back toward the drain. A fridge that leans forward can spill melt water out of the drain trough and onto the shelves or floor.

**Fix:**

- Put a bubble level on top of the fridge, side to side and front to back.
- Remove the front grille and turn the leveling legs or roller screws to raise the front. Check your manual for the recommended tilt.
- Open each door about halfway and let go. It should swing closed by itself.

## 6. A worn door gasket or condensation

If warm, humid kitchen air gets in past a torn or dirty door gasket, it turns to water inside. You may see drops on the shelves, wet walls or water running down the door frame. Leaving a door open, or opening it often on humid days, does the same.

**Fix:**

- Close the door on a dollar bill at several points. You should feel resistance when you pull it out. If it slides out easily, the gasket isn't sealing there.
- Clean the gasket with warm, soapy water and dry it. Sticky gaskets can fold and leave gaps.
- Replace a torn or flattened gasket with the part for your model number.
- Make sure nothing in the fridge stops the door from closing fully.

## 7. Ice maker or dispenser fill problems

The ice maker can leak if its fill tube is out of position, the ice maker isn't level, or the water inlet valve doesn't close fully. A frozen fill tube can also make water spill over the mold and run down the freezer. Low household water pressure can stop some inlet valves from closing properly, and an overflowing dispenser drip tray is easy to mistake for a leak.

**Fix:**

- Look for ice buildup around the ice maker and fill tube, and see our [ice maker troubleshooting guide](/appliances/ice-maker-not-working/) for thawing a frozen fill tube.
- Check the ice maker is mounted straight and the fill tube points into the mold.
- Empty and dry the dispenser drip tray.
- Check the minimum water pressure in your manual. If your whole house has weak flow, see our guide to [low water pressure](/plumbing/low-water-pressure/).
- If water keeps dripping into the ice maker with the fridge unplugged, the inlet valve is likely leaking through and needs replacing.

## Leak won't stop or keeps coming back?

- **Water keeps flowing with the fridge unplugged:** shut off the water supply valve. The leak is in the supply line, a fitting or the inlet valve.
- **The defrost drain refreezes within days:** the defrost heater, sensor or control may be failing. That's a job for a technician.
- **Water is near the power cord, outlet or compressor area:** leave the fridge unplugged and dry the area before using it again.
- **The floor is soft or stained:** the leak may have been running for a while. Check under the floor if you can, and dry it out to prevent mold.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call an appliance technician if:

- The defrost drain keeps freezing even after you clear it.
- Water drips from inside the cabinet walls or from the back panel.
- The water inlet valve or filter housing needs replacing and you're not comfortable doing it.
- The leak comes with cooling problems, frost buildup or a burning smell.

Call a plumber if the shutoff valve itself leaks, or if you want a saddle valve replaced with a proper shutoff valve.
