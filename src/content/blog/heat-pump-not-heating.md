A heat pump heats your home by pulling warmth out of the outdoor air and moving it inside. When it stops heating, you'll notice one of three things: **no heat at all**, heat that **can't keep up** with the cold, or an outdoor unit that's **iced over or not running**.

The most common causes are **a thermostat that isn't calling for heat, a tripped breaker, a clogged filter, a frozen outdoor coil and very cold weather**. The first four are often things you can check yourself in under an hour.

> **Safety first:** The outdoor unit runs on 240 V and has a capacitor that can hold a dangerous charge even with the power off. Don't open its electrical panel. Turn the system off at the thermostat and the outdoor disconnect before clearing debris or ice. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification, so leaks, recharging and compressor problems are jobs for a licensed HVAC technician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the thermostat.** Mode on **HEAT**, fan on **AUTO**, set point a few degrees above the room temperature. Wait 5 to 10 minutes, since many systems have a built-in start delay.
2. **Go outside and listen.** Is the outdoor fan spinning and the compressor humming? Or is the unit silent, buzzing, or covered in ice?
3. **Feel and measure the air.** After 15 minutes, hold a thermometer at a supply vent and another at the return grille. Supply air should be clearly warmer than return air.
4. **Check the filter.** A grey, clogged filter cuts airflow and heating.
5. **Note the weather.** If it's unusually cold and the system runs constantly while the house slowly loses ground, see cause 5.

If the system heats but the air comes out cold for a few minutes, or only cold air comes from the vents, our guide to a [heat pump blowing cold air](/hvac/heat-pump-blowing-cold-air/) covers defrost cycles, fan settings and O/B wiring in detail.

## 1. The thermostat isn't calling for heat

If nothing happens when you turn up the heat, the thermostat may not be sending a signal at all.

**Fix:**

- Confirm the mode is **HEAT** (not OFF, COOL, or ECO with a low set point) and that no schedule or hold is overriding your setting.
- Replace the batteries if the display is dim, blank or shows a low-battery icon. A thermostat with no display can't call for heat. See [thermostat screen blank](/smart-home/thermostat-blank-screen/) if it won't come back on.
- A smart thermostat that lost Wi-Fi should still heat on its own schedule, but check that it's still set to a heat pump system and not a furnace.
- Look for a tripped breaker or blown fuse at the indoor air handler, which often powers the thermostat too.

## 2. The outdoor unit has no power

If the indoor blower runs but the outdoor unit is silent, the heat pump isn't gathering any heat. The house will cool off unless the backup heat covers it.

**Fix:**

1. Find the outdoor unit's breaker in your main panel (usually a double breaker labeled heat pump, AC or condenser). If it has tripped, push it fully to OFF, then back ON, **once**.
2. Check the **outdoor disconnect**, the small box on the wall near the unit. Make sure the switch or pull-out block is in the ON position.
3. Wait at least 5 minutes. Some units have a protective delay after power returns.
4. If the breaker trips again or won't stay on, stop. See [breaker won't reset](/electrical/breaker-wont-reset/) for what that means, then call a technician.

## 3. A dirty filter or blocked airflow

A heat pump needs plenty of air moving across the indoor coil. A clogged filter, closed vents or blocked return grilles cut airflow. The system heats poorly, and some units shut down on a high-pressure or temperature safety.

**Fix:**

- Turn the system off and replace the filter if it's grey or you can't see light through it. Match the size printed on the frame, with the arrow pointing toward the air handler.
- Open supply vents and move furniture, rugs and curtains off vents and return grilles.
- Check the filter monthly during heating season.

## 4. The outdoor coil is frozen over

Light frost on the outdoor coil is normal in cold, damp weather. The heat pump clears it with a **defrost cycle**: it briefly switches to cooling mode for a few minutes, the outdoor fan usually stops, and you may see steam. A unit that's **encased in solid ice**, or ice that never clears, can't absorb heat.

Common causes are a faulty **defrost control or sensor**, snow or leaves packed around the unit, **water dripping from a gutter** onto it, a failed outdoor fan, or **low refrigerant**.

**Fix:**

- Set the thermostat to **emergency heat** or off.
- Clear snow, leaves and debris from the top and sides. Keep the area around it clear as your manual recommends, and make sure the unit sits above snow level.
- Melt the ice with water from a garden hose or bucket. **Never chip ice with tools** or use a heat gun; the fins and tubes puncture easily.
- Fix any gutter or roof drip over the unit.
- If it ices up solid again within a day or two, call a technician. The defrost board, sensor or refrigerant charge needs testing.

## 5. It's colder than the heat pump can handle

A heat pump's heating output falls as the outdoor temperature drops, while your house loses heat faster. The outdoor temperature where the two meet is called the **balance point**. Below it, the heat pump runs nonstop and can't keep up by itself. That's why most systems have **auxiliary heat**, usually electric heat strips in the indoor air handler, that the thermostat adds automatically.

The balance point depends on your unit, your insulation and your climate, so there's no single number. Cold-climate heat pumps keep more of their output in freezing weather than standard models.

**Fix:**

- Running constantly in very cold weather is normal. Watch whether the thermostat shows **AUX** or "AUX HEAT".
- If it never shows AUX on the coldest days and the house keeps falling behind, check the panel for a tripped **heat strip breaker** (often a separate double breaker, sometimes at the air handler). Reset it once.
- Avoid large set point jumps and deep night setbacks, which make recovery slow and can bring on expensive aux heat.
- If the house can't keep up even in mild weather, the unit may be undersized or the ducts may be leaking. A technician can check.

## 6. A failed capacitor, contactor or fan motor

If the outdoor unit **hums, buzzes or clicks** but the fan doesn't spin or the compressor doesn't start, a starting part has probably failed. Common culprits are the **run capacitor**, the **contactor** (the switch that gets the 24 V signal from the thermostat) or the outdoor **fan motor**.

**Fix:** Turn the system off at the thermostat to protect the compressor, and call a technician. These parts are inside the 240 V electrical panel, and the capacitor can shock you even with power off. Don't poke the fan blades with a stick to start it.

## 7. Low refrigerant from a leak

Refrigerant isn't used up. If the charge is low, there's a leak. Signs include weak heating with the unit running, frequent icing, **hissing** or bubbling sounds, and oily spots on the copper lines.

**Fix:** Call a licensed HVAC technician. Finding and repairing leaks and recharging refrigerant require EPA certification and special tools. Topping it up without fixing the leak only delays the problem.

## 8. Reversing valve or control board problem

The **reversing valve** sends refrigerant in the direction needed for heating or cooling. If it sticks, the system may cool in heat mode or heat poorly. A failed **control board** or defrost board can stop the heat pump from starting or defrosting.

**Fix:** These need a technician with gauges and meters. If the problem started right after a new thermostat was installed, first check its heat pump and O/B settings against your manual.

## Heat pump not running at all?

If neither the indoor blower nor the outdoor unit runs:

- Check **every related breaker**: outdoor unit, air handler and heat strips. Reset each tripped one once.
- Check the **power switch** on or near the indoor air handler, which looks like a light switch.
- Make sure the air handler's **access panel** is fully closed. A door safety switch keeps it off when the panel is loose.
- Look for a **float switch** or water in the condensate pan if your air handler is in an attic or closet. Some systems shut off when it trips.
- Use **emergency heat** to stay warm while you wait for service, if the backup heat works.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed technician if:

- A breaker for the outdoor unit, air handler or heat strips **trips again** after one reset.
- The outdoor unit **hums or clicks but won't start**, or the fan won't spin.
- The coil **ices up solid repeatedly** or never clears after defrost.
- The unit runs but the supply air is barely warmer than the room, with a clean filter and a clear outdoor unit.
- You notice hissing, oily spots on the lines, or a burning smell.
- You're relying on emergency heat for more than a day or two.

Annual maintenance before heating season, including a check of the defrost controls, capacitor and refrigerant charge, is the best way to avoid a breakdown during the first cold snap.
