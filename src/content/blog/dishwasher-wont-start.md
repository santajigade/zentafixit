When a dishwasher won't start, it is usually not broken. It's waiting for something: a door that's fully latched, a setting turned off, or power that never reached it. A few minutes of checks solve most of these.

The most common causes are **a door that isn't latched, the control lock (child lock) turned on, a delay start that's counting down, a tripped breaker or GFCI, and a wall switch that's been flipped off**. Less often, a stuck float switch, a blown thermal fuse or a failed control board is to blame.

> **Safety first:** A dishwasher combines 120 V power and water. Turn off its breaker (or unplug it under the sink) before removing the kick plate, the inner door panel or any cover, and confirm it's dead with a non-contact voltage tester. Never touch wiring with wet hands or while standing in water. If you're not comfortable with this, stop at cause 7 and call a technician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/dishwasher-wont-start-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: press a button and see if lights come on. If there are no lights, check the breaker and GFCI, the wall switch and plug, do a power reset, then suspect the thermal fuse or control board. If the lights come on, check the door latch, control lock and delay start, whether the door was shut right after Start, and whether it fills with water" width="640" height="470" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Whether the panel lights up splits the problem into a power side and a settings side.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Press any button.** Do lights, a display or a beep come on?
    - **Nothing at all?** It isn't getting power, or a fuse or board has failed (causes 5 and 8).
    - **Lights come on?** Power is fine. The dishwasher is being blocked by the door or a setting (causes 1 to 4).
2. **Look at the display** for a lock symbol, a clock, a countdown or a blinking light.
3. **Close the door firmly** until you hear or feel the latch click, then press Start again.
4. **Listen for a minute.** A short hum and then water flowing is normal. A hum with no water points to causes 6 and 7.
5. **Note any error code** and look it up in your manual before replacing parts.

## 1. Door not latched or a bad door switch

A dishwasher won't run unless a switch inside the latch tells the control board the door is shut. An overfilled rack, a utensil poking through the basket or a dish touching the door can stop it closing fully, even if it looks shut.

**Fix:**

- Open the door and push both racks all the way in. Check that nothing sticks out, especially from the silverware basket and the bottom of the lower rack.
- Close the door firmly until it clicks. Push on the top corners to make sure it's seated.
- Look at the latch at the top of the door for cracked plastic or a hook that doesn't catch the strike in the tub.
- If the latch catches but the dishwasher still acts as if the door is open, the door switch may have failed. With the power off, the latch assembly can usually be reached by removing screws on the inner door panel, and the switch can be checked for continuity with a multimeter. The part and steps vary by model, so check your manual or the tech sheet first.

## 2. Control lock is on

The **control lock** (often called child lock) disables the buttons so a child can't start or change a cycle. It's easy to turn on by accident while wiping the panel. When it's on, buttons may do nothing, or the panel may beep or flash a lock symbol.

**Fix:**

- Look for a lock icon or a lit "lock" light on the display.
- On many models you turn it off by pressing and holding the lock button, or a button marked with a small lock symbol, for a few seconds.
- The button and timing differ between brands and models, so check your owner's manual if that doesn't work. Don't press random button combinations, because some models use them to enter test modes.

## 3. Delay start is set

**Delay start** lets you run the dishwasher hours later, for example at night. If it's been set, the dishwasher accepts the cycle and then waits silently. Many displays show a clock, a number of hours or a countdown.

**Fix:**

- Check the display for a clock icon or a number like 2h, 4h or 8h.
- Press the delay button until the delay is off, or cancel the cycle and start again. Check your manual for how your model clears it.

## 4. The cycle never actually started

On many models, you press Start and then have to **close the door within a few seconds**, or the cycle cancels. On others with controls on the top edge of the door, you choose the cycle, press Start and shut the door, and it begins only once the latch closes. If the door stays open too long, the panel may simply time out and go dark.

**Fix:**

- Choose your cycle, press Start, then shut and latch the door straight away.
- Check your manual for the exact start sequence on your model, since it varies.

## 5. No power reaching the dishwasher

If the panel is completely dark, start with the power supply. Dishwashers are usually on their own 120 V circuit, either hardwired into a box behind the kick plate or plugged into an outlet under the sink.

**Fix:**

- **Breaker:** Find the dishwasher breaker in your panel. If it's tripped (in the middle or off position), push it fully to OFF, then back to ON.
- **GFCI:** Many dishwasher circuits are GFCI protected. Press RESET on any GFCI outlets in the kitchen, and check for a GFCI breaker in the panel. If it trips again straight away, see our guide on [why a GFCI keeps tripping](/electrical/gfci-outlet-keeps-tripping/).
- **Wall switch:** Some kitchens have a wall switch near the sink that controls the dishwasher, often right next to the garbage disposal switch. Make sure it's on.
- **Plug:** If your dishwasher plugs in under the sink, check that the plug hasn't been knocked loose by items stored there.

If a breaker trips again the moment you reset it, leave it off and call an electrician or appliance technician.

## 6. Water supply valve is closed

If the lights come on and you hear a hum or a click but no water flows in, the water supply may be shut off. The shutoff valve is usually under the kitchen sink, on the hot water line, with a small tube running to the dishwasher. It's often closed during plumbing work and then forgotten.

**Fix:**

- Look under the sink for a small valve on the line feeding the dishwasher. Turn it fully counterclockwise to open it.
- Check that the supply tube isn't kinked, and that the kitchen hot tap runs normally.
- If the valve is open and it still won't fill, the inlet valve behind the kick plate may have failed. That's usually a technician job.

## 7. Float switch stuck by debris

The **float** is a small dome or cylinder in the floor of the tub, usually near the front. It rises as water fills and trips a switch that stops the fill. If food, a utensil or broken glass jams it in the raised position, the dishwasher thinks it's already full and won't fill or run properly.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the power and pull out the bottom rack.
2. Find the float and lift it gently. It should move up and down freely by about an inch and drop back on its own.
3. Clean around it and under it. Some floats lift off for cleaning: check your manual first.
4. Clean the filter at the same time. If water is sitting in the tub, our guide to a [dishwasher that's not draining](/appliances/dishwasher-not-draining/) covers why.

## 8. Blown thermal fuse or failed control board

If the panel stays completely dark after you've confirmed power reaches the dishwasher, an internal part has likely failed. Many dishwashers have a **thermal fuse**, often on or near the control board, that blows if the controls overheat and cuts all power. The **control board** itself can also fail after a power surge or simply with age.

**Fix:**

- A thermal fuse usually means something overheated, so a new fuse alone may not fix the root cause.
- If you're comfortable with careful DIY: turn off the breaker, confirm the power is off with a voltage tester, open the panel described in your manual and test the fuse for continuity with a multimeter. Take a photo of the wiring before you disconnect anything.
- Look for scorch marks, melted connectors or a burnt smell on the board. If you see any, call a technician rather than replacing parts yourself.
- Use the model number (on a label at the edge of the door or tub) to order the correct part. Boards and fuses are model specific.

## Completely dead? Do a power reset

A control glitch, often after a brief power cut or surge, can leave a dishwasher frozen with a dark or unresponsive panel. A hard reset clears most of them.

1. Turn off the dishwasher's breaker, or unplug it under the sink.
2. Wait about one minute.
3. Turn the power back on and wait a few seconds for the controls to wake up.
4. Close the door firmly, choose a cycle, press Start and shut the door right away.

If the panel shows an error code after the reset, look it up in your owner's manual or on the maker's website for your exact model number. Codes differ by brand, so don't guess.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician if:

- Power reaches the dishwasher but the panel stays dark (likely a thermal fuse or control board).
- The breaker or GFCI trips again as soon as you reset it.
- You smell burning, see scorch marks or find water near the wiring box behind the kick plate.
- The water valve is open but the dishwasher hums and never fills.
- The latch or door switch needs replacing and you'd rather not open the door panel.

Call an electrician if there's no power at the dishwasher's outlet or wiring box even though its breaker is on.
