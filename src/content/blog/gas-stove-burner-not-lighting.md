When a gas burner won't light, the knob is opening the gas valve and an igniter is supposed to spark it. If either half of that fails, you get a clicking burner with no flame, a silent burner, or a flame that looks wrong.

Most of the time the cause is simple: **a wet burner after a spill, a burner cap that isn't seated, clogged burner ports or a dirty igniter**. If no burner clicks at all, think **power** first. If no burner gets gas, think **the supply valve or an outage**.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>If it clicks but won't light, turn it off, let it cool, then clean and dry the burner head and cap, clear the ports with a pin and seat the cap flat. If nothing clicks, check that the range is plugged in and its breaker is on. If no burner gets gas, check the supply valve behind the range and ask your gas utility about outages.</p></div>

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas (a rotten egg smell), stop. Do not light a flame or touch light switches, appliances or your phone inside. Leave the home and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Before cleaning any burner, turn the knob to OFF and let the grates and burner parts cool fully. Unplug the range or switch off its breaker before you clean or touch an igniter. Never loosen, disconnect or repair a gas line or fitting yourself.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

<figure><img src="/images/posts/gas-stove-burner-not-lighting-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart: with no gas smell, turn the knob to LITE and listen and look for a spark. A spark but no flame points to a wet burner, a misplaced cap, clogged ports or a dirty igniter (causes 1 to 4). No gas on any burner means checking the supply valve and outages" width="640" height="480" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Listening for the click splits the problem in two.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Check for a gas smell first.** If you smell gas, follow the safety steps above and do nothing else.
2. **Turn the knob to LITE and listen.** Is there a steady clicking? On many ranges every igniter clicks at once, whichever knob you turn.
3. **Look at the igniter in a dim room.** You should see a small blue-white spark jumping to the burner.
4. **Try another burner.** One bad burner points to that burner's parts. All burners failing points to power, the spark module or the gas supply.
5. **Optional hand-light test.** Only if there is no gas smell and your manual allows it: hold a lit long lighter at the burner edge, then turn the knob to LITE. If it lights by hand, gas is fine and the spark is the problem. If it does not light within a few seconds, turn the knob off and wait for the gas to clear. This is a test, not a fix.

## 1. A wet burner after a spill or boil-over

Water, broth or cleaning spray can soak the burner head, the igniter or the ports. A wet igniter sparks to the wrong place or not at all, and wet ports may not carry the flame.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the knob off and let the burner cool.
2. Lift off the grate, cap and (if it comes off) the burner head. Pat everything dry.
3. Let the parts air-dry fully before putting them back. Some people use a hair dryer on a cool setting.
4. Wipe the igniter dry with a cloth. Do not pour or spray liquid into the burner base opening.

## 2. A burner cap or head out of place

If the cap sits crooked or the head is not fully seated, gas escapes in the wrong spots and the flame never reaches the igniter. This is very common after cleaning.

**Fix:**

- Set the burner head back on its base so it drops into place without rocking.
- Place the cap flat and centered. Gently move it until it settles. It should not wobble.
- Make sure each cap is on its own burner. Many cooktops use different sizes, so a small cap on a large head will not work.

## 3. Clogged burner ports

The small holes around the burner head (the ports) let gas out in a ring. Grease and burnt food can block the ones nearest the igniter, so the gas never meets the spark. You may hear gas but see no flame.

**Fix:**

1. Knob off, parts cool, range unplugged.
2. Remove the cap and burner head. Wash them in warm, soapy water and scrub with a soft toothbrush.
3. Clear each port by pushing a **straight pin or sewing needle** straight through. Avoid toothpicks, which can snap off inside.
4. Rinse and let the parts dry completely, then reassemble. Check your manual before using a dishwasher or harsh cleaners, since some finishes can be damaged.

## 4. A dirty or cracked igniter

The igniter is the small white ceramic piece with a metal tip next to the burner. Grease or food on the tip can weaken the spark. A crack in the ceramic lets the spark leak away.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the range or switch off its breaker.
- Gently clean the tip with a dry toothbrush or a cloth with a little rubbing alcohol. Let it dry.
- Look for cracks or a loose igniter. A cracked igniter should be replaced with the part listed for your model. Many owners can do this with the manual, but if you would need to go under the cooktop near gas fittings, call a technician.

## 5. No power to the range

Gas burners need electricity to spark. If no burner clicks and the clock or display is dark, the range is not getting power.

**Fix:**

- Make sure the range is plugged in firmly. Most gas ranges plug into a regular 120 V outlet behind or below the unit.
- Check your breaker panel and reset a tripped breaker by pushing it fully to OFF, then ON. If the outlet is GFCI protected, press its RESET button.
- If the breaker trips again, stop and see our guide to a [breaker that keeps tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/).

## 6. Clicking that won't stop

Each knob has a small switch behind it that tells the spark module to fire. If moisture gets into that switch, the igniters can click nonstop, even with the knob off. Grease buildup can do the same.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the knobs off and unplug the range or switch off its breaker.
2. Pull the knob straight off (most do) and dry around the stem.
3. Leave it to dry fully, ideally overnight, then restore power.
4. If one switch keeps clicking once dry, it has likely failed and needs replacing with the correct part.

## 7. A failed igniter, spark module or switch

If one burner never clicks but the others do, its igniter, wire or switch has likely failed. If no burner clicks but the range has power, the **spark module** (the part that makes the spark for all igniters on many ranges) may be bad. If a burner lights by hand but never by spark, that also points here.

**Fix:**

- Check that the wire to the igniter has not come loose (power off first).
- Use your model number, often on a label near the oven door frame or storage drawer, to find the correct replacement part.
- Spark modules and switches sit under the cooktop near gas lines and connectors. Unless you are comfortable and the manual covers it, have an appliance technician replace them.
- Until it is fixed, only light that burner by hand if your manual allows it and there is no gas smell.

## 8. A weak, uneven or yellow flame

A good flame is mostly blue, steady and even all the way around. A weak or patchy flame often means partly clogged ports or a crooked cap. A flame that stays large, yellow or orange, or leaves soot on pans, means the gas is not burning cleanly.

**Fix:**

- Clean the ports and reseat the cap (causes 2 and 3).
- A quick orange flicker from dust or a humidifier nearby is usually harmless.
- If the flame stays yellow or sooty after cleaning, stop using that burner and call a technician or your gas utility. Poor combustion can produce carbon monoxide, so make sure your CO alarm works. If it sounds, follow our [carbon monoxide alarm guide](/home-safety/carbon-monoxide-alarm-going-off/).
- Air shutter adjustment and conversion between natural gas and propane are jobs for a pro.

## No burner will light at all?

If the igniters click but none of the burners get gas, the problem is the supply.

- **Gas supply valve.** There is usually a shutoff valve on the gas line behind the range. On a common lever-style valve, the handle is in line with the pipe when open and across the pipe when closed. If it was turned off during a move, a repair or after a new range was installed, ask whoever closed it why. If you are unsure, or it was closed because of a leak, call your gas utility or a licensed pro rather than opening it.
- **Gas outage or meter shutoff.** Check whether other gas appliances (water heater, furnace) work. Check your utility's website or call them. If the gas at the meter was shut off, only the utility should turn it back on.
- **Power outage.** No power means no spark. Many manuals allow lighting surface burners by hand during an outage. Check yours, and never try this if you smell gas.

## When to call a pro

Call an appliance technician, or your gas utility for anything on the gas supply side, if:

- You smell gas at any time, even faintly. Leave first, then call from outside.
- The flame stays yellow, sooty or lifts off the burner after cleaning.
- The clicking will not stop after the switch has fully dried.
- A spark module, burner valve or switch under the cooktop needs replacing.
- No burner gets gas and the supply valve is open with no outage reported.

Never try to repair, loosen or reconnect a gas line, valve or fitting yourself. That work belongs to a licensed gas fitter, plumber or your gas utility.
