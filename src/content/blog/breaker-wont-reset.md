A breaker that won't reset is telling you one of three things: it **wasn't reset the right way**, there's **still a fault on the circuit**, or the **breaker itself is worn out**. A fourth possibility, **heat, water or burning at the panel**, means you should stop right away.

Often the fix is simply pushing the handle firmly to OFF before ON. If that doesn't work, how the breaker behaves tells you what's wrong. Here's how to read it without opening the panel.

> **Safety first:** Your job stops at the outside of the panel: flipping breakers, pressing TEST buttons, unplugging devices. Never remove the panel cover (the dead front). Parts inside stay live even with the main breaker off. Stand on a dry floor and use one hand. If you see scorch marks, smell burning, hear buzzing, feel heat, or the panel or floor is wet, don't touch it.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Look and sniff first.** Shine a flashlight on the panel. Any scorch marks, melted plastic, burning smell, buzzing or water? Stop and go to cause 5 or 7.
2. **Reset it properly once.** Push the handle firmly to **OFF** until it clicks, then to **ON**.
3. **Watch what happens.**
    - **Stays on?** You're done. If it trips again later, see our guide to a [circuit breaker that keeps tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/).
    - **Snaps back off the instant you flip it?** There's a fault on the circuit (cause 2).
    - **Won't latch, feels loose or flops around?** The breaker is likely worn (cause 4).
4. **Check for a TEST button** on the breaker. If it has one, it's an AFCI or GFCI type (cause 3).

## 1. The handle wasn't pushed fully to OFF

This is the most common reason a breaker "won't reset". When a breaker trips, the handle usually moves to a **middle position** between ON and OFF. If you push it straight to ON from there, nothing happens, because the internal mechanism has to re-latch first.

**Fix:**

1. Push the handle **firmly to the full OFF position**. Use more force than you'd expect. You should feel or hear a **click**.
2. Then push it **firmly to ON**.
3. On a **two-pole breaker** (a double-wide breaker with its handles tied together, used for dryers, ranges, water heaters and AC units), move the whole tied handle as one.
4. Some breakers have a small window that shows a colored flag when tripped. Check that it clears after the reset.

## 2. A fault is still on the circuit

If the breaker latches but **trips the instant you switch it on**, often with a snap, it's sensing a **short circuit or ground fault** right now. A short is a hot wire touching a neutral. A ground fault is a hot wire touching a ground wire, a metal box or something else connected to ground. A breaker trips on these instantly, and it will keep doing so until the fault is gone. This is different from an overload, which takes seconds or minutes to trip.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug everything** on that circuit and **switch off its lights**. Turn off hardwired appliances on it (a dishwasher, disposal or bathroom fan) at their own switch.
2. Reset the breaker **once more** using the OFF then ON method.
3. **Stays on?** Plug devices back in **one at a time**. If one trips it right away, that device or its cord has the fault. Stop using it and repair or replace it.
4. **Still trips instantly with everything off?** The fault is in the wiring, an outlet, a switch or a fixture. **Leave the breaker off and call an electrician.** Don't try again.

If the circuit has gone dead and outlets that "should" be on it don't respond, use our guide to an [outlet that's not working](/electrical/outlet-not-working/) to map which outlets are affected.

## 3. An AFCI or GFCI breaker still sees a problem

Breakers with a **TEST button** are AFCI (arc-fault), GFCI (ground-fault) or dual-function types. They're more sensitive than standard breakers and won't stay on while they still detect arcing or current leaking to ground.

**Fix:**

- Reset it the same way: **firmly OFF, then ON**.
- If it trips immediately, unplug and switch off everything on the circuit and try once more, as in cause 2.
- Look for **moisture** at outdoor, garage, basement or bathroom outlets on the circuit (GFCI), and **crushed or damaged cords** behind furniture (AFCI).
- Some AFCI and dual-function breakers have an **indicator light or flag** that shows why they last tripped. What it means varies by brand, so check the **label on the breaker** or the **maker's instructions**. Don't guess.
- If it won't reset with nothing connected, it needs an electrician. Never swap it for a standard breaker to make the problem go away.

## 4. The breaker is worn out or damaged

Breakers are mechanical. After years of use or many trips, the latch can wear. A worn breaker may feel **loose or mushy**, have **no spring resistance**, **refuse to latch** at OFF, or flop back to the middle on its own.

**Fix:**

- Try the full OFF then ON reset one more time, firmly.
- If the handle still feels wrong, or the breaker won't latch at all, **leave it off** and call an electrician.
- Replacing a breaker means removing the panel cover and working next to live parts. It's not a DIY job, even though a replacement breaker itself is usually not expensive.

## 5. Heat, a burning smell or buzzing

Stop here if you notice any of these. **A hot breaker or panel, a burning or hot plastic smell, buzzing or crackling, scorch marks or melted plastic** point to a bad connection or failing breaker that can start a fire.

**Fix:**

- **Don't reset it.** Leave the breaker off and keep your hands off the panel.
- If it's safe to do so, unplug devices on that circuit from the room side.
- **Call an electrician** right away. If you see smoke or flames, get everyone out and call 911.

## 6. The main breaker tripped

If **the whole house** lost power, check the **main breaker**. It's usually the large breaker at the top of the panel, often labeled MAIN. Some homes have the main disconnect in a separate box outside, near the meter.

**Fix:**

1. First check whether **neighbors have power**. If they don't, it's a utility outage. Report it to your utility and wait.
2. If only your home is out, switch **off the individual branch breakers** so the main isn't reset into the full house load.
3. Reset the main **firmly to OFF, then ON**.
4. Turn the branch breakers back on **one at a time**.
5. If the main trips again, won't reset, or some rooms are dead while lights elsewhere look oddly dim or bright, **stop**. Call an electrician, and your utility if you see damage at the meter or the service wires.

## 7. Water, flooding or a wet panel

Water and electricity at the panel is a serious shock hazard. If there's **standing water near the panel**, the **panel is wet**, or the basement has **flooded**, don't touch the panel or try to reset anything.

**Fix:**

- **Stay out of the water.** Don't walk through a flooded area to reach the panel.
- **Call your utility** and ask them to shut off power at the meter.
- Have a **licensed electrician** inspect the panel before power is restored. Breakers and wiring that have been underwater often need replacing rather than drying out, and an electrician should make that call.

## 8. It resets, but the power is still off

Sometimes the breaker resets fine but the outlets or lights still don't work. The breaker may not have been the problem, or something else on the circuit also tripped.

**Fix:**

- Look for a **tripped GFCI outlet** in a bathroom, kitchen, garage, basement or outside. It can cut power to regular outlets in other rooms. Press RESET.
- Make sure you reset the **right breaker**. Panel labels are often wrong. Test the outlet with a lamp or outlet tester after each change.
- Flip nearby **wall switches**. Some outlets are switch-controlled.

## Why you shouldn't keep resetting it

Every time you switch a breaker on into a short or ground fault, a burst of current flows through the wiring and the breaker's contacts. Doing it again and again can damage the breaker, overheat a weak connection and turn a small fault into a fire. **One careful retry with the loads switched off is enough.** If it won't hold after that, the answer is to find the fault, not to keep flipping, holding or taping the handle.

## Still won't reset?

- **Nothing connected and it still trips instantly:** there's a wiring fault. Leave it off.
- **Handle won't latch or feels mushy:** the breaker is worn or broken.
- **Main breaker won't hold:** treat the whole house as unsafe to power until an electrician checks it.

Label the breaker so no one else keeps trying it, and leave the circuit off until it's checked.

## When to call an electrician

Call a licensed electrician if:

- The breaker **trips instantly** with everything on the circuit unplugged and switched off.
- The handle is **loose, mushy or won't latch**.
- You notice **heat, a burning smell, buzzing or scorch marks** at the breaker or panel.
- The **main breaker** trips again or won't reset.
- The panel has been **wet or flooded**.
- An **AFCI or GFCI breaker** won't reset and you can't trace it to a device or moisture.

A breaker that won't reset is doing its job, keeping power off a circuit that isn't safe right now. Do the outside checks, then let a pro handle anything inside the panel or walls.
