When Bluetooth won't connect, the phone and the accessory usually just aren't ready at the same moment. The most common causes are an accessory **not in pairing mode**, one that's **still connected to another device**, a **stale saved pairing**, or **distance and interference**.

These fixes work for earbuds, headphones, speakers, car systems and smartwatches on both iPhone and Android. Work through them in order: the first four solve most problems.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Open the Bluetooth screen** in your phone's Settings app and leave it open.
2. **Put the accessory in pairing mode** and hold it next to the phone.
3. **Watch the list of devices.**
    - **It never appears?** The accessory isn't discoverable. See causes 2, 3 and 6.
    - **It appears but fails or asks again and again?** See causes 4 and 6.
    - **It connects but drops out or crackles?** See causes 5 and 7.
4. **Try it with a different phone or computer.** If it won't pair with anything, the accessory itself is the likely problem.

## 1. Turn Bluetooth off and on again

Bluetooth on the phone can get stuck after sleep, an update or a lot of switching between devices. Cycling it is quick and often enough.

**Fix:**

- **iPhone:** open **Settings > Bluetooth** and turn it off, wait 10 seconds, then turn it on. Use Settings, not Control Center. Tapping the Control Center button only disconnects accessories and leaves Bluetooth partly on.
- **Android:** open **Settings** and find **Bluetooth** (often under **Connections** or **Connected devices**), turn it off, wait 10 seconds and turn it on. You can also use the Quick Settings tile.
- Try connecting again.

## 2. Put the accessory in pairing mode

A Bluetooth accessory only shows up to a new phone when it's in **pairing mode** (also called discoverable mode). Turning it on isn't always enough, especially if it remembers another device.

**Fix:**

- **Earbuds:** with the buds in the case and the lid open, many models enter pairing mode when you hold the button on the case for several seconds. Others use a long press on both buds.
- **Headphones and speakers:** often a long press on the power or Bluetooth button until the light flashes, or a separate pairing button.
- **Car systems:** start pairing from the car's screen first, then from the phone.
- A **flashing light** or a voice prompt usually means it's ready. Check your manual for the exact method and how long pairing mode lasts.

## 3. Make sure it isn't connected to another device

Most earbuds and speakers connect to one device at a time, and they automatically reconnect to the last one they used. If your tablet, laptop or a family member's phone grabs it first, your phone can't connect.

**Fix:**

- Turn off Bluetooth on nearby devices that have used the accessory before, or disconnect the accessory from them.
- If your accessory supports **multipoint** (two devices at once), check the manual. It may be connected to two others already.
- Then connect from your phone's Bluetooth list.

## 4. Forget the device and pair it again

The saved pairing on your phone holds a security key. If the accessory was reset, updated or paired with too many devices, that key no longer matches and connections fail. Forgetting and re-pairing creates a fresh one.

**Fix:**

1. **iPhone:** go to **Settings > Bluetooth**, tap the **info (i)** button next to the device and tap **Forget This Device**.
2. **Android:** in the Bluetooth or Connected devices screen, tap the **gear** or the device name and choose **Forget** or **Unpair**.
3. If the accessory has a reset option (often a long press of a button, see the manual), reset it too.
4. Put the accessory in pairing mode and select it from the list on your phone.

## 5. Move closer and away from interference

Bluetooth is a short-range radio. Walls, your body, metal furniture and other devices on the 2.4 GHz band (Wi-Fi routers, microwave ovens, cordless phones and some USB 3 hubs) can weaken the signal.

**Fix:**

- Pair with the phone and accessory **side by side**, then test from farther away.
- Keep the phone in a pocket on the same side of your body as the earbud that drops out most.
- Move away from a running microwave or a busy router.
- In a crowded place like a gym or train station, some dropouts are normal.

## 6. Charge the accessory and restart both

A low accessory battery often causes failed pairing or dropouts before it shuts off completely. A restart clears stuck Bluetooth processes on both sides.

**Fix:**

- Charge the accessory (and its case, for earbuds) for at least 15 to 30 minutes.
- Turn the accessory off and on.
- Restart your phone fully, then try again.

## 7. Update your phone and the accessory

Software bugs can cause pairing problems that updates fix, on either side.

**Fix:**

- **iPhone:** **Settings > General > Software Update**.
- **Android:** look in **Settings** for **System update** or **Software update** (the location varies by brand).
- Many headphones, earbuds, speakers and cars get firmware updates through the maker's companion app or website. Check the manual.

## 8. Reset network settings as a last resort

If nothing will connect to your phone, but the accessory pairs fine with other devices, reset the phone's network settings. This is a last resort because it **erases all saved Wi-Fi networks and passwords, paired Bluetooth devices and VPN settings**. It doesn't delete photos, apps or messages.

**Fix:**

- **iPhone:** **Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > Reset Network Settings**.
- **Android:** search Settings for **reset**. The option is usually called something like **Reset network settings** or **Reset Wi-Fi, mobile and Bluetooth**, under **System** or **General management**.
- Write down your Wi-Fi password first. After the reset, reconnect to Wi-Fi and pair your accessory again.

## Connected but no sound?

- **Check the audio output.** On iPhone, open Control Center, tap the AirPlay icon on the music controls and pick your accessory. On Android, use the media output button in the Quick Settings panel or volume menu.
- **Check the volume** on both the phone and the accessory. Some accessories have their own volume.
- **Check the device's audio settings.** On Android, tap the device in the Bluetooth list and make sure media audio or call audio is turned on.
- **Car stereo:** set the car's source to Bluetooth audio.

## When to call a pro

Contact the accessory maker, your carrier or a repair shop if:

- The accessory won't pair with **any** phone or computer, even after a reset. It may be faulty, and it may be under warranty.
- Your phone can't see **any** Bluetooth device after a network settings reset and a software update.
- Bluetooth won't turn on at all, or the switch is grayed out.
- Problems started after a drop or water exposure.

Before you go, back up your phone in case it needs repair.
