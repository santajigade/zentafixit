Refrigerators are never fully silent. The compressor hums, fans whir, refrigerant gurgles and the liner pops. Most of these sounds are normal, and a new fridge often sounds different from the old one.

A sound is worth checking when it is new, louder than usual, or comes with the fridge warming up. The most common problem noises are **a buzzing water valve on an ice maker with no water**, **a squealing evaporator fan**, **a condenser fan hitting debris**, and **clicking from a compressor that won't start**. Where and when you hear it tells you which part to look at.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the refrigerator before removing any panel, cleaning the coils or touching a fan. Condenser fan blades can start without warning while the fridge is plugged in. Never chip ice with a sharp tool or bend the tubing: puncturing a refrigerant line releases refrigerant and means a sealed system repair. Sealed system work must be done by an EPA-certified technician.

## How to tell what's making the noise: quick test

1. **Note when it happens.** Right after the compressor starts, while the ice maker fills, after you close the door, or all the time? Timing narrows it down fast.
2. **Find where it comes from.** The freezer back wall, the ice maker, or the bottom near the floor?
3. **Open the freezer door.** On many models the evaporator fan pauses while the door is open or the door switch is released. If the noise stops, the fan is the likely source. Designs vary and some fans keep running, so check your manual.
4. **Turn the ice maker off.** If a buzzing sound stops coming back, it was the water valve.
5. **Unplug the fridge and listen.** A mechanical noise from a fan or the compressor stops at once. Gurgling or dripping that fades over a few minutes is refrigerant or defrost water settling, which is normal.

## 1. Humming or a low buzz from the back

**Normal:** the compressor hums while it runs, and the hum stops when it cycles off. Many newer fridges use variable-speed (inverter) compressors that run longer at lower speed, so you may hear a quiet hum most of the time. That is how they are designed to work.

**Problem:** a hum that is much louder than usual, a hum with the fridge getting warm, or a hum that starts and stops with a click every few minutes (see section 5).

**Fix:**

- Clean the condenser coils (section 8). Dirty coils make the compressor run longer and louder.
- If the hum is loud and cooling is poor, call an appliance technician.

## 2. Gurgling, hissing or sizzling

**Normal:** refrigerant flowing through the tubing gurgles and hisses, especially right after the compressor starts or stops. During defrost, water dripping onto the warm heater sizzles.

**Problem:** a constant loud hiss with poor cooling, or oily spots near the tubing at the back or bottom. That can mean a refrigerant leak.

**Fix:**

- Nothing is needed for normal gurgles and sizzles.
- For a suspected leak, unplug the fridge, don't touch the tubing and call an EPA-certified appliance technician.

## 3. Cracking, popping or ice dropping

**Normal:** the plastic liner and shelves expand and contract as the inside temperature changes, especially during and after defrost. You will also hear a crack or clatter when the ice maker releases a batch of cubes into the bin.

**Problem:** almost never on its own. If loud popping comes with a door that won't seal, check the gasket and leveling (section 8).

**Fix:**

- No fix is needed. If ice dropping bothers you at night, you can usually switch the ice maker off for a while.

## 4. Loud buzzing every few minutes

**Normal:** a short buzz or hum for a few seconds while the water inlet valve opens to fill the ice maker or dispenser.

**Problem:** a loud buzz that repeats every few minutes. The most common cause is an ice maker that is switched on with **no water line connected** or the water supply shut off. The valve keeps opening, gets no water, and buzzes each time.

**Fix:**

1. If your fridge has no water line, turn the ice maker off using its switch, wire arm or control panel setting. Check your manual for where it is.
2. If it does have a water line, make sure the shutoff valve behind or under the fridge is fully open and the line isn't kinked.
3. A clogged water filter can also restrict flow. Replace it if it is overdue.

If the buzzing continues with water flowing and the ice maker still isn't making ice, see our [ice maker troubleshooting guide](/appliances/ice-maker-not-working/).

## 5. Clicking, then the compressor won't start

**Normal:** a single click when the compressor starts or stops, or when the defrost cycle switches on.

**Problem:** a click every few minutes with no compressor hum afterward, and the fridge slowly getting warm. This usually means the start relay or overload protector is trying to start the compressor and failing. The fault can be the relay or the compressor itself.

**Fix:**

- Clean the condenser coils (section 8) and make sure there is space around the fridge for airflow, because an overheated compressor can trip its overload.
- Move perishable food to a cooler while you arrange a repair.
- Call an appliance technician to test the relay, overload and compressor. A compressor replacement is a sealed system job.

## 6. Squealing, chirping or whirring in the freezer

**Normal:** a soft, steady whir of air from the freezer vents while the fan runs.

**Problem:** a squeal, chirp or uneven whir that changes or stops when you open the freezer door. That points to the **evaporator fan motor**, which sits behind the freezer back panel. A worn motor often gets noisy before it fails.

**Fix:**

1. Do the door test from the quick test to confirm it's the fan.
2. Unplug the fridge, empty the freezer and remove the back panel following your manual.
3. Check that nothing is touching the blade and that the blade isn't cracked.
4. If the motor is still noisy, replace it with the part for your model number. If you're not comfortable removing panels, an appliance technician can do it.

## 7. Scraping from a fan hitting ice

**Normal:** never.

**Problem:** a scraping, ticking or grinding sound from the freezer wall means the evaporator fan blade is hitting ice. Frost builds up around the fan when the defrost system (heater, thermostat or control) stops melting it.

**Fix:**

1. Move food to coolers, unplug the fridge and prop the doors open with towels down.
2. Let all the ice melt, often 24 hours or more. Don't use sharp tools or heat guns.
3. Plug it back in. If the noise and frost return within days or weeks, a defrost part has failed. Our guide to a [refrigerator not cooling while the freezer works](/appliances/refrigerator-not-cooling-freezer-works/) explains the defrost system.

## 8. Rattling or grinding at the bottom

**Normal:** a light vibration while the compressor runs.

**Problem:** a rattle, grind or tick from near the floor. On models with coils underneath, a **condenser fan** cools the coils and compressor, and its blade can hit debris, dust or a loose part. Other causes are a loose drain pan, dirty coils, or a fridge that isn't level.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the fridge.** Remove the front kick plate or the rear access panel.
2. Check the condenser fan blade for lint, pet hair or debris. Make sure it spins freely by hand. A fan that wobbles or grinds needs replacing.
3. Clean the condenser coils with a coil brush and a vacuum, gently, without bending the tubing or fins.
4. Check the drain pan is seated properly and not touching the fan or compressor.
5. Put a bubble level on top of the fridge. Adjust the front leveling legs or rollers so it's steady and the front is slightly higher than the back. Check your manual for the recommended tilt. The doors should swing shut on their own.

## 9. Knocking or banging

**Normal:** a single soft thud when the compressor shuts off, as it settles on its mounts.

**Problem:** repeated knocking or banging. Worn or loose compressor mounts let it shake inside the cabinet, or the water line behind the fridge hits the wall when the inlet valve opens and closes.

**Fix:**

- Unplug the fridge, pull it out slowly and look for a water line or loose tubing tapping the wall. Coil the line loosely and secure it so it can't touch the wall.
- Make sure the fridge isn't touching the wall or cabinets, and nothing loose sits on top or behind it.
- If the knock comes from the compressor itself, call an appliance technician.

## Noisy and getting warm?

If a noise comes with the fridge or freezer warming up, protect your food first. Keep the fridge at 40°F or below and the freezer at 0°F, and move perishables to a cooler with ice if the fridge rises above 40°F. Then check the clues:

- **Clicking with no hum:** the compressor isn't starting (section 5).
- **Freezer quiet or scraping:** the evaporator fan has stopped or is iced (sections 6 and 7).

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a qualified appliance technician if:

- The fridge clicks every few minutes and the compressor doesn't start.
- The compressor is loud, knocks, or runs nonstop while the fridge stays warm.
- You hear a loud hiss, see oily residue on the tubing, or smell burning.
- Frost and scraping come back after a full manual defrost.

Anything involving refrigerant or the compressor is a sealed system repair that requires an EPA-certified technician. On an older fridge, ask for an estimate first, because a compressor repair can approach the cost of a new refrigerator.
