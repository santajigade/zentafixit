**Stick drift** is when a controller's thumbstick sends movement while you're not touching it. Your camera slowly pans, your character walks off a ledge, or menus scroll on their own.

Most of the time it's caused by **worn sensors inside the stick**, **dust or debris** under the stick, or a **calibration problem** after an update or a knock. Some of these you can fix at home in a few minutes. Worn sensors need a repair or a replacement controller.

## How to tell if it's really stick drift

1. **Put the controller down** on a flat surface in a game or menu. If the view or cursor still moves, the stick is sending input on its own.
2. **Try a second controller** on the same console or PC. If the problem stays, it's a game or system setting, not the stick.
3. **Try the drifting controller on another device**, such as a PC. Drift that follows the controller is a controller problem.
4. **Use a stick tester.** Many consoles and PC tools show stick position. A dot that sits off center or jitters at rest confirms drift.

## 1. Restart, recharge and re-pair

A low battery or a confused wireless connection can cause erratic input that looks like drift.

**Fix:**

- Charge the controller fully or fit fresh batteries.
- Turn the console or PC off completely and back on, not just sleep mode.
- Re-pair the controller, or connect it with a USB cable as a test.
- Many controllers have a small reset button on the back. Check your manual for its location and how to use it.

## 2. Update the controller firmware

Controllers have their own firmware, and makers release updates that can improve how stick input is read.

**Fix:**

- **Xbox controllers:** use the **Xbox Accessories** app on the console or on a Windows PC to check for updates.
- **PlayStation controllers:** the console usually prompts you when a controller update is available. Keep the console updated and accept the prompt.
- **Nintendo Switch:** look for **Update Controllers** under Controllers and Sensors in System Settings.
- **Third-party controllers:** check the maker's app or support page.

## 3. Recalibrate the sticks

Calibration tells the controller where "center" is. If that point is off after a knock or update, the stick reads as slightly pushed at rest.

**Fix:**

- **Nintendo Switch:** System Settings > Controllers and Sensors > **Calibrate Control Sticks**. Follow the on-screen steps.
- **PC:** in Windows, search for "Set up USB game controllers", select the controller, then open Properties. Some controllers show a **Calibrate** option there. Steam also offers controller calibration in its controller settings for many gamepads.
- **Xbox and PlayStation:** the consoles don't offer a general stick calibration tool for standard controllers. Skip to deadzone settings, and check your controller's app if it's a premium or third-party model.

Leave the sticks untouched when the calibration asks for the center position.

## 4. Raise the deadzone in game settings

The **deadzone** is a small area around the center where the game ignores input. If your drift is small, a slightly larger deadzone hides it completely.

**Fix:**

- Open the game's **controls** or **controller** settings and look for "deadzone", "inner deadzone" or "stick sensitivity". The name and location vary by game.
- Increase it in small steps until the drift stops. Too large and fine aiming feels sluggish.
- Some controller apps and Steam Input let you set a deadzone for all games at once.

This doesn't repair the stick. It buys time while drift is mild.

## 5. Clean around the stick with compressed air

Dust, pet hair and snack crumbs can get under the rubber skirt at the base of the stick and interfere with the sensor.

**Fix:**

1. Turn the controller off and unplug it.
2. Hold the can of compressed air **upright** and use short bursts.
3. Push the stick to one side and blow air into the gap at its base. Rotate the stick slowly and repeat all the way around.
4. Click the stick down a few times and roll it in full circles to work loose any debris.
5. Turn it on and test again.

Don't spray liquids, contact cleaner or lubricant into the controller from outside. Liquid can reach the circuit board and cause more problems than it solves.

## 6. Use the warranty or a repair service

If drift returns or gets worse after cleaning and calibration, the sensor is worn. The real fix is a new stick module or a new controller.

**Fix:**

- **Check your warranty first.** Visit the controller maker's support site, look up your warranty status and start a repair request. Drift from normal use is commonly accepted as a fault within the warranty period. Even out of warranty, it's worth asking what the maker offers.
- **Store warranty or protection plan:** if you bought one, check whether it covers controllers.
- **Repair shops:** local game and phone repair shops often replace stick modules. Prices vary, so compare the quote with the cost of a new controller.

> **Before you open it:** Opening the controller usually **voids the warranty**. Replacing a stick module on most controllers involves soldering small joints on the circuit board. Only try it if the warranty has ended and you're comfortable with fine soldering.

## Drift that won't go away?

If you've replaced the controller once already and drift returns, consider a controller with **Hall effect sticks**. They read stick position with magnets instead of a sliding contact, so there's no track to wear out. To see how this kind of sensor works, read this [Hall effect sensor explainer](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/how-a-hall-effect-sensor-works/).

You can also reduce wear on any controller:

- Store it where dust and crumbs can't get in, and wash your hands before long sessions.
- Avoid pushing sticks hard against their edges for long periods.
- Don't leave the controller with a stick pressed down under its weight, such as face down on a couch cushion.

## Drift only on a PC?

If the controller behaves on a console but drifts on a computer, the cause may be software rather than the stick.

- **Two programs reading the same controller.** Steam Input, the maker's app and a game's own controller support can all apply their own settings. Close extra controller apps, or turn Steam Input on or off for that game, and test again.
- **An old calibration saved in Windows.** In "Set up USB game controllers", some controllers have a **Reset to default** button under Properties. Use it, then recalibrate.
- **A wireless adapter or Bluetooth issue.** Try a USB cable. If the drift disappears, update the adapter's driver or the PC's Bluetooth driver.

## When to call a pro

Send the controller for repair, or contact the maker's support, if:

- Drift continues after a firmware update, calibration and cleaning.
- The stick feels loose, clicks oddly or doesn't spring back to center.
- The controller is still under warranty. Let the maker fix it before trying any DIY repair.
- Other buttons or the triggers also misbehave, which suggests a board fault.
