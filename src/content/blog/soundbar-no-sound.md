When a soundbar is on but silent, the problem is almost always a setting or a port, not a broken speaker. The most common causes are the **soundbar on the wrong input**, the **TV still sending sound to its own speakers**, the **HDMI cable in the wrong port**, **HDMI-CEC switched off**, or an **audio format the soundbar can't play over optical**.

Start by checking which cable connects your TV and soundbar: **HDMI** (a wide, flat plug) or **optical** (a small square plug, often with a red light glowing inside). The fixes depend on which one you use.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the soundbar's display or lights.** Is it on, and which input is it showing (HDMI, ARC, TV, optical, Bluetooth)?
2. **Press volume up on the soundbar's own remote or buttons.** If the volume display changes but there's no sound, the signal isn't arriving (fixes 1 to 5).
3. **Check the TV's speakers.** If sound still comes from the TV, the TV's audio output setting is wrong (fix 2).
4. **Play something from a different source**, such as a built-in TV app and then a cable box or game console. If only one source is silent, see fix 5 and the section on one source near the end.

## 1. Set the soundbar to the right input

Soundbars have several inputs, and pressing the wrong button on the remote can switch it to Bluetooth or another input. Many soundbars switch automatically, but not all.

**Fix:**

- Press the **input** or **source** button on the soundbar or its remote until it shows the input that matches your cable: **HDMI ARC** (sometimes shown as ARC, eARC or TV) or **optical**.
- If you're not sure which label your soundbar uses, check the manual. Cycle slowly, waiting a few seconds on each input.

## 2. Change the TV audio output setting

The TV decides where to send sound. If it's set to its own speakers, the soundbar gets nothing.

**Fix:**

- Open the TV's **sound** or **audio** settings and find **sound output**, **speakers** or **audio output**.
- Choose the soundbar, **HDMI ARC**, **receiver**, **external speaker** or **optical**, whichever matches your connection.
- If the TV has a separate **TV speakers on or off** option, turn the TV speakers off.
- Menu names vary by brand. Your TV's manual or the maker's support site will show the exact steps.

## 3. Use the HDMI ARC or eARC port

Only one HDMI port on most TVs can send audio out to a soundbar. It's labeled **ARC** or **eARC**. If the cable is in a different HDMI port, the soundbar stays silent. The same goes for the soundbar: use the port labeled **ARC**, **eARC** or **TV**, often marked HDMI OUT.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off both devices.
2. Move the HDMI cable to the **ARC or eARC port** on the TV and the **ARC, eARC or TV port** on the soundbar.
3. Push each plug in fully. Wall-mounted TVs often have loose plugs.
4. Use a **High Speed HDMI cable with Ethernet** or an **Ultra High Speed** cable. eARC works best with Ultra High Speed. Older or very cheap cables can fail.
5. Turn on the TV first, then the soundbar.

For the difference between the two, see [HDMI ARC vs eARC](/tv-audio/hdmi-arc-vs-earc/).

## 4. Turn on HDMI-CEC on the TV

HDMI ARC relies on **HDMI-CEC**, a feature that lets devices control each other over HDMI. On most TVs, ARC won't work if CEC is off. CEC is also what lets your TV remote control the soundbar's volume.

**Fix:**

- Find CEC in the TV's settings, usually under **general**, **external devices**, **connections** or **inputs**.
- TV makers often use their own name for it, for example **Anynet+** (Samsung), **SIMPLINK** (LG) or **BRAVIA Sync** (Sony). Others simply call it HDMI-CEC or HDMI control. Check your manual if you can't find it.
- Some soundbars also have a CEC or **HDMI control** setting. Turn it on there too.
- If the TV has an **eARC** setting, set it to **auto** or **on** if your soundbar supports eARC.

## 5. Set optical or digital audio to PCM

Through an optical cable, the TV can send stereo (PCM) or a compressed surround format such as Dolby Digital or DTS. If the soundbar can't decode the format the TV sends, you get silence, often only on some channels, apps or movies.

**Fix:**

- In the TV's sound settings, find **digital audio out**, **digital output format** or **audio format**, and set it to **PCM** (or stereo).
- If your soundbar supports Dolby Digital, you can try **auto** later. If the silence comes back, go back to PCM.
- Check that both ends of the optical cable have their **clear plastic caps removed**. New cables ship with them on.
- Push the plug in until it clicks, and make sure you see a **red light** at the cable end when the TV is on. No light usually means the TV's optical output is off or the cable is faulty.
- Don't bend optical cables tightly. A sharp kink can break the fiber.

## 6. Check volume and mute on both

It sounds obvious, but the TV and soundbar can each have their own volume and mute.

**Fix:**

- Press **mute** on the soundbar remote, then press it again, and turn the soundbar volume up.
- With optical, the TV remote often doesn't change the soundbar volume unless the TV has a variable output setting. Use the soundbar remote.
- Check that **night mode** or a similar setting isn't set to very low volume.
- If a subwoofer is silent but the bar plays, check that the sub is powered and paired. Many subwoofers have a pairing button. See the manual.

## 7. Power cycle the TV and soundbar

HDMI and CEC handshakes can get stuck, especially after a power cut or a TV update. A full restart in the right order clears them.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the TV and soundbar.
2. **Unplug both from the wall** and wait **60 seconds**.
3. Plug in and turn on the **TV first**, then the **soundbar**.
4. If you have a cable box, game console or streaming stick, turn it on last.
5. Check for a **firmware update** for both the TV and the soundbar (in settings or the maker's app).

## Only one source is silent?

If TV apps play through the soundbar but a game console, cable box or Blu-ray player doesn't, check that device's **audio output** setting. Set it to **PCM** or **auto**, or try **bitstream** if your soundbar supports surround formats. If the device plugs into the soundbar rather than the TV, check the soundbar's manual for passthrough settings.

## When to call a pro

Contact the soundbar or TV maker, or a home theater installer, if:

- The soundbar makes no sound from **any** input, including Bluetooth.
- The TV's ARC port won't work with a known-good soundbar and cable, after a reset and update. The port may be faulty.
- You hear crackling, popping or buzzing from the soundbar itself.
- You see damage to an HDMI port, or a burning smell.

Check whether the TV or soundbar is still under warranty before paying for a repair.
