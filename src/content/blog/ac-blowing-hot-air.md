When your AC blows warm or hot air, the indoor blower is working but the system isn't removing heat. On a central air system, the cooling happens at the **outdoor unit**, so the question is usually why it's off, blocked, or not doing its job.

The most common causes are **a thermostat set to the wrong mode**, **a clogged air filter**, **a tripped outdoor breaker or disconnect**, and **a full condensate pan that shut the system off**. Most of these are quick checks you can do yourself before you call anyone.

> **Safety first:** Turn the system OFF at the thermostat and switch off the AC breaker and the outdoor disconnect before you touch or clean anything at the outdoor unit. Never open the outdoor unit's electrical panel: the capacitor inside can hold a dangerous charge even with the power off. Refrigerant work requires a licensed technician.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the thermostat.** It should say COOL (not HEAT, AUTO or FAN), with the set temperature a few degrees below the room temperature.
2. **Feel the air at a vent** after 10 minutes. Is it room temperature, or hotter than the room? Hotter than the room often points to a heat pump in heat mode or ducts pulling in attic air.
3. **Go outside and listen.** Is the outdoor unit silent, humming, or running with its top fan spinning?
4. **Look at the indoor unit.** Is there water in the drain pan, or ice on the copper lines or coil?
5. **Check the filter.** If you can't see light through it, it's too dirty.

## 1. Thermostat mode or fan setting

A thermostat set to FAN or ON (instead of AUTO) runs the blower all the time, even when the AC isn't cooling, so you feel room temperature air between cooling cycles. A thermostat set to HEAT, or AUTO with a high heating setpoint, may be heating the house.

**Fix:**

- Set the mode to **COOL** and the fan to **AUTO**.
- Set the temperature at least 3 °F below the current room temperature.
- If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries (if it uses them). A blank screen can also mean a tripped breaker or a blown fuse on the indoor unit.
- Give the system 5 to 10 minutes. Many thermostats and systems have a built-in delay before the compressor starts.

## 2. A clogged air filter

A dirty filter chokes the airflow over the indoor coil. Cooling drops, and the coil can get cold enough to freeze. Once it ices over, air barely gets cooled at all.

**Fix:**

- Turn the system OFF and pull out the filter (at the indoor unit or behind a return grille).
- Replace it with the same size, printed on the filter's edge. The arrow points toward the blower.
- If you see ice on the coil or copper lines, leave the system OFF with the fan set to ON for a few hours to thaw it. See [AC unit freezing up](/hvac/ac-unit-freezing-up/) for the full steps.

## 3. Outdoor unit has no power

If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit is completely silent, it may have lost power. The outdoor unit has its own 240 V breaker in the main panel and usually a disconnect box on the wall next to it.

**Fix:**

- Check the main panel for a tripped breaker labeled AC, condenser or heat pump. Switch it fully OFF, then ON.
- Check the disconnect box by the outdoor unit. Make sure the handle or pull-out block is in the ON position.
- Set the thermostat back to COOL and wait several minutes.
- If the breaker trips again, leave it OFF and call a technician. Repeated tripping points to a failing compressor, fan motor or wiring fault.

## 4. A failed capacitor or contactor (pro only)

The capacitor gives the outdoor fan motor and compressor a boost to start. The contactor is the switch that turns the outdoor unit on when the thermostat calls for cooling. When either fails, the unit may hum without the fan spinning, or not start at all. Both are common outdoor unit repairs.

**Fix:**

- Turn the system OFF at the thermostat so the compressor doesn't sit straining.
- Call an HVAC technician. Do not open the outdoor unit's electrical panel or push the fan with a stick: the capacitor can shock you even with the power off, and the fan can start suddenly.

## 5. The condensate safety switch shut it off

Your AC pulls moisture out of the air and drains it away. If the drain line clogs, water backs up into the pan, and a float switch (a safety switch) cuts power to the outdoor unit to prevent a leak. The blower may keep running, so you get warm air.

**Fix:**

- Turn the system OFF and look in the drain pan under the indoor coil, and at any float switch on the drain line.
- Use a wet/dry vacuum on the outside end of the drain line (often near the outdoor unit or a floor drain) to pull out the clog.
- Empty and dry the pan. Once the water drops, the float switch resets on its own.
- If water is already on the floor, see [AC leaking water inside](/hvac/ac-leaking-water-inside/).

## 6. A dirty or blocked outdoor coil

The outdoor coil releases the heat taken from your house. If it's covered in dirt, grass clippings, cottonwood fluff or leaves, or boxed in by plants, it can't shed heat. The air may be cool at first, then turn warm on hot afternoons.

**Fix:**

- Turn off the breaker and disconnect first.
- Clear plants and debris to leave about 2 feet of open space around the unit (check your manual for its recommendation).
- Rinse the coil fins gently from the inside outward if you can, or from the outside, with a garden hose on a gentle setting. Never use a pressure washer, which bends the thin fins.
- Restore power and test.

## 7. Low refrigerant from a leak (pro only)

Refrigerant doesn't get used up. If it's low, there's a leak. Signs include weak cooling that gets worse over weeks, ice on the large copper line, and a hissing or bubbling sound.

**Fix:** Turn the system OFF and call a licensed HVAC technician. They need to find and fix the leak, then recharge the system. Topping it up without fixing the leak only buys a short time, and in the US, handling refrigerant requires EPA certification.

## 8. Leaky ducts in a hot attic

If the ducts run through a hot attic or crawl space, a torn or disconnected duct can pull in hot air or dump your cool air where it does no good. The tell: one or two rooms get warm air while the rest of the house is fine, or the air at the vent is hotter than the room.

**Fix:**

- Make sure every supply vent in the warm rooms is open.
- If you can safely get into the attic (watch your footing on the joists and avoid the heat of the day), look for ducts that have fallen apart or have torn insulation.
- Small joints can be sealed with metal foil tape or duct mastic. Have an HVAC contractor repair crushed, disconnected or long runs of damaged duct.

## Heat pump heating when it should cool?

A heat pump switches between heating and cooling with a reversing valve. If the thermostat is set to COOL, the outdoor unit runs, and the air coming out is clearly hot, the valve or the thermostat wiring that controls it may be the problem. Some thermostats also need to be told whether the valve is energized in cooling or heating mode during setup. Check your thermostat's manual, and if it's set up correctly, call a technician. Running a heat pump in the wrong mode won't damage it quickly, but it won't cool your house either.

If you have an air handler with electric heat strips, a heat relay that is stuck on can also blow warm air with the AC running. That's a job for a technician.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a pro if:

- The outdoor unit hums but the fan doesn't spin, or it doesn't start at all with power on.
- The AC breaker trips again after one reset.
- You see ice on the lines, hear hissing, or suspect a refrigerant leak.
- The thermostat is set correctly, the filter is clean, the coil is clear, and the air is still warm.
- You smell burning or see scorch marks: turn it off at the breaker right away.

Tell the technician what you already checked and what you saw outside. It saves them time and can save you money on the visit. Many warm air problems, like a capacitor, contactor or clogged drain, are quick repairs once they're found.
