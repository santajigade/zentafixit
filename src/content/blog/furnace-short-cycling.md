A furnace that keeps turning on and off is **short cycling**: it starts, runs for a short time, shuts off before your home reaches the set temperature, then starts again. Sometimes it lights and goes out within seconds. Other times it heats for a few minutes, stops, and the blower keeps running.

The most common causes are **a clogged air filter tripping the high-limit switch, closed or blocked vents, a thermostat in a bad spot, a dirty flame sensor and, on high-efficiency furnaces, a blocked vent or intake pipe outside**. Most of these you can check yourself in under an hour.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Replace the air filter, open all supply vents and clear the return grilles. > **Safety first:** If you smell gas (a rotten egg smell), don't touch any switches or the thermostat. Get everyone out of the house and call your gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. If your carbon monoxide alarm goes off, leave right away and call 911. Turn off the furnace power switch before removing any panel. Never bypass, jump or remove a limit switch, rollout switch or pressure switch.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Read any status light first.** Look through the small window in the lower furnace door and note the blink pattern before you open anything.
2. **Set the thermostat** to HEAT, a few degrees above room temperature, with the fan on AUTO.
3. **Watch one full cycle at the furnace.** Note when it stops: seconds after the flame lights, after a few minutes of heating, or after what looks like a normal cycle.
4. **Sort it out:**
    - **Flame goes out within seconds:** cause 5.
    - **Inducer fan runs, burners stop or never light:** causes 6 and 7.
    - **Heats for a few minutes, burners stop, blower keeps running:** causes 1, 2 and 9.
    - **Normal cycle, just too short and too often:** causes 3, 4 and 8.

## 1. Clogged air filter tripping the high limit

This is one of the most common causes. A dirty filter chokes airflow, heat builds up inside the furnace, and the **high-limit switch** shuts the burners off to protect the heat exchanger. The blower keeps running until things cool, then the furnace lights again and the loop repeats. After enough trips, some furnaces lock out.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the furnace power switch.
2. Slide out the filter and note the size printed on the frame.
3. Replace it if it's grey or clogged, with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
4. Turn the power back on and watch a cycle.

See [how often to change your furnace filter](/hvac/how-often-change-furnace-filter/) for a schedule that fits your home. A very restrictive high-MERV filter can also cut airflow on some systems, so check what your furnace manual recommends.

## 2. Closed or blocked supply and return vents

Closing lots of supply registers to "save heat" in unused rooms, or blocking return grilles with furniture, rugs or drapes, has the same effect as a dirty filter: less airflow and an overheating furnace.

**Fix:**

- Open supply vents throughout the house, including in rooms you don't use much.
- Clear at least several inches around every return grille, and vacuum dust off it.
- If you have dampers in the ductwork, make sure nobody has closed them for the season.

## 3. Thermostat in a bad spot

The thermostat shuts the furnace off when it thinks the room is warm enough. If warm air from a **supply register** blows on it, or it sits in **direct sun**, near a lamp, TV or kitchen, it warms up fast and ends each cycle early. A draft from an outside door or the wall hole behind it can do the opposite and start the furnace too often.

**Fix:**

- Check what's near it: registers, windows, appliances, doors.
- Put a separate thermometer beside it for 15 minutes and compare readings.
- Redirect or deflect a nearby register, close blinds at the sunny time of day, and seal the wire hole behind the thermostat with a little insulation if you feel a draft.
- If the spot is simply wrong, a pro can move the thermostat to an inside wall away from vents and windows.

## 4. Thermostat settings, batteries or wiring

Many thermostats have a **cycle rate**, **cycles per hour** or **swing (differential)** setting. If it's set for a different type of system, or set to cycle very often, the furnace runs short bursts even though nothing is broken.

**Fix:**

- Look in the thermostat's installer or advanced settings for the system type and cycle rate. Your manual lists the recommended setting for a gas furnace, so don't guess.
- Replace **low batteries**. A weak battery can make some thermostats drop the call for heat and restart.
- With power off, remove the thermostat face and check for **loose wires** at the terminals. A wire that makes and breaks contact can start and stop the furnace.

## 5. Dirty flame sensor

The **flame sensor** is a thin metal rod in the burner flame. It proves to the control board that the gas is burning. When residue coats it, the furnace lights, can't "see" the flame and **shuts the gas off within seconds**. It retries, fails again and may lock out.

**Fix:**

- Cleaning it is a common job: power and gas off, remove the sensor (often one screw), lightly rub the rod with a fine abrasive pad or emery cloth, wipe it and refit it. Our [furnace not turning on](/hvac/furnace-not-turning-on/) guide shows where it sits.
- If you're not comfortable working near the burners and gas valve, call a technician. It's a quick, routine visit.
- If cleaning doesn't help, the sensor, its wire or the ground connection may need a pro.

## 6. Blocked vent or intake pipe

**High-efficiency furnaces** usually vent through two **white plastic (PVC) pipes** on an outside wall or the roof: one pulls in air, one carries exhaust out. Snow, ice, leaves, a nest or an insect screen clogged with frost can block them. The furnace senses it and shuts down, often right after the inducer fan starts or shortly after lighting.

**Fix:**

- Go outside and find where the pipes end. Clear snow, ice and debris **by hand**, gently, without bending or loosening the pipes.
- Keep the area around them clear of drifts during snowstorms.
- On older furnaces with a metal flue into a chimney, you can't safely inspect it yourself. If you see soot, rust or water at the flue, or a CO alarm has gone off, call a technician.

## 7. Pressure switch or condensate problems

The **pressure switch** confirms the inducer fan is moving combustion gases out before and during burning. If the vent is restricted, the switch's small rubber tube is cracked or full of water, or a high-efficiency furnace's **condensate trap** is clogged, the switch opens and the furnace shuts off.

**Fix:**

- Clear the outside pipes first (cause 6).
- Power off and check the condensate trap and drain hose for sludge or kinks. Check your manual before removing the trap.
- Look at the rubber tube to the switch for cracks or a loose end, and note any blinking code.
- Don't replace the switch just because a code names it. It's often correctly reporting a venting or drain problem. Never jump it.

## 8. Furnace is too big for the house (pro)

An **oversized furnace** heats the air near the thermostat so fast that it satisfies the set point in a short burst, shuts off, and the house cools again quickly. Everything works, but cycles stay short, rooms far from the thermostat stay cool, and the system wears faster.

**Fix:** Rule out causes 1 to 4 first. Then ask an HVAC contractor to do a proper heat load calculation (often called a Manual J) for your home. Some two-stage or modulating furnaces handle this better, and thermostat settings can help a little, but a badly oversized unit is a replacement-time decision.

## 9. Failing limit switch or control board (pro)

If the filter is new, vents are open and it still trips on the limit, the problem may be a **weak blower motor or capacitor**, a **failing limit switch** that opens too early, or a **control board** fault. These involve line voltage and gas controls.

**Fix:** Write down the blink pattern and call a technician. A limit that trips with good airflow can also point to a heat exchanger problem, which needs a professional inspection.

## Furnace locks out or blinks a code?

- **Read the code** against the chart on the inside of the furnace door. Our [furnace blinking light codes](/hvac/furnace-blinking-light-codes/) guide explains how to count and match it.
- **Reset once:** power switch off, wait about 30 seconds, back on.
- **Same code again?** Stop resetting. Repeated limit, flame or pressure switch trips point to a real fault, and each failed start is a reason to get it checked.

## When to call an HVAC technician

Call a licensed HVAC technician if:

- You **smell gas** (after leaving the house and calling the gas company from outside).
- Your **carbon monoxide alarm** sounds. Get everyone out and call 911 first.
- It keeps short cycling with a clean filter, open vents and a well-placed thermostat.
- The same code returns after one reset, or the chart points to a limit, rollout, pressure switch or control board fault.
- You see soot, scorch marks, rust or water around the burners or flue.
- You suspect the furnace is oversized.

An annual tune-up before winter checks the flame sensor, venting, pressure switch and airflow, which covers most of the causes in this guide.
