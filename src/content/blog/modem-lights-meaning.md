The lights on the front of your modem are a quick status report. They show whether the modem has power, whether it can **hear** your internet provider (downstream), whether it can **talk** back (upstream), whether it is **online**, and whether your router or computer is connected.

Most cable modems use the same basic set of lights, but **names, colors and blink patterns vary by brand and model**. Use this guide for the general meaning, then **check the label on your modem or its manual** for the exact details.

<div class="key"><strong>Quick answer</strong><p>Solid usually means ready and blinking usually means connecting or sending data. On a working cable modem, power, downstream, upstream and online are solid, and Ethernet is on or flickering. If the online light is off or keeps blinking, restart the modem, check the cables and check for an outage.</p></div>

## Read the lights in order

The lights usually come on in the same order the modem starts up. Where they stop tells you where the connection is failing.

1. **Find the labels.** Look at the front panel, the label on the bottom or the manual. Note the name of each light.
2. **Watch a restart.** Unplug the power for 30 seconds, plug it back in and watch.
3. **Note which light stops** at blinking or stays off. That's the step that is failing.
4. **Give it time.** Most modems finish within a few minutes. Don't restart it again while the lights are still changing.

## 1. Power light

Shows the modem has power and has started.

- **Solid:** powered on and running normally.
- **Off:** no power. Check the power adapter, the outlet and any power strip switch.
- **Blinking or a different color:** on some models this means it's starting, updating firmware or has a fault. Check the manual.

If the power light stays off with a known-good outlet, the power adapter or the modem may have failed.

## 2. Downstream (DS) or Receive light

Shows the modem has found and locked onto the signal **from** your provider. It may be labeled **DS**, **Receive**, a down arrow, or similar.

- **Blinking:** scanning for a signal. This is normal for a short time after a restart.
- **Solid:** locked onto the downstream signal.
- **Keeps blinking:** the modem can't find a usable signal. Causes include a loose or damaged coax cable, a splitter, an outage, or a problem outside your home.

## 3. Upstream (US) or Send light

Shows the modem can send data **back** to your provider. It may be labeled **US**, **Send**, or an up arrow.

- **Blinking:** connecting upstream. Normal for a short time after the downstream light goes solid.
- **Solid:** connected upstream.
- **Keeps blinking:** the modem can hear the provider but can't talk back reliably. This often points to a signal problem on the cable line or at the provider's end.

## 4. Online or Internet light

This is the light that matters most. It may be labeled **Online**, **Internet**, a globe or an @ symbol.

- **Blinking:** registering with your provider. Normal for a short time at startup.
- **Solid:** the modem is connected to your provider and internet service should be working.
- **Off or blinking for a long time:** no internet connection. See [Online light off? What to do](#online-light-off-what-to-do) below.

If this light is solid but your devices still can't load anything, the problem is more likely your router or devices. See [Wi-Fi connected but no internet](/networking/wifi-connected-no-internet/).

## 5. Ethernet or LAN lights

Show a wired connection between the modem and your router or computer. There may be one per port, sometimes numbered.

- **Solid:** a device is connected and the link is up.
- **Flickering:** data is moving. This is normal.
- **Off:** no link. Check both ends of the Ethernet cable, try a different cable or port, and make sure the router or computer is powered on.
- **Different colors:** on some models, the color shows the link speed (for example, one color for gigabit and another for slower speeds). Check the manual.

## 6. Wi-Fi and WPS lights

These only appear on a **gateway**, a modem with a built-in router. A plain modem has no Wi-Fi.

- **Wi-Fi solid or flickering:** Wi-Fi is on. Separate lights may show 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz.
- **Wi-Fi off:** Wi-Fi is turned off or has a problem. Some gateways have a Wi-Fi button that toggles it.
- **WPS blinking:** the gateway is waiting for a device to connect with the WPS button. It stops after a couple of minutes.

## 7. Phone, Tel or Voice lights

Found on modems that also provide home phone service from your provider.

- **Solid:** the phone line is set up and ready.
- **Blinking:** a call is in progress, or the phone is off the hook, depending on the model.
- **Off:** phone service isn't active on that port. That's normal if you don't have phone service.

## What the colors mean

<figure><img src="/images/posts/modem-lights-meaning-flow.svg" alt="Flowchart for when the online light is off: check power, then whether downstream and upstream are solid, check coax cable and splitters, restart, check for an outage, then contact your provider" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Follow the lights from power to online to find where it stops.</figcaption></figure>

Colors are the least standard part. In general:

- **Green, white or blue** usually means normal operation.
- **Amber, orange or red** often means a problem or a slower or limited connection.
- On some cable modems, the downstream and upstream color shows **how many channels are bonded**. One color can mean one channel and another color multiple channels, which gives faster speeds.
- Some newer modems and gateways have a **single status light** that changes color or blink pattern for each state.

Because this varies so much, **always confirm with the chart on the label, in the manual or on the maker's support site**. Don't assume that orange means broken on your model.

## DSL and fiber lights

Not every internet connection uses a cable modem.

- **DSL modems** have a **DSL** or **Sync** light instead of downstream and upstream. Blinking usually means it's trying to sync with the phone line, and solid means synced. An **Internet** light then shows whether you're online.
- **Fiber** service uses an **ONT** (optical network terminal), often on a wall or in a garage. Look for **Power**, **PON** and **LOS** lights. **LOS** (loss of signal) lit up, often red, usually means no light is reaching the box from the fiber line. Don't unplug or bend the fiber cable. Contact your provider.

## Online light off? What to do

1. **Check power and wait.** Make sure all the lights have finished starting up, which can take a few minutes.
2. **Restart the modem.** Unplug it for 30 seconds, plug it back in and wait for the lights to settle. Use the power plug, not the reset pinhole, which erases settings.
3. **Check the coax cable.** It should be finger-tight at the modem and at the wall. Remove any extra splitters between the wall and the modem as a test.
4. **Check for an outage** with your provider's app or website, using your phone on mobile data.
5. **Check your account.** A new or replacement modem must be activated by your provider before it will go online.
6. **Contact your provider** if downstream or upstream keeps blinking after all of this. They can see the signal levels on your line.

If it connects but keeps dropping, see [why your internet keeps disconnecting](/networking/internet-keeps-disconnecting/).

## When to call your internet provider

Call your provider if:

- The online light won't go solid after a restart and cable checks, and there's no outage reported.
- The downstream or upstream light keeps blinking, which often means a line or signal problem they need to fix.
- A fiber box shows a **LOS** or red alarm light.
- You just installed a new modem and it never comes online. It may need activating.
- The modem gets hot, smells of burning or the power light won't come on with a working outlet. Unplug it and ask for a replacement, or replace it if you own it.
