"Reset your router" can mean two very different things. A **restart** (power cycle) turns it off and back on and fixes most everyday glitches in a couple of minutes. A **factory reset** wipes every setting and is the last resort for a forgotten password or a router that won't behave.

Always **try a restart first**. Restart the **modem before the router**, and only do a factory reset when you have the router's **default login details** at hand.

## Restart vs factory reset: what's the difference?

| | Restart (power cycle) | Factory reset |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Turns the router off and on | Erases all settings |
| Wi-Fi name and password | Kept | Back to the label defaults |
| Admin password | Kept | Back to the default |
| Time | About 2 to 5 minutes | About 10 to 30 minutes with setup |
| Use it for | Slow, dropped or stuck connections | Forgotten password, broken settings, selling or giving away the router |

## 1. Restart (power cycle) the router and modem

A restart clears the router's memory and makes it renegotiate its connection with your internet provider.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/how-to-reset-router-order.svg" alt="Step diagram: unplug the modem and router and wait 30 seconds, plug in the modem and wait for its online light, plug in the router and wait for Wi-Fi, then reconnect your devices" width="640" height="380" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Power the modem up first so the router finds a working connection.</figcaption></figure>

1. **Unplug the power** from both the modem and the router. If you have a single gateway (modem and router in one box), unplug just that.
2. **Wait about 30 seconds.**
3. **Plug in the modem first.** Wait until its online or internet light is steady, often 1 to 2 minutes. Our [modem lights guide](/networking/modem-lights-meaning/) explains what each light means.
4. **Plug in the router** and wait another 1 to 2 minutes for the Wi-Fi light.
5. **Reconnect a device** and test a website or a speed test.

Mesh systems: restart the main unit (the one wired to the modem) first, then the satellites.

## 2. Restart from the app or admin page

Many routers can restart without unplugging, which is handy if the router is hard to reach.

1. Open the router's app or type its address into a browser. The address and login are usually on the label (common addresses include 192.168.0.1 and 192.168.1.1).
2. Look for an option named Reboot, Restart or Power, often under a System, Maintenance or Advanced menu. Menu names vary by brand.
3. Confirm and wait a few minutes.

Some routers let you schedule an automatic weekly reboot in the same menu.

## 3. Before a factory reset: what you will lose

A factory reset returns everything to the defaults. Before you do it, write down or screenshot:

- Your **Wi-Fi name and password** (set them the same again and your devices reconnect on their own).
- Any **internet login** your ISP gave you (needed for PPPoE on some DSL and fiber plans).
- **Port forwarding**, reserved IP addresses, parental controls and guest network settings.
- Whether the router is **your own or rented from your ISP**. ISP gateways may need the ISP's app or a call to finish setup.

If you can still log in, some routers let you save a backup of the settings first, which you can restore afterward.

## 4. Factory reset with the reset button

The reset button is a small recessed hole, usually on the back or bottom, labeled RESET.

1. Leave the router **powered on**.
2. Press and hold the button with a straightened paperclip or SIM tool. On many routers, hold for **about 10 seconds** until the lights flash or go out. Some models need 30 seconds, so check the label or manual.
3. Let go and wait several minutes while it restarts. Don't unplug it during this time.
4. When the lights settle, the router is back to factory settings.

A quick tap usually restarts the router instead of resetting it. On some ISP gateways, the WPS button sits right next to the reset hole, so make sure you're pressing the right one.

## 5. Set the router up again

1. Connect to the default Wi-Fi name on the label, or plug a computer into a LAN port with an Ethernet cable.
2. Open the router's app or admin page and sign in with the default login on the label.
3. **Change the admin password** straight away to something only you know.
4. Set your Wi-Fi name and password. Using the same ones as before saves you reconnecting every device.
5. Re-enter your ISP login if needed, and set up port forwarding or parental controls again.
6. Check for a firmware update and turn on automatic updates if offered. Our [router security guide](/networking/secure-home-wifi-router/) covers the other settings worth changing.

## 6. Resetting a modem or ISP gateway

Modems have a reset button too, but a factory reset can erase settings your ISP needs, and some providers have to re-provision the modem afterward. For a modem or gateway you rent, **restart it rather than factory resetting it**, and call your ISP or use its app if a restart doesn't help. Many ISP apps also have a restart option that runs a line check at the same time.

## 7. How often should you restart your router?

There's no fixed rule. Restart it whenever the connection feels slow or stuck, after a power outage if things don't come back normally, and after a firmware update if the router asks you to.

If you find yourself restarting it every few days, that's a sign of a deeper problem rather than normal behavior. Common reasons include:

- **Overheating:** the router is in a closed cabinet, on top of other electronics or in direct sun. Move it somewhere open with airflow around it.
- **Old firmware:** check for an update in the app or admin page.
- **An aging router:** older models can struggle with the number of phones, TVs and smart home devices in a modern home. If it's several years old and still drops out after these steps, a newer router or a mesh system may be the real fix.

## Reset didn't fix the problem?

- **Connected but no internet:** the problem may be with your ISP or the modem. Check for an outage in your ISP's app or website, then follow our guide to [Wi-Fi connected but no internet](/networking/wifi-connected-no-internet/).
- **Drops every so often:** check for overheating (give the router space and airflow), an old or loose cable, or interference from where it sits.
- **Router won't power on or lights stay off:** try a different outlet and check the adapter is firmly plugged into the router. If the lights still stay off, the power adapter or the router itself has likely failed.
- **One device can't connect:** forget the Wi-Fi network on that device and join it again.

## When to call your ISP

Contact your internet provider if:

- The modem's online light never goes steady after a restart.
- You reset an ISP gateway and can't get it back online.
- Your connection needs a login you don't have.
- Outages or slow speeds keep coming back even with a fresh restart. They can test the line from their end.
