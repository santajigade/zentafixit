When your refrigerator ice maker stops making ice, the cause is usually simple: it's switched off, the freezer is too warm, or water isn't reaching the mold. Parts do fail, but they are rarely the first thing to blame.

The most common causes are **the shutoff arm or ice setting turned off**, **a freezer that's too warm**, **a closed or kinked water line**, **a clogged water filter**, and **a frozen fill tube**. You can check every one of these yourself in under an hour.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the refrigerator before thawing ice near the ice maker, and keep a hair dryer on low, moving, and away from plastic parts and standing water. Don't chip at ice with a knife or screwdriver.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the switch.** Look for a wire arm on the side of the ice maker or an ice on/off setting on the control panel.
2. **Check the freezer temperature.** Put a thermometer in the freezer for a few hours. It should read close to 0 °F.
3. **Try the water dispenser** if your fridge has one.
    - **No water or a weak trickle:** start with causes 3 to 5.
    - **Water flows fine:** go to causes 6 to 8.
4. **Look inside the ice maker.** Is the mold empty, full of frozen cubes, or covered in a sheet of ice?

## 1. The ice maker is switched off

Most ice makers in a freezer have a **wire shutoff arm** (also called a bail arm). When the arm is raised, the ice maker stops. Others use a paddle sensor, a rocker switch, or an **ice off** setting on the display. It's easy to bump the arm up while reaching for food.

**Fix:**

- Lower the wire arm fully, or switch the ice maker or ice setting back to on.
- Check that a bag or food container isn't pushing the arm up.
- Give it several hours. A full batch takes a while even when everything works.

## 2. The freezer is too warm

Ice makers only harvest once the cubes are frozen solid, and a sensor in the mold waits for a cold enough reading. If the freezer runs warm, ice comes slowly or not at all.

**Fix:**

- Set the freezer to **0 °F**, the setting the FDA recommends for food storage.
- Keep vents inside the freezer clear and don't overpack it.
- Check the door seals close tightly all the way around.
- If both sections are warm, or the fridge side is warm but the freezer is fine, see our guide to a [refrigerator not cooling](/appliances/refrigerator-not-cooling-freezer-works/).

## 3. The water supply is off or the line is kinked

The ice maker gets water from a small line, usually connected to a shutoff valve under the sink, in the basement, or in the wall behind the fridge. A valve that's partly closed, or a line kinked when the fridge was pushed back, stops the flow.

**Fix:**

1. Find the shutoff valve and make sure it's fully open (a quarter-turn valve handle sits in line with the pipe when open).
2. Pull the fridge out gently and look for a kinked or crushed line. Straighten it, or replace it if it's creased.
3. If you have a **saddle valve** (a small clamp-on valve pierced into a pipe), these often clog or leak. Many plumbers replace them with a standard shutoff valve.

## 4. A clogged or badly seated water filter

An old filter slowly clogs and cuts the water flow, so the mold fills only partly or not at all. A new filter that isn't fully locked in can block water completely.

**Fix:**

- Replace the filter if it's past the interval in your manual. Many makers suggest about every 6 months, but check yours.
- Make sure it twists or clicks fully into place. Some fridges need the old filter's bypass plug removed first.
- After a new filter, run a few gallons through the dispenser to clear air from the line, as the filter instructions usually say.
- To test, some fridges can run without the filter or with a bypass plug. Check your manual first.

## 5. Low water pressure

The water inlet valve needs a minimum pressure to open properly. Many manuals list a minimum around 20 psi, but check yours. Low pressure gives **small, hollow or misshapen cubes** and a slow dispenser.

**Fix:**

- Check other taps in the house. If they're weak too, see our guide to [low water pressure](/plumbing/low-water-pressure/).
- A **reverse osmosis** system feeding the fridge often lowers pressure. Ask the installer about a booster pump or a direct line.
- Make sure the shutoff valve is fully open, not just cracked open.

## 6. A frozen fill tube

The fill tube carries water from the back of the fridge into the ice mold. If the freezer runs too cold, or the water valve drips, the tube can freeze shut. You'll often see a lump of ice where the tube enters the mold.

**Fix:**

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** and turn off the ice maker.
2. Remove the ice bin and put towels below the ice maker.
3. Warm the area where the tube enters with a hair dryer on low for a few minutes, keeping it moving. Some people use a turkey baster with warm water instead. Either way, keep water away from the ice maker's wiring.
4. Once clear, plug the fridge back in and turn the ice maker on.

If it freezes again within days, the inlet valve may be leaking slowly (cause 8), or the freezer may be set too cold.

## 7. Ice jammed in the bin or ejector

Ice that sits for weeks can melt slightly and fuse into one big lump. The auger (the spiral that pushes ice to the door) can't turn, or the full bin holds the sensor arm up so the ice maker thinks the bin is full.

**Fix:**

- Remove the bin and break up or empty the clumped ice in the sink.
- Look for a cube stuck in the ejector arms or mold and remove it gently by hand once it softens.
- Use ice regularly, or empty the bin now and then, so cubes don't fuse.
- Frequent clumping can mean the freezer warms up too often, so check the door seal and the temperature.

## 8. A failed water inlet valve or ice maker module

If water reaches the dispenser but never fills the mold, the **water inlet valve** (usually at the bottom back of the fridge) may have failed for the ice maker side. If the mold fills and freezes but never dumps the ice, the **ice maker module** itself has likely failed.

**Fix:**

- Some ice makers have a test button. If your manual describes one, use it and listen for the motor and a water fill.
- Replacement modules and valves are sold by model number. Look for the model label inside the fridge, often on a side wall or the ceiling of the fresh food section.
- Replacing a module is often a few screws and a plug. Replacing the inlet valve means working on the water and power connections at the back, so many people leave it to a technician.

## Water leaking or ice maker overflowing?

- **Water on the floor behind the fridge:** turn off the water valve and check the line and its connection to the fridge. A cracked line or a loose fitting needs replacing.
- **Ice maker overflows or the bin fills with frozen water:** the inlet valve may not be closing, or the fridge isn't level. Turn the ice maker off and call a technician if leveling doesn't help.
- **The fridge itself isn't cooling:** deal with that first. The ice maker can't work until the freezer stays near 0 °F.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call an appliance technician if:

- The water inlet valve needs replacing and you're not comfortable working on water and electrical connections at the back.
- The fill tube keeps freezing after you've thawed it and checked the freezer setting.
- Water leaks inside the fridge or onto the floor and you can't find a loose fitting.
- The freezer itself won't hold temperature.
- Your refrigerator is still under warranty. Opening it yourself may affect coverage, so check the terms first.
