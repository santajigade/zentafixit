When your iPhone storage is full, you get **"Storage Almost Full"** warnings, the camera won't save, apps won't update and iOS updates won't install. The good news is that most of the space is usually taken by a few big things you can clear safely.

The usual culprits are **photos and videos**, **Messages attachments**, **large apps and their downloads**, and a growing **System Data** section. Start by finding out what's actually using the space, then work down the list.

> **Back up first:** Before deleting photos, videos or messages, make sure you have a backup in iCloud or on a computer. Deleted items in Recently Deleted can't be recovered once you empty it.

## How to find what's filling it: quick check

1. **Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage** and wait a moment for the bar to load.
2. **Read the colored bar.** It shows Apps, Photos, Messages, iOS, System Data and other sections.
3. **Scroll the app list.** Apps are sorted by size, with the largest at the top. Tap any app to see how much is the app and how much is its documents and data.
4. **Note the top three.** Fixing just the biggest few sections usually solves the problem.

## 1. See what's using space in iPhone Storage

The iPhone Storage screen also shows **recommendations** near the top, such as offloading unused apps or reviewing large attachments. These are tailored to your phone.

**Fix:**

- Tap **Show All** under recommendations if you see it, and read each one before enabling it.
- Look for apps with large **Documents & Data**. Streaming, podcast, music, map and messaging apps often store gigabytes of downloads. Delete downloads inside those apps.
- Delete apps you no longer use at all.

## 2. Offload apps you rarely use

**Offloading** removes the app but keeps its documents and data. The icon stays on your Home Screen with a small cloud symbol, and tapping it reinstalls the app.

**Fix:**

- **One app:** in iPhone Storage, tap the app and choose **Offload App**.
- **Automatically:** turn on **Offload Unused Apps** in the recommendations (or in the App Store section of Settings). iOS then offloads apps you haven't used in a while when space runs low.

Offloading frees the app size only. If most of an app's space is Documents & Data, clear downloads inside the app instead.

## 3. Clear Messages attachments

Years of photos, videos and voice notes in iMessage and text threads can take many gigabytes.

**Fix:**

1. In iPhone Storage, tap **Messages**. Use **Review Large Attachments** or the categories listed there (such as photos, videos or GIFs).
2. Swipe left on items you don't need, or tap Edit to select several, and delete them.
3. To stop the buildup, go to **Settings > Messages > Keep Messages** and choose **1 Year** or **30 Days**. This deletes older messages and their attachments, so back up anything you want first.
4. Long group chats with lots of media are often the biggest. Deleting a whole conversation removes all its attachments.

## 4. Optimize storage with iCloud Photos

Photos and videos are usually the largest section. **iCloud Photos** with **Optimize iPhone Storage** keeps full-resolution originals in iCloud and smaller versions on the phone.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/iphone-storage-full-optimize.svg" alt="Diagram: with Optimize iPhone Storage on, full-resolution originals live in iCloud while the iPhone keeps smaller versions and downloads the original when you open a photo" width="640" height="380" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Originals stay in iCloud. Your phone keeps lighter copies.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

1. Go to **Settings > Photos** (or Settings > Apps > Photos in newer iOS versions) and turn on **iCloud Photos**.
2. Choose **Optimize iPhone Storage**.
3. Check that your iCloud plan has room for your whole library. The free plan is 5 GB, which is rarely enough for a photo library. Paid plans start at a few dollars a month.
4. Leave the phone on Wi-Fi and charging so it can finish uploading. Space is freed gradually, as iOS needs it.

Prefer not to use iCloud? Copy photos to a computer or external drive, confirm they opened correctly, then delete them from the phone.

## 5. Empty the Recently Deleted album

Deleting a photo or video doesn't free space right away. It moves to **Recently Deleted**, where it stays for about 30 days.

**Fix:**

1. Open **Photos** and find **Recently Deleted** (under Utilities or Albums, depending on your iOS version).
2. Unlock it with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode if asked.
3. Tap **Select**, then **Delete All**, or pick individual items.

Once you empty it, those items are gone from the phone (and from iCloud Photos if it's on). Recently Deleted in the **Files** app works the same way for documents.

## 6. Delete large videos

A few minutes of 4K video can take more space than hundreds of photos. Screen recordings and slow-motion clips add up too.

**Fix:**

- In Photos, open **Media Types > Videos** to see only your videos. Review long clips and delete the ones you don't need, then empty Recently Deleted.
- Lower future file sizes in **Settings > Camera > Record Video**. A lower resolution or frame rate uses less space. The **Formats** option set to **High Efficiency** also saves space.
- Curious how much space a recording uses? This [video file size calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/other/video-file-size/) gives a rough estimate from resolution, bitrate and length.

## 7. Clear browser cache and downloads

Browsers store website data, and the Files app keeps everything you've downloaded.

**Fix:**

- **Safari:** go to **Settings > Safari** (Settings > Apps > Safari in newer iOS versions) and tap **Clear History and Website Data**. This signs you out of some websites.
- **Chrome and other browsers:** clear browsing data from the browser's own settings.
- **Downloads:** open the **Files** app, check the **Downloads** folder, delete what you don't need, then empty Recently Deleted in Files.

## 8. Shrink System Data

**System Data** holds caches, logs and temporary files. It can't be deleted directly, but it often shrinks on its own after a few simple steps.

**Fix:**

1. **Restart the iPhone.** This clears some temporary files.
2. **Update iOS** when you have room. Updates often clean up old system files.
3. **Clear Safari data** (fix 7) and **delete downloads in streaming apps**, which often count toward System Data.
4. Give it a day. The number shown can drop after iOS finishes housekeeping.
5. **Last resort:** back up the iPhone, erase it (Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone) and restore from the backup. Make sure the backup is complete before you erase anything.

## 9. Move files off the phone

If you've done everything above and still need room, move large files elsewhere.

**Fix:**

- Move documents and downloads to iCloud Drive or another cloud service, or copy them to a computer.
- Copy music or video files you bought or recorded to a computer, then remove them from the phone.
- If you want a sense of how many photos or songs fit in a given space, this [data storage converter](https://zentacalculator.com/other/data-storage-converter/) helps translate MB and GB.

## Not enough space for an iOS update?

- Update from a computer using Finder (Mac) or the Apple Devices app or iTunes (Windows). This needs far less free space on the phone.
- Offload a few large apps temporarily (fix 2) and reinstall them after the update.
- Some updates prompt you to remove apps temporarily and reinstall them afterward. Follow the prompt if it appears.

## When to call a pro

Contact Apple Support or visit an Apple Store or Apple Authorized Service Provider if:

- System Data keeps growing to a large size even after restarts, updates and a restore.
- The storage bar never finishes loading, or the numbers look wrong.
- The phone restarts or freezes when storage is nearly full and clearing space doesn't help.

If you regularly run out, consider a larger iCloud plan or, at upgrade time, an iPhone with more storage.
