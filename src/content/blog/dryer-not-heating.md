When a dryer tumbles but the clothes come out cold or damp, the drum motor is working but the heat source isn't. On an **electric** dryer that means no current is reaching the heating element. On a **gas** dryer, the burner isn't lighting or staying lit.

The most common causes are a **half-tripped 240 V breaker**, a **clogged vent** that overheated the dryer and **blew the thermal fuse**, and a **burned-out heating element** or **gas igniter**. Start with the free checks before buying parts.

> **Safety first:** Unplug the dryer before removing any panel. Electric dryers run on 240 V. For a gas dryer, also turn off the gas valve behind it. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Don't bypass any fuse or thermostat: they are there to prevent fires.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check the setting.** Make sure the cycle isn't on Air Fluff, Air Dry or No Heat.
2. **Run a timed dry on high for 5 minutes**, then open the door and feel the drum and the air at the lint screen.
3. **Go outside and feel the exhaust** at the vent hood while it runs. You should feel strong, warm airflow.
    - **Weak airflow outside:** suspect the lint screen or vent (cause 3), and then the thermal fuse (cause 4).
    - **Strong airflow but cold, electric dryer:** check the breaker (cause 2), then the element and thermostats.
    - **Strong airflow but cold, gas dryer:** check the gas supply and burner (causes 7 and 8).

## 1. Wrong cycle or settings

Air-only cycles, a delicate setting with very low heat, or a damp-sensing cycle ending early can look like a heating failure.

**Fix:** Run a timed cycle on the highest heat setting with a few damp towels. If that heats, the dryer is fine.

## 2. A half-tripped 240 V breaker

An electric dryer uses a **double-pole breaker** (two handles tied together). The drum motor and controls run on 120 V from one side, but the heating element needs both sides for 240 V. If one half trips, the dryer tumbles but never heats.

**Fix:**

- At the panel, find the dryer breaker. Push it firmly to **OFF**, then back to **ON**, even if it looks fine.
- If it trips again, stop. Have the dryer or the circuit checked. Our guide on [why breakers keep tripping](/electrical/breaker-keeps-tripping/) explains the usual reasons.
- A worn or burnt power cord or outlet can also drop one leg. Look for scorch marks at the plug (power off first).

## 3. Clogged lint screen or vent

A dryer needs a lot of airflow. When the lint screen, the vent hose or the outside duct is blocked, heat builds up inside, safety thermostats shut the burner or element off, and drying takes far longer. Lint buildup is also a fire hazard.

**Fix:**

- Clean the lint screen and wash it with warm, soapy water if dryer sheets have left a film.
- Pull the dryer out and check the hose behind it for kinks or crushing. Replace flimsy plastic or foil hose with semi-rigid or rigid metal duct.
- Use a vent brush kit to clean the duct from both ends, and check the outside flap opens freely.

## 4. Blown thermal fuse

The thermal fuse is a one-time safety device, usually on the blower housing or the exhaust duct inside the dryer. When the dryer overheats, it blows and cuts the heat (on many models it also stops the whole dryer). It almost always blows **because of poor airflow**.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/dryer-not-heating-parts.svg" alt="Diagram of the air path inside an electric dryer: room air passes the heating element in its housing with a high-limit thermostat, through the drum and lint screen, past the blower, cycling thermostat and thermal fuse, and out the vent" width="640" height="420" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>The heat safety parts sit along the air path, which is why a clogged vent trips them.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix:**

1. Unplug the dryer and remove the back or front panel (check your manual for which).
2. Find the thermal fuse using the wiring diagram, often taped inside the cabinet.
3. Disconnect one wire and test it with a multimeter on continuity or the lowest ohms range. A good fuse reads close to 0 ohms. An open reading (OL) means it's blown.
4. Replace it with the exact part for your model number, and **clean the vent first**.

## 5. Burned-out heating element

On electric dryers, the element is a coil of resistance wire in a metal housing. Over time the coil can break, often after years of restricted airflow.

**Fix:**

- With the dryer unplugged, look at the coil for a visible break or a spot where it touches the housing.
- Test across the two terminals with a multimeter. An open reading means it's broken. The expected resistance is often printed on the tech sheet, and this [Ohm's law calculator](https://zentacalculator.com/physics/ohms-law/) shows how resistance relates to the current it draws.
- Replace it with the correct part for your model. A coil touching the housing is a shock and fire risk, so replace that element too.

## 6. Failed high-limit or cycling thermostat

The **cycling thermostat** turns the heat on and off to hold the drum temperature. The **high-limit thermostat** cuts the heat if it gets too hot. If either fails open, there's no heat.

**Fix:** Test each one for continuity at room temperature with the dryer unplugged. Both should normally show continuity when cool. Replace any that read open, with the correct part number. Many people replace the high-limit thermostat and thermal fuse together, since they're often sold as a kit.

## 7. Gas dryer: igniter, flame sensor or valve coils

Gas dryers use a **glow-bar igniter** that heats up and lights the gas. A **flame sensor** confirms heat, and the **gas valve coils** open the valve.

**Fix:**

- First, make sure the gas valve behind the dryer is open (handle in line with the pipe) and that other gas appliances work.
- Remove the small access panel at the front bottom (on many models) and watch through it as the dryer starts on heat.
    - **Igniter doesn't glow:** the igniter, flame sensor or a thermal fuse is likely bad.
    - **Igniter glows, then goes out with no flame:** the gas valve coils are a common cause.
- Igniters are fragile. Don't touch the glowing part. Gas valve work is best left to a qualified technician.

## 8. Timer or control board

If the fuse, element, thermostats and wiring test good, the timer or electronic control may not be sending power to the heat circuit.

**Fix:** This is harder to diagnose without the wiring diagram and live voltage tests. Consider an appliance technician, especially on newer electronic models.

## Dryer won't start at all?

- **Door switch:** if the light doesn't come on or the dryer doesn't click when you press start, the door switch may have failed.
- **Thermal fuse:** on many models a blown fuse stops the motor too, not just the heat.
- **Power:** check the breaker and the plug, and for a gas dryer, the 120 V outlet it plugs into.
- **Child lock** on electronic models: check your manual for how to turn it off.

## When to call an appliance technician

Call a technician if:

- You smell gas, or the gas dryer has no flame after you checked the gas supply.
- The breaker trips again after one reset, or you see scorch marks at the cord or outlet.
- New fuses keep blowing even after cleaning the vent.
- The dryer is under warranty, or the problem is in the control board.

If the vent run is long or goes through a roof or crawlspace, a professional vent cleaning service can clear it safely.
