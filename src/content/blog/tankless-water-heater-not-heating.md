A tankless water heater only fires when it senses water moving through it, then heats that water on the way past. If anything stops it from sensing flow, getting fuel or power, or moving heat into the water, you get cold or lukewarm water.

The most common causes are **water flow too low to switch the unit on, too many taps running at once, scale buildup, and a gas or power supply problem**. This guide covers both gas and electric tankless units.

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas, do not touch switches or try to relight anything. Leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Turn off the power (and gas, on gas units) before you remove the front cover. Electric tankless units run on 240 V at high current, often through several breakers, so treat every wire inside as live until the breakers are off.

## How to tell what's causing it: quick test

1. **Check other taps.** If no tap gets hot, start with the unit. If only one tap is cold, suspect that fixture (cause 1 or 9). For a tank heater, see our [no hot water guide](/appliances/no-hot-water/) instead.
2. **Open one hot tap fully,** with no other water running, and wait 30 seconds.
3. **Watch the display, flame icon or status light.**
    - **Dark display:** power problem or a frozen unit (cause 8, or the frozen section).
    - **Display on, but it never shows heating:** flow, gas, venting or an error (causes 1, 5, 6, 7).
    - **Fires, but water is only warm:** demand, setpoint, scale or crossed plumbing (causes 2, 3, 4, 9).
4. **Write down any error code** before you reset anything.

## 1. Flow too low to turn the heater on

Every tankless unit has a minimum activation flow. Below it, the flow sensor never tells the burner or elements to start. Many units need somewhere around a quarter to a half gallon per minute; your manual lists the exact figure. Low-flow faucets, a tap opened only slightly, and a clogged **inlet water filter screen** can all keep flow under that line.

**Fix:**

- Open the hot tap fully. If it heats at full flow but not at a trickle, that is the cause.
- Clean the faucet aerator or showerhead, which can clog with grit and scale.
- Clean the inlet filter screen. With power and gas off, close the cold isolation valve, open a hot tap to relieve pressure, then unscrew the screen (usually on the cold inlet at the bottom of the unit, check your manual). Rinse it, brush it with an old toothbrush and refit it.

## 2. Too many fixtures running at once

A tankless heater has a fixed output. The colder your incoming water, the fewer gallons per minute it can bring up to temperature. Two showers plus the dishwasher can be more than it can handle, so everything runs lukewarm.

**Fix:**

- Stagger showers, laundry and dishwasher runs.
- Expect lower output in winter, when the water coming into the house is colder.
- If it is never enough, the unit may be undersized for the house. A plumber can check its rating against your fixtures.

A short burst of cold between hot water (when you turn a tap off and back on) is a different, normal effect called the cold-water sandwich. See the FAQ below.

## 3. Temperature set too low

The setpoint may have been turned down, reset after a power cut, or limited by a separate remote controller.

**Fix:** Check the setpoint on the unit and on any wall controller. Raise it a few degrees at a time. Many homes use about 120°F; going higher increases scald risk, especially with children or older adults in the house.

## 4. Scale buildup in the heat exchanger

Hard water leaves minerals inside the heat exchanger (gas) or on the elements (electric). Scale insulates the water from the heat, so output drops slowly over months. Some units show a scale or maintenance warning.

<figure><img src="/images/posts/tankless-water-heater-not-heating-flush-setup.svg" alt="Descaling setup: the cold and hot isolation valves to the house are closed, a pump in a bucket pushes descaling solution into the cold service port, and it returns from the hot service port back to the bucket" width="640" height="440" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Most kits pump solution in on the cold side and return it from the hot side.</figcaption></figure>

**Fix (follow your manual, steps vary):**

1. Turn off power to the unit, and the gas valve on gas models.
2. Close the cold and hot isolation valves to the house.
3. Connect a hose from a small submersible pump to the cold service port, and a second hose from the hot service port back into a bucket.
4. Fill the bucket with white vinegar or the descaler your manual names, open both service ports and run the pump for the time the manual gives.
5. Drain, flush with clean water, close the service ports, clean the inlet filter screen, then open the isolation valves and restore power.

If your unit has no isolation valves with service ports, a plumber can fit them.

## 5. Gas supply problems (gas units)

The unit may fire weakly or not at all if gas is not reaching it at full pressure.

**Fix:**

- Make sure the gas valve at the unit is fully open (handle in line with the pipe).
- Check whether other gas appliances work. If none do, call your gas utility.
- **Propane:** check the tank gauge. A low tank, or a regulator struggling on a very cold day, can starve the heater.
- If it was recently installed or converted from a tank heater, the gas line may be too small. A whole-house tankless can need several times the gas of a tank heater. This needs a licensed gas fitter.

## 6. Blocked vent or air intake (gas units)

Gas tankless units need a clear exhaust vent and, on sealed units, a clear air intake. Leaves, bird nests, snow, ice or a crushed pipe can make the unit shut down to protect you.

**Fix:**

- Look at the vent and intake terminations outside. Clear snow, ice or debris you can reach safely.
- Do not run the heater with a damaged, disconnected or blocked vent, and make sure you have working carbon monoxide alarms.
- Anything beyond clearing the outside opening is a job for a pro.

## 7. Ignition or flame failure error

On gas units, an ignition or flame failure code means the unit tried to light and could not, or lost the flame. Causes include gas supply (cause 5), venting (cause 6), a dirty flame sensor or a failing igniter. Tankless units usually do not have a standing pilot like a tank heater, so our [pilot light guide](/appliances/water-heater-pilot-light-out/) applies only to tank models.

**Fix:**

- Look up the exact code in your manual or on the label inside the cover. Our guide to [reading appliance error codes](/appliances/how-to-read-appliance-error-codes/) explains how to find it. Do not guess from codes for a different brand.
- Turn the unit off and on once (or use its reset). If the same code returns, stop and call a technician.

## 8. Tripped breakers (electric units)

Whole-house electric tankless units draw a lot of current, often through two or more double-pole breakers. If one trips, the unit may run on reduced power and give lukewarm water, or not run at all.

**Fix:**

- At the panel, find every breaker labeled for the water heater. Push any tripped one fully to OFF, then back to ON.
- If a breaker trips again, do not keep resetting it. That points to a wiring, element or unit fault for an electrician.
- A display that is lit does not mean every heating circuit is on, so check them all.

## 9. Crossed plumbing or a faulty mixing valve

A worn single-handle faucet or shower cartridge, or a recirculation valve, can let cold water leak into the hot line. You get lukewarm water at some or all taps even though the heater is fine.

**Fix:**

1. Close the cold water supply valve to the tankless unit.
2. Open a hot tap. Water should slow to a stop within a minute or so.
3. If water keeps flowing from the hot tap, cold is crossing into the hot line somewhere. Shut off fixtures one at a time to find it, then replace the faulty cartridge or valve.

## No hot water at all, or frozen in winter?

- **Dark display:** check the breakers or the 120 V outlet that powers a gas unit's fan and controls. A GFCI on that circuit may have tripped.
- **Frozen unit:** units in garages, attics or outside walls can freeze, especially during a power outage, because built-in freeze protection needs power. Do not run it. Restore power and let it thaw, then check for leaks. If it is cracked or leaking, call a plumber. Your manual explains how to drain it before long outages in freezing weather.

## When to call a plumber or technician

Call a licensed plumber, gas technician or electrician if:

- You smell gas, or suspect a venting problem or carbon monoxide.
- The same error code returns after one reset.
- A breaker trips again after you reset it.
- You see leaks, scorch marks or a cracked unit after a freeze.
- It never keeps up even with one tap running, after flushing and cleaning the filter.

Gas line sizing, venting and wiring on tankless units all follow manufacturer instructions and local codes, so leave those to a pro.
